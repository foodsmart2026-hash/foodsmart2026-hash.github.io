Spelt Salad with Tuna and Vegetables

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

A balanced and fresh one-pot dish that transforms humble ingredients into a complete meal, ideal for weekly meal prep. The savouriness of the tuna meets the crunchiness of seasonal vegetables for a typically Mediterranean experience.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🧒 Kid-friendly🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free

A versatile cold salad where the texture of pearled spelt meets fresh raw vegetables and savory tuna. Perfect for a quick lunch or an outdoor picnic. A balanced mix of Mediterranean aromas featuring fresh basil and zesty lemon.

Ingredients

  • Pearled spelt320 g
  • Tuna in oil (drained weight)160 g
  • Medium courgettes300 g
  • Yellow bell pepper200 g
  • Cherry tomatoes200 g
  • Red Tropea onion (sweet Italian red onion)40 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 ml
  • Lemon juice15 ml
  • Fresh basil10 g
  • Fine salt5 g

Allergens

Gluten, Fish

Method

  1. Start by rinsing 320 g of pearled spelt under cold running water using a fine-mesh sieve to remove impurities. Pour it into a pot with plenty of lightly salted water and bring it to a boil over high heat. Cook the grain for about 20-25 minutes, or following the time indicated on the package, until the grains are tender but still firm to the bite. The final result must be a well-swollen and not falling-apart grain; the chef's tip is to taste two minutes before the deadline to ensure a perfect al dente texture.
  2. Once cooked, drain the spelt and spread it out on a large tray or a flat platter, separating it gently with a fork. Add a teaspoon of extra virgin olive oil to prevent the grains from sticking together during cooling. Let it rest at room temperature, moving it occasionally to let the steam out evenly. You will get a dry and separated base, fundamental for the success of the salad; absolutely avoid rinsing it under cold water so as not to wash away its flavour and compromise its texture.
  3. Carefully wash 300 g of courgettes and 200 g of yellow pepper, then proceed to cut them into small cubes of about half a centimetre (mirepoix). In a large non-stick pan, heat a tablespoon of oil and sear the vegetables over high heat for only 4-5 minutes. The vegetables should brown slightly on the outside while remaining extremely crunchy and with bright colours. This step gives a toasted note to the dish; the chef's tip is not to cover the pan with the lid to prevent moisture from making the vegetables soft.
  4. Prepare the raw ingredients by cutting 200 g of cherry tomatoes into four parts and slicing 40 g of red onion into almost transparent veils. If you want a milder onion flavour, soak the slices in cold water and a drop of vinegar for 10 minutes before using them. The tomatoes will release a small part of their juice which will help bind the flavours of the salad. You will get a fresh and lively mix; the chef suggests salting the tomatoes only at the last moment so as not to make them lose too much water immediately.
  5. Take 160 g of drained tuna in oil and break it up coarsely with the tines of a fork in a small bowl. Do not reduce it to a mush, but try to maintain visible pieces that will give consistency and structure to the dish. Make sure to choose a good quality tuna, preferably fillets, to ensure a clean and not too metallic flavour. The tuna must be moist but not excessively oily; the chef's tip is to keep the tuna oil if it is of high quality to use a small part in the final seasoning.
  6. Prepare a tasty emulsion by combining 40 ml of extra virgin olive oil, 15 ml of filtered lemon juice and a pinch of black pepper in a jar or a small bowl. Whisk vigorously with a small whisk or shake the closed jar until you obtain a slightly thick, opaque and well-bound liquid. This citronette will serve to distribute the seasoning evenly over every grain of spelt. The appearance will be that of a silky and fragrant sauce; the chef recommends adding a teaspoon of the spelt cooking water to make the emulsion even more stable.
  7. In a large serving bowl, combine the now cold spelt, the sautéed vegetables, the cherry tomatoes, the onion and the previously prepared tuna. Pour the lemon emulsion over the ingredients and mix with wide movements from bottom to top so as not to damage the vegetables. Add 10 g of basil leaves strictly broken by hand to prevent them from blackening on contact with the knife blade. The scent of basil will be released immediately; the chef's tip is to lightly massage the spelt with clean hands while seasoning to ensure perfect distribution.
  8. Cover the bowl with cling film and let the spelt salad rest in the refrigerator for at least 30 minutes before serving. This time is necessary for the cereal to absorb the aromas of the seasoning and for the flavours to blend perfectly. Before bringing to the table, give a final stir and, if necessary, add a drizzle of raw oil to polish the dish. The salad will be harmonious, fresh and nutritious; the chef recommends serving it at room temperature rather than excessively cold to fully appreciate the sweetness of the vegetables.
  9. Visually check the final presentation: the dish must present a chromatic balance between the yellow of the peppers, the green of the courgettes and the red of the tomatoes. If the consistency seems too dry, you can add a tablespoon of natural Greek yogurt or a drop of water to soften everything without making it heavy. The portion must appear rich and inviting, perfect for a summer lunch or an office meal. The chef's secret is to garnish each dish with some freshly grated lemon zest for a final explosion of citrus freshness.

Tips

  • Cool the spelt by spreading it on a large tray to keep the grains separate and firm.
  • Drain the tuna thoroughly to better control the final balance of oil in the dressing.
  • Let the salad rest in the fridge for thirty minutes to allow the flavors to meld perfectly.

Variations

  • Add some rinsed capers to provide a stronger burst of Mediterranean savory flavor.
  • Replace the lemon juice with apple cider vinegar for a milder and more fruity acidity.
