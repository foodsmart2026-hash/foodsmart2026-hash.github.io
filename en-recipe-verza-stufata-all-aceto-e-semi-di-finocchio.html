Braised Savoy Cabbage with Vinegar and Fennel Seeds

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 30 min · Medium

A humble winter side dish that is rich in flavour thanks to the acidity of the vinegar which balances the sweetness of the cabbage. Fennel seeds provide a fresh aroma and aid digestion.

A rustic and aromatic side dish, where cabbage is softened by vinegar and fennel seeds. The stewing process makes the leaves tender and flavorful, perfect for pairing with main courses.

Ingredients

  • Savoy cabbage800 g
  • Golden onion1 pcs
  • White wine vinegar30 ml
  • Fennel seeds5 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil20 ml

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Start by preparing the Savoy cabbage (800 g): remove the tough or damaged outer leaves. Cut the head in half, place it on a chopping board, and cut out the hard white central core, as it takes much longer to cook than the leaves. Slice the remaining cabbage into thin strips, about 5 mm wide, using a sharp chef's knife. Transfer the shredded cabbage to a colander and rinse thoroughly under cold running water to remove any grit; let it drain but do not dry it completely. Chef's tip: do not discard the core; you can dice it very finely and add it to the pan to minimise food waste.
  2. Peel the golden onion, removing the papery skin and ends, then cut it in half lengthways. Place the flat side down and slice it very thinly to create almost translucent half-moons. This precise slicing ensures the onion practically melts during the stewing process, providing a delicate sweetness without overpowering the dish. Keep the onion ready in a small bowl until needed. Chef's tip: if onions make you cry, try chilling them in the fridge beforehand or wetting your knife blade with cold water before slicing.
  3. Take a wide, heavy-bottomed pan or a wok and pour in 20 ml of extra virgin olive oil, heating it over a medium-low flame. Add the sliced onion and let it soften gently for about 5 minutes, stirring occasionally with a wooden spoon. The onion should not brown or fry aggressively; it should become translucent, soft, and release a sweet aroma. If it starts to colour too quickly, add a teaspoon of water to lower the temperature. Chef's tip: the secret to a great stewed cabbage lies in this initial slow 'sweating' of the onions.
  4. Increase the heat slightly and add all the sliced cabbage to the pan. Use kitchen tongs to toss the cabbage, ensuring it is evenly coated with the oil and combined with the onions for about 3-4 minutes. You will notice that the cabbage, which initially fills the pan, will begin to wilt and release a more intense vegetal scent. Keep stirring constantly to prevent the leaves at the bottom from scorching. Chef's tip: do not worry if the pan looks overfilled; the cabbage loses a lot of water and will reduce drastically in volume within minutes.
  5. Once the cabbage is hot and slightly glossy, pour 30 ml of white wine vinegar directly over the leaves. Let the alcohol evaporate over a brisk heat for about a minute without covering the pan, until the sharp vinegary scent mellows into a pleasant tangy note. This step balances the natural sweetness of the cabbage and onions while helping to keep the vegetable's colour vibrant. Stir well to distribute the liquid throughout. Chef's tip: use a good quality vinegar, as its flavour will be a key characteristic of the finished dish.
  6. Now, turn the heat down to the lowest setting and add a pinch of salt and 5 g of fennel seeds. Scatter the seeds evenly; their warmth will release essential oils that make the cabbage much more digestible and fragrant. Stir one last time to incorporate the seasonings and cover the pan with a tight-fitting lid to trap the steam. The cabbage should now simmer gently, cooking in its own juices. Chef's tip: rub the fennel seeds between your palms before adding them to instantly activate their aromatic fragrance.
  7. Let the cabbage stew for about 20 minutes over a very low heat, checking the texture occasionally. If you notice the bottom of the pan becoming too dry or hear a loud sizzling, add two or three tablespoons of warm water to maintain a moist environment. The cabbage should be tender to the bite but not mushy, retaining a defined structure though softened. The ideal result is a dull forest green colour, moist and glossy. Chef's tip: avoid adding too much water at once to prevent 'boiling' the vegetable instead of stewing it.
  8. Once the time is up, remove the lid and taste to adjust the salt if necessary. If there is too much liquid left at the bottom, increase the heat for a minute to reduce it and lightly glaze the cabbage strips. The dish is ready when it has lost about two-thirds of its original volume and is coated in a savoury, fennel-scented base. Turn off the heat and let the dish rest for two minutes before serving to allow the flavours to settle perfectly. Chef's tip: this recipe tastes even better the next day when gently reheated in a pan.

Tips

  • Slice the cabbage thinly to ensure even and quick cooking in the pan.
  • Deglaze with vinegar over high heat to allow the sharpest part to evaporate, leaving only the aroma.
  • Let the cabbage rest covered for a few minutes after cooking to blend the flavors together.

Variations

  • Add a thinly sliced apple during cooking for a sweet and sour note.
  • Use apple cider vinegar instead of white wine vinegar for a milder taste.
