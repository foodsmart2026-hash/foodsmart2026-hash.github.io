Roasted Pumpkin Cubes with Rosemary and Honey

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

Roasted pumpkin gains a unique depth of flavour thanks to a touch of honey that enhances its natural sweetness. A simple yet visually striking autumnal side dish.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🥕 Vegetarian🌾 Gluten-free🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free

Ingredients

  • Pumpkin (Delica or Mantovana variety - typical Italian firm-fleshed pumpkins)900 g
  • Acacia honey15 g
  • Fresh rosemary3 sprigs
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Coarse sea salt5 g
  • Black pepperto taste

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Start by carefully cleaning 900g of Delica or Mantovana pumpkin, removing the seeds and internal fibres with a sturdy spoon. Peel the skin only if it is excessively tough, then cut the flesh into regular 3cm cubes using a sharp chef's knife. Precision in cutting is essential as it ensures all pieces cook evenly. You will have bright orange cubes with clean, geometric shapes. Chef's Tip: don't throw away the seeds; you can wash and toast them separately for a crunchy, healthy snack.
  2. In a small bowl or saucepan, pour 30ml of extra virgin olive oil and add 15g of acacia honey. If the honey is too thick, warm it slightly in a bain-marie or microwave for a few seconds until it becomes fluid and easy to emulsify. Mix vigorously with a small whisk or fork to create a smooth, glossy emulsion. The result should be a fluid dressing with a delicate scent of flowers and olives. Chef's Tip: ensure the honey is well dissolved in the oil to prevent it from settling on only a few pumpkin cubes.
  3. Transfer the pumpkin cubes into a large bowl and pour over the previously prepared oil and honey emulsion. Add the needles from 3 sprigs of fresh rosemary, ensuring they are well distributed, and finish with a generous grinding of fresh black pepper. Mix everything with your hands or two wooden spoons, making sure every single side of each cube is perfectly coated in the aromatic dressing. The pumpkin should look glossy and release an intense balsamic aroma. Chef's Tip: gently massaging the pumpkin with your hands helps the dressing penetrate the surface fibres better.
  4. Line a large baking tray with parchment paper and arrange the pumpkin cubes in a single layer, making sure to space them at least one centimetre apart. It is essential not to overlap them, otherwise the pumpkin will steam in its own moisture, becoming soggy instead of roasting properly. This strategic arrangement allows the oven heat to hit every side of the cube evenly, promoting browning. The tray should look tidy and colourful, ready for the baking phase. Chef's Tip: if the tray is too small, use two to avoid compromising the final crispness.
  5. Take 5g of coarse salt and sprinkle it evenly over the pumpkin cubes already arranged on the tray. Coarse salt is not just for seasoning; it has the technical function of drawing internal moisture outwards through osmosis during cooking, facilitating the formation of a tasty crust. This process will intensify the contrast between the sweetness of the pumpkin and honey and the saltiness of the salt. You will see the salt grains remain visible on the surface, adding a pleasant crunchy note. Chef's Tip: avoid fine salt, as coarse salt ensures a superior texture and a more gradual release of saltiness.
  6. Preheat the oven to 200°C (static mode) and bake the tray on the middle rack for about 15 minutes. In this first phase, the flesh will begin to soften and the scents of rosemary and honey will start to spread intensely through the kitchen. Do not open the door frequently to avoid losing the heat necessary to stabilise the cooking. The result will be pumpkin that begins to change texture, becoming tender at the base. Chef's Tip: if your oven is very powerful or fan-assisted, lower the temperature to 180°C to prevent the rosemary tips from burning.
  7. Carefully monitor the cooking during the last 5-10 minutes, checking that the edges of the cubes begin to caramelise, taking on an inviting golden-brown colour. If you notice the honey is browning too quickly due to the sugars, lower the temperature by 10 degrees and move the tray to a lower oven rack. The pumpkin is perfectly cooked when, pressing with a fork, the flesh is tender but perfectly maintains its cubic shape. You should achieve crispy edges and a creamy heart that melts in the mouth. Chef's Tip: caramelisation is the critical point of the recipe; stay near the oven during this final phase.
  8. Remove the tray from the oven and let the pumpkin rest for at least 5 minutes at room temperature before serving. This short rest allows the internal structure to stabilise and the outer crust to become even firmer as the honey cools. Transfer the cubes to a warm serving dish using a flat spatula to avoid breaking them, optionally garnishing with the crispy rosemary from the tray. The final dish will be a gourmet side dish with a balanced flavour of sweet, salty, and aromatic notes. Chef's Tip: serve it as an accompaniment to fresh goat's cheese for an unforgettable flavour pairing.
