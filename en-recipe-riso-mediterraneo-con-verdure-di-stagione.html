Mediterranean Rice with Seasonal Vegetables

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 45 min · Medium

A balanced main course combining the complex carbohydrates of brown rice with plant-based protein from chickpeas. An economical and versatile recipe, perfect for the family and rich in fibre thanks to fresh vegetables.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🌱 Vegan🌾 Gluten-free🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free

Ingredients

  • Brown rice320 g
  • Pre-cooked chickpeas (jarred)240 g
  • Medium courgettes300 g
  • Cherry tomatoes200 g
  • Yellow onion60 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 ml
  • Vegetable stock900 ml
  • Fresh parsley10 g
  • Lemon (zest)1 pc
  • Salt2 g

Allergens

Celery

Method

  1. Start by thoroughly washing 300g of courgettes and 200g of cherry tomatoes under cold running water. Using a sharp chef's knife and a stable cutting board, dice the courgettes into even 1cm cubes and quarter the tomatoes. This precise cutting, which takes about 5 minutes, is not just for aesthetics but ensures that all ingredients cook at the same rate. You should end up with firm courgette cubes and tomato segments ready to release their juices. Chef's tip: try to dice the vegetables to a size similar to the rice grains to achieve perfect harmony in every spoonful.
  2. Prepare the aromatic base by finely chopping 60g of yellow onion with a kitchen knife. In a wide pan or a heavy-bottomed casserole, heat half of the extra virgin olive oil (20ml) along with two tablespoons of vegetable broth. Sweat the onion over a medium-low heat for about 5 minutes, stirring frequently with a wooden spoon to prevent browning. The onion should become translucent, soft, and fragrant, creating a sweet and digestible base for your rice. Chef's tip: adding a little broth at the beginning allows the onion to stew gently without frying, keeping the nutrients of the oil intact.
  3. Pour 320g of wholegrain rice into the pan with the onion and toast it over a high heat for about 2 minutes. Stir continuously to ensure every grain is coated in the oil and receives heat evenly. The rice will be perfectly toasted when the grains look glossy, slightly translucent at the edges, and feel very hot to the touch. This process is vital as it seals the grain's surface, preventing excessive starch release and keeping the rice 'al dente'. Chef's tip: do not rush this stage; proper toasting is the secret to wholegrain rice that maintains a firm, distinct texture.
  4. Begin the actual cooking by adding 900ml of boiling vegetable broth, one ladle at a time, just covering the surface of the rice. Adjust the heat so the simmer is constant but very gentle, and continue cooking according to the package instructions, roughly for the first 18-20 minutes. Stir occasionally to encourage even liquid absorption, without overworking it to avoid breaking the grains. The rice will gradually swell, absorbing the broth's flavour and releasing a toasted aroma. Chef's tip: always keep the broth at a light simmer in a small pot nearby; adding cold broth would stop the cooking process and ruin the texture.
  5. After the first 20 minutes of cooking the rice, add the 300g of diced courgettes to the pan. Stir gently with an upward motion to incorporate them evenly with the rice. Continue to cook over a low heat for another 10 minutes, adding more broth if necessary to maintain the desired moisture. The courgettes should cook until tender but still firm to the bite, retaining a bright green colour—a sign that the fibres and vitamins have been preserved. Chef's tip: adding the courgettes halfway through prevents them from turning into a mush, maintaining a pleasant 'bite' that contrasts with the softness of the rice.
  6. While the rice finishes cooking, rinse 240g of canned chickpeas under cold running water in a fine-mesh sieve. Continue rinsing until the water runs clear to remove the canning liquid and excess sodium. Add the chickpeas to the pan about 5 minutes before the rice finishes cooking, stirring well to combine. The chickpeas only need to heat through and absorb the flavours, integrating perfectly with the other ingredients without falling apart. Chef's tip: thoroughly rinsing canned legumes drastically reduces preservatives and salt, making the dish much healthier and easier to digest.
  7. In the final 3 minutes of cooking, when the broth is almost entirely absorbed, stir in the 200g of quartered cherry tomatoes. Mix with extreme gentleness to avoid crushing the tomatoes, which should only wilt slightly from the residual heat. At this stage, the tomatoes will release a fresh, acidic note that balances the natural sweetness of the chickpeas and courgettes. The visual result will be a celebration of Mediterranean colours, with the red popping against the green and the golden rice. Chef's tip: add the tomatoes only at the end because their acidity, if released too early, could slow down the optimal cooking of the rice grains.
  8. Turn off the heat when the rice is perfectly al dente and the cooking liquid is creamy but not watery. Add the remaining 20ml of raw extra virgin olive oil and the grated zest of one unwaxed lemon, avoiding the bitter white pith. Stir vigorously for one minute (the 'mantecatura' stage) to emulsify the fats with the remaining starch, achieving an inviting gloss. The lemon scent will immediately enhance the freshness of the ingredients, providing an irresistible citrus note. Chef's tip: lemon zest is the secret ingredient that transforms a simple vegetable rice dish into a restaurant-quality meal; never forget it.
  9. Complete the dish by adding 10g of freshly chopped parsley and 2g of salt, if necessary after tasting. Cover the pan with a lid and let the rice rest off the heat for about 2 minutes before serving. This short rest allows the flavours to stabilise and the fibres of the wholegrain rice to relax, ensuring a silky and uniform consistency. Serve the rice in warm shallow bowls, garnishing with an extra sprinkle of lemon zest if desired. Chef's tip: the final rest is the professional's touch; it ensures the heat distributes perfectly from the centre to the edge of every grain.
