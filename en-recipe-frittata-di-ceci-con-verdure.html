Chickpea Frittata with Vegetables

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 35 min · Medium

A plant-based take on the classic frittata, prepared with legume flour for a balanced and protein-rich meal. This recipe is ideal for an economical family dinner, using humble but nutrient-rich ingredients typical of the Mediterranean diet.

Inspired by Ligurian tradition, this chickpea frittata with zucchini and red onion is fragrant and flavorful. The sweetness of Tropea onion pairs with the aroma of marjoram in a soft-textured dish. Ideal served in slices for an appetizer or a light lunch.

Ingredients

  • Chickpea flour (Gram flour)250 g
  • Still water600 ml
  • Green courgettes400 g
  • Tropea red onion (sweet Italian red onion)100 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 ml
  • Fine salt6 g
  • Ground black pepper2 g
  • Fresh or dried marjoram2 g

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Begin by carefully sifting 250g of chickpea flour into a large bowl to eliminate any lumps. Gradually pour in 600ml of still water, whisking vigorously with a manual whisk in circular motions starting from the centre. You should achieve a very liquid, smooth, and perfectly homogeneous batter, free from any floury residue. My professional advice is to add the water very slowly at first to create a thick paste, then thin it out to prevent stubborn lumps from forming.
  2. Allow the batter to rest for 40 to 60 minutes at room temperature, covering the bowl with a clean cloth. This stage is crucial as it allows the chickpea flour fibres to hydrate fully, significantly improving the final texture and making the dish much easier to digest. You will notice a light natural foam forming on the surface, which will be removed later. Do not rush this: a prolonged rest ensures an omelette that doesn't crumble when sliced.
  3. While the batter rests, prepare the vegetables: wash 400g of courgettes, trim them, and cut them into regular cubes about half a centimetre wide. Take 100g of Tropea red onion, peel it, and slice it thinly with a knife or a mandoline for uniform slivers. A consistent size for the courgette cubes ensures they cook evenly without some pieces remaining raw. Remember that precision in cutting translates to a better mouthfeel.
  4. In a large non-stick frying pan, heat 10ml of extra virgin olive oil over a low heat and add the sliced onion. Let it sauté gently for about 5 minutes, stirring occasionally, until it becomes translucent, glossy, and releases its sweet aroma. It is important not to burn the onion, so add a tablespoon of water if necessary to aid softening without excessive browning. The result should be a soft and fragrant aromatic base.
  5. Turn the heat up slightly and add the courgette cubes to the pan with the onion, adding a pinch of salt from the total amount. Sauté the vegetables over a brisk heat for about 7-8 minutes, tossing the pan frequently so they brown slightly on the outside while remaining crunchy inside. Do not cover with a lid, as the trapped moisture will make them soggy and grey. Aim for a bright green colour with small golden highlights on the edges of the cubes.
  6. Return to the chickpea batter and, using a slotted spoon or a regular spoon, gently skim off the white foam that has settled on the surface. Add the remaining 6g of fine salt, 2g of ground black pepper, and 2g of fresh or dried marjoram, mixing well with the whisk. Finally, stir in the sautéed vegetables, now lukewarm, incorporating them evenly into the liquid. Make sure not to skip skimming the foam, as it can impart a slightly bitter and unpleasant aftertaste.
  7. Prepare a round baking tin (24-26cm in diameter) by greasing it generously with the remaining 30ml of extra virgin olive oil, ensuring the sides are well coated. Pour the chickpea and vegetable mixture into the tin and use a spatula to level the surface, ensuring the courgettes are evenly distributed and not clumped in the middle. The ideal thickness should be about 1.5-2cm for perfect cooking. A chef's trick: slightly warm the greased tin before pouring the mixture to create an instant non-stick base.
  8. Place the tin in a static oven preheated to 200°C and bake for approximately 25-30 minutes on the middle rack. The omelette is ready when the surface is well golden, with characteristic small cracks appearing, and the edges begin to pull away clearly from the sides of the tin. If it still looks too pale after 25 minutes, switch on the grill function for the final 2 minutes for an irresistible crust. Let it cool for 5-10 minutes before slicing: the structure will set and the flavour will be more intense.

Tips

  • Let the chickpea batter rest for the specified time to allow the flour to hydrate properly and remove lumps.
  • Carefully remove any foam that forms on the surface of the batter for a more even cooking result.
  • Ensure the pan is very hot and lightly greased before pouring the mixture to prevent the frittata from sticking.

Variations

  • Replace the zucchini with small diced bell peppers for a more vibrant and colorful taste.
  • You can use chopped rosemary instead of marjoram to evoke the classic flavors of farinata.
