Forest Fruit Sweet Schiacciata

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

A soft sweet flatbread that uses jam as both filling and decoration. It is a simple Tuscan-style dessert, perfect for those who like treats that aren't too cloying.

A sweet twist on the classic Italian flatbread, featuring a soft dough with a delicious jam filling. The use of extra virgin olive oil provides a rustic note that pairs perfectly with the vibrant taste of forest fruits.

Ingredients

  • Strong white bread flour (Type 0)400 g
  • Warm water250 ml
  • Caster sugar60 g
  • Dried yeast (Italian 'lievito di birra')5 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 ml
  • Forest fruit jam200 g

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Begin by activating the yeast: in a small bowl, pour 250ml of lukewarm water (around 30-35°C) and dissolve 5g of dried yeast with a teaspoon taken from the 60g of sugar. Stir thoroughly with a teaspoon and let the mixture rest for 5-7 minutes in a draught-free spot until a thick foam forms on the surface. This biochemical reaction indicates the yeast is active and ready to leaven your cake. You should achieve a cloudy, frothy liquid with a distinct aroma of fresh fermentation. Chef's tip: use a kitchen thermometer for the water; if it exceeds 40°C, you risk killing the yeast and the dough won't rise.
  2. Prepare the dough base by combining 400g of strong white bread flour (tipo 0) in a large bowl with the remaining sugar and 30ml of extra virgin olive oil, setting aside the final 10ml. Gradually pour the yeast-water mixture into the centre of the flour, starting to mix with a fork or wooden spoon, drawing the flour inwards. Work the mixture until the liquids are fully absorbed and a rough but cohesive mass has formed. The result should be a shaggy ball that begins to pull away from the sides of the bowl. Chef's tip: do not add all the water at once; pour it in a steady stream to allow the flour proteins to hydrate correctly without forming lumps.
  3. Transfer the mixture onto a lightly floured work surface and begin kneading vigorously with the heel of your hand for about 10 minutes. Use a stretch-and-fold technique to incorporate air and develop the gluten network, which is essential for the cake's structure. The dough should gradually become smooth, very elastic, and maintain a slight, pleasant surface tackiness. You will notice the pastry becoming silky to the touch and offering slight resistance when stretched. Chef's tip: avoid adding too much extra flour to the surface; a slightly hydrated dough ensures a much softer 'schiacciata' after baking.
  4. Place the dough in a bowl lightly greased with oil, cover it with cling film or a damp cloth, and let it rise in a warm place for about 2 hours. Ideally, place it inside an oven that is switched off but with the light on, maintaining a constant temperature between 26-28°C. The dough must visibly double in volume, and the surface should look taut and dotted with tiny air bubbles. This resting period allows the fermentation gases to expand, creating a light texture. Chef's tip: be patient; proving depends heavily on ambient temperature; if the kitchen is cold, it might need an extra 30 minutes.
  5. After the proving time, gently divide the dough into two equal parts using a bench scraper or a sharp knife. Take a rectangular baking tray (approx. 30x40 cm), grease it evenly with the remaining 10ml of oil, and stretch the first half of the dough using your fingertips, working from the centre towards the edges. Aim for an even thickness of about half a centimetre without tearing the pastry, covering the entire surface of the tray. The base should appear well-distributed and slightly glossy from the oil. Chef's tip: if the dough keeps springing back as you stretch it, let it rest for 5 minutes on the tray then try again; the gluten will relax and be more cooperative.
  6. Spread roughly 100g of the mixed berry jam over the stretched base, using the back of a spoon to level it out evenly. Ensure you leave a clean border of about 2 centimetres all around the edge to allow for a perfect seal with the top layer. The jam should create a bright, fragrant film without being excessive, to prevent the dough from becoming too soggy in the middle. The result will be a vibrant fuchsia base ready to be covered. Chef's tip: choose a high-quality jam with whole fruit pieces for a more rustic texture and a stronger aromatic punch.
  7. Roll out the second half of the dough on a sheet of baking parchment, trying to match the shape of the tray, then gently flip it over onto the jam layer. Remove the parchment and join the edges of the two dough layers by pressing firmly with your fingertips to seal the filling inside. Using your fingers, create deep dimples across the entire surface of the cake—typical of Tuscan schiacciata—which will hold the final topping. The surface should look irregular and wavy, with the edges tightly closed to prevent leaks. Chef's tip: don't be afraid to press down firmly with your fingers; the dimples prevent the pastry from puffing up like a balloon during baking.
  8. Complete the preparation by dotting the remaining 100g of jam into the dimples and across the surface, letting the dough show through. Preheat a static oven to 200°C and bake the tray on the middle rack for about 20-25 minutes. Monitor the bake until the schiacciata turns a deep golden brown and the sugar in the surface jam starts to caramelise slightly, releasing a scent of sweet bread and berries. The exterior texture should be slightly crisp while the heart remains soft. Chef's tip: in the last 5 minutes, you can switch to the fan setting for a more even browning and an extra touch of crunch.
  9. Remove the schiacciata from the oven and, while still piping hot, brush it lightly with a touch of extra virgin olive oil or a tablespoon of sugar water to keep the crust soft and shiny. Let it cool slightly in the tray for about 15 minutes before cutting it into generous rectangles to serve. When sliced, you will see the layer of jam encased between two soft veils of leavened dough. The cake will be fragrant, with a perfect balance between the tartness of the berries and the sweetness of the dough. Chef's tip: do not cut it immediately after baking; the residual heat helps stabilise the internal jam, which would otherwise run out too much.

Tips

  • Ensure the water is lukewarm and not hot to avoid damaging the activity of the dry yeast.
  • Let the dough rise in a warm spot away from drafts to ensure it develops perfectly.
  • Brush the top with a thin layer of olive oil before baking to achieve an even and golden finish.

Variations

  • Use cherry or apricot jam instead of forest fruits to change the sweetness profile of the flatbread.
  • Sprinkle coarse sugar pearls on the surface before baking to add a pleasant crunchy texture.
