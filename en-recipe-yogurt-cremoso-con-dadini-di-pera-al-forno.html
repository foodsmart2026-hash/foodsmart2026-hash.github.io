Creamy Yogurt with Oven-Baked Pear Cubes

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 10 min · Easy

A classic pairing made safe by cooking the fruit until it is incredibly tender. Full-fat yogurt provides the necessary fats for children's growth in this age group.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🥕 Vegetarian🧒 Kid-friendly🌾 Gluten-free

A simple dessert or fresh snack combining creamy plain yogurt with the natural sweetness of Abate or Conference pears. Gently simmering the fruit creates tender cubes that offer a delightful contrast to the smooth yogurt base.

Ingredients

  • Full-fat natural white yogurt400 g
  • Ripe Abate or Conference pears2 pcs
  • Water20 ml

Allergens

Milk

Method

  1. Start by thoroughly washing the 2 pears under running water, then peel them using a vegetable peeler to minimise waste and achieve a smooth surface. Remove the core and any fibrous central parts using a sharp paring knife or a corer, ensuring all woody residues are eliminated. You should end up with clean fruit flesh, ready to be processed with precision. Chef's tip: use pears that are fully ripe yet firm, such as Abate or Conference, so they hold their shape without breaking down too much during cooking.
  2. Dice the pear flesh into regular cubes of about half a centimetre, ensuring they are a safe and uniform size. This cutting technique guarantees even cooking and a pleasant mouthfeel, making them ideal for mixing with the yoghurt. Place the pear on a stable chopping board and work steadily to achieve perfect cubes. Chef's tip: keep the cubes small to allow the fruit's natural sugars to release more effectively, creating a perfect contrast with the yoghurt's acidity.
  3. Transfer the pear cubes into a heavy-bottomed saucepan and add the 20 ml of water (approximately two tablespoons). Immediately cover with a tight-fitting lid to trap the moisture and steam inside. Turn the heat to a very low setting to begin the process of softening the fruit flesh. Chef's tip: do not add extra sugar, as the natural sweetness of ripe pears is more than sufficient for a balanced and healthy flavour.
  4. Cook the fruit for approximately 10 minutes, taking care to stir occasionally with a wooden spoon to prevent the cubes from sticking to the bottom. The heat must remain gentle to allow the fibres to break down softly without scorching or over-caramelising. Towards the end of the specified time, observe how the water is absorbed by the fruit. Chef's tip: if you notice the water evaporating too quickly before the 10 minutes are up, add a teaspoon of warm water to maintain a moist cooking environment.
  5. Check the progress of the fruit: the pear is ready when the cubes become slightly translucent and squash easily with light pressure from a fork. Turn off the heat as soon as you achieve this tender consistency, preventing the pieces from turning into a purée. The fruit should be soft but still clearly defined to the eye. Chef's tip: do not overcook beyond what is necessary, or you will lose the pleasant textural contrast between the fruit cube and the yoghurt cream.
  6. Carefully drain any residual water using a fine-mesh sieve to avoid thinning the yoghurt during the assembly stage. Allow the pear cubes to cool completely at room temperature; you can spread them on a flat plate to speed up the process. This step is vital: adding hot fruit to the yoghurt would irreparably alter its protein structure and creaminess. Chef's tip: the drained cooking liquid is a flavour concentrate; do not discard it, as you can use it to naturally sweeten a drink.
  7. Pour the 400 g of plain whole milk yoghurt into a large bowl and work it vigorously for a few moments with a spoon or a small whisk. Stir with circular motions until the mixture looks glossy, fluid, and completely free of surface lumps. The yoghurt should take on the appearance of a velvety and inviting cream. Chef's tip: use yoghurt that has been at room temperature for a few minutes before use to fully appreciate the richness of the lactic flavour.
  8. Add the now-cold pear cubes to the yoghurt base, folding everything together with a gentle upward motion to keep the mixture light. Distribute the preparation into four small cups or bowls, ensuring the fruit is shared equally in each portion. Serve immediately to enjoy the freshness or store in the fridge covered with cling film. Chef's tip: for a restaurant-style presentation, set aside a couple of pear cubes to decorate the centre of each bowl before serving.

Tips

  • Drain the pears thoroughly after cooking to prevent excess liquid from making the yogurt watery.
  • Choose ripe but firm pears to ensure the cubes hold their shape during preparation.
  • Let the fruit cool completely before mixing it with the yogurt to preserve its texture.

Variations

  • You can replace the pears with Golden apples following the same cooking process.
  • Add a pinch of cinnamon while cooking the pears for a more intense aromatic note.
