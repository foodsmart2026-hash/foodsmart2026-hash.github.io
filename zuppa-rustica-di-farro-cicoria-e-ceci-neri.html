Rustic pearled spelt, chicory and black chickpea soup

Puglia · 4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 45 min · Medium

A hearty soup where the bitterness of wild chicory meets the texture of black chickpeas and pearled spelt in an autumnal embrace.

🌿 Seasonal recipe🇮🇹 Italian

A robust and comforting soup celebrating the flavors of the land and Italian peasant tradition. The combination of black chickpeas and pearled spelt creates a dense texture, enriched by the bold note of chicory. An ideal one-pot meal for cold days.

Ingredients

  • Wild chicory or catalogna700g
  • Pearled spelt200g
  • Black chickpeas (already soaked and boiled)250g
  • Vegetable stock1.2L
  • Garlic2 cloves
  • Fresh chilli1
  • Extra virgin olive oil60ml
  • Grated Pecorino Romano PDO40g
  • Bay leaves2 leaves
  • Salt and black pepperto taste

Allergens

Gluten, Milk
May contain traces of: Celery.

Method

  1. Start by thoroughly cleaning 700g of chicory, removing the tough ends of the stalks and cutting any particularly long leaves in half. Plunge the greens into a pot of plenty of boiling, lightly salted water and cook for about 6-7 minutes over medium heat. This step is crucial to mellow the natural bitterness of the chicory and soften its fibres. Once ready, drain well and squeeze gently to remove excess water. My chef's tip: do not overcook it at this stage, as it will finish cooking in the soup while absorbing the rich broth flavours.
  2. Take a large pot, preferably a terracotta one for even heat distribution, and heat 60ml of extra virgin olive oil. Add two slightly crushed garlic cloves and one finely chopped fresh chilli, maintaining a medium-low flame. Let them sizzle gently for a couple of minutes until the garlic turns golden and releases its full aroma, being careful not to burn it. You will obtain an intense aromatic base that will be the soul of your soup. My chef's tip: crush the garlic 'in camicia' (with the skin on) if you prefer a more subtle aroma that is easier to remove later.
  3. Pour 200g of pearled spelt into the pot, slightly increasing the heat to begin the toasting process. Stir constantly with a wooden spoon for about 2 minutes, ensuring every grain is well coated with the aromatic oil. Toasting seals the grain, allowing it to maintain an 'al dente' texture during the subsequent boiling. You will notice a pleasant nutty fragrance and see the grains become slightly translucent and shiny. My chef's tip: never skip this step; it is the secret to a professional soup texture that isn't sticky or mushy.
  4. Add 250g of pre-cooked black chickpeas and 2 bay leaves to the spelt, keeping the heat brisk for another 2 minutes. Stir carefully so the pulses absorb the flavour of the base and aromatics before adding any liquid. Black chickpeas, which are earthier and more flavourful than standard ones, will begin to release their fragrance. You will see the ingredients blending harmoniously at the bottom of the pot. My chef's tip: if using home-cooked chickpeas, add a tablespoon of their cooking water for an even deeper depth of flavour.
  5. Cover the ingredients with 1.2 litres of vegetable broth, which must be boiling hot to avoid stopping the spelt's cooking process. Lower the heat to minimum, partially cover with a lid, and let it simmer gently for about 10 minutes. The spelt will begin to swell gradually, soaking up the flavour-rich liquid. At this stage, the soup should only bubble gently on the surface—a technique we chefs call 'simmering.' My chef's tip: always keep a little extra broth aside, as spelt tends to absorb a lot of liquid even after the heat is off.
  6. After the first 10 minutes, when the spelt is roughly halfway cooked, add the blanched chicory you set aside earlier. Use a fork or spoon to distribute it evenly among the spelt grains and black chickpeas, integrating the dark green and black colours beautifully. Continue cooking for another 10 minutes or until the spelt reaches your desired texture, keeping the heat low. The chicory will finish softening by absorbing the savoury broth instead of plain water. My chef's tip: chop the chicory into 3-4 cm pieces to make the soup easier and more pleasant to eat with a spoon.
  7. Check that the spelt is tender but still has a slightly firm bite to it, and that the broth has reduced to a 'loose' but not too dry consistency. Remove the garlic cloves and bay leaves, as they have now fulfilled their aromatic duty. Adjust the salt and add a generous grind of fresh black pepper to taste, always tasting as you go to balance the flavours. The soup should appear thick, fragrant, and inviting, with a perfect balance between grains, pulses, and greens. My chef's tip: if the soup seems too thick, add a ladle of boiling broth right at this moment.
  8. Turn off the heat and proceed with the final 'mantecatura' by adding 40g of grated Pecorino Romano PDO and a final drizzle of raw extra virgin olive oil. Stir vigorously to create an emulsion that binds the ingredients together, then cover the pot and let it rest for exactly 3 minutes. This resting period is vital to allow the temperatures to equalise and the flavours to settle perfectly. Serve the soup hot, finishing with an extra pinch of pecorino in each bowl if desired. My chef's tip: never perform the mantecatura over direct heat, otherwise the cheese might become stringy instead of melting creamily.

Tips

  • Use an earthenware pot for slow and uniform heat distribution that enhances the legumes.
  • Add the chicory only halfway through the spelt cooking to maintain its color and firm texture.
  • You can store the soup in the refrigerator for two days, reheating it with a splash of water if needed.

Variations

  • Replace Pecorino Romano with Parmigiano Reggiano shavings for a sweeter and milder flavor.
  • For a more aromatic version, add a sprig of fresh rosemary while cooking the chickpeas.
