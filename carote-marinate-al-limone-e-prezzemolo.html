Lemon and Parsley Marinated Carrots

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 5 min · Easy

A fresh, crunchy and colourful side dish that enhances the natural sweetness of carrots. Ideal for accompanying structured or protein-rich main courses.

A fresh and vibrant carrot salad, ideal as a light appetizer or a side dish for summer lunches. The lemon and parsley marinade provides a citrusy note that enhances the natural sweetness of the carrots while keeping them crunchy.

Ingredients

  • Fresh carrots600 g
  • Lemon (juice and zest)1 pcs
  • Fresh parsley1 bunch

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Begin by thoroughly washing 600g of fresh carrots under cold running water to remove any soil residue. Use a vegetable peeler to strip away the outer skin with smooth, fluid strokes, then trim the ends with a sharp chef's knife. You should be left with perfectly clean, smooth, and vibrant orange roots ready for prep. Chef's tip: don't discard the peelings and ends; you can use them to enrich a homemade vegetable stock.
  2. Proceed to cut the carrots by aiming for a matchstick shape (julienne) or very thin rounds using a mandoline for maximum precision. Hold the carrot firmly against the board and maintain a consistent thickness of about 2-3mm to ensure even cooking and a refined texture. This task requires calm and precision to prevent the pieces from being too coarse. The final result should be a collection of carrots with a regular and elegant geometric shape. Chef's tip: secure your cutting board with a damp cloth underneath to prevent it from slipping while using the mandoline.
  3. Fill a large pot with water, add a pinch of salt, and bring it to a rolling boil over high heat. Once the water is boiling, plunge the carrots in and let them blanch for exactly 3-4 minutes, keeping the heat at a medium level to stabilise the temperature. The carrots should soften slightly but retain a crunchy core, that classic 'al dente' texture that preserves their nutrients. The colour will become even more vivid due to the brief heat exposure. Chef's tip: do not exceed 4 minutes, as overcooking will make the carrots mushy and unsuitable for marinating.
  4. While the carrots are cooking, prepare a large bowl filled with cold water and plenty of ice. Drain the carrots using a slotted spoon and immediately submerge them in the ice bath for about 2 minutes, moving them gently. This thermal shock instantly stops the residual cooking and sets the chlorophyll and carotene, maintaining the bright orange hue. You will have cool, firm carrots ready to absorb the dressing's flavours. Chef's tip: drain the carrots well and pat them dry with a clean tea towel after the ice bath to prevent water from diluting the emulsion.
  5. Take the lemon, finely grate the zest being careful not to include the bitter white pith, then squeeze the juice, filtering out any seeds. In a small bowl, pour the juice, grated zest, two tablespoons of high-quality extra virgin olive oil, and a pinch of sea salt. Use a small whisk or a fork to beat the ingredients vigorously until you achieve a slightly thick and opalescent emulsion. The citrus aroma should be intense and refreshing. Chef's tip: zest the lemon before squeezing it; it is much easier to handle the peel on a whole fruit.
  6. Pick the leaves from the bunch of fresh parsley, discarding the tougher stems, and wash them thoroughly before drying them perfectly with kitchen paper. Place the parsley on the board and chop it finely with a very sharp knife, using rhythmic and decisive strokes. It is crucial that the parsley is dry to prevent it from turning into a dark paste and losing its herbaceous aroma. You should obtain a light, almost bright green and fragrant dust. Chef's tip: avoid using electric mixers for parsley, as the blades heat the herb, oxidising it and altering its taste.
  7. Transfer the blanched and dried carrots into a ceramic or glass dish large enough so they don't overlap too much. Pour the lemon and oil emulsion over the carrots, distributing it evenly with the help of two spoons or kitchen tongs. Toss with gentle movements from bottom to top to ensure every carrot matchstick is perfectly glossy and coated by the dressing. The dish will begin to release a fresh and inviting fragrance. Chef's tip: use a glass container, as plastic might absorb odours or become stained by the carotene from the carrots.
  8. Cover the dish with cling film or an airtight lid and place it in the central part of the fridge for at least 30 minutes. During this resting time, the acidity of the lemon penetrates the carrot fibres, softening them further and creating a perfect balance with the vegetable's natural sweetness. Cold marinating is the secret to achieving deep flavour without altering the freshness of the ingredients. The result will be a tasty, harmonious, and refreshing side dish. Chef's tip: if you have time, let them marinate for up to two hours for an even more intense flavour.
  9. Remove the carrots from the fridge about 10 minutes before serving to bring them to a pleasant room temperature. Just before going to the table, sprinkle with the freshly chopped parsley and add a generous grind of black Tellicherry pepper for a pungent aromatic note. Toss one last time and arrange the carrots on a serving plate, ensuring any emulsion left at the bottom is poured over. The dish should look shiny, colourful, and with a scent that immediately evokes Mediterranean freshness. Chef's tip: a final drizzle of raw oil will add a touch of brightness and roundness to the palate.

Tips

  • Plunge the carrots into ice water immediately after blanching to set their bright orange color and stop the cooking.
  • Use only the yellow part of the lemon zest to avoid introducing any bitter notes into the marinade.
  • Chop the fresh parsley just before serving to maintain its full aroma and vibrant green color.

Variations

  • Add some toasted sesame seeds to provide a pleasant crunchy texture and a nutty flavor to the dish.
  • Replace the lemon juice with lime juice for a version with a more exotic and tangy flavor profile.
