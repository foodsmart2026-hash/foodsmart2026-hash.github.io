Soft Pear and Spelt Flour Pancakes

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 10 min · Easy

These little pancakes use the natural sweetness of ripe pears for a soft and nutritious treat. Spelt flour gives a rustic note while remaining easy to swallow.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⬇️ Big saving🥕 Vegetarian🧒 Kid-friendly🇮🇹 Italian

These soft fritters use the delicate flavor of spelt flour to wrap the grated pulp of ripe pears. Pan-fried with minimal oil, they offer a spongy texture and naturally sweet taste, perfect for any time of day.

Ingredients

  • Ripe pears (Abate or Conference type)250 g
  • White spelt flour80 g
  • Medium egg1 pcs
  • Full-fat milk40 ml
  • Baking powder2 g
  • Sunflower oil10 ml

Allergens

Gluten, Eggs, Milk

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the fruit base: carefully peel 250g of ripe pears (Abate or Conference variety) and grate them finely using a coarse grater directly into a bowl. This technique creates a watery pulp rich in juice, which is essential for the natural hydration of the batter. Ensure you discard the core and seeds, keeping all the liquid released by the fruit. The result should be a coarse, fragrant purée. Chef's tip: Grate the pear at the last moment to prevent oxidation from darkening the mixture excessively.
  2. Move on to the liquid components: in a second large bowl, crack one medium egg and add 40ml of whole milk at room temperature. Whisk the two ingredients with a fork or a small hand whisk for about a minute until the mixture is homogeneous and slightly frothy. You don't need to whip the egg, just integrate it perfectly with the milk. You will obtain a liquid with a uniform pale yellow colour. Chef's tip: Use room temperature eggs to encourage better emulsification with the milk.
  3. Combine the previously grated pear pulp (250g) with the egg and milk mixture, stirring gently with a spoon. It is important to incorporate all the juice released by the pears, as it contains the natural sugars that flavour the fritters. Stir with circular motions to distribute the pulp evenly throughout the liquid. The base will be quite fluid with clearly visible bits of fruit. Chef's tip: Do not drain the pulp; the watery component of the pear is essential for the final fluffiness.
  4. Prepare the dry ingredients by sifting 80g of white spelt flour together with 2g of baking powder directly over the bowl with the wet mixture. Sifting the flour is a crucial technical step to eliminate lumps and incorporate air, ensuring a light texture. Add the flour gradually, rather than all at once, to better manage the consistency. You should achieve a very fine powder covering the surface of the liquid. Chef's tip: Always sift baking powder, as it tends to form small granules that could leave a bitter aftertaste if not properly dissolved.
  5. Mix the batter with a spatula or spoon, working from the bottom upwards until the flour has been completely absorbed. Do not overwork the mixture: stop as soon as you no longer see traces of white flour to prevent the fritters from becoming rubbery. The final batter should be thick but not too compact, with a consistency that allows it to be scooped. The aroma will be that of sweet pear combined with the nutty note of the spelt. Chef's tip: A slightly lumpy batter is preferable to an over-mixed one to preserve softness after cooking.
  6. Prepare for cooking by heating a high-quality non-stick frying pan over medium heat. Pour in 10ml of sunflower oil and distribute it evenly, then use a piece of kitchen paper to remove the excess, leaving only a protective film. The pan must be hot but should definitely not be smoking. To test the temperature, drop a tiny bit of batter in: if it sizzles immediately, it is ready. Chef's tip: A well-heated pan from the start ensures even browning without the fritters absorbing too much oil.
  7. Cook the fritters by dropping one tablespoon of batter at a time into the pan, aiming for small circles about 5cm in diameter. Maintain a distance of at least a couple of centimetres between them so you can flip them easily. Let them cook over medium-low heat for about 2 minutes without touching them. You will know they are ready to flip when small bubbles appear on the surface and the edges begin to set. Chef's tip: Use a measuring spoon to obtain fritters of the same size and ensure even cooking.
  8. Flip the fritters using a thin spatula and continue cooking on the second side for about one minute. The temperature must remain constant: a flame that is too high will burn the outside while leaving the inside raw. The fritters should be lightly golden on both sides and puff up slightly thanks to the baking powder. They should feel springy and soft to the touch. Chef's tip: If you notice them browning too quickly, lower the heat slightly; the sweetness of the pear tends to caramelise rapidly.
  9. Transfer the ready fritters to a plate and let them cool for a few minutes before serving. This resting time allows the internal structure to stabilise and the flavours to balance perfectly. If the recipe is intended for a young child, you can break the fritters into smaller pieces to facilitate chewing. The final result is a wholesome, naturally sweet, and very soft snack. Chef's tip: Serve them lukewarm to maximise the fragrance of the spelt flour and the fruity aroma of the pear.

Tips

  • Grate the pears just before using them to prevent the pulp from darkening too much.
  • Keep the heat medium-low during cooking to allow the baking powder to work without burning the surface.
  • Use a thin spatula to flip the fritters only when the first bubbles appear on the surface.

Variations

  • Replace the whole milk with a plant-based drink for a version with a different flavor profile.
  • Try using oat flour instead of spelt for a more rustic texture.
