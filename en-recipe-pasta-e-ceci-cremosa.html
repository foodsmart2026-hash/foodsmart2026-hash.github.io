Creamy Pasta and Chickpeas

💰 Save Euro · about €1.17 per person

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

A nutritious and comforting version of the classic Mediterranean comfort food, made thick thanks to the natural creaminess of the starches from the pulses and the pasta. It is a budget-friendly one-pot meal using humble but high-quality ingredients, perfect for the family routine.

Ingredients

  • Ditalini rigati semolina pasta (small, ridged tube-shaped pasta)320 g
  • Canned chickpeas (drained weight)480 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 g
  • Carrots100 g
  • Brown onion80 g
  • Celery50 g
  • Tomato purée15 g
  • Fresh rosemary1 sprig
  • Unrefined sea salt5 g
  • Hot water or vegetable stock1200 ml

Allergens

Gluten, Celery

Method

  1. Prepare a very fine dice with the onion, carrot, and celery (soffritto), ensuring the pieces are uniform for even cooking.
  2. In a large, heavy-bottomed pot, heat 20g of extra virgin olive oil with the rosemary sprig for 1 minute, allowing the essential oils to release.
  3. Add the diced vegetables and sauté over a medium-low heat for about 6-8 minutes, until the onion becomes translucent and the vegetables soften without browning.
  4. Add the tomato purée and the chickpeas, well-rinsed; let them cook while stirring for 3 minutes to lightly toast the pulses.
  5. Remove the rosemary, take about two ladles of chickpeas and vegetables, blend them with a little water and return the resulting cream to the pot: this will ensure a 'creamy' texture without adding extra fats.
  6. Pour in the hot water (or stock) and bring to the boil; adjust the salt and add the pasta directly into the pot to cook it using the 'one-pot' method.
  7. Cook for the time indicated on the pasta packet, stirring often to prevent it from sticking to the bottom and checking that the liquid is absorbed to create a thick emulsion.
  8. Turn off the heat when the pasta is al dente, add the remaining 20g of raw oil and a grind of pepper, then leave to rest covered for 2 minutes before serving.
