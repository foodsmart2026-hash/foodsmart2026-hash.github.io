Creamy Pasta and Chickpeas

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

A nutritious and comforting version of the classic Mediterranean comfort food, made thick thanks to the natural creaminess of the starches from the pulses and the pasta. It is a budget-friendly one-pot meal using humble but high-quality ingredients, perfect for the family routine.

A timeless classic of Mediterranean cuisine, ideal for warming up on cooler days. This version stands out for its thick and enveloping consistency, achieved by blending part of the legumes.

Ingredients

  • Ditalini rigati semolina pasta (small, ridged tube-shaped pasta)320 g
  • Canned chickpeas (drained weight)480 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 g
  • Carrots100 g
  • Brown onion80 g
  • Celery50 g
  • Tomato purée15 g
  • Fresh rosemary1 sprig
  • Unrefined sea salt5 g
  • Hot water or vegetable stock1200 ml

Allergens

Gluten, Celery

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the aromatic base by finely dicing 100g of carrots, 80g of onion, and 50g of celery using a sharp knife or a food processor. Aim for small, uniform cubes of about 2-3 millimetres to ensure they cook evenly and melt into the final cream. The result should be a colourful and fragrant mix, ready to release its natural sugars during the cooking process. My professional advice is not to rush this: a coarse dice would compromise the elegance of the velvety texture typical of this recipe.
  2. Take a large, heavy-bottomed pot, preferably cast iron or stainless steel, and heat the first 20g of extra virgin olive oil with the fresh rosemary sprig. Keep the heat very low for about 1-2 minutes, swirling the pot to allow the heat to gently extract the aromatic essential oils from the rosemary. You will smell an intense fragrance filling the kitchen, indicating the oil is infused and ready for the vegetables. Be very careful not to let the oil smoke, as burnt rosemary would release an unpleasant bitter aftertaste.
  3. Add the previously prepared vegetable mix to the pot and let it soften over medium-low heat for about 6-8 minutes, stirring frequently with a wooden spoon. The vegetables should become tender and the onion translucent, without browning or caramelising excessively. This stage is crucial for creating a sweet, aromatic base that will support the flavour of the chickpeas. If you notice the sauté drying out too quickly, add a tablespoon of hot water to encourage gentle cooking.
  4. Stir in 15g of tomato concentrate and the 480g of carefully rinsed and drained chickpeas, slightly increasing the heat for about 3 minutes. Stir vigorously to allow the legumes to absorb the flavours of the aromatic base and the concentrate to toast lightly, losing its initial acidity. You will notice the chickpeas start to shine and the aroma becomes more complex and toasted. Remember to rinse canned chickpeas thoroughly under running water to remove the canning liquid and excess salt.
  5. Remove the rosemary sprig, take out about two generous ladles of chickpeas and vegetables, and transfer them to a tall container with a small ladle of hot water. Use an immersion blender to process them into a smooth, silky purée, then pour this cream back into the pot with the rest of the ingredients. This professional technique allows you to achieve an enveloping, emulsified consistency without using cream or added fats. The chef recommends passing the cream through a fine-mesh sieve if you desire a result worthy of a Michelin-starred restaurant.
  6. Pour 1200ml of hot water or unsalted vegetable stock into the pot and bring to a gentle boil over high heat. Once boiling point is reached, add 5g of unrefined sea salt and pour the 320g of ditalini pasta directly into the liquid. This cooking method, known as 'risottata', allows the pasta to release its starch directly into the sauce, making the dish incredibly creamy. Maintain a medium heat so the boil is constant but not too vigorous throughout the cooking time.
  7. Cook the pasta for the time indicated on the packet (usually about 8-10 minutes), stirring frequently and carefully to prevent the ditalini from sticking to the bottom of the pot. As the water is absorbed, the consistency will change from a liquid soup to a thick, creamy emulsion that perfectly coats every single piece of pasta. If the pasta seems too dry before it is cooked, add another half ladle of hot water. The final result should be 'azzeccata' (stuck together), as they say in Naples, meaning thick and not watery.
  8. Turn off the heat when the pasta is still very al dente and proceed with the 'mantecatura' by adding the remaining 20g of raw extra virgin olive oil and, if liked, a crack of black pepper. Cover the pot with a lid and let everything rest for exactly 2 minutes before serving. This resting period allows the pasta to finish cooking passively and the flavours to stabilise and meld perfectly. Serve your creamy pasta and chickpeas in warm bowls, adding a small sprig of fresh rosemary as a final decorative touch for a bit of class.

Tips

  • Cook the pasta directly with the chickpeas so it absorbs all the flavor from the cooking base.
  • Adjust the final thickness by gradually adding hot broth until you reach the desired creaminess.
  • Let the pasta rest for two minutes before serving to allow the flavors to blend perfectly.

Variations

  • Add a handful of fresh spinach or chard at the end of cooking for a touch of color and flavor.
  • Replace ditalini with mixed pasta shapes for a result even more faithful to tradition.
