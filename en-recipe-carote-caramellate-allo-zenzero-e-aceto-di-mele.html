Ginger and Apple Cider Vinegar Caramelised Carrots

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 20 min · Easy

A budget-friendly and vibrant side dish that transforms simple carrots into a gourmet accompaniment. The spicy note of fresh ginger pairs perfectly with the delicate acidity of apple cider vinegar.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🌱 Vegan🌾 Gluten-free🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free

Ingredients

  • Medium carrots800 g
  • Fresh ginger20 g
  • Apple cider vinegar40 ml
  • Brown sugar15 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Fine salt5 g
  • Fresh parsley10 g

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Begin by thoroughly washing 800g of medium carrots under cold running water to remove any soil residue, then peel them using a vegetable peeler. Use a sharp chef's knife to slice them into diagonal rounds about 1cm thick, ensuring a consistent size for even cooking throughout the process. This cutting technique not only increases the surface area for better caramelisation but also gives the dish a more refined and professional aesthetic. The final result should be a batch of uniform slices ready to be transformed into a succulent side dish. The chef's tip is to avoid slicing the carrots too thinly, as they will lose their structure and become overly mushy during the braising stage.
  2. Take approximately 20g of fresh ginger, remove the skin by scraping it with the edge of a teaspoon to minimise waste, and grate it finely using a microplane or fine grater. Collect all the pulp and the resulting juice in a small bowl and set them aside, ready to be added at the correct stage of cooking. This step is crucial for extracting the ginger's essential oils, ensuring a pungent and citrusy aroma that distributes evenly without leaving stringy fibrous bits in the mouth. You will obtain a highly fragrant paste that acts as the aromatic heart of the dish. Remember to grate the ginger at the last moment to preserve all its freshness and antioxidant properties.
  3. In a very large non-stick frying pan or a sauteuse, pour 30ml of extra virgin olive oil and heat it over a high flame for about a minute. When the oil is hot but not smoking, add all the carrot rounds, spreading them out in a single layer if possible. Sear the carrots for 5 minutes, tossing them frequently or turning them with a wooden spatula until the edges begin to show a light caramelised browning. This Maillard reaction process is essential for developing the carrot's natural sweet notes. Be careful not to burn them; the heat must be intense yet controlled to achieve an inviting amber colour.
  4. Slightly reduce the heat to medium, add 5g of fine salt and the previously prepared ginger pulp, stirring carefully for about a minute. The salt will help the carrots release some of their moisture, while the ginger will start to release its intoxicating scent, merging with the oil. Ensure every single round is well-seasoned and glossy, indicating that the flavours are being distributed evenly. The aroma released at this stage should be a perfect balance between the earthiness of the carrot and the spiciness of the ginger. The chef's tip is to add the ginger only now to prevent it from burning and turning bitter under the high heat of the initial searing.
  5. At this point, pour 40ml of apple cider vinegar evenly over the carrots and immediately sprinkle 15g of brown sugar on top. Maintain a medium heat and allow the alcohol and the sharp acidity of the vinegar to evaporate for about 2 minutes, moving the pan in a circular motion. The sugar will begin to melt and combine with the vinegar, creating a liquid base that will serve for the subsequent braising and glazing phase. You will notice the liquid starting to simmer gently and the colour of the carrots becoming more vivid. Do not cover the pan immediately, allowing the strongest vinegar vapours to escape, leaving only the fruity aftertaste.
  6. Lower the heat to the minimum setting, cover the pan with a tight-fitting lid, and continue cooking for approximately 10 minutes. During this stage, the moist heat will allow the carrots to cook through to the centre while maintaining an 'al dente' texture, which is the hallmark of a professional preparation. Check the consistency by piercing a carrot with the tip of a knife: it should offer slight resistance without falling apart. The carrots will absorb the blend of vinegar, sugar, and ginger, becoming tender and flavourful. If you notice the bottom drying out too quickly, you can add a tablespoon of warm water to maintain the necessary humidity.
  7. Remove the lid and turn the heat back up to medium-high for the final 2 minutes of cooking, moving the pan constantly. This step serves to rapidly reduce the remaining liquid at the bottom, transforming it into a shiny, syrupy glaze that coats every round like a lacquer. The carrots will appear brilliant, almost reflective, and the aroma will become more intense and caramelised. It is vital to keep a close eye on the pan now, as the sugar could burn quickly and turn into bitter caramel. You will achieve a visually stunning side dish where the gloss enhances the bright orange colour typical of high-quality carrots.
  8. While the glaze finishes setting, finely chop 10g of fresh parsley after washing and drying it very well. Turn off the heat, transfer the carrots to a warm serving dish, and sprinkle them with the chopped parsley, which adds a note of herbaceous freshness and a vibrant green colour contrast. Stir one last time to ensure the parsley adheres to the still-warm glaze. The dish is ready to be served as an accompaniment to meat dishes or as part of a sophisticated vegetarian buffet. The final chef's tip is to serve the carrots warm, as this is when the glaze reaches its ideal consistency and the ginger flavours are most balanced.
