Vanilla Rice Pudding

4 servings · Prep 5 min · Cooking 30 min · Medium

A thick and nutritious breakfast inspired by grandmother's rice treats, very economical and filling. Excellent both warm in winter and cold in summer.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🥕 Vegetarian🧒 Kid-friendly🌾 Gluten-free🇮🇹 Italian

This rice pudding evokes childhood flavors with its creamy texture and delicate scent of vanilla and lemon. A slow-cooked preparation that transforms simple grains into a soft, fragrant dessert typical of rural traditions.

Ingredients

  • Originario rice (A short-grain Italian rice variety ideal for puddings)150 g
  • Whole milk800 ml
  • Sugar60 g
  • Vanilla extract5 ml
  • Lemon (whole zest peel)1 pcs
  • Ground cinnamon1 pizzico

Allergens

Milk

Method

  1. Start by pouring 800 ml of whole milk into a heavy-based saucepan, adding 60 g of sugar, 5 ml of vanilla extract, and the whole zest of one lemon. Heat the mixture over medium heat, stirring gently with a wooden spoon to encourage the sugar to dissolve and prevent it from settling at the bottom. Maintain this temperature for about 4-5 minutes until you see the first small bubbles appearing on the surface, indicating the milk is about to boil. You will obtain a fragrant, homogeneous liquid, ready to receive the rice without thermal shock. Chef's tip: use an organic lemon and remove the zest with a vegetable peeler to get a single piece, avoiding the bitter white pith.
  2. When the milk is just about to boil, pour 150 g of short-grain rice directly into the saucepan, distributing it evenly. Immediately lower the heat to the minimum: the rice must simmer very gently, almost imperceptibly, so as not to burn the milk proteins. This initial phase allows the grains to start absorbing the liquid without breaking apart. The result will be a mixture that is still very liquid but already intensely fragrant. Chef's tip: do not rinse the rice under water, as the surface starch is essential to achieve the pudding's characteristic creaminess.
  3. Proceed with slow cooking for about 25-30 minutes, stirring regularly every 3-4 minutes to ensure even heat distribution. Use a very low flame and make sure the rice remains covered by the milk during the first stage. As the minutes pass, you will notice the milk beginning to reduce and the rice grains swelling visibly. You will achieve a consistency that gradually shifts from liquid to velvety. Chef's tip: if you notice the milk rising too much in volume and creating foam, momentarily lift the saucepan off the heat and lower the flame even further.
  4. Towards the twentieth minute of cooking, increase the frequency of stirring, making sure to scrape the edges and the bottom of the pot. The starch released by the rice will begin to create a natural bond with the fats in the milk, making the mixture increasingly thick. Always keep the heat at minimum to prevent the bottom from sticking or browning, which would alter the delicate vanilla flavour. The rice should now appear very soft, almost ready to break down. Chef's tip: move the spoon in a 'figure of eight' pattern on the bottom to ensure no grains stay stuck and risk burning.
  5. After 25-28 minutes, assess the pudding's density: it should have the consistency of a thick custard, keeping in mind that it will firm up further as it cools. If the mixture seems excessively dry before the rice is fully cooked, add a small ladle of warm milk to correct the fluidity. The surface should appear glossy and the colour should be a warm ivory speckled with vanilla seeds. The final result must be enveloping and creamy, not compact like a risotto. Chef's tip: taste a grain; it should offer no resistance under your teeth and should be completely tender.
  6. Turn off the heat and immediately remove the lemon zest using kitchen tongs or a spoon. This must be done while hot to prevent the zest from releasing too many essential oils that could overpower the delicate vanilla aroma. Let the pudding rest in the pot for a couple of minutes with the heat off so the temperature stabilises slightly. You will notice that during these moments of rest, the cream settles further, becoming even more velvety. Chef's tip: if you want an extremely 'silky' texture, you can finish it with a tablespoon of cold milk just before serving.
  7. Prepare 4 individual small bowls and distribute the rice pudding while still hot, dividing the approximately 200 g of product per portion equally. Use a small ladle to avoid smudging the edges of the bowls, ensuring a clean and elegant presentation. If you intend to serve it cold, cover the bowls with cling film in direct contact with the surface to prevent a skin from forming, which is typical of milk-based preparations. The scent of vanilla and lemon will now be at its peak. Chef's tip: lightly tap the bottom of the bowls on the palm of your hand to perfectly level the surface of the pudding.
  8. Complete the dish by dusting each portion with a pinch of ground cinnamon, letting it fall from a fine-mesh sieve for a rain-like distribution. The cinnamon will add a spicy note and an amber chromatic contrast that invites tasting. Serve the pudding warm if you want an enveloping comfort food, or leave it in the fridge for at least 2 hours for a firmer, more refreshing consistency. The final result is a balanced, nutritious dessert with an ancient flavour. Chef's tip: for a gourmet touch, add some freshly grated lemon zest over the cinnamon to brighten the citrus notes.

Tips

  • Use a starch-rich rice like Originario to achieve a naturally thick cream.
  • Stir frequently during the final minutes of cooking to prevent the rice from sticking to the pan.
  • Remove the lemon zest immediately after cooking to prevent it from releasing bitter notes.

Variations

  • Sprinkle the surface with plenty of ground cinnamon just before serving.
  • Add grated orange zest instead of lemon for a warmer, citrusy aroma.
