Sweet Little Risotto with Carrots and Red Split Lentils

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 30 min · Medium

A complete meal disguised as a first course, where the red lentils melt away completely to create a thick, nutritious cream. The bright orange colour and the naturally sweet flavour of the carrot make it a firm favourite with children.

This risotto combines the natural sweetness of carrots with the softness of red lentils, which break down during cooking to create a natural sauce. A rustic and comforting recipe, great as a colorful one-pot meal.

Ingredients

  • Baby rice or Originario rice (a small, round-grain Italian rice)160 g
  • Medium carrots200 g
  • Red split lentils60 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil15 ml
  • Water600 ml

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Begin by carefully peeling 200g of medium carrots and finely grating them using the smallest holes of your grater. Work patiently until you obtain a pulp that is almost liquid and homogeneous, which will release all its natural sugars during cooking. Perform this on a chopping board or directly into a bowl to save the precious juice that will flavour the rice. The result should be a thick, bright orange purée, ready to naturally colour your risotto. Chef's tip: use a sharp grater to prevent excessive oxidation of the carrots and keep all the water-soluble vitamins intact.
  2. Take 60g of red split lentils and rinse them thoroughly under cold running water using a fine-mesh sieve. Move them with your fingers until the water runs perfectly clear and is free of the white surface foam caused by natural saponins. This process takes about 2 minutes and serves to remove any impurities and excess surface starch that could make the dish too sticky. You will see that the lentils appear shinier and cleaner, ready for quick and even cooking. Chef's tip: never skip this step as it ensures better digestibility and a cleaner, more delicate flavour in the final dish.
  3. Pour the carrot pulp and well-rinsed lentils into a heavy-bottomed saucepan, adding about 300ml of water (half of the total amount). Turn on the heat and keep it very low, preferably covering with a lid to retain moisture and aromas inside the pot. Allow to cook for about 10 minutes, stirring occasionally with a wooden spoon or silicone spatula to prevent the mixture from sticking to the bottom. You will obtain a soft, fragrant base where the lentils begin to lose their original shape, merging harmoniously with the carrot. Chef's tip: if you notice the liquid evaporating too quickly, lower the flame to the absolute minimum to avoid burning the precious pulp.
  4. Now add 160g of rice (baby rice or Originario) to the saucepan along with the rest of the water (the remaining 300ml), which should already be hot to avoid stopping the boil. Stir well to evenly distribute the grains among the carrots and lentils, ensuring everything is well submerged in the cooking liquid. Maintain a medium-low heat so that the heat spreads constantly, allowing the rice to start its swelling process. The rice will immediately take on the orange hue of the carrot and begin to incorporate the flavours of the vegetable base. Chef's tip: pre-heat the remaining water in a separate small pan to avoid thermal shock to the rice grain, which would compromise its texture.
  5. Cook the rice for between 15 and 18 minutes, stirring regularly and gently to encourage the release of the grains' natural starches. This constant circular motion is fundamental to creating the natural creaminess typical of risotto, making it inviting even without adding animal fats or cheeses. Ensure the boil is just a gentle simmer, allowing the grain to cook evenly through to the core. The consistency will begin to turn velvety and the sweet scent of carrots will spread pleasantly through the kitchen. Chef's tip: use a wooden spoon with a hole in the middle to incorporate air and make the risotto even fluffier.
  6. During cooking, closely observe the density of the rice and, if you see the liquid drying out excessively before the time is up, add small ladles of hot water. Pour the water in a little at a time, waiting for it to be almost entirely absorbed before adding more, following the classic gourmet risotto technique. For young children, the rice must be extremely soft and give way to tongue pressure against the palate, ensuring maximum safety. The final result should be a fluid consistency (wavy) but not watery, with a rich and homogeneous appearance. Chef's tip: do not overdo the water towards the end of cooking to avoid an unappealing 'soup' effect.
  7. Check that the dish is ready by observing the lentils, which by now should be completely broken down and indistinguishable from the rest of the carrot cream. The rice should appear well-cooked and very tender, with the grains having doubled their original volume thanks to the absorption of the nutritious liquid. Turn off the heat when the rice has reached the desired consistency, thick and bound by the vegetable pulp now reduced to a purée. You will notice that the colour has become a soft, pasty orange, extremely inviting and lump-free. Chef's tip: squash a grain between two fingers; if you feel no resistance or graininess in the centre, the cooking is perfect for your child.
  8. Remove the saucepan from the heat and proceed with the 'cold mantecatura' by adding 15ml of raw extra virgin olive oil. Stir vigorously for about a minute to emulsify the oil fats with the starch released by the rice, creating a silky and glossy cream that enhances all the flavours. Let the risotto rest covered for two minutes so the flavours can settle and the temperature drops to a lukewarm level, ideal for immediate consumption. Serve the portions and, for adult diners only, optionally add a pinch of salt and turmeric directly into individual plates. Chef's tip: the final rest off the heat is the true secret to obtaining a perfect consistency and highlighting the natural sweetness of the ingredients.

Tips

  • Grate the carrots finely so they melt completely with the rice during preparation.
  • Rinse the red lentils well under running water until the water runs clear.
  • Keep the risotto moist by adding warm water a little at a time during the last few minutes.

Variations

  • You can use brown rice instead of 'originario' for a firmer texture.
  • Try flavoring the dish with a bay leaf, to be removed before serving.
