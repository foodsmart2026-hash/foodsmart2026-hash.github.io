Tofu in a Potato and Rosemary Crust

4 servings · Prep 25 min · Cooking 12 min · Medium

A recipe that transforms tofu into a delicious and crispy main course using potatoes as a 'breading'. An original way to enjoy plant-based proteins with Mediterranean flavours.

A creative recipe where tofu is wrapped in a crust of crispy potatoes scented with rosemary. The combination of textures, between the soft heart and the browned exterior, transforms a simple ingredient into a tasty dish.

Ingredients

  • Plain tofu400 g
  • Yellow-fleshed potatoes300 g
  • Cornflour20 g
  • Fresh rosemary2 rametti
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 ml
  • Salt and Pepperq.b.

Allergens

Soy

Method

  1. Begin by preparing 400g of plain tofu: wrap it thoroughly in several layers of kitchen paper and place it on a chopping board, topping it with a heavy weight like a large pot. Let it press for at least 15 minutes to expel the soaking water, making its structure denser and ready to hold the crust. This step is vital to prevent the tofu from releasing steam during cooking, which would ruin the crispiness of the potatoes. You will end up with a dry, firm block ideal for handling. My chef's tip is to change the paper halfway through if it becomes overly saturated.
  2. Once dried, take the tofu and slice it with a sharp knife into 8 uniform pieces, choosing either rectangular or triangular shapes depending on your aesthetic preference. Aim for a consistent thickness of about 1.5 cm to ensure that the heat penetrates evenly during pan-searing. Arrange the slices on a clean plate and pat them dry one last time if needed. The result should be a series of identical pieces, ready for coating. Remember: precise geometry ensures that every bite cooks for the exact same amount of time.
  3. Move on to preparing the crust base: peel 300g of yellow potatoes and grate them raw using a coarse grater to obtain irregular matchsticks. Gather the grated potatoes in a clean tea towel or muslin cloth and squeeze with all your might over the sink to remove the liquid starch and vegetable water. You should be left with a pile of potatoes that is very dry and almost crumbly to the touch. This physical effort is the secret to avoiding a 'boiled' effect and achieving perfect browning. Do not rinse the potatoes after grating, as a hint of natural starch helps them stick together.
  4. In a large bowl, combine the squeezed potatoes with 20g of cornstarch, the chopped leaves of 2 fresh rosemary sprigs, a generous pinch of salt, and a crack of black pepper. Mix vigorously with your hands or a fork to distribute the starch and aromatics evenly across every strand of potato. The cornstarch acts as an invisible glue between the tofu and the vegetable crust. The mix should be fragrant, well-seasoned, and slightly tacky. Chef's trick: finely mince the rosemary with a knife to release all the essential oils without leaving woody bits in the dish.
  5. Take one slice of tofu at a time and press it into the potato mix, using the palm of your hand with significant force so the potatoes adhere well to both sides and the edges. Repeat the operation by flipping the slice, ensuring every centimetre of surface is covered with an even layer of about 3-4 millimetres. You must almost 'embed' the tofu into the potato mass to create a solid shell. By the end, the slices will look like small, compact golden ingots. Tip: work on one slice at a time and don't prep them too far in advance, or the potatoes will start to release moisture.
  6. Prepare for cooking by heating 40ml of extra virgin olive oil in a wide non-stick frying pan, ensuring the oil coats the entire bottom. Set the heat to medium-high and wait until the oil is shimmering and hot; you can test it by dropping in a single potato strand—it should sizzle immediately. The correct temperature is essential to instantly seal the crust and prevent the tofu from absorbing too much oil. The oil should appear fluid and slightly rippled on the surface. Be careful not to reach the smoke point; if you see smoke, lower the heat slightly before proceeding.
  7. Gently lay the tofu slices into the hot pan, spacing them out so you don't cause a sudden drop in oil temperature. Cook for about 5-6 minutes without touching or moving the slices: patience during this phase is the key to forming an intact, deep brown crust. You will see the edges start to turn amber and the scent of toasted rosemary will fill the kitchen. The result must be a perfectly golden side that feels rigid to the touch of a spatula. If you move the tofu too early, you risk tearing the potato crust away.
  8. Flip the slices using a wide, flat spatula and continue cooking for another 5-6 minutes on the other side, keeping the heat at medium. If necessary, add a tiny pinch of salt to the freshly flipped surface to enhance the crust's flavour. The crust must become extremely crunchy and a uniform golden-brown colour, similar to a well-executed Swiss rosti. The heat should warm the tofu's core, making it soft in stark contrast to the exterior. Check that the texture is firm before turning off the heat.
  9. Once ready, transfer the tofu ingots onto a sheet of kitchen paper for just a few seconds to drain any excess oil, then plate up immediately. Serve the dish piping hot, perhaps accompanied by a fresh mixed salad or a light yoghurt dip to balance the crunchy texture. The crust is at its peak fragrance the moment it leaves the pan; if you wait too long, the internal steam will soften the potatoes. The final touch? A small sprig of fresh rosemary for garnish and a final crack of freshly ground pepper.

Tips

  • It is essential to press the tofu well with paper towels to remove excess moisture before breading it.
  • Use a coarse grater for the potatoes to get a more rustic and crunchy crust.
  • Cook over medium heat to allow the potatoes to brown without burning before the tofu is thoroughly heated.

Variations

  • Add a pinch of freshly ground black pepper to the potato mix for a more aromatic touch.
  • You can place a whole rosemary leaf between the tofu and the potato crust for a more refined look.
