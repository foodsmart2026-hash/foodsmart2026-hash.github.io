Baked Anchovies with Potatoes and Cherry Tomatoes

4 servings · Prep 25 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

Oily fish is the most affordable and healthy choice (rich in Omega-3) in supermarkets. This one-pan meal is simple, quick, and typically Mediterranean.

A Mediterranean classic combining the saltiness of bluefish with the sweetness of potatoes and cherry tomatoes. This one-pan meal is easy to prepare and brings authentic coastal flavors to your table. The contrast between soft potatoes and golden breadcrumbs is truly irresistible.

Ingredients

  • Fresh cleaned anchovies500 g
  • Yellow-fleshed potatoes500 g
  • Cherry tomatoes200 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 ml
  • Garlic1 spicchio
  • Fresh parsley1 ciuffo
  • Breadcrumbs20 g

Allergens

Fish, Gluten

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the base: peel 500g of yellow-fleshed potatoes and, using a mandoline, cut them into very thin slices about 2mm thick. Soak the slices in a bowl of cold water for a few minutes to remove excess starch, then dry them perfectly with a clean tea towel; this step is crucial for achieving even cooking and a melting texture. The final result should be a series of translucent potato discs ready for the tray. Chef's tip: do not hand-cut the potatoes unless you have expert knife skills, as uneven thickness will lead to some slices being raw while others burn.
  2. Now move on to the aromatics and vegetables: carefully wash 200g of cherry tomatoes and cut them in half, then finely mince a clove of garlic (with the germ removed) and a bunch of fresh parsley. Mix the garlic and parsley in a small bowl to create a homogenous herb mix that you will distribute in several stages of the recipe. You should have juicy tomatoes and a highly fragrant mix releasing its citrusy and pungent essence. Chef's tip: crush the garlic with the flat of your knife blade before mincing to release the essential oils that will give the fish more depth.
  3. Prepare the baking tray by pouring about 10ml of extra virgin olive oil onto the bottom, spreading it well with a brush or your fingers. Create an even layer by slightly overlapping the dried potato slices, ensuring there are no gaps on the bottom of the tray. Season this first layer with a pinch of salt, another drizzle of oil, and a dusting of the garlic and parsley mix. The tray should look like an orderly mosaic of potatoes ready for the first bake. Chef's tip: do not over-salt at this stage, as anchovies are naturally salty.
  4. Place the tray with only the potatoes into the preheated oven at 200°C (fan oven 180°C) on the middle shelf for about 10 minutes. This pre-cooking is essential because potatoes require longer cooking times than fish, which would otherwise dry out. By the end of the time, the potatoes should be tender when pierced with a fork and the edges should just be starting to become translucent. Chef's tip: if you notice the potatoes are drying out too quickly, cover the tray with foil during this phase to trap the moisture.
  5. While the potatoes are cooking, prepare the 500g of anchovies: ensure they have been properly cleaned, heads and innards removed, and butterflied open while keeping the tail joined. Quickly rinse them under cold water and pat them dry with kitchen paper to remove residual moisture that would hinder browning. The anchovies should look shiny, intact, and ready to be placed on the potato bed. Chef's tip: always check with your fingers for any stray scales along the sides of the fish, gently stroking from tail to head.
  6. Carefully remove the tray from the oven and lay the anchovies over the potatoes, placing them skin-side up to protect the delicate flesh from direct heat. Try to cover the potato layer almost entirely, creating a natural shield that will keep the tubers soft and tasty thanks to the juices released by the fish. You should achieve a uniform silvery surface covering the vegetable base. Chef's tip: arrange the anchovies in a radial pattern or neat parallel rows for a restaurant-quality final presentation.
  7. Complete the dish by distributing the 200g of halved cherry tomatoes over the anchovies and sprinkling everything with 20g of breadcrumbs, the remaining garlic and parsley mix, and the final 20-30ml of extra virgin olive oil. The breadcrumbs should form a light dusting rather than a heavy layer, while the tomatoes should be scattered to provide acidity and colour. The visual result should be vibrant, with the red of the tomatoes contrasting with the green herbs and golden oil. Chef's tip: don't completely bury the anchovies under breadcrumbs; allow the heat to reach the skin to make it slightly crisp.
  8. Bake again at 200°C for a further 10-12 minutes, moving the tray to the upper shelf for the last 2 minutes if you desire a more pronounced gratin. Monitor closely: the anchovies are ready when the flesh turns white and opaque and the breadcrumbs take on a light golden-brown colour. Remove immediately to prevent the fish from losing its natural succulence and becoming tough. Chef's tip: the cooking is perfect when the cherry tomatoes are just starting to wilt and release their juices, creating a delicious emulsion with the oil at the bottom.
  9. Let the dish rest out of the oven for 2 to 3 minutes before portioning and serving directly from the tray. This short rest allows the juices to redistribute through the fish fibres and the potatoes to fully absorb the aroma of the sea and tomatoes. Upon serving, the aroma should be a perfect balance between the saltiness of the anchovies, the aromatic parsley, and the sweetness of the baked potatoes. Chef's tip: serve the dish warm rather than piping hot, as excessive heat masks the delicacy of the anchovies, preventing you from appreciating their subtle taste nuances.

Tips

  • Slice the potatoes with a mandoline to get thin, uniform pieces that cook evenly through.
  • Ensure the anchovies are well-dried before placing them on the potatoes to maintain the right consistency.
  • Add fresh chopped parsley only after cooking to preserve its natural color and vibrant aroma.

Variations

  • Enrich the tray with black olives for a bolder, more savory Mediterranean touch.
  • Replace breadcrumbs with cornmeal for a more rustic crunch and a beautiful golden finish.
