Bean and Vegetable Burgers

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 15 min · Medium

A tasty and economical plant-based alternative to classic burgers, ideal for integrating legumes into the family diet. The combination of cannellini beans and fresh vegetables ensures an excellent protein and fibre profile with a texture that is crispy outside and soft inside.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🥕 Vegetarian🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free

Ingredients

  • Cooked cannellini beans (drained)480 g
  • Carrots150 g
  • Courgettes150 g
  • Wholemeal breadcrumbs80 g
  • Egg1 pc
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Brown onion50 g
  • Fresh parsley10 g
  • Unrefined sea salt4 g
  • Black pepper1 g

Allergens

Gluten, Eggs

Method

  1. Start by thoroughly draining 480g of cooked cannellini beans from their canning liquid and rinsing them well under cold running water. Use a fine-mesh sieve and then pat them dry with kitchen paper to remove every trace of residual moisture. This process takes about 2 minutes and should leave you with beans that look matte and perfectly dry. Chef's tip: moisture is the main enemy of a firm burger; the drier the beans, the less breadcrumbs you will need to add later.
  2. Wash and trim 150g of carrots and 150g of courgettes, then grate them using the coarse side of a grater and collect them in a clean tea towel. Wrap the vegetables in the towel and squeeze with significant force to eliminate as much vegetable water as possible. Spend 5 minutes on this step until you have a vegetable mass that feels almost dry to the touch. Chef's tip: do not skip this step, otherwise the water released during cooking will make the burgers soggy and difficult to flip.
  3. Finely chop 50g of yellow onion and place it in a non-stick frying pan with 10ml of extra virgin olive oil. Let it soften over a very low heat for about 5 minutes, adding a tablespoon of water if necessary to prevent it from browning too much. The onion should be tender, translucent, and release a sweet aroma. Chef's tip: slow-cooking the onion removes its sharpness and makes it digestible, ensuring a balanced aromatic base.
  4. Transfer the dry cannellini beans into a large bowl and mash them vigorously using a fork or a potato masher. Work the mixture for about 3 minutes until you achieve a thick but intentionally coarse puree, with some bean pieces still visible. Absolutely avoid using a blender to prevent breaking the fibres too much and making the mixture too runny. Chef's tip: an irregular texture is essential to give the burger a 'chewy' consistency similar to meat.
  5. Add the well-squeezed grated vegetables, the sautéed onion, 10g of finely chopped fresh parsley, the whole egg, 4g of salt, and 1g of pepper to the bean puree. Mix with a wooden spoon to evenly distribute the seasonings and vegetables into the protein base. The mixture will look colourful and smell very fragrant thanks to the fresh herbs. Chef's tip: ensure the onion has cooled down slightly before adding it to avoid cooking the egg prematurely.
  6. Gradually incorporate 80g of wholemeal breadcrumbs, working the dough first with the spoon and then directly with your hands. Continue kneading for 2-3 minutes until the mass is mouldable, no longer sticky but still slightly moist on the inside. You should have a compact ball that easily pulls away from the sides of the bowl. Chef's tip: only add the last handful of breadcrumbs if necessary; too much will make the burger dry and floury after cooking.
  7. Divide the mixture into 4 equal portions of about 180g each, shape them into perfect spheres and gently flatten them until they are about 10cm in diameter and 2cm thick. Compact the edges well with your fingers to prevent cracks that might open during cooking. Place the burgers on a tray lined with baking parchment. Chef's tip: let the burgers rest in the fridge for 15 minutes before cooking; the cold will help the bread starch stabilise the structure.
  8. Heat the remaining 20ml of extra virgin olive oil in a large non-stick frying pan over medium-high heat. Place the burgers in the pan and cook for about 6 minutes per side, turning them gently with a wide spatula only once a brown, crispy crust has formed. The heat must penetrate gradually to cook the egg inside without burning the outside. Chef's tip: don't rush to flip them; the Maillard reaction (the crust) is not just for flavour, it acts as a structural 'glue' for the burger.
