Simplified Trieste-Style 'Patate in Tecia' (Pan-Fried Potatoes)

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 45 min · Medium

A budget-friendly version of the classic recipe from Trieste where potatoes are crushed and sautéed for a long time with plenty of onion until a delicious crust forms.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🌱 Vegan🧒 Kid-friendly🌾 Gluten-free🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🇮🇹 Italian

A rustic side dish typical of Trieste, featuring a creamy texture paired with a golden crust. Slowly stewed onions provide a sweet note that wraps around the pan-seared boiled potatoes. It is the perfect accompaniment for traditional roasts or meat dishes.

Ingredients

  • Yellow-fleshed potatoes800 g
  • Large golden onions2 pcs
  • Vegetable stock (or water)100 ml
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Begin by thoroughly washing 800g of yellow-fleshed potatoes under running water to remove any traces of soil. Place them in a large pot, cover with cold, lightly salted water, and bring to a boil over a high heat, cooking them whole with their skins on for about 25 minutes. The final result should be a potato that is tender when pierced with a fork but still firm at the core. The chef's tip is to always start with cold water to ensure even cooking between the centre and the outside of the tuber.
  2. Once ready, drain the potatoes and let them cool for a few minutes, just long enough to handle them without burning yourself. Peel them whilst they are still warm, as the skin will come off much more easily, and cut them into irregular chunks or slices about one centimetre thick. You will get pieces ready to absorb the flavours of the seasoning; avoid cutting them too thin so they don't fall apart completely during cooking. The secret is to work the potato while it is still releasing heat to favour the subsequent creaming process.
  3. Take the 2 large golden onions, peel them, and slice them thinly and uniformly using a sharp knife or a mandoline. In a large non-stick frying pan (the traditional 'tecia'), heat 30ml of extra virgin olive oil over a gentle heat. Add the onions and let them stew slowly for about 10-12 minutes until they become translucent, sweet, and a beautiful dark amber colour. Be careful not to burn them: if necessary, add a tablespoon of water to encourage even caramelisation without any bitter notes.
  4. Add the cut potatoes into the pan with the stewed onions and slightly increase the heat to a medium level. Using a fork or a wooden spoon, roughly crush the potatoes as you mix them into the onion base. You shouldn't reduce them to a purée, but rather create a mix of whole pieces and crushed parts that will begin to bind together. At this stage, the texture should appear rustic and the aroma of the onion should completely envelop the potatoes.
  5. Add about half of the 100ml of vegetable stock (or water) to moisten the mixture and distribute the flavours better. Level the potatoes with the back of the spoon, pressing them firmly against the bottom of the pan to create an even, compact layer. Cook over a medium-high heat for about 5-7 minutes without stirring at all, allowing the heat to work on the base. You should hear the typical sizzle that indicates the evaporation of liquids and the start of the Maillard reaction.
  6. Gently check a corner of the mixture by lifting it with a spatula: when you see that a golden, dark, and crunchy crust has formed, it's time to flip the potatoes. Try to turn them in blocks, as if it were a broken-up omelette, to keep the crunchy parts visible. The result should show a sharp contrast between the dark gold of the toasted surface and the bright yellow of the potato's interior. To prevent them from sticking too stubbornly, ensure the pan is of high quality and well-heated.
  7. Pour the remaining stock around the edges of the pan to keep the core of the preparation moist and creamy as you continue browning. Continue cooking for another 5-8 minutes, pressing the potatoes onto the bottom again to encourage the formation of more crust on the second side. The potatoes should begin to compact almost into a single mass, releasing an intense aroma of roasted potato and sweet onion. If you notice the bottom darkening too quickly, lower the heat slightly and add a splash of water.
  8. Turn off the heat when the potatoes have become a sort of irregular cake, crispy on the outside and very soft inside. Let the side dish rest in the pan for a couple of minutes before serving, to stabilise the textures and make it easier to lift from the bottom. Serve the potatoes piping hot, preferably accompanying meat dishes or typical Julian tradition stews. The chef's tip: a generous grind of fresh black pepper at the end will enhance the natural sweetness of the golden onion.

Tips

  • For a perfect crust, avoid stirring the potatoes too often during the final browning phase.
  • Ensure the onions are sliced very thinly so they melt completely into the base.
  • You can prepare them in advance and reheat them in the pan to revive their characteristic crispness.

Variations

  • Replace the extra virgin olive oil with butter to achieve an even richer and smoother flavor.
  • Add a pinch of freshly ground black pepper to give the dish a spicy and pungent note.
