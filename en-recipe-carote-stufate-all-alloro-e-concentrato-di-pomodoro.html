Carrots Braised with Bay Leaves and Tomato Purée

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

A simple preparation that transforms common carrots into a tasty side dish thanks to the slow braising technique. The bay leaf releases a balsamic aroma that pairs perfectly with the sweetness of the vegetable.

Tender and flavorful carrots, slowly stewed with bay leaves and a touch of tomato paste. A simple yet bold side dish that enhances the natural sweetness of the vegetable.

Ingredients

  • Carrots600 g
  • Tomato purée (highly concentrated tomato paste)15 g
  • Garlic clove1 pcs
  • Bay leaves2 foglie
  • Extra virgin olive oil15 ml

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Begin the vegetable preparation by carefully peeling 600g of fresh carrots, removing the ends with a sharp knife. Slice the roots into regular rounds about half a centimetre thick, aiming for a consistent size to ensure every piece cooks evenly. Keep your cuts clean on a stable chopping board to avoid crushing the internal carrot fibres. The visual result should be a collection of vibrant and precise orange discs. Chef's tip: use a Y-peeler to remove only the outermost layer of the skin, thus preserving most of the vitamins found just beneath the surface.
  2. Take a wide, heavy-bottomed saucepan and pour in 15ml of high-quality extra virgin olive oil. Add one lightly crushed garlic clove (still in its skin) and the 2 whole bay leaves to start the aromatic infusion. Heat over a medium-low flame for about 2 minutes, moving the pan to distribute the heat and aromas. You will know the garlic is ready when it begins to sizzle gently and releases an intense fragrance without becoming overly brown. Chef's tip: crush the garlic with the flat of a knife blade to release the allicin, but do not mince it, so you can easily remove it later to avoid a harsh flavour.
  3. Transfer the previously sliced carrots into the hot saucepan, spreading them in as even a layer as possible. Turn the heat up slightly to a medium-high level and sauté the vegetables for about 2-3 minutes, stirring continuously with a wooden spoon. This step serves to sear the surface of the carrots and allows the flavoured oil to coat every single round. You will get shiny, bright carrots that begin to release their natural saccharine sweetness. Chef's tip: this initial sautéing is essential for developing the Maillard reaction, which gives the finished dish a much deeper flavour compared to simple boiling.
  4. In a small bowl or glass, dilute 15g of tomato purée (paste) in about 100ml of warm water, stirring vigorously with a teaspoon. Ensure the concentrate is completely dissolved and there are no remaining lumps before proceeding. This emulsion will create the base for the cooking liquid, which will later turn into a velvety sauce. The liquid should appear a deep, uniform red. Chef's tip: always use warm water to avoid thermal shock to the cooking carrots, which would harden the outer fibres and slow down the stewing process.
  5. Pour the tomato solution directly over the carrots in the saucepan and add a pinch of salt and a fresh grinding of black pepper. Stir carefully to distribute the seasoning and ensure the liquid reaches the bottom of the pan. Quickly bring to a gentle boil over medium heat. You should notice the liquid starting to simmer softly around the edges of the saucepan. Chef's tip: do not over-salt at this stage, as the liquid will reduce during cooking, naturally concentrating the saltiness of the ingredients.
  6. Lower the heat to the minimum, ensuring the heat is just enough to maintain a constant simmer. Cover the saucepan with a tight-fitting lid to trap the steam inside and encourage gentle cooking. Let it stew for about 15-20 minutes, taking care to shake the pan occasionally without opening it too often. The result will be absorption cooking, where the carrots become tender thanks to their own moisture. Chef's tip: if you notice excessive steam escaping, place a sheet of parchment paper between the pot and the lid to create a perfect seal.
  7. After the indicated time, check the consistency of the carrots by piercing one with the prongs of a fork. The carrots should be soft and easily pierced but should retain a slight resistance at the centre so they aren't mushy or rubbery. The appearance should be translucent, and the orange colour will have become more intense and warm. If the fork enters too easily, turn off the heat immediately. Chef's tip: precision in consistency distinguishes a mediocre side dish from a professional one; always aim for an 'al dente' texture even for stewed vegetables.
  8. To complete the dish, remove the lid and slightly increase the heat for the final 2 minutes of cooking. Stir gently as the remaining liquid evaporates, transforming into a shiny glaze that coats the carrots thanks to the natural sugars and pectin. The cooking base should reduce until it becomes a thick, velvety sauce that clings perfectly to the rounds. The carrots will look brilliant, almost lacquered, and the bay leaf will have released all its balsamic aroma. Chef's tip: remove the garlic clove before serving to prevent a guest from finding it, but leave the bay leaves as a rustic and elegant garnish.

Tips

  • Slice the carrots into uniform rounds to achieve even cooking throughout.
  • Dissolve the tomato paste well in warm water before adding it to the carrots in the pan.
  • Keep the heat very low and the lid tightly closed to preserve the moisture of the carrots.

Variations

  • Add a pinch of ground cumin to give the dish a spicy and exotic fragrance.
  • Substitute the garlic with finely chopped shallot for a sweeter and milder flavor profile.
