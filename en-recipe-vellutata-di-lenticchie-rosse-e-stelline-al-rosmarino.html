Red Lentil Velouté and Stelline Pasta with Rosemary

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

Red split lentils melt during cooking, eliminating the risk of annoying skins and providing iron and protein. A nutritious and colourful one-pot meal.

A comforting cream soup made with hulled red lentils, which cook quickly and become incredibly tender. Fresh rosemary infuses a Mediterranean scent that pairs beautifully with the sweetness of carrot and potato. The star-shaped pasta makes it thick and pleasant on the palate.

Ingredients

  • Split red lentils120 g
  • Stelline pasta (small star-shaped pasta for soup)100 g
  • Carrot1 pc
  • Small potato1 pc
  • Extra virgin olive oil20 ml
  • Fresh rosemary1 sprig

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Begin by carefully peeling the carrot and the small potato, then dice them into very small, uniform cubes to promote even cooking. This task takes about 5 minutes using a sharp chef's knife and a stable cutting board. Ensure the pieces are of similar size so they soften simultaneously during the boiling phase. The final result should be a clean mirepoix that will cook quickly alongside the legumes. Chef's tip: cut the potato last and submerge it in water immediately to prevent it from browning due to oxidation.
  2. Take 120g of split red lentils and rinse them thoroughly under cold running water in a fine-mesh sieve. Move them with your hands until the water runs clear, removing any dusty residue or surface impurities. This step is essential to ensure a clean flavour and optimal digestibility of the legume. You will get bright orange lentils ready to be cooked without the need for prior soaking. Chef's tip: never skip this step as it removes saponins that could give the dish a slightly bitter aftertaste.
  3. Transfer the washed lentils, diced vegetables, and the whole rosemary sprig into a large pot, adding exactly 600ml of cold water. Turn the heat to medium and bring the liquid to a boil, partially covering with a lid to retain the aromatic vapours. This cold-start method allows the vegetables and legumes to gradually release their nutrients and aromas into the cooking liquid. You will notice the water starting to colour slightly and release the balsamic scent of the rosemary. Chef's tip: do not add salt at this stage as it could toughen the outer skin of the lentils.
  4. Once boiling, lower the heat to minimum and simmer gently for about 20 minutes, stirring occasionally with a wooden spoon. During this time, the red lentils will naturally break down, creating a base that is already partially thick and creamy. Check that the liquid does not dry out too quickly and, if necessary, keep some hot water handy to top it up. The ideal consistency is reached when the potato and carrot are tender enough to be easily mashed against the side of the pot. Chef's tip: skim the surface with a ladle if you see light foam forming to achieve a more vibrant final colour.
  5. After 20 minutes of cooking, temporarily turn off the heat and carefully remove the whole rosemary sprig using kitchen tongs. Ensure no needles are left in the mixture to guarantee a velvety texture that is pleasant on the palate, especially if the dish is intended for children. The rosemary scent should be present but delicate, without overpowering the sweet and earthy taste of the lentils. The result will be a thick, fragrant soup ready for mechanical transformation. Chef's tip: lightly rub the rosemary sprig between your hands before putting it in the pot at the start to awaken its essential oils.
  6. Use an immersion blender directly inside the pot to blend the mixture until you obtain a perfectly smooth, silky, and lump-free velouté. Tilt the blender slightly and work at medium speed using vertical movements to incorporate some air, making the cream lighter and fluffier. You will see the colour turn towards a softer, uniform orange thanks to the natural emulsion of the potato and lentil starches. The consistency should be that of a liquid but full-bodied cream. Chef's tip: if you want a top-tier velouté, pass it through a fine-mesh sieve after blending.
  7. Bring the lentil cream back to the hob over medium-low heat and, as soon as it returns to a light boil, pour the 100g of star-shaped pasta directly into it. Cook the pasta for the time indicated on the packet, usually between 5 and 7 minutes, stirring frequently because the density of the cream tends to make it stick to the bottom of the pot. If the cream dries out too much while cooking the pasta, add a small ladle of boiling water to maintain the desired fluidity. The stars should be tender and well integrated into the cream. Chef's tip: keep the heat very low and do not leave the pot, as small pasta cooks very quickly.
  8. When the pasta is perfectly cooked, turn off the heat and complete the dish by adding 20ml of raw extra virgin olive oil. Stir vigorously for a few seconds to create a final emulsion that will glaze the velouté and enhance the natural flavours of the lentils and rosemary. Pour the preparation into four individual bowls and let it cool for a couple of minutes before serving, ensuring the temperature is ideal for consumption. The dish will appear glossy, fragrant, and with an enveloping texture that invites tasting. Chef's tip: a pinch of grated Parmesan cheese can add a natural savoury note without overusing salt.

Tips

  • Rinse the red lentils thoroughly under running water to remove any residue before cooking.
  • Remove the rosemary sprig before blending to prevent the flavor from becoming too bitter or sharp.
  • If the soup appears too thick, add a ladle of hot water before stirring in the pasta.

Variations

  • You can add a teaspoon of tomato paste during cooking for a more vibrant color.
  • Substitute the star pasta with small-grain rice for a gluten-free version.
