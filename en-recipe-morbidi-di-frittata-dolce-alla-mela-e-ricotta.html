Soft Apple and Ricotta Sweet Frittata Bites

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 8 min · Easy

These fluffy squares combine egg proteins with the lightness of ricotta and the sweetness of grated apple. Perfect for children starting to feed themselves, they offer a soft and safe texture.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🥕 Vegetarian🧒 Kid-friendly🌾 Gluten-free🇮🇹 Italian

Ingredients

  • Large fresh eggs2 pcs
  • Fresh cow's milk ricotta (a soft, creamy Italian whey cheese)120 g
  • Golden Delicious apple1 pcs
  • Butter5 g

Allergens

Eggs, Milk

Method

  1. Begin by carefully washing and peeling the Golden apple, then grate it using the large holes of a box grater to obtain thick strands. Take the pulp in your hands and squeeze it gently to remove the excess juice, which could otherwise make the omelette too watery. This process takes about 2 minutes, and the apple should end up moist but not dripping. The fresh fragrance of the fruit will start to release as soon as the skin is removed. My advice is not to throw away the squeezed juice: it's delicious to drink immediately for a quick vitamin boost.
  2. Take a large bowl and pour in the 120g of fresh cow's ricotta, ensuring it is well-drained of any whey. Work it vigorously with a fork for about a minute until it loses its grainy consistency and becomes a smooth, velvety cream. This creamy base is essential for ensuring the final softness of the dish. You should achieve a homogeneous mass, free of large lumps and ready to receive the other ingredients. The chef suggests: if the ricotta is particularly dry, add a teaspoon of milk to make the process easier.
  3. Crack the 2 fresh eggs into a separate small dish to check their quality, then add them to the ricotta cream in the bowl. Use a hand whisk or a fork and beat the mixture with circular motions from the bottom upwards for about 2-3 minutes. The goal is to incorporate air to make the omelette as fluffy as a cloud once cooked. The result will be a thick, pale yellow liquid that is slightly frothy on the surface. The secret to success here is patience: the more you whisk, the more the omelette will rise during cooking.
  4. Now add the previously grated and squeezed apple to the egg and ricotta mix, sprinkling it in evenly. Stir gently with a spatula or spoon, making sure the apple pieces are completely coated by the mixture and do not form clumps. This phase takes only a few seconds but is crucial for an even distribution of flavour in every bite. The batter will now appear thicker and more structured, while still maintaining its characteristic lightness. A little trick: add a pinch of cinnamon if you want to enhance the natural aroma of the apple without adding sugar.
  5. Heat a small non-stick frying pan (about 18-20 cm in diameter) over a very low heat and add the 5g of butter. Let the butter melt slowly without letting it sizzle too much or brown, spreading it evenly over the bottom and sides with a pastry brush. The temperature must be gentle to avoid burning the fats and compromising the delicate flavour of the ricotta. You should obtain a shiny, transparent film perfectly covering the cooking surface. The chef advises never to raise the heat, as burnt butter would give an unpleasant bitter aftertaste.
  6. Pour all the egg, ricotta, and apple mixture into the hot pan, levelling the surface with the back of a spoon if necessary. Immediately cover with a lid of the correct size to trap the steam inside, which is vital for even cooking. Keep the heat at the minimum and cook for about 5 minutes without constantly lifting the lid. You will see the edges start to pull away slightly and the surface become matte and set, a sign that the heat is penetrating the heart of the omelette. The chef's tip: the lid acts like a small oven, making the omelette much taller and softer.
  7. When the surface of the omelette is no longer liquid to the touch, it's time to flip it using the lid method. Place the lid over the pan, invert with a firm but controlled movement, and slide the omelette back into the pan on its uncooked side. Cook for a further 2 minutes, still over a very low heat, to brown the base. The final result should be an omelette that is lightly golden on the outside but incredibly tender and moist on the inside. Be careful during this step: use a flat lid without raised edges to make the sliding easier.
  8. Once cooking is complete, turn off the heat and gently slide the omelette onto a clean chopping board. Let it rest and cool for at least 5 minutes; this will allow the apple fibres and egg proteins to stabilise, making the cutting cleaner. Using a straight-edged knife, cut the omelette into small cubes or strips of about 1-2 cm, ideal for eating with hands or a small fork. The portions will be soft, smelling of apple, with a pleasant dairy note. The final tip: serve at room temperature to best appreciate the natural sweetness of the ingredients.
