Strascinati with crispy Senise peppers

Basilicata · 4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

Fresh pasta from Basilicata with crunchy dried peppers, garlic and toasted breadcrumbs.

A cornerstone of Lucanian cuisine featuring semolina strascinati and the unique crunchiness of Senise dried peppers. The dish is topped with fried breadcrumbs for a rustic interplay of textures.

Ingredients

  • Semolina strascinati pasta360 g
  • Peperoni cruschi (dried Senise peppers)30 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil60 ml
  • Garlic6 g
  • Breadcrumbs40 g
  • Fine salt6 g

Allergens

Gluten
May contain traces of: Soy, Eggs.

Method

  1. Start by bringing a large pot of water to a boil. Once the water reaches a boil, pour in the 6 g of fine salt to correctly season the pasta. It is essential to salt the water now to allow the strascinati to absorb the seasoning evenly.
  2. Take the 30 g of peperoni cruschi and, with great care, remove the stem and the internal seeds without breaking the flesh excessively. Cleaning the inside well is a chef's secret to prevent the remaining seeds from burning and releasing a bitter aftertaste during cooking.
  3. In a large pan, heat 40 ml of extra virgin olive oil over medium heat. Dip the peppers for just a few seconds, about 2 or 3 per side, until they puff up slightly and take on a bright red color. Pay close attention: if the oil is too hot or they stay too long, the peppers will turn dark and bitter.
  4. Drain the peppers from the oil and let them cool on a plate for a couple of minutes; only then will they become fragrant and 'cruschi' (crunchy). Once cold and stiff, coarsely crumble a portion with your fingers, listening for the typical crunchy sound that gives the recipe its name.
  5. In the same pan with the oil flavored by the peppers, add the 6 g of garlic and let it brown over low heat for about 2 minutes. When the garlic releases an intense aroma and turns a golden blonde, it will have transferred all its character to the cooking base.
  6. In a separate small pan, pour the remaining 20 ml of oil and add the 40 g of breadcrumbs. Toast over medium heat for about 2 minutes, stirring often with a wooden spoon, until it turns a beautiful amber color and feels crunchy to the touch. This crumb will act as a savory contrast to the softness of the pasta.
  7. Cook the 360 g of strascinati in the boiling salted water; being fresh pasta, they will take only a few minutes to float to the surface. Drain them al dente, ensuring they still have a firm texture, which is fundamental for the success of the final sauté in the pan.
  8. Transfer the pasta directly into the pan with the garlic oil and turn the heat to high. Sauté the strascinati for one minute, tossing them well so that every groove of the pasta is evenly coated by the shiny and fragrant seasoning.
  9. Turn off the heat and add half of the toasted breadcrumbs and crumbled peppers, stirring one last time to distribute the flavors. Plate immediately, garnishing the surface with the remaining peppers and crumbs to ensure an explosion of crunchiness in every bite.

Tips

  • Fry the dried peppers for just a few seconds in hot oil to prevent them from turning bitter.
  • Let the peppers air cool after frying to make them perfectly glassy and crunchy.
  • Toast the breadcrumbs until deep golden brown to ensure maximum savoriness for the final garnish.

Variations

  • Add fresh chili to the flavored oil if you want an extra touch of heat in the seasoning.
  • Sprinkle with grated ricotta salata when serving for a sharp and savory contrast.
