Pasta with courgette cream and ricotta

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

A creamy and balanced main course that makes the most of the sweetness of seasonal courgettes and the lightness of fresh ricotta. An affordable and quick solution for a complete family meal, enriched by the crunchy touch of walnuts.

A fresh and velvety first course that combines the rusticity of whole wheat pasta with the delicacy of cow's milk ricotta. The zucchini cream coats the fusilli, while walnut kernels add a crunchy and toasted note to every bite.

Ingredients

  • Wholewheat durum wheat pasta (e.g. fusilli or penne)320 g
  • Fresh seasonal courgettes500 g
  • Fresh cow's milk ricotta (Italian whey cheese)250 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 g
  • Shallot1 pz
  • Walnut halves20 g
  • Fresh basil leaves10 foglie
  • Wholegrain sea salt5 g
  • Ground black pepper1 g

Allergens

Gluten, Milk, Tree nuts

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the cooking base: fill a large pot with approximately 3 litres of water and bring it to a boil over a high heat. As soon as the water reaches boiling point, add 5g of unrefined sea salt to season the pasta from its core. This step takes about 8-10 minutes depending on your hob's power. You should achieve a steady, vigorous boil before proceeding. Chef's tip: only add the salt when the water is boiling to avoid slowing down the time it takes to reach temperature.
  2. While the water is heating, prepare the vegetables: thoroughly wash 500g of courgettes and trim the ends, then cut them into small, even cubes of about 1 cm. Use a sharp knife and a stable chopping board to ensure uniform cooking in the pan. This manual chopping task takes about 5 minutes. The courgettes should look like a homogenous dice, ready to be sautéed. Chef's tip: even cutting is the secret to avoiding some cubes being mushy while others are still raw.
  3. Move on to the aromatic base: finely mince one shallot and place it in a large non-stick frying pan with 20g of extra virgin olive oil. Turn the heat to low and let the shallot soften for about 3 minutes, stirring occasionally with a wooden spoon. The shallot should become translucent and release a sweet aroma without browning or burning at all. Chef's tip: if you see the shallot drying out too quickly, add a tablespoon of hot water to steam it without exceeding the oil's smoke point.
  4. Cook the courgettes: slightly increase the heat to medium and add the diced courgettes to the pan with the shallot. Cook for 8-10 minutes, stirring frequently to ensure even browning. The courgettes should be tender when pierced with a fork but still retain a bright green colour and a slight bite. Chef's tip: do not cover the pan with a lid, otherwise the steam will cause the courgettes to lose their vibrant colour and turn grey.
  5. Cook the pasta: submerge 320g of whole wheat pasta into the boiling salted water and set a timer according to the 'al dente' instructions on the packet. Stir immediately to prevent the fusilli or penne from sticking to the bottom of the pot. Whole wheat pasta usually requires a few extra minutes compared to refined pasta but will maintain a hardier texture. Chef's tip: draining the pasta one minute before the suggested time allows you to finish cooking it in the pan without it becoming overcooked.
  6. Prepare the velvety sauce: take about three-quarters of the cooked courgettes and transfer them to a hand blender beaker, adding 250g of fresh ricotta, 10 basil leaves, and a ladle of the pasta cooking water. Blend everything for 30-45 seconds at maximum speed until you get a thick, lump-free cream. You will obtain a smooth mixture with an intense basil aroma and an enveloping consistency. Chef's tip: add the cooking water gradually to control the cream's fluidity, which should be similar to a light béchamel.
  7. Assemble the sauce: pour the resulting ricotta cream back into the frying pan where you left the remaining whole courgette cubes. Turn the heat to the lowest setting to keep the sauce warm, stirring gently to combine the two textures. The cream will warm gently without separating thanks to the fat content of the ricotta. Chef's tip: do not let the cream boil once the ricotta is added, as excessive heat could cause it to become grainy, ruining the silkiness of the dish.
  8. The finishing touch: drain the pasta and add it directly to the pan, tossing it over medium heat for about 60 seconds with the cream. Add the remaining 10g of raw oil, 20g of coarsely chopped walnuts, and 1g of freshly ground black pepper to enhance the flavours. The pasta should be perfectly glazed by the sauce, with the walnuts adding a crunchy, toasted note. Chef's tip: serve immediately in warmed bowls to prevent the whole wheat pasta from absorbing the moisture of the cream too quickly.

Tips

  • Keep a ladle of the pasta cooking water to make the zucchini and ricotta cream smoother.
  • Lightly toast the walnuts in a pan before adding them to the dish to enhance their aroma and crunchiness.
  • Use very fresh basil added only at the end to preserve its bright green color and fragrance.

Variations

  • Replace the walnuts with toasted pine nuts for a sweeter taste and more delicate texture.
  • Add grated organic lemon zest to give the dish a citrusy and refreshing note.
