Rice with Vegetables, Eggs and Legumes

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 35 min · Medium

A balanced and complete one-dish meal that combines wholemeal rice fibre with high-quality egg protein and plant-based chickpea protein. An affordable, colourful preparation perfect for managing the family's weekly menu.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🥕 Vegetarian🌾 Gluten-free🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free

Ingredients

  • Wholemeal rice280 g
  • Cooked chickpeas (drained)240 g
  • Medium eggs4 pz
  • Fresh courgettes300 g
  • Carrots200 g
  • Golden onion80 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 ml
  • Fine salt5 g
  • Black pepper2 g

Allergens

Eggs

Method

  1. Rinse the wholemeal rice under running water and cook it in plenty of salted water for about 30-35 minutes, or according to the package instructions, keeping it al dente.
  2. While the rice is cooking, wash the vegetables and cut the carrots and courgettes into small, uniform cubes of about 5 mm to ensure quick and even cooking.
  3. In a large non-stick frying pan or a wok, heat half of the extra virgin olive oil and soften the finely chopped onion over a low heat for 4 minutes until it becomes translucent.
  4. Add the carrots to the pan, cook for 3 minutes over a medium flame, then add the courgettes; sauté everything for another 5 minutes so the vegetables remain vibrant and crunchy to the bite.
  5. Drain the chickpeas from the preserving liquid, rinse them thoroughly under running water and add them to the vegetables, letting them season for 2 minutes with a pinch of salt.
  6. In a bowl, lightly beat the 4 eggs with pepper and a pinch of salt; move the vegetables to the edges of the pan and pour the eggs into the centre, stirring with a spatula until they form small scrambled ribbons.
  7. Drain the cooked wholemeal rice and pour it into the pan along with the vegetable, egg and legume mixture.
  8. Add the remaining raw extra virgin olive oil and sauté everything over a high heat for 2-3 minutes, stirring continuously to distribute the flavours evenly.
  9. Serve the dish hot or warm, checking that the seasoning is balanced and the rice texture is well-separated.
