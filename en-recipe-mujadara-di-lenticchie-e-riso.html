Lentil and Rice Mujadara

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 45 min · Medium

A pillar of Middle Eastern cuisine, humble yet incredibly rich in flavour thanks to caramelised onions. It is a complete protein dish thanks to the combination of grains and legumes.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🌱 Vegan🌾 Gluten-free🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🌍 International

Ingredients

  • Basmati or long-grain rice200 g
  • Dried brown lentils200 g
  • Large yellow onions3 pcs
  • Extra virgin olive oil60 ml
  • Cumin seeds or powder5 g
  • Ground cinnamon1 g
  • Saltq.b.

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Begin by thoroughly rinsing 200g of dried brown lentils under cold running water to remove any impurities. Place them in a large pot with plenty of unsalted cold water and bring to a boil, then lower the heat and simmer for about 15-20 minutes. The lentils should be tender to the bite but still firm and intact, preventing the skins from breaking. My professional advice is to avoid salting the water at this stage to keep the lentil skins soft, ensuring an even cook throughout.
  2. While the lentils are simmering, turn your attention to the 3 large yellow onions, which provide the aromatic soul of this dish. Peel and slice them into very thin, uniform rings, ideally using a mandoline to achieve a consistent thickness of about 2mm. Precision-cut onions ensure that every slice softens and browns at the same rate, preventing smaller bits from burning while larger ones stay raw. Maintain focus during this step, as it is the foundation of the final texture.
  3. Prepare the frying base by pouring 60ml of extra virgin olive oil into a large, heavy-based pan or wok. Heat the oil over medium heat and, once it begins to shimmer, add all the sliced onions, spreading them out evenly. Let them sizzle gently for about 20 minutes, stirring occasionally with a wooden spatula to prevent sticking. You are looking for a deep dark brown colour and a crispy texture, a process that requires patience for the sugars to caramelise without scorching.
  4. Once the onions have reached a perfect dark golden hue, use a slotted spoon to remove about half of them from the pan, leaving the oil and the remaining onions behind. Place the removed portion onto a plate lined with kitchen paper, spreading them out so they lose residual moisture and become even crispier as they cool. These onions will be the savoury, visual highlight of your final dish. Do not underestimate this resting period; it is the secret to that irresistible Middle Eastern 'crunch'.
  5. In the pan containing the remaining dark onions and fragrant oil, add 200g of raw Basmati or long-grain rice. Slightly increase the heat and toast the rice for about 2 minutes, stirring constantly so that every grain is glossy and coated in the aromatic fat. Now add 5g of cumin and 1g of cinnamon, allowing the heat to release the spices' essential oils for a few seconds. You will immediately smell a warm, toasted aroma, indicating the rice is absorbing all the flavours from the pan's base.
  6. Drain the pre-cooked lentils you set aside and add them to the toasted rice in the pan, stirring gently to avoid breaking the grains. Pour 400ml of boiling water over the mixture and season with salt according to your taste. Using already boiling water prevents thermal shock and ensures that the cooking resumes immediately and evenly. Make sure the rice and lentils are level beneath the water's surface before moving to the next stage.
  7. Cover the pan with a tight-fitting lid (if it's not perfectly airtight, place a clean tea towel between the pot and the lid) and reduce the heat to the absolute minimum. Let it cook for 12-15 minutes without ever lifting the lid, allowing the steam to cook the grains by absorption. By the end, the water should be completely absorbed, and the rice should look plump and separate. Remember, peeking is the enemy of pilaf rice; the trapped steam is crucial for the perfect texture.
  8. Turn off the heat and, keeping the lid firmly in place, let the Mujadara rest for at least 5 minutes before serving. This resting phase allows the residual moisture to distribute evenly within the grains, making them elastic and ready to be fluffed. After the time is up, use a fork to gently fluff the rice, working from the edges towards the centre with light movements. You will notice how the scents of cinnamon and cumin have harmoniously fused with the sweetness of the caramelised onions.
  9. Transfer the preparation to a large serving platter or individual bowls for a more intimate touch. Generously garnish the surface with the crispy onions you set aside on the kitchen paper, distributing them evenly. The final result is a dish with warm, earthy tones, where the softness of the rice and lentils contrasts beautifully with the crunch of the onions. Serve the dish warm, perhaps accompanied by a dollop of Greek yoghurt for a truly professional contrast in freshness.
