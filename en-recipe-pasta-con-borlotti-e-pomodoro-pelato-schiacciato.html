Pasta with Borlotti Beans and Crushed Peeled Tomatoes

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

The classic Italian 'pasta e fagioli' revisited for babies: the beans have their skins removed for optimal digestibility and the sauce is sweet and velvety.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⬇️ Big saving🌱 Vegan🧒 Kid-friendly🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🇮🇹 Italian

A classic of Italian rustic cuisine, this pasta with borlotti beans and tomato offers a full and comforting flavor. The bean cream obtained through a food mill coats the pasta, creating a perfect blend of textures.

Ingredients

  • Short pasta such as tubetti (small tube-shaped pasta)120 g
  • Pre-cooked borlotti beans (organic, salt-free)200 g
  • Peeled plum tomatoes150 g
  • Small carrot1 pcs
  • Extra virgin olive oil15 ml

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the aromatic base by finely mincing a small carrot with a sharp knife or a fine grater. Place the minced carrot in a non-stick pan with two or three tablespoons of water and turn the heat to medium. Let it stew gently for about 5-7 minutes until the carrot is completely tender and the water has evaporated. This step allows the fibres to soften without frying, making the base perfect for young children. Chef's tip: add a splash of water if the carrot starts to brown, as it should remain a bright orange colour.
  2. Now process the 200g of pre-cooked borlotti beans by carefully passing them through a food mill fitted with a fine-hole disc. Position the mill over a large bowl and work the pulses with steady circular motions to extract all the pulp. This technique is essential for separating the velvety cream from the outer skins, which can be difficult to digest. You will obtain a thick, smooth purée free from lumps. Chef's tip: slightly warm the beans before milling; the pulp will separate from the skins much more easily.
  3. Take 150g of canned peeled tomatoes and transfer them to a shallow dish for manual preparation. Use a fork to crush them vigorously until you obtain a rustic pulp free of any large whole pieces. Ensure you remove any green parts or hard cores from the tomatoes to guarantee a uniform sauce. The result should be a juicy pulp ready for brief cooking. Chef's tip: always crush tomatoes while cold to prevent accidental splashes of hot juice.
  4. Pour the crushed tomatoes into the pan with the now-tender carrots and stir with a wooden spoon. Cook over low heat for about 5 minutes, allowing the flavours to meld and the acidity of the tomato to mellow slightly. The consistency will begin to thicken, and the colour will turn a deeper, more fragrant red. Keep the heat low to avoid burning the carrot base. Chef's tip: if the tomato appears too dry, cover with a lid to retain the natural moisture.
  5. Add the previously obtained borlotti bean cream to the tomato and carrot sauce in the pan. Mix thoroughly to combine the two mixtures and continue cooking over low heat for about 10 minutes, stirring occasionally. The sauce should appear velvety, with a pinkish hue typical of the legume and tomato combination, and be very fragrant. This slow cooking allows the cream to become deeply flavoured. Chef's tip: use a hand whisk if you notice the bean cream is struggling to blend perfectly into the tomato.
  6. Bring a large pot of water to the boil, carefully avoiding adding salt as per nutritional guidelines. Pour in the 120g of tubetti pasta and cook according to the package instructions, but extend the cooking time by a couple of minutes if necessary. The pasta should be well-cooked and soft, suitable for easy chewing, but should not fall apart completely. Drain the pasta, reserving a cup of the cooking water. Chef's tip: stir the pasta frequently during the first few minutes of cooking to prevent the tubetti from sticking to the bottom.
  7. Transfer the drained tubetti directly into the pan with the borlotti and tomato cream. Toss the pasta over medium-low heat for one minute, stirring vigorously so the sauce enters the small holes of the short pasta. If the preparation seems too thick, gradually add a tablespoon or two of the reserved cooking water. The final result should be pasta coated in a creamy sauce, almost a thick 'spoonable' soup. Chef's tip: do not drain the pasta too dry, as the residual starch will help create a perfect emulsion.
  8. Turn off the heat and proceed with the final raw seasoning by adding the 15ml of extra virgin olive oil. Drizzle the oil in and stir one last time to glaze the dish and preserve all the nutritional properties of the high-quality fat. If desired, add a pinch of very finely rubbed dried oregano for a Mediterranean aromatic note without overpowering the dish. Serve the pasta lukewarm, distributing it into four shallow bowls. Chef's tip: adding oil off the heat maintains that fresh olive aroma that enhances the sweetness of the borlotti beans.

Tips

  • Pass the beans through a food mill instead of blending them to remove skins and achieve a silky consistency.
  • Crush the peeled tomatoes with a fork on a plate before adding them to the pan for a rustic sauce.
  • Cook the pasta al dente to prevent the thickness of the bean cream from making it too soft.

Variations

  • Replace the tubetti with ditaloni or mixed pasta for an even more traditional touch.
  • You can enrich the aromatic base by adding a finely chopped celery stalk along with the carrot.
