Orange and Rosemary Caramelised Carrots and Fennel

4 servings · Prep 5 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

A fragrant and crunchy side dish that highlights the natural sweetness of autumn vegetables thanks to a citrus reduction. This preparation transforms humble ingredients into a refined and quick accompaniment, perfect for the whole family.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🌱 Vegan🌾 Gluten-free🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🇮🇹 Italian

Ingredients

  • Fresh carrots500 g
  • Large fennel bulbs600 g
  • Filtered orange juice150 ml
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Fresh rosemary2 rametti
  • Apple cider vinegar10 ml
  • Fine salt5 g
  • Black pepper1 g

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Begin by thoroughly washing the carrots and fennel; peel the carrots with a vegetable peeler and remove the tougher outer parts of the fennel.
  2. Cut the carrots into sticks of about 5 cm and the fennel into thin slices of half a centimetre: uniform thickness is essential to ensure quick and simultaneous cooking in less than 15 minutes.
  3. Heat the extra virgin olive oil in a large non-stick pan (at least 28 cm) for 1 minute over medium-high heat until it becomes fluid and coats the pan.
  4. Pour the vegetables into the pan and sauté them for 5 minutes, stirring often with a spatula; the edges should begin to brown slightly, a sign of the Maillard reaction which releases flavour.
  5. Add the roughly chopped rosemary needles, salt, and pepper; deglaze with the apple cider vinegar for 30 seconds to balance the natural sweetness of the vegetables with an acidic note.
  6. Pour in the orange juice and turn up the heat; leave to cook for about 7-8 minutes without a lid, allowing the liquid to reduce and transform into a shiny glaze.
  7. Check the progress: the vegetables are ready when the juice has become syrupy and completely coats the sticks, which should be tender when pierced with a fork but still intact and not mushy.
  8. Turn off the heat and let the side dish rest for 2 minutes in the pan before serving; this step allows the glaze to thicken further and the flavours to stabilise.
