Orange and Rosemary Caramelised Carrots and Fennel

4 servings · Prep 5 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

A fragrant and crunchy side dish that highlights the natural sweetness of autumn vegetables thanks to a citrus reduction. This preparation transforms humble ingredients into a refined and quick accompaniment, perfect for the whole family.

A refined side dish that enhances the natural sweetness of roots and bulbs. The orange and apple cider vinegar glaze coats the carrots and fennel, while fresh rosemary adds a Mediterranean aromatic note.

Ingredients

  • Fresh carrots500 g
  • Large fennel bulbs600 g
  • Filtered orange juice150 ml
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Fresh rosemary2 rametti
  • Apple cider vinegar10 ml
  • Fine salt5 g
  • Black pepper1 g

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the vegetables: thoroughly wash 500g of carrots and 600g of fennel under running water to remove any soil residue. Use a vegetable peeler to remove the outer skin from the carrots, and trim the woody base and any fibrous outer layers from the fennel. The result should be clean, bright raw materials ready to be transformed into a gourmet side dish. Chef's tip: do not discard the trimmings; you can use them to make a light vegetable stock.
  2. Proceed with technical cutting to ensure even cooking: cut the carrots into uniform sticks approximately 5cm long and slice the fennel lengthwise into 0.5cm thick slices. Aim to keep the dimensions as consistent as possible, as this allows both vegetables to soften within the same timeframe. The vegetables should look like a geometrically ordered 'macedonia' of produce. Chef's tip: if the carrots are very large, cut them in half lengthwise first to stabilise them on the chopping board.
  3. Take a large non-stick frying pan, at least 28cm in diameter, and pour in 30ml of high-quality extra virgin olive oil. Heat the oil over medium-high heat for about a minute, moving the pan so the fat evenly coats the entire surface. You will know it is ready when you see the oil become fluid and form small, shimmering ripples. Chef's tip: avoid letting the oil smoke, as exceeding the smoke point alters its flavour and nutritional properties.
  4. Carefully add the carrot sticks and fennel slices to the hot pan, being mindful of any splattering. Sauté the vegetables over a brisk heat for about 5 minutes, stirring frequently with a silicone or wooden spatula to avoid scratching the coating. You will notice the edges of the vegetables beginning to brown slightly due to the Maillard reaction, releasing a sweet and toasted aroma. Chef's tip: do not overcrowd the pan; if necessary, use two pans to allow every piece to touch the hot base.
  5. It is time to introduce the aromatics: add the needles from 2 rosemary sprigs, finely chopped, 5g of fine salt, and 1g of freshly ground black pepper. Distribute the seasonings evenly by tossing the vegetables for another 30 seconds, allowing the heat to activate the rosemary's essential oils. You will immediately smell an intense Mediterranean aroma that heralds the dish's character. Chef's tip: chop the rosemary very finely to prevent the needles from being unpleasant when eating.
  6. To balance the natural sweetness of the ingredients, deglaze the mixture by pouring 10ml of apple cider vinegar directly onto the sizzling vegetables. Let the pungent alcohol evaporate for about 30 seconds over high heat, continuing to move the pan. This step serves to 'cleanse' the palate and create an acidic base necessary for the subsequent caramelisation with the orange. Chef's tip: stand back slightly while deglazing to avoid the sharp vapours of the evaporating vinegar.
  7. Pour 150ml of strained orange juice into the pan and maintain a high heat to accelerate the reduction process. Cook uncovered for about 7-8 minutes, allowing the water in the juice to evaporate and the sugars to concentrate around the vegetable fibres. The liquid will begin to transform from a watery juice into a glossy, amber glaze that clings to every single piece. Chef's tip: if the juice reduces too quickly before the vegetables are tender, add a tablespoon of hot water.
  8. Carefully monitor the final texture: the carrots and fennel should be tender when pressed with a fork but maintain a pleasant bite (al dente). The orange glaze should have become syrupy, adhering perfectly to the vegetables without pooling excessively at the bottom. The colour should be a vibrant, glossy orange, signifying a perfect emulsion of sugars and oil. Chef's tip: use a spatula to scrape all the glaze from the bottom of the pan, as this is the most flavourful part.
  9. Turn off the heat and let the side dish rest directly in the pan for 2 minutes before serving. This brief rest allows the cellular structure of the vegetables to stabilise and the glaze to thicken further as the temperature drops slightly. You will achieve a balanced, fragrant dish with a texture that is velvety on the outside and firm on the inside. Chef's tip: serve on a pre-warmed plate to maintain the optimal temperature and the fluidity of the caramelised glaze.

Tips

  • Cut carrots and fennel into similar sizes to ensure even cooking in the pan.
  • Always filter the orange juice to prevent pulp residue from burning during the caramelization phase.
  • Use a wide pan at least 28 cm in diameter to allow vegetables to brown without overlapping too much.

Variations

  • Replace apple cider vinegar with balsamic vinegar for a bolder taste and a deep amber color.
  • Add a sprinkle of freshly ground black pepper at the end of cooking to balance the orange's sweetness.
