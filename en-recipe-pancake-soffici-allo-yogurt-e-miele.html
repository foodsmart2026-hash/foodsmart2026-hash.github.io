Fluffy Yogurt and Honey Pancakes

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

A light and protein-rich variation of classic pancakes, made with affordable plain yogurt. The texture is spongy, ideal for absorbing a drizzle of honey or jam.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⬇️ Big saving🥕 Vegetarian🧒 Kid-friendly🌍 International

These pancakes stand out for their incredibly fluffy texture thanks to the addition of yogurt to the batter. Millefiori honey adds a delicate sweet note, making them perfect for weekend brunch.

Ingredients

  • Plain natural yogurt250 g
  • Medium eggs2 pcs
  • Strong white flour (Type 0, an Italian flour with medium protein content)150 g
  • Wildflower honey (Millefiori, a common multi-flower Italian honey)30 g
  • Baking powder8 g
  • Vegetable oil20 ml
  • Semi-skimmed milk50 ml

Allergens

Eggs, Gluten, Milk

Method

  1. Begin by cracking 2 medium eggs into a large mixing bowl and add 250g of natural plain yoghurt along with 30g of wildflower honey. Use a hand whisk to thoroughly combine the ingredients until you achieve a fluid and perfectly smooth emulsion. Whisk with circular motions for about 1-2 minutes, ensuring the honey is completely dissolved and hasn't settled at the bottom. The final result should be a sleek cream without any visible streaks of egg yolk. My professional advice is to use room-temperature ingredients to facilitate a better bond between the fats and sugars.
  2. Take a fine-mesh sieve and sift 150g of plain flour and 8g of baking powder directly over the liquid mixture. This step is crucial for incorporating air and preventing lumps that would compromise the fluffy texture of your pancakes. Use a spatula or the whisk to fold in the dry ingredients with gentle movements, taking your time. You should obtain a fairly thick and still slightly rustic batter. Chef's tip: do not sift the flour too far in advance, or it will lose the aerating effect needed for instant leavening.
  3. Now, slowly pour in 50ml of semi-skimmed milk and 20ml of vegetable oil in a steady stream, stirring gently from the bottom upwards so as not to deflate the mixture. Work the batter only as long as necessary to make it uniform, avoiding overworking the flour's gluten. The movement should be slow and steady, using a spatula or a wooden spoon until the liquids are fully absorbed. The perfect consistency is a thick, velvety batter that 'ribbons', meaning it leaves a trail on the surface when dropped. The secret to extra-tall pancakes is precisely not to overmix: a few small lumps are better than a runny batter.
  4. Prepare for cooking by heating a high-quality non-stick frying pan over medium-low heat for about 2-3 minutes so the heat is evenly distributed across the base. Take a paper towel dampened with a drop of vegetable oil and quickly wipe the surface to create an almost invisible protective film. The temperature is correct when a drop of water flicked onto the pan dances across the surface before evaporating instantly. You should feel a gentle warmth when holding your hand near it, without the pan smoking. Tip: avoid using too much oil directly in the pan, otherwise the pancakes will fry instead of browning evenly.
  5. Using a small ladle or a kitchen spoon, pour a portion of batter into the centre of the pan, letting it expand naturally into a regular circle. Try to maintain a distance of at least a couple of centimetres between pancakes if you are cooking more than one at a time. Keep the flame constant and never too high, avoiding heat spikes that would burn the outside while leaving the inside raw and doughy. The shape should be circular, with a thickness of about half a centimetre as soon as it is poured. The chef suggests pouring the batter into a single central point to allow gravity to create a perfect disc.
  6. Cook the first side for about 2 minutes, carefully monitoring the top surface of the pancake. The crucial moment to act is when you see small air bubbles appearing and popping, leaving tiny holes, and the edges begin to look dry and matte. Use a thin spatula to slightly lift one edge and check that the bottom is a beautiful, inviting golden hazelnut colour. The batter on top will still be slightly damp, but the internal structure must already be stable enough to support the flip. Do not rush: if you flip the pancake too early, the structure will ruinously collapse.
  7. Decisively slide the spatula under the pancake and flip it with a quick, confident flick of the wrist to cook the other side. Let it cook for another minute, or until you see the pancake puff up visibly due to the heat's effect on the baking powder. The aroma of biscuits and yoghurt will start to fill the kitchen, a sign that the honey's sugars are caramelising correctly. Both sides should be evenly golden, and the texture should feel springy and spongy to the touch. My trick is to lightly press the centre with the tip of the spatula: if it bounces back immediately, it is perfectly cooked.
  8. As the pancakes are ready, stack them on top of each other on a serving plate to utilise their residual heat and keep them soft and moist. Serve your breakfast while still warm, garnishing as desired with an extra drizzle of honey or fresh seasonal fruit to balance the natural sweetness. Each portion will be extremely light and digestible thanks to the acidic component of the yoghurt making the internal crumb silky. The chef’s final touch? Serve on slightly warmed plates to prevent thermal shock from deflating the freshly cooked pancakes.

Tips

  • Flip the pancakes only when you see small bubbles appearing on the upper surface of the batter.
  • Do not overmix the batter after adding the flour to prevent them from becoming chewy.
  • Heat the non-stick pan well before pouring the first portion to ensure even cooking.

Variations

  • Use Greek yogurt instead of plain yogurt for a thicker and denser consistency.
  • Replace millefiori honey with acacia honey for an even subtler and more floral aroma.
