Baked Mackerel with Potatoes and Lemon

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

A main course cornerstone of the Mediterranean diet that promotes oily fish, rich in Omega-3, paired with complex carbohydrates. This preparation is extremely economical, easy to find in any supermarket, and ensures an excellent nutritional profile for the whole family.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⚖️ Good value for money🌾 Gluten-free🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free

A flavorful bluefish main course, where the tender flesh of the mackerel meets the crunchiness of roasted potatoes. Lemon and rosemary provide a Mediterranean freshness that perfectly balances the intense and characteristic taste of this fish.

Ingredients

  • Fresh mackerel (whole, gutted)800 g
  • Waxy potatoes800 g
  • Unwaxed lemons2 pcs
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 ml
  • Garlic2 cloves
  • Fresh rosemary2 sprigs
  • Fine salt5 g
  • Black pepper1 g

Allergens

Fish

Method

  1. Start by preheating your oven to 200°C (fan mode) to ensure even heat distribution from the beginning. Peel 800g of yellow potatoes and cut them into very thin round slices, about 3-4 millimetres thick, using a sharp chef's knife or a mandoline for maximum precision. Rinse the slices under cold water to remove excess starch, which prevents them from sticking together during roasting. The result should be uniform discs ready for parboiling. My tip is to keep the thickness consistent to prevent some slices from remaining raw while others burn.
  2. Bring a pot of lightly salted water to the boil and parboil the potato slices for exactly 5 minutes. This professional technique allows the potato starches, which take longer to cook than fish, to start softening. Drain the potatoes very gently with a slotted spoon to keep them intact and let them cool slightly on a clean tea towel. You should achieve slices that are tender to the touch but still firm. Do not skip this step, or your potatoes will likely be undercooked by the time the fish is done.
  3. Take your 800g of cleaned mackerel and rinse them briefly under a cold tap, ensuring you remove any blood residue from the abdominal cavity. Dry each fish meticulously, both inside and out, by patting it with kitchen paper. The skin must feel completely dry to the touch to allow the heat to create a light crust rather than a 'steamed' effect. A well-dried fish is the secret to preserving the delicate sea flavours. Ensuring dryness is essential for a professional roasted finish.
  4. Using a smooth-edged knife, make two or three diagonal slashes about half a centimetre deep into the sides of each mackerel. Inside the belly and within the slashes, evenly distribute 5g of salt and 1g of black pepper, then insert a piece of the 2 garlic cloves and a slice of the 2 lemons. This step ensures the aromatics penetrate the firm flesh during the cooking process. You will achieve a fish that is fragrant inside and out. Be careful not to cut through the backbone to keep the fillets whole.
  5. Line a large baking tray with baking parchment and arrange the parboiled potatoes to create a uniform, compact bed. Season the vegetable base with 20ml of extra virgin olive oil (half the total amount) and scatter the needles from 2 sprigs of fresh rosemary. Make sure the potatoes aren't overlapping too much to allow for optimal browning and crisping. You will see a fragrant, golden mosaic forming. The chef's tip is to lightly toss the potatoes with the oil to ensure they are fully coated.
  6. Carefully place the four mackerels on top of the potato bed, spacing them slightly apart. Drizzle the surface of the fish with the remaining 20ml of extra virgin olive oil and the juice of half a freshly squeezed lemon. The natural emulsion created during roasting will protect the mackerel's muscle fibres, which can dry out quickly. The fish should look glossy and ready for the heat. Avoid overusing lemon juice at this stage to prevent 'curing' the raw flesh before it hits the oven.
  7. Slice the remaining lemon into thin rounds and arrange them harmoniously over the mackerel and in the gaps between the potatoes. During roasting, the heat will release the essential oils from the zest, providing a persistent citrus note to the entire dish. The lemon slices will become slightly charred, adding a professional aesthetic touch. Ensure you use unwaxed lemons as the peel is in direct contact with the food. This step perfectly balances the richness of the oily fish.
  8. Place the tray on the middle shelf of the preheated oven and bake for 20-25 minutes, depending on the size of the fish. The mackerel is cooked when the eyes turn opaque white and the flesh near the head is no longer translucent when tested with a fork. The potatoes should be golden at the edges and soft in the centre. Remove from the oven and let it rest for two minutes before serving to allow the juices to redistribute. Avoid cooking for longer than 25 minutes to keep the flesh moist and succulent.

Tips

  • Pat the mackerel dry with paper towels before baking to achieve crispier skin.
  • Cut the potatoes into uniform slices to ensure they cook perfectly alongside the fish within the indicated time.
  • Make sure the garlic is well inserted into the cuts of the fish to prevent it from burning excessively during baking.

Variations

  • Add halved cherry tomatoes to the baking tray for a touch of color and sweetness.
  • Replace the rosemary with fennel seeds for a different aroma that pairs well with bluefish.
