Baked Mackerel with Potatoes and Lemon

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

A main course cornerstone of the Mediterranean diet that promotes oily fish, rich in Omega-3, paired with complex carbohydrates. This preparation is extremely economical, easy to find in any supermarket, and ensures an excellent nutritional profile for the whole family.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🌱 Vegan🌾 Gluten-free🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free

Ingredients

  • Fresh mackerel (whole, gutted)800 g
  • Waxy potatoes800 g
  • Unwaxed lemons2 pcs
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 ml
  • Garlic2 cloves
  • Fresh rosemary2 sprigs
  • Fine salt5 g
  • Black pepper1 g

Allergens

Fish

Method

  1. Preheat the oven to 200°C (180°C fan). Peel the potatoes and cut them into very thin round slices, about 3-4 millimetres thick, so they cook evenly with the fish.
  2. Parboil the potato slices in lightly salted water for about 5 minutes: this step is crucial to ensure a tender texture inside without the risk of them remaining raw or burning in the oven.
  3. Rinse the mackerel under cold running water to remove any residue and dry them thoroughly with kitchen paper; dry skin will allow for better cooking rather than 'steaming'.
  4. Make two or three diagonal slashes on the sides of each fish and place a pinch of salt, pepper, a slice of lemon, and a piece of garlic inside the cavity to flavour the flesh deeply.
  5. Line a large baking tray with baking parchment and create an even bed with the parboiled potatoes. Season them with half of the extra virgin olive oil and the fresh rosemary needles.
  6. Place the mackerel on top of the bed of potatoes. Drizzle the fish with the remaining oil and the juice of half a lemon, which will help keep the fish fibres hydrated and soft.
  7. Cut the remaining lemon into thin slices and distribute them over the mackerel and among the potatoes to release the essential oils from the peel during cooking.
  8. Bake the tray on the middle shelf for 20-25 minutes. The fish is ready when the eye becomes completely white and opaque and the flesh, when tested with a fork, easily pulls away from the central bone.
