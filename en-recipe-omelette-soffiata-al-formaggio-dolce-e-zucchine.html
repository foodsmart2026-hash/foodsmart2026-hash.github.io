Fluffy Soufflé Omelette with Mild Cheese and Courgette

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 10 min · Easy

An incredibly soft omelette enriched with ricotta which makes it similar to a soufflé, perfect for children from 1 to 3 years old. The grated courgettes melt into the egg for a texture that is easy to manage.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⬇️ Big saving🥕 Vegetarian🧒 Kid-friendly🌾 Gluten-free

This omelette stands out for its airy and light texture, achieved by combining eggs with cow's milk ricotta. Grated zucchini add freshness and color to this simple yet refined main course.

Ingredients

  • Medium eggs3 pcs
  • Fresh cow's milk ricotta [A mild, creamy Italian whey cheese]80 g
  • Courgettes150 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil10 ml

Allergens

Eggs, Milk

Method

  1. Begin by thoroughly washing 150g of fresh courgettes and trimming the ends with a sharp knife. Use a fine box grater to shred them completely, then gather the pulp in a clean tea towel or squeeze it between your hands. Squeeze firmly to remove as much excess water as possible, as this is crucial to prevent the omelette from becoming soggy. You should aim for a dry, velvety courgette pulp that will integrate perfectly into the mixture. My advice is not to skip this step, as the moisture from the courgettes would prevent the eggs from rising properly.
  2. Take 3 medium room-temperature eggs and crack them into a large, high-sided bowl. Use a hand whisk and beat them vigorously for at least 2-3 minutes using circular, upward motions to incorporate as much air as possible. Continue until a light foam forms on the surface and the mixture appears pale and frothy. This step is the secret to the 'soufflé' effect, which will give your dish a cloud-like texture. Ensure the bowl is perfectly clean to help the volume develop effectively.
  3. In a small separate dish, mash 80g of fresh cow's ricotta with a fork until it becomes a smooth cream without any lumps. Gradually add it to the beaten eggs, folding it in with gentle movements using the whisk or a spatula so as not to deflate the mixture. Mix until you achieve a homogeneous emulsion where the cheese is perfectly distributed. The visual result should be a thick, pale yellow, and very airy cream. A little trick: use well-drained ricotta for a better structural hold during cooking.
  4. Now add the previously grated and squeezed courgettes to the egg and ricotta mixture. Stir with a spatula, using slow bottom-to-top folding motions to distribute the vegetable strands evenly without expelling the incorporated air. Ensure there are no clumps of courgettes in one spot, guaranteeing a balanced flavour in every bite. The mass should appear rich, smell fresh, and look visibly aerated. Avoid over-mixing to ensure the final fluffiness of the omelette is not compromised.
  5. Place a non-stick frying pan, approximately 20-22 cm in diameter, over the heat and pour in 10ml of extra virgin olive oil, spreading it well over the entire surface with a brush or kitchen paper. Turn the hob to medium heat and wait until the oil is hot but not smoking. Pour the egg mixture into the centre of the pan, gently levelling it towards the edges with the back of a spoon. You should hear a slight sizzle, indicating the base has started cooking immediately. Do not disturb the mixture too much once poured to maintain the soft structure.
  6. Immediately cover the pan with a tight-fitting lid and turn the heat down to minimum. Cook for about 5 minutes without ever lifting the lid; the steam trapped inside will act as a proofing chamber, making the omelette rise spectacularly. You will see the edges start to pull away and the surface become opaque and set. The colour should remain light, a sign of gentle cooking that doesn't burn the egg proteins. The chef's tip is to use a transparent lid to monitor the growth without losing heat.
  7. When the surface feels dry to the touch, flip the omelette with extreme care using a wide spatula or by sliding it onto a plate. Cook the other side for about 2 minutes, still over very low heat and without the lid, until it is lightly golden. The omelette should be thick, springy, and have a texture similar to a savoury sponge cake. Check that the core is well-cooked by pressing lightly in the centre: no liquid should escape. Be careful not to overcook, or the texture may become rubbery.
  8. Transfer the omelette to a wooden chopping board and let it rest for one minute to allow the structure to stabilise. Using a sharp, plain-edged knife, cut it into regular cubes of about 2 cm per side, ideal for little hands to pick up easily. Arrange the cubes on a serving plate, noting the aerated internal structure and the bright green specks of courgette. The final result is a nutritious, very light finger food with a delicate milky and vegetable flavour. Serve the cubes warm to best appreciate their softness.

Tips

  • Grate the zucchini using large holes and squeeze them between your hands to remove excess water.
  • Use a high-quality non-stick pan to prevent the omelette from sticking during slow cooking.
  • Serve immediately to enjoy its maximum fluffiness straight from the stove.

Variations

  • You can enrich the mixture with aged grated cheese for a bolder flavor.
  • Replace the zucchini with chopped zucchini flowers for an even more seasonal version.
