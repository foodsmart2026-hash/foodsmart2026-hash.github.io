Baked Polenta with Mushrooms and Taleggio

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

A rustic one-pot dish that uses instant polenta to speed up cooking without sacrificing taste. Taleggio cheese creates an enveloping creaminess.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🧒 Kid-friendly🇮🇹 Italian

A hearty and comforting one-pot dish, ideal for cold family gatherings. Freshly made creamy polenta is enriched with the intense flavor of melted Taleggio and sautéed mushrooms. This rustic combination celebrates the culinary traditions of Northern Italy.

Ingredients

  • instant polenta flour250 g
  • frozen mixed mushrooms450 g
  • Taleggio (a soft, pungent Northern Italian cheese)150 g
  • garlic1 spicchio
  • butter20 g
  • parsleyq.b.

Allergens

Milk

Method

  1. Begin by heating a drizzle of extra virgin olive oil in a large frying pan with a crushed garlic clove, then add the 450g of frozen mixed mushrooms. Keep the heat high for the first few minutes, stirring frequently with a wooden spoon to encourage the rapid evaporation of the vegetation water without boiling the mushrooms. You are looking for well-browned mushrooms with a firm texture and an intense forest aroma. My chef's tip is to avoid adding salt at this stage: salt draws out moisture too early, preventing the Maillard reaction that creates that delicious toasted flavour.
  2. Once the mushrooms are dry, deglaze them with a splash of water or white wine and continue cooking over medium heat for about 10 minutes until tender. At the end of the cooking time, turn off the heat, season with salt and pepper, and stir in the finely chopped fresh parsley. You should obtain a flavourful, dry mixture, which is essential for preventing the polenta from becoming soggy during assembly. Remember to remove the garlic clove now, so its flavour remains only as a delicate background note.
  3. While the mushrooms rest, bring 1 litre of lightly salted water to a boil in a large, heavy-based saucepan. Once boiling, pour in the 250g of instant polenta flour in a steady stream, whisking vigorously with a hand whisk to prevent any lumps from forming. Cook for about 5 minutes, or as directed on the packet, maintaining a medium-low heat and stirring constantly. The polenta is ready when it is thick, velvety, and begins to pull away slightly from the sides of the pan.
  4. Prepare a rectangular baking dish (approx. 20x25 cm) and pour half of the boiling, fluid polenta onto the bottom, working quickly before it begins to set. Use a silicone spatula or the back of a spoon dipped in cold water to level the surface into an even layer about one centimetre thick. This technique prevents the polenta from sticking to the tool, allowing you to achieve a perfectly smooth base. The chef's tip is to keep the remaining polenta in the pot covered with a lid to preserve its moisture and heat.
  5. Spread the sautéed mushrooms over the polenta base, ensuring you cover the entire surface evenly right up to the edges. Take the 150g of Taleggio cheese and cut it into small cubes, scattering them uniformly over the mushroom layer to ensure every portion has the right amount of creaminess. The residual heat from the polenta will start to melt the cheese, releasing its characteristic sweet and buttery aroma. Try not to clump the cheese in one spot to maintain a balanced flavour profile across all layers.
  6. Cover the filling with the remaining polenta, pouring it by the spoonful over the areas where the filling is most sparse, then spreading it gently. Use the wet spatula again to level the final surface, sealing the filling inside as if it were a savoury pie. The surface must appear compact and free of holes, ready for the butter topping. If the polenta has firmed up too much in the pot, stir in a tablespoon of hot water and mix vigorously before spreading it out.
  7. Cut the 20g of butter into small knobs and distribute them across the surface of the polenta to encourage an even and inviting golden finish. Place the dish in the upper part of the preheated oven at 200°C and activate the grill function for about 10 minutes in total. Monitor the cooking closely: the polenta is ready when a golden, crispy crust forms on the surface and the cheese begins to bubble slightly at the sides. Be careful not to burn the top, as the grill acts very quickly once the temperature is reached.
  8. Once removed from the oven, let the 'polenta pasticciata' rest for at least 5-7 minutes before cutting it into squares. This step is crucial to allow the starches to stabilise and the cheese to regain an ideal consistency, preventing the portions from falling apart when served. Use a smooth-bladed knife lightly greased with oil to achieve clean and precise cuts. Serve the squares hot, garnishing as desired with a final pinch of fresh parsley for a touch of colour and freshness.

Tips

  • Whisk the polenta flour into boiling water gradually to prevent lumps from forming.
  • Distribute the Taleggio in small, even pieces so it melts consistently between the layers.
  • Let the dish rest for five minutes after baking to help the portions set before serving.

Variations

  • Swap Taleggio for sweet Gorgonzola cheese for a more distinct flavor profile.
  • Add crispy strips of speck between the layers for a savory and smoky element.
