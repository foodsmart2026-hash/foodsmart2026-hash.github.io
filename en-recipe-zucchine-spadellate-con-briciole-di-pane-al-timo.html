Sautéed Courgettes with Thyme Breadcrumbs

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

A clever way to use stale bread and make courgettes crunchy in a few minutes. An affordable side dish that the whole family will love.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🌱 Vegan🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🇮🇹 Italian

Ingredients

  • Medium courgettes600 g
  • Coarsely grated stale bread40 g
  • Dried or fresh thyme1 tsp

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the vegetables: thoroughly wash 600g of medium courgettes under cold running water and pat them dry with a clean cloth to prevent spitting in the pan. Use a stable chopping board and a sharp chef's knife to trim the ends and dice the courgettes into even cubes of about 1cm. This precision is essential for fast and uniform cooking, allowing each piece to brown on the outside without falling apart. You should end up with a pile of bright green, perfectly uniform cubes ready for the pan. Chef's tip: ensure all cubes are the same size to prevent smaller pieces from burning while larger ones remain undercooked.
  2. Now, toast the crunchy element by heating a large non-stick frying pan over a medium heat. Add 40g of coarsely grated stale breadcrumbs, a teaspoon of thyme, and a drizzle of extra virgin olive oil, stirring constantly with a wooden spoon. Toast the crumbs for about 3-4 minutes until they release an intense herbal aroma and turn a deep golden brown. The final result should be extremely crunchy, fragrant crumbs that will provide a textural contrast to the dish. Chef's tip: do not leave the pan unattended, as breadcrumbs can turn from golden to burnt in a matter of seconds.
  3. Once ready, immediately transfer the thyme-infused breadcrumbs into a small dry bowl and set them aside at room temperature. This step is crucial to prevent the residual heat of the pan or moisture from the cooking vegetables from making the crumbs soggy. The crumbs need to cool away from any steam to maintain their signature 'crunch'. You will have a golden, grainy mixture ready to be added at the very last moment. Chef's tip: wipe the pan clean with a piece of kitchen paper before moving on to remove any bread residues that might burn during the next step.
  4. In the same perfectly clean pan, pour 2 tablespoons of extra virgin olive oil and heat over a high flame for about one minute. The oil should become fluid and coat the bottom of the pan but must absolutely not reach the smoking point. A high initial temperature is the secret to instantly searing the surface of the courgettes and locking in their juices. The pan must be hot enough to encourage the Maillard reaction, which creates that desirable golden-brown crust. Chef's tip: if the oil smokes, remove the pan from the heat, let it cool slightly, and start over to avoid compromising the flavour of the dish.
  5. Add the diced courgettes to the pan in a single layer, avoiding overcrowding which would drop the temperature too sharply. Let them sizzle on a very high heat for the first 2 minutes without touching them too much, allowing a golden crust to form on the base. Then, begin to sauté them with a firm flick of the wrist or toss them with a spatula to distribute the heat. The courgettes will start to change from white to translucent with amber edges, while remaining firm to the touch. Chef's tip: if you notice the courgettes releasing water, increase the heat further and shake the pan more vigorously.
  6. Continue cooking for another 6-8 minutes, tossing the courgettes frequently to ensure even browning on all sides. Maintain a medium-high heat and monitor the consistency: the vegetables should be tender inside but retain an 'al dente' bite. The aroma will turn into that of roasted courgettes—sweet and slightly caramelised—indicating the natural sugars are concentrating. Towards the end, you'll notice the volume of the courgettes has reduced slightly due to water evaporation. Chef's tip: never cover the pan with a lid, as the trapped steam will make the courgettes soggy and greyish.
  7. Only at this point, when the courgettes are almost finished cooking, add a pinch of salt and a grinding of black pepper to taste. Salting the courgettes at the start would draw out water through osmosis, making them stewed rather than sautéed. Stir well to distribute the seasoning so that every cube is savoury and glossy from the remaining film of oil. The colour of the courgettes should remain vibrant, with beautiful golden and charred highlights that enhance their rustic flavour. Chef's tip: taste a cube before turning off the heat to adjust the salt according to your personal preference.
  8. Turn off the heat and add the reserved thyme breadcrumbs, scattering them evenly over the vegetables. Toss everything together for one final minute using the residual heat of the pan to help the bread adhere to the courgettes and meld the flavours. The crumbs will lightly stick to the moist surface of the cubes, creating a perfect marriage between the soft flesh and the crunch of the bread. The dish is now a balanced mix of fresh aromas and contrasting textures, ready to be enjoyed. Chef's tip: serve immediately, as the residual moisture from the courgettes will inevitably soften the bread if left to sit for too long.
