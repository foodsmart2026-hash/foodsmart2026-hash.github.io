Storage

How to store food in the fridge

A practical guide to organising your fridge, ensuring food safety and reducing household waste.

The fridge is the central appliance in our kitchen, but all too often it is used as a simple, indiscriminate container. In reality, it is a technological system designed to slow down bacterial proliferation and preserve the organoleptic characteristics of food. Correct management of the domestic 'cold chain' is not just a matter of order, but a fundamental pillar of food safety, as constantly highlighted by Ministry of Health guidelines and scientific assessments by EFSA (European Food Safety Authority).

Thermal stratification: a shelf for every food

Not every part of the fridge maintains the same temperature. Cold air, being denser, tends to sink to the bottom, while slightly warmer air stays at the top. For optimal storage, the thermostat should be set to around 4°C (in the central zone). Following the correct arrangement allows you to meet the specific needs of each product category, in accordance with the provisions of EU Regulation 1169/2011 regarding the storage conditions to be indicated on the label.

Here is how you should organise your products to maximise their shelf-life:

  • Upper section (mildest, approx. 5-8°C): Ideal for matured cheeses, eggs (if not kept in the specific door compartment), yoghurt and cream-based desserts.
  • Middle section (approx. 4-5°C): This is where sliced deli meats, fresh cheeses and cooked foods (leftovers) should be placed, properly sealed in containers.
  • Lower section (coldest, approx. 0-2°C): This zone is intended for raw meat and fish. Here, the minimum temperature slows the growth of spoilage and pathogenic microorganisms.
  • Specific drawers: Designed for fruit and vegetables, which could be damaged by excessively low temperatures and require a slightly more humid environment.
  • Door (warmest zone): Reserved for products that only need light refrigeration, such as drinks, butter and sauces (mustard, ketchup).

Containers and protection: avoiding cross-contamination

One of the most common mistakes is storing food 'naked' or in open packaging. Cross-contamination occurs when microorganisms present in one food (especially raw ones) are transferred to another ready-to-eat food. To prevent this risk, it is essential to use airtight glass or plastic containers certified for food use (FCM). Cooked foods must always be separated from raw ones. Furthermore, it is vital not to overload the fridge: air must be able to circulate freely between products to maintain a constant set temperature.

Reading the label: expiry dates and storage instructions

EU Regulation 1169/2011 requires manufacturers to clearly state storage instructions. It is fundamental to distinguish between the 'Use-by date', which concerns safety and beyond which the food must not be consumed, and the 'Best-before date', which concerns quality. Once a vacuum-sealed or modified atmosphere pack is opened, the indicated shelf-life is no longer valid and the food should generally be consumed within 2-3 days, treating it as a fresh product.

Appliance hygiene and maintenance

A clean fridge is a safe fridge. Internal surfaces should be cleaned regularly with water and bicarbonate of soda or vinegar, avoiding overly aggressive detergents that could contaminate food. It is equally important to monitor frost build-up on the walls: an excessive layer reduces cooling efficiency and increases energy consumption. Finally, pay attention to condensation: if excessive, it could indicate a seal malfunction or an incorrect internal temperature setting.

Always remember the 'FIFO' rule (First-In, First-Out): products purchased first should be placed at the front, to be consumed before those just bought. This simple habit drastically reduces domestic food waste.

Conclusion

Storing food correctly in the fridge is not just a good organisational habit, but an act of responsibility towards your health and the environment. By following these simple technical rules, you can enjoy food that is always fresh, safe and nutritious, minimising biological risks and optimising the resources in your refrigerated pantry.
