Saltimbocca alla romana

Lazio · 4 servings · 20 min · Easy

Veal scallops topped with prosciutto crudo and sage.

🇮🇹 Italian

An iconic dish from Rome, saltimbocca combines the tenderness of veal with the saltiness of prosciutto and the aroma of sage. It is a quick preparation that results in a juicy and very fragrant main course.

Ingredients

  • Veal escalopes (topside or loin)480 g (8 slices)
  • Cured Prosciutto ham100 g (8 thin slices)
  • Fresh sage8 large leaves
  • Clarified or fresh butter50 g
  • Dry white wine100 ml
  • All-purpose flour30 g
  • Fine salt2 g
  • Ground black pepper1 g
  • Water (for emulsion)20 ml

Allergens

Gluten, Milk, Sulphites

Method

  1. Flatten the veal slices using a meat mallet between two sheets of parchment paper to a thickness of 4-5 mm.
  2. Place a slice of prosciutto and a sage leaf in the center of each veal slice.
  3. Secure the prosciutto and sage by weaving a wooden toothpick horizontally through the meat.
  4. Flour only the bottom side of the meat (the side without prosciutto), shaking off any excess to prevent clumps.
  5. In a large skillet, melt the butter over medium-high heat until it begins to foam (approx. 140°C).
  6. Place the slices meat-side down first and cook for 2 minutes, then flip briefly to the prosciutto side for 30 seconds to release flavor.
  7. Deglaze with white wine over high heat and let the alcohol evaporate for about 1 minute.
  8. Remove the meat and keep warm; add water to the pan juices and whisk to create a thick, glossy emulsion.
  9. Nappe the slices with the pan sauce and serve immediately while hot.

Tips

  • Do not flip the slices too early to allow the prosciutto to adhere perfectly to the meat.
  • Use a good quality dry white wine to deglaze and create a velvety sauce in the pan.
  • You can store them for one day in the fridge, but the meat will tend to toughen slightly when reheated.

Variations

  • Replace butter with extra virgin olive oil for a lighter, though less traditional, version.
  • You can use thin chicken breast slices instead of veal for a more economical and delicate variant.
