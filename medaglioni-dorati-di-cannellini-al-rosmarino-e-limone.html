Golden Cannellini Bean Medallions with Rosemary and Lemon

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 10 min · Easy

A nutritious and crunchy main course that transforms simple beans into aromatic burgers. Ideal for a quick dinner, it utilises the natural creaminess of pulses without the need for eggs or soy products.

These medallions are a practical and tasty solution for a plant-based main course. The softness of the cannellini beans pairs perfectly with fresh lemon and intense rosemary, creating a pleasant contrast with the golden crust.

Ingredients

  • Canned cannellini beans (small white beans, drained)480 g
  • Fresh spinach leaves250 g
  • Cornflour30 g
  • Gluten-free breadcrumbs60 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Unwaxed lemon zest1 pcs
  • Fresh rosemary1 rametto
  • Fine salt4 g

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Begin by rinsing 480g of pre-cooked cannellini beans under cold running water to remove the canning liquid and excess sodium. Place them in a fine-mesh sieve and let them drain thoroughly for at least 2 minutes, giving them an occasional shake. You should aim for beans that are dry on the surface, ready to be processed without adding unwanted moisture. Chef's tip: pat the beans dry with kitchen paper after draining to ensure the mixture stays firm.
  2. Take 250g of fresh spinach, wash it, and chop it roughly with a sharp knife. Heat a large non-stick frying pan over a high heat and wilt the spinach without adding any fat or water for about 3 minutes. The spinach will quickly soften, releasing its earthy aroma while maintaining a vibrant green colour. Chef's tip: do not cover the pan with a lid, as this allows the moisture to evaporate instantly rather than boiling the vegetables.
  3. Once cooked, transfer the spinach to a sieve or wrap it in a clean tea towel and squeeze very firmly to remove every trace of residual liquid. This step is crucial for the recipe's success, as excess water would make the medallions too soft. You will end up with a small, compact, and dry mass of concentrated spinach. Chef's tip: spend an extra minute on this task; it is the secret to preventing the medallions from falling apart during frying.
  4. In a large mixing bowl, pour in the drained beans and add 4g of fine salt, then mash them using a fork or a potato masher. Work the mixture until you have a thick, coarse paste, ensuring you leave about a quarter of the beans whole or in larger chunks. This creates a rustic and interesting texture that provides a better mouthfeel. Chef's tip: avoid using an electric blender as it would make the mixture too smooth and sticky, making it difficult to handle by hand.
  5. Add the well-squeezed spinach, the grated zest of an unwaxed lemon, a finely chopped sprig of rosemary, and 30g of cornflour to the bean purée. Mix vigorously with a spoon or your hands to distribute the aromatics evenly throughout the dough. The cornflour will act as a natural binder, reacting with the remaining moisture to hold the ingredients together. Chef's tip: grate only the yellow part of the lemon peel, avoiding the white pith which would impart an unpleasant bitter taste.
  6. Divide the mixture into 8 equal parts and shape each portion with the palms of your hands to form circular medallions about 2cm thick. Compact each piece well by pressing on the sides to eliminate any internal air bubbles and to smooth the edges. The medallions should look solid and hold their shape perfectly once placed on a chopping board. Chef's tip: if the mixture feels slightly sticky, lightly dampen your hands with water to shape them more easily.
  7. Pour 60g of gluten-free breadcrumbs onto a flat plate and coat each medallion in the breading, ensuring both faces and the side edges are well covered. Apply gentle pressure with your fingers to make the breadcrumbs adhere evenly and firmly. You will have medallions completely encased in a thin, protective layer. Chef's tip: gently shake each medallion to remove excess breadcrumbs that might burn in the oil and spoil the flavour of the dish.
  8. In a large non-stick frying pan, heat 30ml of extra virgin olive oil over a medium heat until it starts to shimmer slightly. Gently place the medallions into the hot oil and let them brown for about 4 minutes per side without moving them constantly. Once the crust is golden-brown, firm, and fragrant, flip them over using a flat spatula. Chef's tip: do not overcrowd the pan; cook in two batches if necessary to keep the oil temperature steady and achieve perfect browning.
  9. Drain the golden medallions onto a tray lined with kitchen paper and let them rest for a minute so the structure can set. Serve the dish while still hot to enjoy the contrast between the crispy coating and the soft, lemon-scented heart. The final result will be delicious flattened patties with an intense golden colour and a fresh Mediterranean aroma. Chef's tip: serve with a light yoghurt mayonnaise or a simple rocket salad to balance the heartiness of the pulses.

Tips

  • Squeeze the cooked spinach very well in a cloth to prevent the mixture from becoming too moist.
  • Let the medallions rest in the refrigerator for 15 minutes before cooking for better stability.
  • Mash the beans with a fork leaving some pieces whole to achieve a more rustic texture.

Variations

  • Add a pinch of ground black pepper to the bean puree for a spicier finish.
  • Use traditional breadcrumbs for the coating if you don't need to avoid gluten.
