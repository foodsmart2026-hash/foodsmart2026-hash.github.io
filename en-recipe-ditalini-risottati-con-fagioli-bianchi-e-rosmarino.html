'Risottata' Ditalini with White Beans and Rosemary

4 servings · Prep 5 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

A 'pasta e fagioli' in a 'risottata' version where the pasta cooks directly with the pulses for a dense and comforting consistency. A complete and very filling vegan dish.

A classic 'cucina povera' dish reinterpreted with the 'risottato' technique to achieve an incredibly thick creaminess. Fresh rosemary provides an intense aromatic note that perfectly balances the delicate flavor of cannellini beans.

Ingredients

  • Smooth ditalini (small, short tube-shaped pasta)320 g
  • Canned Cannellini beans400 g
  • Fresh rosemary1 rametto
  • Garlic1 spicchio
  • Tomato passata50 ml
  • Hot water or stock800 ml

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Drain the 400g of Cannellini beans from their canning liquid and rinse them thoroughly under cold running water. Use a fine-mesh sieve, gently shaking the beans to remove any starchy residue or preservatives from the tin. This process takes about 1 minute and ensures the legumes have a clean, natural flavour. By the end, the beans should appear shiny and free of any surface foam. Chef's Tip: Lightly pat the beans dry with kitchen paper to prevent hot oil splatters when you sauté them later.
  2. In a wide, heavy-bottomed saucepan, pour a drizzle of extra virgin olive oil and add the whole, unpeeled garlic clove and the fresh rosemary sprig. Place over medium heat and sauté gently for about 2-3 minutes until the garlic turns golden and the rosemary releases its piney aroma. Take care not to burn the herbs, as the aroma should be delicate rather than bitter. You will achieve a fragrant oil base that serves as the aromatic foundation of the dish. Chef's Tip: Lightly crush the garlic with the palm of your hand to better release its essential oils without it breaking apart.
  3. Take about 130g of the beans (one-third of the total) and transfer them to a jug for a hand blender along with a ladle of the 800ml hot water or broth. Blend at maximum speed for 30-40 seconds until you obtain a perfectly smooth, velvety cream with no visible skins. This cream is the secret to giving the dish body without adding animal fats. Set the mixture aside in a small bowl. Chef's Tip: If the cream seems too thick, add another tablespoon of water to make it as fluid as a sauce.
  4. Add the remaining whole beans and 50ml of tomato purée to the saucepan with the infused oil. Stir with a wooden spoon and sauté over medium-high heat for about 3 minutes, allowing the tomato to lose its raw acidity and the beans to absorb the rosemary scent. You will notice the base starting to sizzle and darken slightly. The result should be a thick, fragrant base. Chef's Tip: Do not add salt at this stage, as tinned beans and broth are often already quite salty.
  5. Pour the 320g of Ditalini pasta directly into the saucepan with the beans and tomato. Toast the pasta for 1 minute, stirring constantly just as you would for a risotto, to seal the surface of the ditalini. Cover just to the level of the pasta with about half of the hot water (or broth) and maintain medium heat so the liquid simmers steadily. The pasta will immediately begin to release its starches, creating a creamy base. Chef's Tip: Ensure the liquid is boiling to avoid dropping the cooking temperature, which would ruin the pasta's texture.
  6. Continue cooking for about 6-7 minutes, adding the rest of the hot water one ladle at a time, only once the previous one has been almost completely absorbed. Stir frequently to prevent the ditalini from sticking to the bottom of the pan, especially in the corners. The pasta must always be covered by a thin layer of liquid to cook evenly. You will see the water gradually transform into a thick sauce thanks to the 'risottato' technique. Chef's Tip: Do not rush; the gradual addition of liquid is essential for the perfect outcome.
  7. Halfway through the pasta's cooking time, stir in the prepared bean cream and mix vigorously to incorporate it into the cooking juices. Continue to cook for the final 4-5 minutes, adjusting the consistency with a little more liquid if necessary: the final result should be 'all'onda' (wavy), neither too dry nor too soupy. Taste to check the ditalini, which should be al dente but tender on the outside. The cream will coat the pasta, making it incredibly tempting. Chef's Tip: This is the time to adjust the salt if needed, after tasting.
  8. Turn off the heat and remove the garlic clove and the rosemary sprig, as they have served their purpose. Let the pasta rest in the saucepan for 1 minute covered with a clean tea towel; this step stabilises the starches and makes the cream even silkier. Finish the dish with a final drizzle of raw extra virgin olive oil and a generous crack of fresh black pepper. Serve immediately in warm shallow bowls to maintain the heat. Chef's Tip: A dusting of pepper at the moment of serving releases aromas that pair perfectly with the sweetness of the beans.

Tips

  • Rinse the canned cannellini beans thoroughly to remove excess salt and the preserving liquid.
  • Keep the water or broth at a steady boil to maintain the pasta's cooking pace during the risotto process.
  • Blend a portion of the beans with a hand mixer to create an even more velvety and thick consistency.

Variations

  • Use ridged ditalini if you prefer a more rustic texture and a firmer bite.
  • Add a pinch of dried chili flakes along with the garlic for a subtle spicy finish.
