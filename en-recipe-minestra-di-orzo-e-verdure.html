Pearl barley and vegetable soup

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 40 min · Medium

A nutritious and satiating main course that combines the fibre of pearl barley with the plant-based proteins of pulses. This affordable preparation uses the staple vegetables of the Mediterranean diet for a balanced and warming meal.

A thick and comforting soup, perfect for cold days or when looking for a hearty meal. The sweetness of carrots and potatoes balances with the aromatic touch of fresh rosemary.

Ingredients

  • Pearl barley240 g
  • Cooked cannellini beans240 g
  • Carrots150 g
  • Celery100 g
  • Golden onion100 g
  • Potatoes200 g
  • Fresh chard200 g
  • Water or vegetable stock1500 ml
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 ml
  • Iodised salt5 g
  • Fresh rosemary1 pz

Allergens

Gluten, Celery

Method

  1. Begin by thoroughly rinsing 240g of pearl barley under a cold running tap, using a very fine-mesh sieve. Move the grains with your hands until the water running through is completely clear and free from white starchy residue. This process takes about 2 minutes and is crucial to ensure the soup remains fluid rather than becoming overly gluey during cooking. The final result should be clean, bright grains ready to absorb the flavours of the base. A professional tip: always use very cold water to avoid prematurely activating the surface starches.
  2. Prepare the aromatic base by creating a fine brunoise—even, tiny cubes—of 150g carrots, 100g celery, and 100g yellow onion. In a large, heavy-based pot, heat 20ml of extra virgin olive oil and add the vegetables, letting them sweat over a very low heat for about 7 minutes. Stir frequently with a wooden spoon until the onion becomes translucent and the carrots release their colour into the oil, ensuring nothing browns or burns. You will achieve a sweet, fragrant base that forms the flavour skeleton of the dish. The chef recommends: if the vegetables sizzle too aggressively, add a tablespoon of water to keep the temperature steady.
  3. Peel 200g of potatoes and cut them into uniform 1cm cubes, then add them to the pot along with the whole sprig of fresh rosemary. Turn the heat up slightly to medium-low and let them infuse for 3 minutes, allowing the potato to release its natural sugars and the rosemary to discharge its essential oils. You will notice an enchanting balsamic aroma filling the kitchen as the potato cubes become slightly glossy. The common mistake to avoid is chopping the rosemary: leaving it whole allows for easy removal later, preventing tough needles in the finished dish.
  4. Pour the drained barley into the pot with the vegetables and toast it for approximately 2 minutes over a medium heat, stirring continuously. This technique, similar to making risotto, seals the outside of the grain, ensuring it keeps a pleasant 'al dente' bite even after simmering. You will see the grains become almost translucent and hear them 'sing' as they rub against the bottom of the pan. This step is the secret to a gourmet soup where every grain remains distinct. Do not skip this, otherwise the barley may become too soft during the long simmer.
  5. Add 1500ml of boiling water or vegetable stock and 5g of iodised salt, taking care not to use cold liquid which would shock the grains and halt the cooking process. Bring to a gentle simmer, then turn the heat down to minimum and partially cover the pot with a lid, leaving a small gap for steam. Cook gently for about 25-30 minutes, checking occasionally to ensure the liquid still generously covers the ingredients. The barley should swell gradually, becoming tender but retaining an elastic core. Remember: keeping your stock at boiling point before adding it preserves the nutritional integrity and texture of the ingredients.
  6. While the soup simmers, prepare the remaining vegetables by rinsing 240g of cannellini beans from their canning liquid and slicing 200g of chard into thin 1cm strips. Once the barley has cooked for 30 minutes, add both the beans and the chard to the pot, stirring gently to avoid breaking the pulses. Continue cooking for a further 5 minutes over a medium heat, just long enough for the chard to wilt and the beans to heat through. You will see the soup take on a vibrant green hue and become visibly thicker. Chef's tip: rinse the beans extremely well to remove any excess salt or metallic taste from the brine.
  7. Turn off the heat and remove the rosemary sprig, which will have imparted all its flavour by now. Cover the pot completely with the lid and let the soup rest for 3 to 5 minutes before serving. This resting period is vital as it allows the starches to stabilise and the temperatures to equalise, making the flavour profile deeper and more balanced. You will notice the texture becomes even more velvety and pleasant. Do not rush to plate up: a soup served scolding hot prevents the taste buds from perceiving the subtle nuances of the vegetables.
  8. Plate the soup in individual bowls (warmed if possible), ensuring an even distribution of the thick ingredients and the fragrant broth. Complete each portion with a drizzle of the remaining extra virgin olive oil (about 5ml per person) and a fresh grind of black pepper if desired. Adding the oil raw not only enhances the herbaceous scents but keeps the antioxidant properties of the Vitamin E intact. The result is a balanced, colourful, and incredibly comforting dish. A final tip: a pinch of freshly cracked pepper releases aromatics that pre-ground pepper simply cannot match.

Tips

  • Thoroughly rinse the barley under cold water to remove impurities and excess starch before toasting.
  • Cut all vegetables into uniform cubes to ensure even cooking and a neat presentation.
  • Add the chard only in the last ten minutes to prevent it from losing too much color and texture.

Variations

  • Swap the chard for fresh spinach for a milder flavor and even faster preparation.
  • You can use borlotti beans instead of cannellini for a more earthy flavor and rustic texture.
