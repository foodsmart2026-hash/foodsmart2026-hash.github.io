Red Kidney Bean and Sweetcorn Chilli with Basmati Rice

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 30 min · Medium

An international 'comfort food' classic revisited with an affordable plant-based twist. The red beans and rice form a complete protein, making it a filling and energetic meal.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🌱 Vegan🧒 Kid-friendly🌾 Gluten-free🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🌍 International

A single-course meal inspired by Mexican flavors, where the sweetness of corn and red beans meets the warm aroma of cumin. Served with Basmati rice, it offers a comforting texture and a spiced profile. Ideal for a social dinner or weekly meal prep.

Ingredients

  • Tinned red kidney beans (drained)480 g
  • Tinned sweetcorn140 g
  • Tomato passata400 ml
  • Basmati or Ribe rice240 g
  • Red bell pepper1 pcs
  • Red onion1 pcs
  • Ground cumin1 cucchiaino

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Start by thoroughly rinsing 240g of Basmati rice under cold running water, using a fine-mesh sieve to move the grains with your fingers. Continue until the water runs completely clear, indicating that the surface starch has been removed. This step is crucial for achieving fluffy rice with well-separated grains that won't stick together after cooking. My chef's tip is never to skip this, especially with Basmati, to preserve its natural fragrance and delicate texture.
  2. Bring a large pot of lightly salted water to a boil and add the pre-rinsed rice. Cook over medium heat according to the package instructions (usually 10-12 minutes), maintaining a steady but gentle boil to prevent the grains from breaking. Once cooked, drain well and transfer to a bowl, covering it with a clean tea towel to absorb excess steam and keep it warm and fluffy. Ensure the rice is al dente, as it will continue to soften slightly while resting.
  3. While the rice is cooking, prepare the aromatic base by finely chopping one red onion and dicing one red bell pepper into 5mm cubes. Heat a tablespoon of extra virgin olive oil in a wide saucepan or wok over medium heat and add the chopped vegetables. Sauté for about 7-8 minutes, stirring frequently with a wooden spoon until they soften evenly without browning. The ideal result is a translucent, fragrant base where the peppers are tender but still hold their shape.
  4. Stir one teaspoon of ground cumin and 400ml of tomato passata into the saucepan with the sautéed peppers and onions. Season lightly with salt and mix well to distribute the spice, bringing the sauce to a gentle simmer over low heat. Let the sauce reduce for about 10 minutes, partially covering with a lid to prevent splashes while allowing steam to escape. You are looking for a thick, flavourful tomato base with a vibrant red colour and a rich, spicy aroma.
  5. Take 480g of canned red kidney beans and 140g of sweet corn and rinse them thoroughly under cold running water in a colander. This step is vital to remove the canning liquid, which is often high in sodium and preservatives that could spoil the final taste of your dish. Once well-drained, add them to the tomato sauce and stir gently to avoid breaking the beans. You will notice how the bright yellow corn beautifully contrasts with the deep red of the kidney beans.
  6. Continue cooking the chili for another 10 minutes over very low heat, stirring occasionally to prevent it from sticking to the bottom. During this stage, the beans will absorb the flavours of the cumin and tomato, while the sauce reaches a rich, hearty consistency typical of a proper stew. Check for the perfect thickness: if it seems too dry, add a tablespoon of the rice cooking water. The chef's secret is patience here, as slow heat allows the flavours to harmonise perfectly.
  7. Before turning off the heat, perform a final taste test to balance the flavours according to your personal preference. If you enjoy bold flavours, add a pinch of chili flakes or a squeeze of lime juice for a fresh, acidic note that balances the sweetness of the corn. The seasoning should be balanced, keeping in mind that canned pulses often retain some residual saltiness. The result should be an enveloping mix where no single spice overpowers the others, but all contribute to the dish's complexity.
  8. To serve, arrange the Basmati rice on one side of a large bowl or deep plate and ladle the bean and corn chili onto the other side. This side-by-side presentation allows diners to decide how much rice to mix into each bite, keeping the flavours distinct yet complementary. Serve piping hot, perhaps garnished with a sprig of fresh coriander or parsley if desired. Remember that chili tastes even better if left to rest for a few minutes before serving, allowing the temperature to stabilise.

Tips

  • Rinse the Basmati rice under cold water until it runs clear to remove excess starch.
  • Simmer the chili over very low heat to allow the spice flavors to blend perfectly.
  • Store the chili and rice in separate containers to best preserve their distinct textures.

Variations

  • Replace Basmati rice with Ribe rice for a different cooking consistency.
  • Add a pinch of dried chili flakes if you prefer a more pronounced spicy kick.
