Pasta with Carrot Cream and Caramelised Onion

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 20 min · Medium

A vibrantly coloured first course with a gently balanced flavour, perfect for getting little ones to eat their vegetables. The cream is achieved simply by blending boiled carrots, creating a velvety sauce with no added fats.

A pasta dish with bold contrasts, where the sweetness of carrots meets the sweet and sour notes of caramelized onions. The texture is creamy and enveloping, enhanced by the pungent touch of fresh ginger.

Ingredients

  • Short pasta (e.g. Penne)320 g
  • Carrots500 g
  • Golden onions2 pcs
  • Fresh ginger (or powder)10 g
  • Brown sugar5 g
  • White wine vinegar10 ml

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Begin by carefully washing and peeling 500g of carrots, removing the ends and slicing them into very thin, uniform rounds to ensure even cooking. Transfer them to a large pot with approximately 2 litres of lightly salted water and bring to a boil over medium heat, letting them cook for about 15 minutes. The carrots should be extremely tender when pressed with a fork, ready to be processed into a smooth velvet cream. Chef's tip: do not over-salt at this stage, as the water will be reused for the pasta, concentrating the carrots' mineral flavours.
  2. While the carrots are cooking, focus on the two golden onions by peeling and slicing them finely with a sharp knife to obtain almost transparent strips. Heat a drizzle of extra virgin olive oil in a large non-stick pan, add the onions, 5g of brown sugar, and a pinch of salt to encourage the release of moisture. Maintain a very low heat for about 10 minutes, stirring frequently with a wooden spoon so they soften gently without browning too much. Chef's tip: if you see the onions drying out too quickly, add a tablespoon of hot water to keep them moist and translucent.
  3. When the onions have become tender and a beautiful deep golden colour, slightly increase the heat and pour in 10ml of white wine vinegar to deglaze the pan. Let the alcohol evaporate for a minute, moving the pan in circular motions to scrape up all the caramelised sugars stuck to the bottom. You will obtain a fragrant sweet-and-sour base that will balance the dish's natural sweetness with a necessary touch of acidity. Chef's tip: ensure the harsh vinegar smell has completely dissipated before turning off the heat, leaving only a fruity aroma.
  4. Once the carrots are ready, remove them from the water using a slotted spoon and transfer them to a blender jar, making sure to keep all the cooking water in the pot. Add 10g of finely grated fresh ginger and a drizzle of raw olive oil to emulsify the mixture. Blend at maximum power until you achieve a thick, silky, bright orange cream with no lumps. Chef's tip: if the cream appears too stiff, add half a ladle of their cooking water to make it smoother and more velvety.
  5. Bring the water where the carrots were cooked back to a boil and drop in 320g of short pasta, setting the timer for two minutes less than the package instructions. Cooking the pasta in this liquid allows you to recover the water-soluble nutrients lost by the carrots and gives the pasta a subtle amber hue. Maintain a steady boil so the pasta doesn't release too much starch irregularly. Chef's tip: do not add more oil to the water, as it would prevent the sauce from adhering perfectly to the pasta surface later.
  6. Just before draining the pasta, reserve a generous ladle of cooking water, then drain the pasta directly into the pan with the caramelised onions. Turn the heat to medium and toss the pasta for a minute, allowing the flavours of the onions and vinegar to penetrate the pores of the grain. This pre-mantecatura step is essential for creating a bond between the aromatic base and the pasta's structure. Chef's tip: toss the pan vigorously to ensure every single piece of pasta is glossy and well-seasoned.
  7. Now pour the carrot and ginger cream into the pan and add the reserved ladle of cooking water, stirring energetically with a circular motion. The combination of residual starch, fats from the oil, and carrot fibre will create a creamy emulsion that coats the pasta evenly without feeling heavy. Continue cooking for the final minute over low heat until the sauce reaches a velvety consistency that 'naps' the back of a spoon. Chef's tip: turn off the heat a moment before the cream dries out too much, as the pasta's residual heat will continue to thicken the sauce.
  8. Plate the pasta immediately in pre-heated shallow bowls, evenly distributing the cream and the caramelised onions integrated into the sauce. Finish each portion with a generous grind of fresh black pepper, which provides a pungent kick to break through the carrot's sweetness and the ginger's heat. Serve the dish very hot to enhance the aromatic contrasts and the silkiness of the cream. Chef's tip: for a gourmet touch, add a few small leaves of fresh thyme or finely chopped parsley at the last second for colour contrast and a herbal note.

Tips

  • Blend the carrots with some pasta cooking water to achieve a perfectly silky cream.
  • Caramelize the onions over very low heat to prevent the sugar from burning and turning bitter.
  • Store the pasta in the refrigerator for up to one day in an airtight container.

Variations

  • Replace fresh ginger with turmeric powder for an even more vibrant color.
  • Add toasted sesame seeds before serving for a final crunchy note.
