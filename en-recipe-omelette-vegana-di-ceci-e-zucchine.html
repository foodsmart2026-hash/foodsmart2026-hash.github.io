Vegan chickpea and courgette omelette

2 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

An egg-free omelette made with chickpea flour, courgettes and spring onion.

A colorful dish that reimagines the classic omelet using versatile chickpea flour. Its soft texture meets the sweetness of zucchini and the savory notes of nutritional yeast. Perfect for a light lunch or a flavorful brunch.

Ingredients

  • Chickpea flour120 g
  • Water200 ml
  • Courgettes200 g
  • Spring onion40 g
  • Turmeric1 g
  • Nutritional yeast flakes5 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil20 ml
  • Fine salt3 g

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.
May contain traces of: Gluten.

Method

  1. Start by pouring 120 g of chickpea flour into a large bowl and gradually add 200 ml of water, whisking vigorously. Add 1 g of turmeric for a golden color, 5 g of nutritional yeast for a savory touch, and 3 g of fine salt. Mix well until you obtain a smooth, fluid batter without any lumps.
  2. Let the batter rest for about 10 minutes at room temperature before proceeding with the cooking. This time is crucial because it allows the chickpea flour to hydrate properly, ensuring a softer and less floury final texture. You will notice that the surface becomes slightly denser and more homogeneous.
  3. While the batter is resting, prepare the vegetables by washing and trimming 200 g of zucchini and cleaning 40 g of spring onion. Grate the zucchini using a coarse grater and finely slice the spring onion, including the tender green part. This cut will allow the vegetables to cook quickly and integrate perfectly into the structure of the omelette.
  4. In a 24 cm non-stick pan, heat about half of the extra virgin olive oil (10 ml) over medium heat. Add the sliced spring onion and the grated zucchini, sautéing them for about 4 minutes. The vegetables should soften and become fragrant without burning; the water from the zucchini should almost entirely evaporate.
  5. Evenly distribute the vegetables on the bottom of the pan and then pour the previously prepared chickpea batter over them. Make sure the mixture covers every space, using a spatula if necessary to level the surface. You will immediately smell the aroma of the turmeric releasing intensely with the heat.
  6. Cover the pan with a lid and cook over medium-low heat for about 6 minutes. The lid is essential to create the steam needed to cook the interior evenly without burning the bottom. The omelette is ready to be flipped when the edges detach easily and the top surface looks dry and firm.
  7. Lightly grease a flat plate with a thin layer of oil and use it to cover the pan, then flip with a firm and quick motion. Gently slide the omelette back into the pan, adding the remaining oil (10 ml) if necessary to prevent sticking. Be very careful not to burn yourself with the steam during this process.
  8. Complete cooking the other side for about 4 minutes without a lid, still over medium-low heat. You should achieve a slightly golden and inviting crust on both sides, while the center should remain soft. The elastic texture to the touch will confirm that the chickpea flour has reached ideal cooking.
  9. Turn off the heat and let the omelette rest for a minute in the pan before transferring it to a cutting board or serving plate. Serve it hot or warm, cut into wedges, to best appreciate its delicate flavor and rustic texture. It is excellent when accompanied by a side of fresh vegetables.

Tips

  • Respect the batter's resting time to allow the flour to hydrate properly and prevent lumps.
  • Use a high-quality non-stick pan to flip the omelet easily without it breaking.
  • Cook with a tight lid on to ensure even cooking through to the top of the mixture.

Variations

  • Enrich the mixture by adding finely chopped sun-dried tomatoes for an extra kick of flavor.
  • Replace the zucchini with sautéed mushrooms for a version with more intense, autumnal flavors.
