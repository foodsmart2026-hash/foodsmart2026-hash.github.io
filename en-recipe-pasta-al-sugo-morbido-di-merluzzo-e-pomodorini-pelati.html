Pasta with Soft Cod and Peeled Tomato Sauce

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

A simple and budget-friendly way to introduce fish into the family diet, using cod fillets that flake perfectly into the tomato sauce. The texture is tender and safe, ideal for children starting to chew small pieces.

A delicate pasta dish combining savory cod with the natural sweetness of tomatoes. The texture is pleasantly creamy as the fish gently flakes into the sauce, making it perfect for small pasta shapes.

Ingredients

  • Small short pasta (such as maccheroncini)160 g
  • Cod fillet (fresh or frozen)150 g
  • Peeled tomatoes or fine tomato pulp200 g
  • Dried oregano1 pinch
  • Extra virgin olive oil20 ml

Allergens

Gluten, Fish

Method

  1. Start by carefully preparing 150g of cod fillet, gently running your fingertips over it to locate any remaining bones to remove with tweezers. Once cleaned, coarsely chop it on a board using a sharp knife, reducing it into pieces of about one centimetre. Perform this operation with precision while the fish is cold to maintain the firmness of the flesh. You will achieve a fish dice ready for cooking that smells of the sea. Chef's tip: keep the cod in the fridge until the moment you use it, as cold fish is much easier to cut without it falling apart.
  2. Take a large non-stick frying pan and pour in 200g of peeled tomatoes or fine pulp, adding a couple of tablespoons of water and a pinch of dried oregano. Turn the heat to medium and crush the tomatoes with a fork if necessary, letting them simmer gently for about 5 minutes. Stir occasionally with a wooden spoon to prevent the tomato from sticking to the edges. The result should be a fluid, fragrant, and slightly reduced tomato base. Chef's tip: do not let the tomato dry out too much at this stage, otherwise you will lack the moisture needed to cook the fish later.
  3. Add the chopped cod to the pan with the hot tomato, distributing it evenly across the entire surface. Immediately cover with a lid and lower the heat to a medium-low level for about 10 minutes of slow cooking. This 'stewing' method keeps the fish extremely tender and juicy, allowing the flavours to blend perfectly. You will see the cod change colour, becoming milky white and opaque. Chef's tip: avoid stirring continuously in the first few minutes to allow the trapped steam to cook the fish fibres evenly.
  4. Remove the lid and, using a wooden spoon, gently press down on the cod pieces to break them further until they turn into tiny crumbs integrated into the sauce. Continue stirring over low heat for another 2-3 minutes so that the liquid released by the fish amalgamates with the tomato pulp. The final consistency should resemble a very soft and velvety sea ragù. The aroma will become more intense and the sauce will take on a lighter pinkish hue. Chef's tip: use a hand whisk if you want an even finer consistency, ideal especially if the dish is intended for small children.
  5. While the sauce finishes cooking, bring a pot with plenty of unsalted water to the boil and pour in 160g of small short pasta such as maccheroncini. Cook the pasta according to the times indicated on the package, but make sure to drain it exactly one minute before the expected time. Use a timer to be precise and not lose the 'al dente' texture necessary for the final step. You will get pasta that is cooked but still firm to the bite. Chef's tip: before draining, always take a cup of the starchy cooking water, which will be fundamental for binding the sauce to the pasta.
  6. Drain the pasta and immediately transfer it to the pan with the still hot cod sauce. Slightly increase the heat to medium intensity and sauté everything vigorously for the last minute of cooking. If you see that the sauce is too dry, add a tablespoon of the reserved cooking water to help combine the pasta and sauce. The result should be perfectly glossy pasta evenly coated in the sauce. Chef's tip: the movement must be quick and firm to allow the pasta starches to emulsify with the tomato sauce.
  7. Turn off the heat and move the pan away from the hot burner to proceed with the final 'mantecatura' (creaming) using raw oil. Pour 20ml of high-quality extra virgin olive oil over the pasta and stir carefully to distribute the fat evenly. You will notice how the raw oil gives an immediate gloss to the dish and enhances its aromas without making it heavy. The pasta will appear creamy and inviting, ready to be served. Chef's tip: do not add the oil with the flame on, as excessive heat would alter its nutritional properties and delicate aromatic profile.
  8. Distribute the pasta into shallow bowls and let it rest for a minute until it reaches a lukewarm temperature, ideal for appreciating all the nuances of the cod. For adult diners, you can complete the dish with a pinch of salt and a grinding of fresh or dried chilli to taste. Serve the dish well-mixed, ensuring each portion has a generous amount of fish crumbs. Chef's tip: slightly warm the serving plates before serving to prevent the pasta from cooling down too quickly during the meal.

Tips

  • Cook the cod over low heat with a lid to keep the fish tender and juicy.
  • Flake the fish directly in the pan using a wooden spoon to blend it well with the sauce.
  • If the sauce becomes too dry, add a tablespoon of the pasta cooking water.

Variations

  • Replace dried oregano with fresh chopped parsley at the end for a herbal note.
  • Add some grated lemon zest for a touch of citrusy freshness.
