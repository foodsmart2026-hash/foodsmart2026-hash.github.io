Cinnamon and Apple Sweet Bread

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 40 min · Medium

A soft and fragrant leavened loaf, perfect for starting the day with the aroma of baked apples. The ingredients are simple and budget-friendly, typical of an average Italian pantry.

Ingredients

  • Plain flour (Type 00, a finely milled Italian soft wheat flour)300 g
  • Golden Delicious apples2 pcs
  • Light brown sugar80 g
  • Whole milk120 ml
  • Butter50 g
  • Dried yeast4 g
  • Ground cinnamon5 g
  • Salt1 g

Allergens

Gluten, Milk

Method

  1. Begin by warming 120ml of whole milk until lukewarm (approx. 30-35°C), then dissolve 4g of dried yeast and one teaspoon from the 80g of brown sugar into it. Stir gently and let the mixture rest for about 10 minutes in a draft-free spot in your kitchen. You should see a light foam forming on the surface: this is the visual cue that the yeast is active and ready to leaven your bread. Chef's tip: never overheat the milk, as temperatures above 45°C will kill the yeast, preventing the dough from rising.
  2. In a large mixing bowl, sift 300g of plain flour and combine it with the remaining brown sugar, 5g of ground cinnamon, and 1g of salt. Use a hand whisk to evenly distribute the aromatics and sugar throughout the flour, ensuring there are no lumps. You will immediately notice the intense fragrance of the cinnamon as it blends with the flour. Chef's tip: salt should never directly touch fresh yeast, so mixing it thoroughly into the flour first is the best way to protect the leavening power of the mix.
  3. Create a well in the centre of the dry ingredients and pour in the milk and yeast mixture along with 50g of butter, previously melted and cooled to lukewarm. Knead vigorously by hand or with a stand mixer for at least 10 minutes, applying constant pressure to properly develop the gluten network. You want to achieve a smooth, elastic dough that pulls away completely from the sides of the bowl. Chef's tip: if the dough feels too sticky at first, resist the urge to add more flour immediately; constant kneading will activate the proteins and make it manageable.
  4. While the dough rests for a moment, peel the 2 Golden Delicious apples and cut them into even cubes of about 1 centimetre. Pat the cubes dry with paper towels to remove excess moisture that could make the dough too soggy during baking. This size is ideal for ensuring the fruit is evenly distributed without weighing down the bread's structure. Chef's tip: choose Golden Delicious apples as they hold their natural sweetness and shape beautifully even after a long bake in the oven.
  5. Spread the dough out on a floured surface, scatter the apple cubes over the top, and fold the dough over itself several times to incorporate them. Work with gentle but firm movements to distribute the fruit evenly, trying not to crush the apple pieces to preserve their texture. The dough will now appear more irregular and slightly damp to the touch, which is characteristic of this rustic style of bread. Chef's tip: if the apples struggle to bond with the dough, lightly dust them with flour before adding them to help them stick.
  6. Lightly grease a bowl with a thin layer of butter, place the dough inside, and cover with a clean, slightly damp cotton cloth. Leave to rise in a warm place (an oven with just the light on is ideal) for about 2 hours or until visibly doubled in size. By the end, the dough should look soft, puffy, and release a delicate scent of yeast and cinnamon. Chef's tip: patience is key; a slow, steady rise guarantees a more digestible bread and a much softer crumb.
  7. After 2 hours, deflate the dough by pressing gently with your fingertips and transfer it into a loaf tin lined with baking parchment. Shape the top to make it even and cover again for a second rise of about 30 minutes in a protected spot. This step helps the yeast regain strength after the stress of handling, ensuring an optimal spring in the oven. Chef's tip: do not press the dough too hard into the tin; let it settle naturally into the space so you don't lose the air bubbles created.
  8. Preheat your oven to 180°C (static) and, just before baking, brush the surface of the bread with a tablespoon of room-temperature milk. Place the tin on the middle shelf and bake for 35-40 minutes, constantly monitoring the colour of the crust, which should become golden-brown and glossy. To check the internal bake, use a wooden skewer: it should come out completely dry when inserted into the heart of the bread. Chef's tip: if you notice the top browning too quickly, cover it with a piece of foil for the final 10 minutes of baking.
  9. Once baked, immediately remove the bread from the tin and place it on a wire cooling rack to allow air to circulate around the base. Let it cool completely before slicing: this time is essential for the moisture from the apples to stabilise within the bread fibres. You will be rewarded with a fragrant sweet bread, featuring a crisp crust and a very soft interior enriched with fruit pieces. Chef's tip: store the cooled bread wrapped in a tea towel or a paper bag to keep it soft for up to 3 days.
