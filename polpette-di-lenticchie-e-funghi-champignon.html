Lentil and Button Mushroom Meatballs

4 servings · Prep 25 min · Cooking 30 min · Medium

A plant-based alternative to classic meatballs, these are affordable and rich in 'umami' flavour thanks to the mushrooms. Perfect served with a simple tomato sauce.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🥕 Vegetarian🧒 Kid-friendly🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🌍 International

These plant-based meatballs combine the meaty texture of mushrooms with a lentil base, seasoned with aromatic thyme. Oven-baked for a light result, they are crunchy on the outside while remaining soft and moist inside.

Ingredients

  • Tinned cooked lentils400 g
  • Button mushrooms300 g
  • Breadcrumbs80 g
  • Garlic1 clove
  • Egg1 pcs
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Dried thyme1 tsp

Allergens

Eggs, Gluten

Method

  1. Begin by preparing 300g of chestnut mushrooms: wipe them clean with a damp cloth to remove any soil and dice them into very small pieces. In a large non-stick frying pan, heat about 10ml of extra virgin olive oil over medium-high heat with a crushed garlic clove. Sauté the mushrooms for roughly 10 minutes until they have released and evaporated all their moisture. You should achieve well-dried, aromatic mushrooms; remember to remove the garlic at the end to prevent its flavour from overpowering the dish.
  2. Take 400g of tinned cooked lentils, drain them from their canning liquid, and rinse thoroughly under cold running water. Let them drain in a fine-mesh sieve for at least 5-10 minutes, gently patting them with kitchen paper if they seem too wet. This step is crucial to eliminate excess moisture that would prevent the meatballs from holding their shape. The chef's secret is ensuring the lentils are almost dry to the touch before moving on to the next stage.
  3. Use a food processor to create the base of your meatballs by adding half of the drained lentils and all the sautéed mushrooms. Use the pulse function for a few seconds until you have a coarse mince rather than a smooth or runny purée. Transfer this mixture to a large bowl and stir in the remaining whole lentils to provide texture and bite to the finished dish. Aim for a visual balance between the creamy minced part and the whole grains for a rustic, pleasant mouthfeel.
  4. In the bowl with the mixture, add one whole egg, a teaspoon of dried thyme, and begin incorporating about 60g of the breadcrumbs (keeping 20g aside). Mix the dough with a fork or your hands, combining the ingredients until the mixture feels pliable and easy to mould. If the mix feels too sticky, add another tablespoon of breadcrumbs, but be careful not to overdo it as this can make the meatballs tough. The ideal consistency should resemble a traditional meat-based meatball mix.
  5. Divide the mixture into 20-24 portions and roll them between your palms into walnut-sized balls, making them as uniform as possible. Lightly coat each ball in the remaining 20g of breadcrumbs to ensure a thin, crispy outer layer once baked. Arrange them neatly on a baking tray lined with parchment paper, leaving a couple of centimetres between each one. My tip is to slightly dampen your hands with water or oil to shape perfect spheres without the mixture sticking to your skin.
  6. Preheat your oven to 200°C (180°C fan) and, just before baking, use the remaining 20ml of olive oil to lightly brush or spray the surface of the meatballs. Place the tray on the middle rack and set a timer for a total of 20 minutes. After the first 10 minutes, carefully turn each meatball using two spoons to ensure even browning on all sides. You are looking for a golden-brown colour and a firm texture, indicating that the egg has set and bound the ingredients perfectly.
  7. Once cooked, remove the tray from the oven and let the meatballs rest for about 3-5 minutes before handling or serving. This short resting period allows the lentil fibres and egg to stabilise further, preventing the meatballs from breaking when lifted. You will notice the intense aroma of thyme and mushrooms being released by the residual heat. Serve them hot or warm, perhaps with a yoghurt dip or a side of seasonal vegetables for a balanced meal.
  8. For a professional presentation, stack the meatballs in a small pyramid on a serving platter and garnish with a sprig of fresh thyme or a crack of black pepper. Ensure the interior remains slightly moist while the exterior should be pleasantly crunchy to the bite. If you have leftovers, these meatballs are excellent the next day, reheated briefly in a pan with a splash of water or tomato sauce. Avoid over-baking, otherwise the starch in the breadcrumbs will soak up all moisture, leaving them too dry.

Tips

  • Blend only half of the lentils to maintain a more varied and pleasant texture in every bite.
  • If the mixture is too wet, add an extra tablespoon of breadcrumbs until it is easy to handle.
  • Lightly oil your palms before shaping the balls to ensure a smooth and even surface.

Variations

  • Replace the thyme with ground cumin for a version with a spicier flavor profile.
  • Try adding soaked and minced dried porcini mushrooms for an intense forest aroma.
