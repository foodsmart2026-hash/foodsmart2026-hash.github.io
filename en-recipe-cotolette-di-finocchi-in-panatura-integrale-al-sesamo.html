Fennel Cutlets in Wholemeal Sesame Breadcrumbs

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 15 min · Medium

A crispy transformation of a vegetable often relegated to a boiled side dish. These vegetable cutlets are ideal for getting children to eat greens thanks to the golden breading.

A plant-based take on the classic cutlet, crispy on the outside and tender on the inside. The wholemeal breading, enriched with sesame seeds and paprika, provides a toasted flavor and an inviting texture. A versatile way to serve fennel beyond the usual preparations.

Ingredients

  • Large fennel bulbs2 pcs
  • Gram flour (Chickpea flour)100 g
  • Cold water150 ml
  • Wholemeal breadcrumbs150 g
  • Sesame seeds30 g
  • Sunflower oil50 ml
  • Sweet paprika5 g

Allergens

Sesame, Gluten

Method

  1. Start by cleaning the 2 large fennel bulbs: remove the green stalks, the fronds, and the tough outer layer if it appears too fibrous. Slice the fennel into steaks about 1 cm thick lengthwise, making sure to keep a portion of the core at the base of each slice so they don't fall apart during processing. The result should be a compact, intact slice that is easy to handle. Chef's tip: keep the green fronds; finely chopped, they make an excellent aromatic final garnish.
  2. Bring a large pot of salted water to the boil and submerge the fennel slices for about 5 minutes. The blanching technique softens the tough fennel fibres without cooking them completely, keeping them slightly 'al dente' at the core. Drain them with a slotted spoon and place them on a clean tea towel or kitchen paper to remove excess moisture. Chef's tip: do not exceed 5 minutes of cooking, otherwise the slices will become too soft and break during the breading process.
  3. Prepare the base for the batter by pouring 100g of chickpea flour and 5g of sweet paprika into a bowl, then gradually add 150ml of cold water while whisking vigorously. You should obtain a fluid, smooth batter that is completely free of lumps, with the consistency of a beaten egg. Let the batter rest for 5 minutes so the chickpea flour can hydrate perfectly. Chef's tip: use very cold water from the fridge, as it helps make the batter silkier and less prone to forming lumps.
  4. In a shallow dish or large tray, combine 150g of wholemeal breadcrumbs and 30g of sesame seeds, mixing them thoroughly with a fork. This mixture must be homogeneous to ensure that every bite has the right balance between the rustic wholemeal note and the toasted sesame. The scent of the sesame will be the hallmark of your protein-rich crust. Chef's tip: you can lightly toast the sesame seeds in a pan for one minute before adding them to the breadcrumbs to release their essential oils.
  5. Proceed with the first stage of coating by dipping one fennel slice at a time into the prepared chickpea batter. Ensure that every centimetre of the surface is evenly covered, then lift the slice and let it drain for a few moments over the bowl to remove excess liquid. The batter should adhere like a thin veil without creating heavy build-ups. Chef's tip: use one hand for 'wet' steps and the other for 'dry' steps to avoid getting your fingers too messy.
  6. Transfer the wet slice into the breadcrumb and sesame mix and press firmly with the palm of your hand on both sides. Pressure is essential so that the breading hooks firmly onto the batter, creating a protective shield that will become crispy when cooked. Repeat for all slices, placing them on a plate without overlapping. Chef's tip: press with particular care along the edges of the fennel slice, where the coating tends to come off most easily.
  7. Pour 50ml of sunflower oil into a large non-stick frying pan and heat it over medium heat until it reaches the ideal temperature (about 170°C). Carefully place the cutlets into the hot oil, leaving space between them to allow for even frying. Cook for about 4 minutes on the first side without touching them, until you see the edges turn a deep golden brown. Chef's tip: if the oil starts to smoke, lower the heat immediately to prevent the sesame seeds from burning and becoming bitter.
  8. Turn the cutlets with a spatula and continue cooking for another 4 minutes on the second side, keeping the heat medium and constant. At the end, the outer crust should be brown, extremely crunchy, and the sesame seeds should emit an intense toasted aroma. Drain them from the oil and place them on a tray lined with absorbent paper to remove residual oil. Chef's tip: serve the cutlets piping hot accompanied by a lemon wedge, as the juice will enhance the sweetness of the fennel and the savouriness of the crust.

Tips

  • Parboil the fennel slices for just a few minutes so they stay firm and don't break apart.
  • Ensure the chickpea flour batter is thick enough to adhere properly to the vegetable surface.
  • Press the breading firmly on both sides to create a durable, golden crust while cooking.

Variations

  • Mix grated pecorino cheese into the breadcrumbs for a more savory and bold version.
  • Swap the paprika for curry powder for a spicy note and a deep golden color.
