Ditalini in Creamy Potato and Sweet Leek Sauce

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 30 min · Medium

A thick and velvety soup that uses leek for a delicate but characteristic aroma, replacing the onion. The boiled potato creates a soft base that coats the pasta, making it very easy to swallow.

A home cooking classic where yellow potatoes transform into a thick and enveloping base. The leek provides a sweet and delicate aroma that doesn't overpower the other ingredients.

Ingredients

  • Small ditalini pasta (short, tube-shaped pasta)160 g
  • Yellow-fleshed potatoes250 g
  • Leek (white part only)80 g
  • Grated Parmigiano Reggiano (a hard, aged Italian cheese)20 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil15 ml

Allergens

Gluten, Milk

Method

  1. Start by carefully peeling 250g of yellow-fleshed potatoes and cutting them into uniform cubes of about 1 centimetre. Use a sharp chef's knife on a stable cutting board to achieve similar-sized pieces, ensuring perfectly even cooking. This precision task will take about 5 minutes of your time. You will get clean and regular cubes, essential for the final texture of the dish. My chef's tip is to keep the potatoes in cold water until use to prevent them from browning upon contact with air.
  2. Take 80g of leek (white part only) and slice it into very thin, almost translucent rounds. Transfer everything into a colander and rinse thoroughly under cool running water, moving the slices with your fingers to remove any grit hidden between the layers. This cleaning phase takes about 3 minutes and ensures maximum hygiene and purity of taste. The final result should be clean, bright leek slices ready to be softened. Be careful to discard the tougher outer green leaves which would be too fibrous to eat.
  3. In a large saucepan, pour about 5ml of extra virgin olive oil and add the sliced leek along with two tablespoons of still water. Turn the heat to very low and let it sweat gently for about 5-7 minutes, stirring occasionally with a wooden spoon. The heat must be gentle enough not to colour the vegetable, which should simply become transparent and almost melt. You will obtain a fragrant, sweet base without any bitter burnt notes. The chef's secret is to keep the lid slightly ajar to create a moist environment that promotes cooking without excessive fat.
  4. Now add the 250g of potato cubes into the pot with the leeks and cover everything with 700ml of lukewarm water. Increase the heat slightly to medium, bring to a boil, and then lower it again, letting it simmer gently for about 20 minutes without a lid. The potatoes should become extremely tender, almost falling apart when touched with a spoon. You will notice the water changing colour, becoming slightly cloudy due to the released starch. Do not add salt at this stage to allow the potato fibres to break down more easily.
  5. Temporarily turn off the heat and, using a fork or a potato masher directly in the pot, mash about two-thirds of the potato cubes. Work vigorously until you see a thick, rustic cream forming, but leave some pieces whole to provide texture to the bite. This operation takes about 2 minutes and transforms the broth into a real velouté. The result should be a creamy, enveloping base with a delicate garden scent. My advice is not to use an immersion blender to avoid making the cream too gluey due to the potato starch.
  6. Bring the pot back to medium heat and, as soon as it returns to a boil, pour the 160g of small ditalini pasta directly into the potato cream. Cook the pasta for the time indicated on the package, usually between 8 and 10 minutes, stirring very frequently to prevent the starch from sticking to the bottom. If you notice the liquid drying out too quickly, add a small ladle of boiling water at a time, as if you were preparing a risotto. The pasta must cook 'submerged' in the cream, absorbing all the flavours. Make sure to adjust the salt only towards the end of the ditalini cooking time.
  7. Once cooked, the consistency should be thick and velvety, with the ditalini perfectly coated by the potato and leek cream. Turn off the heat and proceed with the 'mantecatura' by adding 20g of grated Parmigiano Reggiano and the remaining 10ml of raw extra virgin olive oil. Stir energetically for about a minute to emulsify the fats with the starch, creating a glossy and silky structure. Let the preparation rest for two minutes before serving so that the flavours settle perfectly. The secret to a restaurant-quality result is to serve the dish creamy, never too dry or excessively liquid.
  8. Distribute the ditalini into four warm shallow bowls, levelling the surface with a gentle tap on the bottom of the plate. For adults, complete each serving with a grinding of fresh black pepper which will provide a spicy note in contrast to the sweetness of the leek. Serve the dish at a lukewarm temperature, ideal for appreciating the aromatic complexity of the yellow potatoes. The final result will be an excellent comfort food, balanced and nourishing. One last chef's touch: decorate with a spiral of raw oil to enhance the olive aroma just before the first bite.

Tips

  • Cut the potatoes into very small cubes to speed up cooking and make the cream easier to form.
  • Sauté the leek over very low heat to prevent it from browning and becoming bitter.
  • If you prefer a more rustic texture, mash the potatoes coarsely with a fork.

Variations

  • Add a well-cleaned Parmesan rind while boiling the potatoes for a more intense flavor.
  • Replace ditalini with mixed pasta for a result typical of rural tradition.
