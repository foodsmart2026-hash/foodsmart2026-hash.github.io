Crispy Polenta Crostini with Rosemary Bell Pepper Cream

4 servings · Prep 5 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

A tasty starter that plays on the contrast between the crunchy maize base and the softness of a sweet pepper mousse. Entirely free from common allergens, it is a quick and budget-friendly solution perfect for welcoming guests with flavour.

Crunchy appetizers that combine firm polenta with the velvety sweetness of bell peppers. Fresh rosemary completes the sensory profile with its Mediterranean fragrance. Perfect for a colorful happy hour or starter.

Ingredients

  • Instant polenta (precooked cornmeal)150 g
  • Water600 ml
  • Red and yellow bell peppers350 g
  • Garlic1 clove
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Fresh rosemary2 sprigs
  • Fine salt5 g
  • Ground black pepper1 g

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Start by heating 600 ml of water with 5 g of salt in a large, heavy-bottomed saucepan over high heat. The water is ready when you see large, dense bubbles appearing on the surface, indicating a full boil. Choosing a heavy pot is essential to ensure even heat distribution and prevent the bottom from scorching. You will achieve a boiling, seasoned water base, perfect for your polenta. Chef's tip: add the salt only when the water is near boiling to avoid slowing down the heating process.
  2. Rain in the 150 g of instant polenta, which means pouring it slowly while whisking vigorously with a hand whisk. Reduce the heat to medium and continue cooking for about 5 minutes, working the mixture constantly. The result should be a thick, velvety, and smooth polenta that tends to pull away cleanly from the sides of the saucepan. Chef's tip: the secret to avoiding lumps is the initial speed with the whisk; if any form, use an immersion blender for a few seconds to smooth out the mass.
  3. Take a flat baking tray, line it with baking paper, and spread the hot polenta, levelling it with a damp spatula to a thickness of about 1 cm. The wet spatula will allow you to achieve a perfectly smooth surface without the mixture sticking to the tool. Place the tray in the freezer for just 5 minutes: this temperature shock will allow the polenta to firm up quickly, making it easy to cut. Chef's tip: do not exceed the thickness; one centimetre is the ideal size for a crouton that is crispy on the outside but not too heavy on the palate.
  4. While the polenta rests, wash the 350 g of peppers and remove the internal seeds and white ribs, then cut them into tiny cubes of about 5 mm. Use a sharp knife and work with precision on a stable cutting board to ensure uniform size. This 'brunoise' cut is necessary to ensure extremely fast cooking in the pan, maintaining the sweet flavour of the vegetables. Chef's tip: always place the skin side of the pepper on the board and cut through the flesh side to prevent the blade from slipping on the shiny surface.
  5. In a non-stick frying pan, heat 15 ml of extra virgin olive oil with the whole garlic clove and a sprig of fresh rosemary to infuse the fat. Add the peppers and sauté over high heat for about 8 minutes, stirring often to prevent them from burning. You should obtain tender, slightly golden, and very fragrant cubes that have lost their initial sharpness. Chef's tip: remove the rosemary as soon as it starts to darken to prevent it from releasing a bitter aftertaste into the preparation.
  6. Remove the garlic and rosemary from the pan, transfer the peppers to an immersion blender beaker, and blend at maximum speed. Blend until you obtain a very smooth and bright cream, adding a drizzle of extra oil if the texture is too thick or grainy. The final result should be a pepper purée with a vivid colour and a silky texture. Chef's tip: if you want a restaurant-quality cream, pass it through a fine-mesh sieve to remove any remaining pepper skins.
  7. Remove the polenta from the freezer, which will now be quite firm, and cut it into regular squares of about 5 cm per side using a smooth-bladed knife. Heat the remaining 15 ml of oil in the same pan used for the peppers and grill the polenta squares over medium-high heat for 2 minutes per side. You should achieve a brown and very crispy outer crust, while the inside remains hot and soft. Chef's tip: do not rush to flip the croutons; wait for the natural crust to form so they release themselves from the bottom of the pan.
  8. Assemble the dish by placing a generous spoonful of the rosemary pepper cream onto each hot polenta crouton. Complete the preparation with a pinch of freshly ground black pepper and a few fresh rosemary needles taken from the second sprig to echo the aromas of the cream. Serve the starter immediately to preserve the contrast between the crispy base and the soft cream. Chef's tip: slightly warm the serving plates before plating to keep the croutons hot for longer during the tasting.

Tips

  • Spread the polenta to a thickness of about one centimeter for crostini that are crispy outside and soft inside.
  • Let the polenta cool completely before cutting to ensure clean and precise edges.
  • Blend the peppers while still warm to achieve a smoother and more velvety cream.

Variations

  • Use only red peppers for a cream with a more vivid color and even more pronounced sweetness.
  • Cut the crostini into triangles or sticks for a presentation that differs from the usual.
