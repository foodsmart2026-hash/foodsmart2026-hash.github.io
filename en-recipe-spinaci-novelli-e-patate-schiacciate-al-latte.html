Baby Spinach and Potatoes Mashed with Milk

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

A rustic two-tone mash where the spinach is finely chopped to avoid stringy textures. The milk makes everything creamier and smoother.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🥕 Vegetarian🧒 Kid-friendly🌾 Gluten-free

Ingredients

  • Fresh baby spinach300 g
  • White-fleshed potatoes200 g
  • Whole milk30 ml
  • Extra virgin olive oil10 g

Allergens

Milk

Method

  1. Peel 200g of white-fleshed potatoes using a vegetable peeler and slice them very thinly, about 3-4mm thick, to ensure consistent cooking times. Submerge the slices in a large pot with approximately 1.5 litres of cold, unsalted water and bring to a boil over a high heat. Let them cook for about 10 minutes from the point of boiling, until a fork pierces the flesh with no resistance at all. The result should be tender potatoes that are just beginning to crumble at the edges, indicating the starch is ready to be mashed. The chef's tip is to keep the slices uniform in thickness to prevent some staying hard while others dissolve into the water.
  2. While the potatoes are cooking, take 300g of baby spinach and wash them thoroughly under cold running water to remove any traces of grit from the leaves. Dry them roughly and, after the first 10 minutes of potato cooking, tip them directly into the same boiling water. Keep the heat at medium and let everything simmer together for the final 5 minutes required to finish both ingredients. The spinach should wilt completely and turn a vibrant bright green, without becoming dark or mushy. Do not overcook at this stage, or you will lose the vivid colour and the heat-sensitive vitamins in the spinach.
  3. Once the total 15 minutes of cooking are up, drain the contents of the pot using a fine-mesh sieve or a large slotted spoon. Ensure you remove all excess water by pressing the vegetables lightly against the sides of the sieve with the back of a clean spoon. This draining process should take about a minute to guarantee the final mixture is creamy rather than watery. You will be left with soft potatoes and cooked spinach ready for manual processing. The chef suggests keeping a tablespoon of the starchy cooking water if you prefer an even smoother final consistency.
  4. Gently separate the spinach from the potatoes using tongs and transfer them onto a clean wooden chopping board. Use a sharp chef's knife to chop the spinach very finely, moving the blade in a continuous rocking motion until it almost forms a smooth paste. This requires about 2-3 minutes of brisk and precise manual work. The result should be a uniform dark green pulp, free from any long fibres or whole leaves. For an excellent result, absolutely avoid using an electric blender, which would make the spinach too gluey due to excessive oxidation.
  5. Place the still-steaming potato slices into a large bowl and mash them immediately using a potato masher or a sturdy fork. Work the pulp with firm, upward strokes until you have achieved a coarse puree that is completely free from large lumps or hard bits. It is vital to do this while the potatoes are still emitting steam to make the most of the hot starch's softness. You should achieve an airy, fluffy potato base with a pale yellowish colour. The chef's secret is never to use an immersion blender for this task, otherwise the texture will become unpleasantly rubbery.
  6. Now add the finely chopped spinach to the potato puree in the bowl and slowly pour in 30ml of whole milk, which you should have previously warmed slightly in a small pan. Stir with a wooden spoon or a hand whisk, aiming to incorporate air into the mixture as the milk is slowly absorbed. Work everything for about 2 minutes on a stable surface until the two ingredients are perfectly integrated. The mixture should transform into a velvety, fluffy cream with a characteristic marbled green colour. Ensure the milk is lukewarm to avoid a thermal shock that could cause the puree's structure to collapse.
  7. Add 10g of extra virgin olive oil by drizzling it over the still-warm potato and spinach mixture. Continue to stir vigorously for another 60 seconds so the oil emulsifies perfectly with the spinach fibres and the potato starch. The oil does not just act as a seasoning; it provides a natural gloss and a fruity aroma that enhances the potatoes' natural sweetness. The final result should be a silky puree, firm yet very light to the touch of a spoon. The chef's advice is to use a high-quality cold-pressed extra virgin oil to preserve all its nutritional properties.
  8. Divide the resulting mixture into 4 equal portions and serve immediately while it is still nice and hot to enjoy the maximum fluffiness. You can use a circular food ring for an elegant presentation or create quenelles using two spoons for a more refined restaurant touch. Serve this nutritious side dish alongside a simple protein source, such as steamed fish or grilled white meat. The portion will look colourful, smell inviting, and be perfectly balanced in flavour. Serve straight away as the potato tends to firm up and lose its airy texture as it cools down.
