Pasta with Cauliflower Cream, Black Olives and Orange

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 20 min · Medium

A refined yet extremely budget-friendly first course that makes the most of the natural creaminess of blended cauliflower. The contrast between the savouriness of the olives and the citrus aroma makes it a surprising hit for the whole family.

An original pasta dish that transforms cauliflower into a velvety and aromatic cream. The contrast between the vegetable's sweetness, the saltiness of black olives, and the citrusy orange zest creates a surprising balance. A modern recipe to creatively highlight seasonal ingredients.

Ingredients

  • Short semolina pasta (Durum wheat pasta common in Italy)320 g
  • Cauliflower600 g
  • Pitted black olives50 g
  • Organic orange (zest)1 pcs
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Garlic1 spicchio
  • Salt and Pepperq.b.

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Begin by preparing 600g of white cauliflower: break the florets from the central core and dice the core into small cubes to ensure even cooking. Wash them thoroughly under cold running water and drain well in a colander. This preliminary step ensures zero waste while preserving all the healthy fibres. The result should be a mix of small florets and cubes ready for the heat. Chef's tip: cut the florets into similar sizes so they all cook at the same time without falling apart.
  2. Bring a large pot with about 3 litres of lightly salted water to a boil and tip in the prepared 600g of cauliflower. Cook over medium heat for about 8-10 minutes, testing the texture with a fork; they should be very tender but not disintegrating. This technique preserves the bright white colour and the natural sweetness of the vegetable. Once done, remove the cauliflower with a slotted spoon, making sure to save all the cooking water for the pasta. The secret to perfect cauliflower is not to exceed 10 minutes to prevent a strong sulphuric smell.
  3. Take about two-thirds of the boiled cauliflower (approx. 400g) and transfer it to a blender beaker along with 15ml of extra virgin olive oil and a ladle of its cooking water. Blend at maximum speed until you achieve a thick, smooth, and velvety cream, free of any lumps. If the cream appears too stiff, add another tablespoon of cooking water until it drops easily from a spoon. You will get a fragrant, silky base that will act as the binding sauce for the pasta. Chef's tip: blend while the cauliflower is still hot to better emulsify the fats in the oil.
  4. Use the remaining cauliflower cooking water in the pot, bringing it back to a rolling boil, to cook the 320g of short pasta. Cook according to the package instructions, but subtract one minute to finish the cooking in the pan. The water, already infused with the cauliflower flavour, will give the pasta a deeper, more integrated taste. The pasta should be 'al dente', meaning it offers a slight resistance to the bite. Chef's tip: do not add too much salt now, as the water has already concentrated during the vegetable boiling.
  5. In a very large non-stick frying pan, heat the remaining 15ml of extra virgin olive oil with a lightly crushed, unpeeled garlic clove over low heat. When the garlic starts to sizzle and fragrance the oil (about 2 minutes), add the remaining third of the boiled cauliflower florets. Increase the heat and sear the florets for 3-4 minutes until an inviting golden crust forms on the surface. This step adds a toasted note and a crunchy texture that contrasts with the cream. Be careful not to burn the garlic; remove it as soon as it turns golden.
  6. While the pasta finishes cooking, roughly chop 50g of pitted black olives and finely grate the zest of half the organic orange directly into the pan with the sautéed florets. Let the flavours meld over very low heat for one minute, allowing the heat to release the orange's essential oils and the savoury flavour of the olives. The aroma will immediately become citrusy and Mediterranean, creating a perfect aromatic contrast. Ensure you only grate the orange part of the skin, avoiding the bitter white pith. My tip is to use high-quality olives, like Taggiasca, for a more intense flavour.
  7. Drain the pasta al dente and pour it directly into the pan with the seasonings, immediately adding the previously prepared cauliflower cream. Add a ladle of cooking water and toss everything over high heat for about 60 seconds, moving the pan firmly to encourage the emulsion between the pasta starch and the cream. The pasta must be completely coated in a glossy, fluid sauce, not dry or sticky. If necessary, add a little more cooking water to adjust the consistency. The ideal result is a 'mantecatura' that ensures creaminess in every bite.
  8. Turn off the heat and complete the dish by adding the remaining grated organic orange zest and a generous grind of fresh black pepper. Plate immediately, distributing the crunchy florets and olives evenly over each serving. The final touch is a fragrant drizzle of raw extra virgin olive oil to enhance all the vegetable and citrus notes. The dish will look elegant, with soft colours broken by the black of the olives and the orange of the zest. A final tip: serve in pre-warmed plates to keep the sauce creamy for longer.
  9. Your pasta with cauliflower cream is ready to be enjoyed: the sweet taste of the cauliflower pairs perfectly with the acidity of the orange and the saltiness of the olives. Ensure every forkful captures both the creamy and crunchy elements. The final texture should be velvety and coating, almost like a risottato pasta. Enjoy your meal! Remember that this dish is also excellent with a sprinkle of toasted breadcrumbs for an extra touch of crunch.

Tips

  • Blend the cauliflower with a splash of cooking water to achieve a perfectly smooth texture.
  • Use only the outer part of the orange zest, avoiding the bitter white pith, for the best aroma.
  • Briefly sauté the olives in the pan before adding them to intensify their natural flavor.

Variations

  • Add anchovies melted in the oil base for an extra boost of Mediterranean flavor.
  • Top the dish with a handful of toasted flaked almonds to add a crunchy texture.
