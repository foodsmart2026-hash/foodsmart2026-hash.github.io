Fluffy Yogurt Bundt Cake

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 40 min · Medium

The classic Italian breakfast cake (Ciambellone), tall and very soft, ideal for dunking in milk or tea. The recipe uses the yogurt pot as a measuring cup, making it incredibly simple.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🥕 Vegetarian🧒 Kid-friendly🇮🇹 Italian

The yogurt bundt cake is a traditional Italian homemade dessert, loved for its incredible softness and citrus fragrance. A simple preparation that yields a spongy texture, perfect for dipping or enjoying by the slice.

Ingredients

  • Plain yogurt125 g
  • Plain flour (Type 00 flour, finely milled for Italian cakes)250 g
  • Caster sugar150 g
  • Eggs3 pcs
  • Sunflower oil100 ml
  • Baking powder (Lievito per dolci, often vanilla-scented in Italy)16 g
  • Lemon (zest)1 pcs

Allergens

Eggs, Gluten, Milk

Method

  1. Start by cracking 3 room-temperature eggs into a large bowl and adding 150g of granulated sugar. Use an electric hand mixer at maximum speed for about 5-7 minutes, whipping the mixture until it becomes pale, thick, and triples in its initial volume. The result should be a frothy mass that 'ribbons', meaning it leaves a visible trail on the surface when you lift the beaters. Chef's tip: use eggs that are not cold from the fridge to allow the proteins to trap more air and achieve extreme fluffiness.
  2. Reduce the mixer speed to minimum and incorporate 125g of plain white yogurt, adding it a tablespoon at a time. Ensure the yogurt is also at room temperature to avoid a thermal shock that could deflate the egg mass. Continue mixing only for the strictly necessary time to make the mixture homogeneous, maintaining the aerated structure. Chef's tip: choose a full-fat yogurt for a moister texture and a less crumbly finish.
  3. Slowly pour 100ml of sunflower oil down the side of the bowl while keeping the whisks at a very low speed to encourage emulsification. The processing must be gentle so as not to break the air bubbles, resulting in a shiny fluid without oily streaks on the surface. This step ensures the fat is distributed evenly without weighing down the bottom of the cake. Chef's tip: sunflower oil is preferable to butter in this recipe as it guarantees a softness that lasts for several days.
  4. Finely grate the zest of 1 organic lemon directly over the batter, taking extreme care not to include the white pith (albedo) which would be bitter. Stir briefly with a spatula to distribute the essential oils that will release a fresh, natural aroma during baking. You will immediately notice how the citrus scent perfectly balances the sweetness of the sugar. Chef's tip: grate the lemon at the very last second to avoid losing the volatile aromas of the peel.
  5. Carefully sift 250g of plain flour (00 type) together with 16g of baking powder to eliminate any lumps and aerate the powders. Add the mix to the batter in three stages, incorporating it manually with a silicone spatula using circular movements from bottom to top. You must obtain a smooth, velvety, medium-density batter with no visible white flour residues. Chef's tip: never use electric whisks at this stage to avoid developing gluten, which would make the cake rubbery rather than fluffy.
  6. Prepare a 24cm bundt tin by brushing it with a thin film of oil and lightly flouring it, tapping out any excess. Pour the batter starting from a single point and let it distribute naturally, then level the surface with the back of a spoon without tapping the tin on the counter. The tin should be filled to about two-thirds to allow for correct vertical growth. Chef's tip: for a perfect release, you can create a mix of melted butter and flour to brush into the crevices of the tin.
  7. Place the cake on the middle rack of a preheated static oven at 180°C and bake for 35-40 minutes without ever opening the door, especially in the first 30 minutes. The constant heat will allow the leavening agent to work and the proteins to stabilise, leading the cake to brown evenly on the surface. The kitchen will fill with a delicate scent of lemon, a sign that baking is nearly complete. Chef's tip: if you notice the surface browning too quickly, cover it loosely with a sheet of foil without touching the batter.
  8. Perform the skewer test by inserting it into the highest part of the cake; if it comes out dry and clean, remove from the oven immediately. Let the cake rest in the tin for at least 15 minutes, then carefully invert it onto a wire rack to finish cooling. This step is crucial to allow residual moisture to evaporate correctly without making the base soggy. Chef's tip: do not attempt to unmould the cake while it is piping hot, as the structure is still fragile and it would risk breaking irreparably.

Tips

  • Ensure the eggs are at room temperature to facilitate optimal whipping of the mixture.
  • Sift the flour and baking powder well to prevent lumps and ensure even rising.
  • Carefully grease and flour the mold to turn out the cake without damaging its surface.

Variations

  • Replace the plain yogurt with citrus-flavored yogurt to further intensify the lemon aroma.
  • Add some dark chocolate chips to the batter for a more indulgent version.
