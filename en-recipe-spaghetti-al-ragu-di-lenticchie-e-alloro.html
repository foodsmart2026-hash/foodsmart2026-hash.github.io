Spaghetti with Lentil and Bay Leaf Ragù

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 45 min · Medium

A vegetarian twist on the classic ragù, thick and flavourful thanks to brown lentils. It is a nutritious and very affordable dish, perfect for family meals.

A plant-based take on the classic ragu, where tiny lentils provide a pleasant texture and deep flavor. Bay leaf adds a balsamic touch that complements the rustic whole grain spaghetti perfectly.

Ingredients

  • Wholemeal spaghetti320 g
  • Small dried lentils200 g
  • Tomato passata (smooth sieved tomatoes)400 g
  • Yellow onion1 pcs
  • Carrot1 pcs
  • Celery stalk1 pcs
  • Bay leaves2 foglie
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Salt and pepperq.b.

Allergens

Gluten, Celery

Method

  1. Start by rinsing 200g of small dried lentils under plenty of cold running water using a fine-mesh sieve. Gently rub them with your hands to remove any earthy residue or small impurities that could compromise the texture of the dish. Let them drain thoroughly before proceeding, making sure no small stones are hidden among the grains. This step is crucial to ensure the legumes are clean and the final flavour remains bright and pure. Chef's Tip: small lentils do not require soaking, but washing them prevents unpleasant foam from forming during the simmering process.
  2. Prepare the aromatic base by finely mincing 1 yellow onion, 1 carrot, and 1 celery stalk after peeling and washing them carefully. Use a sharp knife to achieve very small, uniform cubes, known as 'brunoise', which will melt into the sauce creating a thick base. In a large saucepan, heat 30ml of extra virgin olive oil with a tablespoon of water over a very low flame. You aim for a glossy, fragrant mixture that begins to soften gently for 5-7 minutes without taking on any brown colour. Chef's Tip: adding water initially allows the vegetables to sweat evenly without burning, keeping the nutritional properties of the oil intact.
  3. Once the soffritto has become translucent, pour the drained lentils into the saucepan. Slightly increase the heat to a medium level and toast them for about 2 minutes, stirring constantly with a wooden spoon. You will notice the legumes starting to smell intense and becoming glossy as they are coated in oil, making them more resilient during cooking. This step helps to partially seal the outer skin so they hold their shape without falling apart. Chef's Tip: do not skip the toasting; it is the technical secret to giving depth and a nutty undertone to your vegetable ragù.
  4. Add 400g of tomato passata and the 2 whole bay leaves, stirring well to blend the primary flavours. Lower the heat to minimum, cover with a lid, and let it simmer gently for about 30-40 minutes so the flavours can concentrate. Periodically check the consistency and, if the sauce looks too dry, add a ladleful of hot water to maintain the necessary moisture for the legumes to cook. The ragù is ready when the lentils are tender to the bite but still whole, and the sauce is thick and velvety. Chef's Tip: bay leaves release their best aroma during long cooking; do not chop the leaves so you can easily remove them later.
  5. While the ragù finishes its transformation, bring a large pot filled with about 4 litres of water to a boil. Only when it reaches a rolling boil, add coarse salt (about 10g per litre) and wait for it to dissolve completely before adding the pasta. The water temperature must remain constant to ensure the whole wheat pasta cooks evenly, as it requires steady heat to hydrate the fibres. Make sure there is enough space so the spaghetti can move freely without clumping together. Chef's Tip: never add oil to the pasta water, as it creates a greasy film that prevents the lentil sauce from clinging perfectly.
  6. Submerge 320g of whole wheat spaghetti into the boiling water and gently push them down with a fork as they soften. Cook them following the package instructions, but taste them a minute before the expected end time to ensure they are perfectly 'al dente'. Before draining, reserve a ladle of the starchy cooking water, which will act as the binding element for the final preparation phase. Whole wheat pasta has a rustier texture that requires precise cooking to avoid being too hard or sticky. Chef's Tip: draining the pasta slightly early is crucial as it will continue to cook through residual heat once mixed with the sauce.
  7. Remove the bay leaves from the ragù and drain the spaghetti directly into the saucepan using kitchen tongs to avoid breaking the strands. Increase the heat to high and toss the pasta for about 60 seconds, stirring vigorously to coat every single strand of spaghetti with the lentil dressing. If necessary, gradually add a splash of the reserved cooking water to create a creamy emulsion that perfectly binds the sauce to the pasta. You should achieve a glossy, harmonious dish where the sauce does not slide off but stays firmly anchored to the spaghetti. Chef's Tip: the tossing motion of the pan combined with the cooking water creates that irresistible creaminess typical of professional kitchens.
  8. Turn off the heat and finish the dish with a generous grind of fresh black pepper and the remaining raw extra virgin olive oil to enhance the aromas. Divide the spaghetti into flat plates, using a ladle and tongs to create an elegant nest in the centre of each portion. Garnish with the lentils remaining at the bottom of the saucepan and serve immediately while the dish emits all the aromatic warmth of the bay leaf. The final result will be a balanced, flavourful, and visually appealing main course thanks to the chromatic contrast between the tomato and the whole wheat. Chef's Tip: the final raw oil is not just a seasoning, but a flavour enhancer that protects the sauce's volatile aromas.

Tips

  • Cook the ragu over very low heat to allow the flavors to merge and the lentils to soften.
  • Keep a ladle of pasta cooking water to better bind the sauce to the whole grain spaghetti.
  • Remove the bay leaves at the end of cooking to prevent the sauce from becoming too bitter.

Variations

  • Add a pinch of dried chili flakes to the initial sauté for a spicy and lively kick.
  • Replace the spaghetti with whole grain rigatoni to better capture the lentil ragu inside the pasta shape.
