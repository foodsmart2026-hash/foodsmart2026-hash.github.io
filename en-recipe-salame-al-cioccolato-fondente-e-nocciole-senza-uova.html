Egg-free Dark Chocolate and Hazelnut Salami

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 0 min · Easy

A family holiday classic, updated to be safe and easy to store without raw eggs. Uses common dry biscuits and cocoa for a very low cost.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🥕 Vegetarian🧒 Kid-friendly🇮🇹 Italian

A delicious version of the classic chocolate salami, made special by the toasted note of hazelnuts. Egg-free, this dessert offers a firm texture and an intense dark cocoa flavor.

Ingredients

  • Rich Tea or Petit Beurre biscuits (plain dry biscuits)200 g
  • Butter80 g
  • Unsweetened cocoa powder40 g
  • Caster sugar60 g
  • Whole milk50 ml
  • Chopped toasted hazelnuts30 g

Allergens

Gluten, Milk, Tree nuts

Method

  1. Begin by taking 200g of plain Petit-style biscuits and crumbling them roughly by hand into a large mixing bowl. Avoid grinding them into a fine powder; instead, aim for irregular pieces ranging from 5 to 10mm, which will mimic the fat marbling in a real salami once sliced. The final result should be a heterogeneous, crunchy mix that provides structure to the dessert. My professional advice is to avoid using a food processor for this task, as it risks creating a sandy and unappealing texture.
  2. Proceed by melting 80g of butter in a small saucepan over a very low heat, ensuring it never fries or browns. Gently swirl the pan until the butter is completely liquid and clear, then remove from the heat and let it cool for about 5 minutes at room temperature. This step is crucial to prevent excessive heat from ruining the texture of the cocoa in the next stage. You will achieve a glossy, fluid melted butter ready for emulsifying. Chef's tip: if preferred, use a microwave at low power, checking and stirring every 20 seconds.
  3. In a separate medium bowl, sift 40g of unsweetened cocoa powder to remove any lumps and combine it with 60g of caster sugar, mixing well with a hand whisk. Slowly pour in 50ml of whole milk in a thin stream, whisking constantly until you obtain a dark, thick, and perfectly smooth paste. Ensure the sugar begins to dissolve into the milk to avoid a gritty mouthfeel. The result should be a silky and fragrant cocoa cream. A little secret: use room temperature milk to encourage the ingredients to bind together immediately.
  4. Now, pour the slightly cooled melted butter into the cocoa and milk paste, stirring vigorously with a spatula or whisk. Work the mixture for about a minute until the fats are completely emulsified with the liquid base, resulting in a thick, extremely glossy, deep brown cream. If you notice the butter starting to separate, continue stirring firmly until the mass becomes homogeneous again. This emulsion is the 'glue' that will hold your salami together. Professional tip: ensure the cream is well-bound before moving on, otherwise the dessert will feel greasy.
  5. Pour the chocolate cream directly into the bowl with the crumbled biscuits and add 30g of chopped toasted hazelnuts. Fold the mixture using a wooden spoon or spatula, working from the bottom upwards, ensuring every single piece of biscuit and hazelnut is perfectly coated in the dark cream. The result will be a heavy, sticky mixture rich in crunchy inclusions. Take your time and check that no 'naked' biscuit areas remain. The chef recommends lightly toasting the hazelnuts beforehand to enhance their aroma and crunchiness.
  6. Lay a sheet of parchment paper on your work surface and transfer the entire mixture to the centre, spreading it out to form a regular strip. With slightly dampened hands or using the paper itself, shape the mixture by pushing it towards the centre until you form a compact cylinder about 20-25cm long. Try to eliminate any internal air pockets by pressing firmly on the sides to create the classic shape of an artisanal salami. The texture should be firm but still malleable. My advice is to work quickly to prevent the heat from your hands from over-melting the butter.
  7. Wrap the cylinder tightly in the parchment paper, rolling it like a large sweet and twisting the ends or folding them over to seal. Next, wrap the log in a sheet of aluminium foil, which will help maintain the circular shape and protect the dessert from fridge odours. Press firmly along the entire surface with your hands to further compact the heart of the salami. The result should be a rigid, well-sealed roll. Chef's tip: roll the wrapped salami back and forth on the counter to ensure it is perfectly cylindrical.
  8. Place the salami in the refrigerator on a flat surface and let it rest for at least 4 hours, though ideally overnight (about 8-12 hours). The cold will allow the butter to solidify again, cementing the biscuits and hazelnuts within the cocoa cream. Once the necessary time has passed, the salami will be hard to the touch and very resilient. The final result will be a solid dessert that you can handle without fear of it falling apart. Never speed up this process by using the freezer, as the texture would become too crystalline.
  9. When ready to serve, remove the salami from the fridge and strip away the layers. For a more realistic visual effect, you can roll it in a dusting of icing sugar (not included in the calorie count) to simulate the white bloom of cured meats. Use a sharp, non-serrated knife, dipped in hot water and dried, to cut slices about 1.5cm thick. The slices should reveal a beautiful mosaic of biscuits and hazelnuts embedded in dark chocolate. Serve while still chilled to enjoy the contrast between the creamy chocolate and the crunchy dry ingredients.

Tips

  • Crumble the biscuits coarsely by hand to maintain the typical mottled texture when sliced.
  • Let the dessert firm up in the refrigerator for at least four hours before slicing.
  • For a clean cut, use a smooth-blade knife slightly warmed under hot water.

Variations

  • Replace hazelnuts with toasted almonds for a more delicate taste.
  • Roll the salami in powdered sugar before serving to simulate the visual effect of real cured meat.
