Soft Cannellini and Courgette Pan-Steamed Bites

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 10 min · Easy

A very tender vegetable patty that melts in the mouth, perfect for baby-led weaning. The sweetness of the courgette meets the creaminess of white beans in a complete, protein-rich main.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⬇️ Big saving🧒 Kid-friendly🇮🇹 Italian

Tasty bites that combine the delicacy of cannellini beans with the freshness of seasonal zucchini. A versatile solution, great as a main course or a flavorful finger food.

Ingredients

  • Canned cannellini beans (drained and rinsed)240 g
  • Medium courgettes200 g
  • Medium egg1 pc
  • Fine breadcrumbs30 g
  • Grated Parmigiano Reggiano aged 30 months (a hard, granular Italian cheese)15 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil10 ml

Allergens

Gluten, Eggs, Milk

Method

  1. Start by thoroughly washing 200g of medium courgettes under cold running water to remove any soil residue. Use a coarse grater to shred them directly over a fine-mesh sieve placed over a deep bowl. Let the courgettes rest for a couple of minutes so they begin to naturally release their vegetable water. You will obtain bright green, uniform shreds ready to be processed. Chef's Tip: do not peel the courgettes, as the skin provides colour and helps the 'morbidi' hold their shape better during cooking.
  2. Take the grated courgettes in your hands or press them firmly against the sides of the sieve using the back of a spoon. You must remove as much water as possible to prevent the final dough from being too soft, which would require too many breadcrumbs and ruin the tenderness. Continue this for about 2 minutes until the liquid stops dripping consistently. The result will be a dry, compact courgette pulp, significantly reduced in volume. Chef's Tip: you can also squeeze them inside a clean tea towel for an even more professional and perfectly dry result.
  3. Take 240g of pre-cooked cannellini beans, drain them well from their canning liquid, and rinse under cold running water to remove excess salt and impurities. Transfer them to a food processor or mash them vigorously with a fork in a large bowl until they become a smooth, lump-free pulp. Work the beans for 1-2 minutes ensuring every bean is turned into a paste. You should obtain a smooth, thick puree that is naturally binding due to the bean starches. Chef's Tip: if using a fork, press with the concave part against the bottom of the bowl to perfectly break every skin.
  4. Add the well-squeezed courgettes, one medium egg, 15g of 30-month aged Parmesan cheese, and 30g of very fine breadcrumbs to the bean puree. Mix vigorously with a spoon or clean hands for about 2 minutes, perfectly integrating all ingredients into a single mixture. The dough should be soft, fragrant, and easily shapeable without falling apart between your fingers. You will get a compact mass, pale green dotted with the white of the cannellini. Chef's Tip: let the mixture rest in the fridge for 5-10 minutes before shaping; this allows the breadcrumbs to absorb residual moisture, stabilising the texture.
  5. With slightly damp hands to prevent the dough from sticking to your skin, take about a tablespoon of the mixture at a time and form walnut-sized balls. Gently flatten them between your palms to obtain discs about 1.5cm thick and 3-4cm in diameter. Proceed calmly until all the mixture is used, placing the 'morbidi' on a clean tray. The result will be a series of small, flat, uniform patties with smooth edges. Chef's Tip: try to keep the same size for all pieces to ensure perfectly simultaneous cooking.
  6. Heat a large non-stick frying pan over medium-low heat and pour in 10ml of extra virgin olive oil along with a tablespoon of water. Wait about 30-45 seconds for the emulsion to start sizzling slightly, creating a warm environment protected by steam. This 'pan-steaming' technique allows the patties to cook with very little added fat while keeping the inside extremely juicy. You should see a light white steam rising from the bottom of the pan. Chef's Tip: ensure the bottom of the pan is well covered by the film of oil and water to prevent the patties from sticking immediately.
  7. Place the patties in the hot pan, leaving at least a centimetre of space between them, and immediately cover with a tight-fitting lid. Cook over medium heat for exactly 4 minutes without ever lifting the lid, to trap the moisture needed to cook the egg and soften the courgette fibres. The base heat will start to form a light crust while the steam cooks the core. You will hear a light sizzle and see condensation forming on the lid. Chef's Tip: use a clear glass lid to monitor the browning of the edges without interrupting the steaming process.
  8. Remove the lid and flip the patties with a thin silicone spatula, acting with extreme care so as not to compromise their tender structure. Cover again and continue cooking for another 4 minutes over a gentle flame, monitoring that the base does not brown too quickly. At the end of this time, the 'morbidi' should feel springy to the touch and show a very light, golden browning on both sides. The interior must remain meltingly soft and very easy to chew. Chef's Tip: if the pan becomes too dry before the end, add another tablespoon of water around the outer edges.
  9. Turn off the heat and transfer the 'morbidi' to a serving plate, letting them cool for about 5-7 minutes before eating. This resting time is essential so that the egg proteins and bean starches finish stabilising, making the texture perfect to the bite. The patties will be fragrant, warm, and ideal even for the youngest palates thanks to the total absence of hard parts. They should look inviting and maintain their flat, regular shape. Chef's Tip: serve them with a side of steamed vegetables or a light yoghurt dip to enhance the natural sweetness of the cannellini beans.

Tips

  • Squeeze the grated zucchini very well to prevent them from releasing too much water during cooking.
  • Lightly moisten your hands with water to shape the bites without the dough sticking to your skin.
  • Use a high-quality non-stick pan to get a golden crust without using excessive oil.

Variations

  • Add some grated lemon zest to the mixture for a fresh and citrusy note.
  • Replace the Parmesan with grated pecorino for a more intense and rustic flavor.
