Creamy Scrambled Eggs with Peas

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

A time-saving and budget-friendly recipe. Peas are blended into a cream to avoid choking hazards (round, hard legumes) and to colour the egg in a fun way for the child.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⬇️ Big saving🥕 Vegetarian🧒 Kid-friendly🌾 Gluten-free

A velvety dish that transforms eggs into a soft, bright green cream. The combination of sweet sieved peas and creamy spreadable cheese creates an enveloping and delicate flavor.

Ingredients

  • Medium eggs3 pcs
  • Fine frozen peas150 g
  • Cream cheese30 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil5 g
  • Waterq.b.

Allergens

Eggs, Milk

Method

  1. Begin the preparation by bringing a small pot of unsalted water to the boil and adding 150g of fine frozen peas. Let them cook over a medium heat for about 10 minutes until they are extremely tender and almost falling apart. This prolonged cooking is essential to achieve a perfectly smooth puree. The final result should be a soft pea that offers minimal resistance when pressed. Chef's tip: do not salt the cooking water for the peas as this toughens the skins; keeping it plain helps preserve their vibrant green colour.
  2. Drain the cooked peas and transfer them to a blender or a jug for an immersion blender along with a tablespoon of their cooking water. Blend at maximum speed until you obtain a thick puree without any obvious lumps, adding only a tiny bit more water if needed to help the blades turn. The mixture should appear as a silky cream with a sweet aroma. The goal is to completely break down the legume fibre for a velvety mouthfeel. Chef's tip: if using a stick blender, use vertical movements to incorporate as little air as possible.
  3. Pass the resulting puree through a fine-mesh sieve using the back of a spoon or a flexible spatula. This step is crucial for removing any remaining skin fragments that would compromise the final creaminess of the eggs. Apply constant pressure until only dry fibre remains in the sieve and a very fine cream is collected in the bowl below. The result will be a homogeneous pea base with an intense colour. Chef's tip: never skip this step if you want a restaurant-quality result; texture is everything.
  4. In a large bowl, crack the 3 medium eggs and add 30g of cream cheese, which provides richness and stability to the structure. Use a hand whisk or a fork to beat the two ingredients vigorously until the cheese has completely dissolved into the egg without leaving any white lumps. You should obtain a pale yellow liquid, slightly frothy and perfectly combined. This cold emulsion is the secret to uniform coagulation in the pan. Chef's tip: use room-temperature eggs to encourage the cream cheese to integrate immediately.
  5. Incorporate the sieved pea puree into the egg and cheese mixture, stirring gently with a whisk until the colour becomes a uniform and bright green. Ensure there are no streaks of different colours, indicating the two mixtures have merged correctly. The consistency should be that of a fluid but rich batter. At this stage, you don't need to whip it, just combine carefully. Chef's tip: add a pinch of white pepper if you want an aromatic note that doesn't visually spoil the vivid green of the dish.
  6. Prepare for cooking by heating a 20-22cm non-stick frying pan with 5g of extra virgin olive oil over a very low heat. Use a pastry brush or a piece of kitchen paper to distribute the oil evenly across the entire surface and up the sides. The pan should be just warm when you pour in the mixture, never scorching hot, to prevent the egg from seizing instantly. The result should be a glossy and lightly greased surface. Chef's tip: controlled temperature is the only way to achieve 'creamy' rather than 'rubbery' eggs.
  7. Pour the egg and pea mixture into the pan and immediately start moving it with a silicone spatula or a wooden spoon. Make constant circular movements from the edges towards the centre, preventing the base from forming a dark crust. Keep the heat at an absolute minimum: cooking should be slow and progressive for about 3-4 minutes. You will see small soft curds forming, surrounded by a semi-liquid part. Chef's tip: if you see the mixture setting too quickly, lift the pan off the heat for a few seconds while continuing to stir.
  8. Continue cooking until the eggs have reached a consistency similar to a thick cream or a soft custard, remaining decidedly glossy. Do not wait for the egg to be completely dry; it should look almost 'wet' but no longer liquid. The aroma will be delicate, with the sweetness of the pea balancing the richness of the egg. Turn off the heat as soon as the spatula leaves a clean trail on the bottom of the pan that slowly closes back up. Chef's tip: perfection is reached by turning off the heat a second before you think it's necessary.
  9. Let the eggs rest in the switched-off pan for about 60 seconds before serving, allowing the residual heat to finish the cooking extremely gently. During this minute, the texture will stabilise, becoming velvety and compact without losing its internal moisture. Serve immediately in small bowls or on toasted wholemeal bread for a contrast of textures. The final result is a pastel-coloured dish, incredibly fluffy and nutritious. Chef's tip: serve on slightly warmed plates to prevent the temperature of the eggs from dropping too quickly, which would ruin the creaminess.

Tips

  • Sieve the pea puree carefully to remove skins and ensure a perfectly smooth consistency.
  • Cook the eggs over very low heat while stirring continuously to achieve an even texture.
  • Serve immediately while the eggs are still glossy and not overly set.

Variations

  • Add some finely chopped fresh chives for an aromatic and sharp touch.
  • Serve on a slice of toasted whole grain bread to add a crunchy element to the dish.
