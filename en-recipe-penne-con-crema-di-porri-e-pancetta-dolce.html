Penne with Leek Cream and Mild Pancetta

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 20 min · Easy

Leeks turn into a velvety cream that coats the pasta, contrasted by the crunchiness of the pancetta. A gourmet first course made with everyday ingredients.

💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🧒 Kid-friendly

A creamy first course where the sweetness of stewed leeks meets the savory crunch of bacon. Parmigiano Reggiano completes the dish, binding the ingredients into a velvety, enveloping sauce.

Ingredients

  • Penne rigate320 g
  • Leeks (white part only)300 g
  • Mild pancetta cubes (Italian salt-cured pork belly)80 g
  • Grated Parmigiano Reggiano (aged hard cheese)30 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil20 ml
  • Pasta cooking water100 ml
  • Salt and pepperq.b.

Allergens

Gluten, Milk

Method

  1. Start by cleaning 300g of leeks, selecting only the white and very light green parts which are the most tender. Slice them lengthways to thoroughly rinse any grit from between the layers, then slice them into very thin rounds with a sharp knife. Aim for a consistent thickness of about 2mm to ensure they soften evenly. You will get a fragrant, clean base ready for the sauce. Chef's tip: do not discard the dark green tops; they are excellent for adding flavour to a vegetable stock.
  2. Take a large frying pan, pour in 20ml of extra virgin olive oil and add the sliced leeks with a pinch of salt. Let them stew gently over a low-medium heat for about 10 minutes, stirring often with a wooden spoon so they don't brown. If they look too dry, add a tablespoon of warm water to maintain the necessary moisture. By the end, the leeks should be completely translucent, tender, and almost buttery to the touch. Chef's tip: slow cooking prevents the leek from turning bitter, releasing its natural sweetness instead.
  3. Transfer the cooked leeks into a blender jug and add about 50ml of hot water. Blend at maximum speed until you achieve a perfectly smooth, thick, pale green cream. If the texture seems too fibrous, pass the cream through a fine-mesh sieve to make it as velvety as a mousse. Taste and adjust the salt, keeping in mind that the pancetta and parmesan will add saltiness later. Chef's tip: blend the leeks while they are still hot to help break down the fibres for a more stable emulsion.
  4. In the same pan used for the leeks, wiped clean with kitchen paper, place the 80g of sweet diced pancetta without adding any extra fat. Turn the heat to medium and let the fat from the pancetta melt slowly, turning the meat golden and crispy in about 5-6 minutes. Stir occasionally for even browning, avoiding burning the edges. The result should be vibrant, crispy pancetta that is tender on the inside, sitting in its own tasty rendered fat. Chef's tip: always start with a cold pan to allow the pancetta fat to render out gradually.
  5. Bring a large pot of salted water to the boil (use about 1 litre of water per 100g of pasta). Add the 320g of penne rigate and set a timer: you will need to drain them about 2 minutes before the time indicated on the packet to finish cooking them in the pan. Stir immediately to prevent the pasta from sticking to the bottom and maintain a constant, steady boil. The penne should be very 'al dente', with a slightly firm core. Chef's tip: do not add oil to the pasta water, as it prevents the sauce from clinging properly to the surface of the penne.
  6. While the pasta is cooking, combine the leek cream in the pan with the crispy pancetta, turning the heat to low to marry the flavours. Add a ladle of pasta cooking water (about 50ml) to the cream to make it more fluid and ready for the penne. Stir with a spatula until you have a smooth, slightly frothy sauce. The aroma of the stewed leek will blend perfectly with the salty, savoury note of the pancetta. Chef's tip: always save a cup of cooking water before draining the pasta; you will need it for the final 'mantecatura'.
  7. Drain the penne directly into the pan with the sauce, turning the heat up slightly to medium. Toss the pasta vigorously for a minute, gradually adding the remaining cooking water if needed, so the starch creates a perfect bond with the leek cream. Now sprinkle in the 30g of grated Parmigiano Reggiano, continuing to stir until it is completely melted. The result must be glossy pasta, coated in a velvety sauce that fills the ridges of the penne perfectly. Chef's tip: perform this step off the heat if you prefer a looser, creamier texture.
  8. Turn off the heat and plate the penne immediately in warmed bowls. Finish each portion with a generous grind of fresh black pepper, which provides a spicy, sharp contrast to the sweetness of the leeks. Ensure each plate has a fair distribution of crispy pancetta cubes to guarantee the right texture in every bite. Serve the pasta steaming hot, enjoying the contrast between the creamy base and the firm pasta. Chef's tip: a final drizzle of raw olive oil will further enhance the natural aromas of the ingredients.

Tips

  • Start browning the bacon in a cold pan to let the fat melt slowly and make it extra crispy.
  • Blend the leeks adding cooking water a little at a time until you reach the desired creaminess.
  • Use only the white and light green parts of the leeks to ensure a sweeter and more delicate flavor.

Variations

  • Substitute the sweet bacon with smoked bacon for a stronger and more intense flavor contrast.
  • Use toasted hazelnut crumbles instead of cheese to add a crunchy and nutty note to the dish.
