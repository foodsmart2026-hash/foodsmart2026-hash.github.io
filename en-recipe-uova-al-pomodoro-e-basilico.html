Eggs with Tomato and Basil

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

A nutritious and balanced version of the classic 'Uova in Purgatorio' (Eggs in Purgatory), a pillar of Mediterranean peasant cooking. This recipe uses healthy fats and fresh herbs to transform simple supermarket ingredients into an excellent protein-rich main course.

A home-cooking classic, these eggs poached in tomato sauce are a cozy and fragrant one-pan meal. The rustic tomato base and fresh basil embrace the creamy yolks for a flavorful and traditional result. Perfect for a quick lunch or a family dinner.

Ingredients

  • Fresh medium eggs8 pcs
  • Rustic tomato passata800 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Copper onion (Cipolla ramata, a mild Italian variety)100 g
  • Garlic2 cloves
  • Fresh basil20 g
  • Iodised salt5 g
  • Ground black pepper2 g

Allergens

Eggs

Method

  1. Begin the preparation by carefully peeling 100g of copper onion and 2 garlic cloves. Finely dice the onion with a sharp chef's knife and lightly crush the garlic cloves, leaving them 'in their skins' (unpeeled) to impart a delicate, lingering aroma without overpowering the dish. This preliminary step takes about 5 minutes and is essential for releasing the aromatic essential oils. The final result should be a uniform onion mince that will soften evenly during cooking. My professional tip is to dampen your knife blade to reduce eye irritation caused by the onion vapours.
  2. Take a wide non-stick frying pan, approximately 30-32 cm in diameter, and pour in 30ml of extra virgin olive oil. Heat the oil over a very low flame, then add the chopped onion and the crushed garlic cloves, letting them sauté gently for about 5-6 minutes. Stir frequently with a wooden spoon to ensure the heat is distributed constantly without burning the ingredients. You should achieve a translucent, soft, and slightly golden onion that smells intensely fragrant. Be careful not to raise the heat too much: the secret to a great sauce lies in never letting the oil smoke, thus preserving its nutritional properties.
  3. Gently pour 800g of rustic tomato passata into the pan, then add 5g of iodised salt and 2g of freshly ground black pepper. Stir well to incorporate the sautéed base into the tomato and partially cover the pan with a lid. Cook over a medium-low flame for about 15 minutes, allowing excess water to evaporate and the flavours to concentrate. At the end of this time, the sauce should be reduced, thick, and vibrant red, having lost the typical acidity of raw tomatoes. I recommend always tasting the sauce halfway through to assess whether the seasoning is to your liking.
  4. Now remove the garlic cloves from the pan and add about 10g of fresh basil (half of the total amount), tearing the leaves roughly by hand. Tearing by hand instead of using a knife prevents the edges of the leaves from oxidising, keeping the herbaceous scent and bright green colour intact. Stir the basil into the bubbling sauce so it instantly releases its classic Mediterranean aroma. You will have a fragrant, velvety base ready to cradle the egg proteins. My chef's trick for an unbeatable scent is adding the basil only now, not at the beginning, to avoid burning its volatile oils.
  5. Use the back of a cooking spoon to create 8 small wells in the surface of the tomato sauce, spacing them well apart. These little 'nests' will contain the eggs, preventing the whites from spreading too much and keeping the plating tidy. Ensure that the bottom of each well just reveals the base of the pan, creating a stable and deep hollow. The visual result will be an undulating surface, ready for the final preparation phase. A chef's tip: if the sauce seems too dry, add a tablespoon of hot water before forming the wells to maintain the correct moisture level.
  6. Crack each of the 8 fresh eggs individually into a small cup before pouring them into the pan. Slide each egg very gently into each previously created well, ensuring the yolk remains perfectly intact and centred within the white. This technique prevents accidental shell fragments from falling in and allows you to check the freshness of each egg individually. You will see the white begin to clear upon contact with the heat of the tomato, while the yolk remains vivid and bright. Never pour eggs directly from the shell into the pan to avoid splashes of hot sauce.
  7. Immediately cover the pan with a lid, preferably a clear glass one so you can monitor the cooking without losing heat and steam. Allow to cook over a low flame for about 3-5 minutes, depending on your preferred yolk consistency. The perfect result is achieved when the white is completely set, opaque, and white, while the yolk remains veiled by a thin glossy film but fluid inside. Overcooking would make the yolk rubbery and less flavourful. Remember: the steam trapped by the lid is essential for cooking the top of the egg white evenly.
  8. Turn off the heat and finish the dish by garnishing with the remaining 10g of fresh basil and, if desired, a very small drizzle of raw oil to enhance the aromas. Let the preparation rest covered for about 1 minute before serving; this short rest allows the flavours to stabilise and makes it easier to lift the eggs from the bottom. Each serving should consist of two eggs napped with plenty of tomato sauce, presented in a shallow bowl or pasta plate. The scent of fresh basil combined with the creaminess of the yolk will make the dish irresistible. Serve with slices of toasted bread to catch every drop of the delicious sauce.
  9. As a nutritionist, I remind you that this dish is an excellent example of Mediterranean balance, rich in high-quality proteins and lycopene, a powerful antioxidant found in tomatoes. To keep the eggs' nutritional properties intact, avoid cooking the yolks until hard: the bioavailability of fat-soluble vitamins is highest when the yolk remains runny. Accompany the portion with a source of complex carbohydrates, such as wholemeal bread, to complete the meal's energy profile. This ensures a moderate glycaemic index and a prolonged sense of satiety. Moderation in salt use, compensated by the aromatic richness of garlic and basil, also promotes cardiovascular health.

Tips

  • Crack each egg into a small cup before pouring it into the sauce to avoid accidentally breaking the yolk.
  • Cover the pan with a lid during the last few minutes of cooking to let the whites set while keeping the yolks soft.
  • Use a rustic tomato puree to ensure a thick and hearty base that holds the eggs better.

Variations

  • For a spicy touch, add some fresh chili together with the onion and garlic sauté.
  • You can enrich the sauce by adding pitted black olives or desalted capers while the tomato is simmering.
