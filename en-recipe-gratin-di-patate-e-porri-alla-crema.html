Creamy Potato and Leek Gratin

4 servings · Prep 25 min · Cooking 45 min · Hard

A rich side dish or a substantial main course that transforms humble vegetables into elegant comfort food. The golden crust and soft heart make it irresistible for the whole family.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🌾 Gluten-free

Ingredients

  • yellow-fleshed potatoes800 g
  • leeks (white part only)2 pcs
  • cooking cream200 ml
  • whole milk100 ml
  • grated Gruyère or Emmental cheese100 g
  • garlic1 spicchio
  • butter20 g

Allergens

Milk

Method

  1. Begin by thoroughly washing 800g of yellow potatoes, peeling them, and slicing into thin 2-3mm rounds using a mandoline. This precision is essential so that the potatoes cook evenly and create a silky texture on the palate. Once sliced, pat them gently with a clean cloth to remove excess moisture. The result should be uniform, intact slices ready for layering. Chef's tip: do not wash the potatoes after slicing to preserve the starch, which helps thicken the cream during cooking.
  2. Prepare the leeks by removing the dark green tops and roots, keeping only the 2 white central hearts. Slice them into thin rounds and sauté in a non-stick pan with 10g of butter and a tablespoon of water over medium-low heat for about 5 minutes. The leeks should become translucent and tender without browning, releasing a sweet and delicate aroma. This pre-cooking is necessary to prevent them from remaining fibrous inside the gratin. Chef's tip: add a pinch of salt immediately to help draw out the leeks' natural moisture.
  3. Prepare your baking dish, which will be the heart of your presentation, by vigorously rubbing the bottom and sides with the halved garlic clove. Next, use the remaining 10g of butter to generously grease the internal surface, ensuring you cover every corner. This step isn't just to prevent sticking, but it infuses a subtle and elegant garlic aroma. The dish should look glossy and ready for the first layer. Chef's tip: use room-temperature butter to achieve an even, protective coating.
  4. In a large bowl or measuring jug, create the liquid base by mixing 200ml of double cream with 100ml of whole milk. Add a generous pinch of salt and a good grinding of black pepper, emulsifying with a small whisk or fork for one minute. The mixture must be smooth, fluid, and well-seasoned, as it will carry the flavour through the potatoes. Ensure the salt is fully dissolved to avoid uneven seasoning. Chef's tip: add a grating of nutmeg to enhance the dairy notes of the dish.
  5. Start the assembly by placing a first layer of potato slices on the bottom of the dish, overlapping them slightly like fish scales. Distribute half of the stewed leeks evenly over them and drizzle with about a third of the cream mixture (approx. 100ml). You should see the liquid starting to seep between the potato gaps, ensuring constant moisture. This initial layer must be compact and well-distributed. Chef's tip: press the potatoes down gently with your palm to eliminate any air pockets.
  6. Proceed with the second layer by repeating the process: place the remaining potatoes to cover the leeks, add the other half of the leeks, and finish by pouring the remaining liquid over the surface. The liquid should cover about two-thirds of the total height of the potatoes without submerging them completely. The gratin will now look tidy, with liquids naturally distributing downwards. Ensure the top layer of potatoes is well-moistened by the cream. Chef's tip: keep the most attractive potato slices for the final top layer for a better visual effect.
  7. Carefully cover the dish with a sheet of foil, sealing the edges tightly to prevent steam from escaping during the first cooking phase. Bake in a preheated conventional oven at 180°C for exactly 30 minutes, placing the dish on the middle rack. During this time, the potatoes will gently steam in the liquid, becoming tender and absorbing the leek flavour. By the end, the potatoes should be easily pierced with the tip of a knife. Chef's tip: if the foil touches the potatoes, brush it with a little oil to prevent sticking.
  8. Cautiously remove the foil cover and sprinkle the entire surface with 100g of finely grated Gruyère or Emmental cheese. Return to the oven for another 15 minutes, slightly raising the rack position if your oven allows, until the cheese has melted and formed a brown crust. You should hear the characteristic sizzling of the cheese and see golden bubbles on the surface. The aroma will become intense and inviting. Chef's tip: in the last 3 minutes, activate the grill function for a more even and crispy browning.
  9. Once removed from the oven, resist the urge to serve immediately and let the gratin rest for at least 5-10 minutes at room temperature. This resting period is vital so the residual cream can set and the potatoes can stabilise their structure, allowing you to cut neat portions. The final texture should be creamy at the core and crunchy on top, with perfectly balanced flavours. Serve the gratin while still hot, bringing the dish directly to the table. Chef's tip: resting for too long might make the potatoes too firm; 5 minutes is the perfect timing.
