Stewed Fine Peas with Sweet Leek

4 servings · Prep 5 min · Cooking 20 min · Easy

Very tender peas cooked with the white part of the leek which becomes almost a cream. A very economical source of fibre and iron, well-liked by little ones.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⬇️ Big saving🌱 Vegan🧒 Kid-friendly🌾 Gluten-free🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free

A classic and delicate side dish, perfect for pairing with meat or fish main courses. The natural sweetness of the leek enhances the peas' flavor, maintaining a soft and velvety texture.

Ingredients

  • Frozen fine peas300 g
  • Leek (white part only)50 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil10 g
  • Water or salt-free vegetable stock150 ml

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Begin by preparing 50g of leek, using only the tender white part; slice it very thinly and then mince it further with a sharp knife until reduced to tiny fragments. This fine chopping is essential so that the leek literally melts during stewing, providing sweetness without being fibrous on the palate. Work calmly on a stable chopping board to achieve a consistent texture. The result should be a very fine mince, almost like a coarse leek paste. My chef's tip: rinse the leek under running water after chopping to remove any residual grit trapped between the layers.
  2. Take a heavy-bottomed saucepan and pour in 10g of extra virgin olive oil along with one tablespoon of the total 150ml of water. Place the pan over a very low heat and let it warm gently for a minute, ensuring the oil does not reach its smoking point. Adding water initially helps keep the fat temperature low, preserving the nutritional properties of the oil. You should see the water just beginning to shimmer. My chef's tip: never leave oil alone on the heat, as with such small quantities, the temperature rises very quickly.
  3. Add the minced leek to the pan and let it soften gently over a very low heat for about 5 minutes, stirring frequently with a wooden spoon. If you notice the leek starting to brown or stick, add another teaspoon of water to keep the environment moist. The heat must be barely perceptible to allow the leek's sugars to release without burning. The result will be translucent, soft, and delicately scented. My chef's tip: patience during this stage is key to eliminating the leek's pungency and achieving a velvety base.
  4. Pour 300g of frozen fine peas into the pan and slightly increase the heat to a medium-low level. Stir carefully for about a minute, ensuring every pea is well coated in the oil and leek seasoning. This step, similar to toasting rice, helps seal in the flavour and ensures a better final texture. You will notice the peas glistening thanks to the thin film of fat covering them. My chef's tip: do not defrost the peas before use to prevent them from losing their natural crunch and bright green colour.
  5. Add the remaining 150ml of water or unsalted vegetable stock, ensuring the liquid almost entirely covers the peas. Immediately cover the pan with a tight-fitting lid to trap the steam inside. Maintain a gentle heat to allow for even and delicate cooking, avoiding violent boiling that could break the skins. The liquid will begin to simmer gently under the lid. My chef's tip: if using stock, ensure it is homemade and salt-free to keep the recipe suitable even for very young children.
  6. Let the peas cook for about 12-15 minutes, keeping the lid closed to promote stewing. Halfway through, give them a quick stir to check the liquid level and ensure it isn't drying out too fast. The peas must become very tender, enough to be easily squashed with the pressure of the tongue against the roof of the mouth. The aroma will become that of sweet, freshly picked legumes. My chef's tip: do not exceed 15 minutes of cooking time, otherwise the green colour will turn an uninviting grey-brown.
  7. Once the time is up, remove the lid and check the consistency of the cooking juices: the liquid should have reduced almost completely, leaving a sort of natural creaminess created by the leek and the starch from the peas. If there is still too much water, increase the heat slightly for a minute and let it evaporate without the lid. The peas should appear glossy and moist, not swimming in liquid but not dry either. The final result is a creamy and coating side dish. My chef's tip: always taste a pea to check that the skin has become thin and digestible.
  8. If the dish is intended for children aged 12 to 18 months, use the back of a spoon to lightly crush the peas directly in the pan. This step serves to break the circular skin, reducing any choking risk and making the legume easier to manage for those learning to chew. Let the dish rest for a few minutes before serving, so the flavours settle and the temperature becomes lukewarm. The consistency should be soft and slightly textured. My chef's tip: do not turn everything into a smooth purée, as leaving some texture helps the child's sensory development.

Tips

  • Slice the leek very thinly so it almost completely melts during cooking.
  • Keep the lid tightly closed to preserve the natural moisture of the vegetables.
  • If the liquid evaporates too quickly, add a tablespoon of warm water to finish cooking.

Variations

  • You can replace the leek with minced shallots for a slightly bolder flavor.
  • Add fresh mint leaves at the end of cooking for an aromatic and refreshing touch.
