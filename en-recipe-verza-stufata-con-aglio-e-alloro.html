Braised Savoy Cabbage with Garlic and Bay Leaves

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 35 min · Medium

Savoy cabbage is one of the cheapest winter vegetables and, if braised slowly, loses its bitter note and becomes very sweet. Bay leaves provide an ancient and comforting scent.

Ingredients

  • Savoy cabbage (Verza)800 g
  • Garlic2 cloves
  • Dried bay leaves2 leaves
  • Extra virgin olive oil20 ml
  • Red wine vinegar15 ml

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Begin by prepping the 800g of Savoy cabbage: remove the tougher, darker outer leaves, cut the head in half, and carve out the hard central core. Place the halves on a chopping board and slice into thin strips, about 5-8mm wide, aiming for a uniform cut to ensure even cooking. You should end up with a large pile of shredded cabbage ready for washing. My tip: don't discard too much of the heart; just slice it very thinly as it's packed with flavour and nutrients.
  2. Thoroughly wash the sliced cabbage in a large bowl of cold water, swishing it around with your hands to remove any grit or dirt. Drain using a colander, but don't dry it completely; the water clinging to the leaves is essential for creating the steam needed for the initial softening. Let it rest in the colander for just a minute before proceeding. The result should be clean, vibrant, and slightly damp cabbage. My tip: use very cold water to keep the fibre structure crisp before it hits the heat.
  3. Take a large, heavy-based saucepan, pour in the 20ml of extra virgin olive oil, and add the 2 garlic cloves (smashed but left whole) and the 2 dried bay leaves. Heat over a medium flame for about 2 minutes, tilting the pan slightly so the garlic and bay are submerged in the oil to release their essential oils. When the garlic is lightly golden and the aroma of the bay leaf is evident, your base is ready. My tip: crush the garlic with the flat of your knife blade to break the fibres and release the allicin without burning it.
  4. Start adding the cabbage to the pan a little at a time, using kitchen tongs or a wooden spoon to stir and coat it immediately in the flavoured oil. Since the initial volume is large, wait for the first handful to wilt slightly before adding the next, keeping the heat at a medium level. This process takes about 5-7 minutes until all the cabbage fits comfortably in the pot. You will notice the cabbage significantly reducing in volume and becoming glossy from the oil. My tip: take your time here, as gradual addition ensures every strip is properly seasoned.
  5. Season with salt and freshly ground black pepper, then pour the 15ml of red wine vinegar directly over the hot cabbage. Increase the heat slightly for 30 seconds to allow the sharp alcoholic part of the vinegar to evaporate, leaving behind a subtle acidity that balances the cabbage's natural sweetness. Stir well to distribute the flavours evenly throughout the mixture. The scent will change from pungent to a more rounded, mellow aroma. My tip: the vinegar also helps preserve a better colour and makes the cabbage fibres more digestible.
  6. Reduce the heat to the lowest setting, cover the pan with a tight-fitting lid, and let it stew gently for about 25 minutes. During this stage, the cabbage will cook in its own steam and the juices released by the salt. Stir every 10 minutes or so to ensure nothing sticks to the bottom, maintaining a constant, gentle heat. The cabbage will start to transition from bright green to a deeper, warmer hue. My tip: use a glass lid if possible so you can monitor the moisture without lifting it and losing heat.
  7. Check the texture and moisture levels about two-thirds of the way through: if the bottom of the pan looks too dry, add a couple of tablespoons of warm water. The cabbage should remain moist and juicy; it must not fry or brown too much, as this would turn the flavour bitter. Continue cooking until the cabbage is completely tender when tested with a fork. The ideal result is a melt-in-the-mouth consistency that still holds its shape. My tip: never add cold water, as it shocks the fibres and halts the cooking process.
  8. Once cooked, remove the garlic cloves and bay leaves to prevent anyone from accidentally eating them. Taste a small piece to check if more salt is needed, then let the side dish rest with the heat off for 2 minutes before serving. The cabbage should be a beautiful dark olive green, glossy, and intensely fragrant. Serve it in a warm dish as the perfect accompaniment to pulses or savoury meat dishes. My tip: if you prepare this a few hours in advance and gently reheat it, the flavours will be even more harmonious and deep.
