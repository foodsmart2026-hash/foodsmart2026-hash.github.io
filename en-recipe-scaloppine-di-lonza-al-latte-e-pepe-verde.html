Pork Loin Scallopini with Milk and Green Peppercorns

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 12 min · Easy

Pork loin is one of the most affordable and versatile cuts, which in this recipe becomes incredibly creamy thanks to the milk. The green peppercorns provide a spicy yet delicate aroma, making the dish refined at a low cost.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🇮🇹 Italian

A creamy and elegant main course where tender pork loin meets the aromatic notes of green peppercorns. Cooking the meat in milk creates a velvety sauce that keeps every slice incredibly moist.

Ingredients

  • Sliced pork loin500 g
  • Whole milk200 ml
  • Plain flour (Farina tipo 0)40 g
  • Butter20 g
  • Green peppercorns1 cucchiaio
  • Fine saltq.b.

Allergens

Gluten, Milk

Method

  1. Place the 500g of pork loin slices between two sheets of baking parchment and gently flatten them with a meat mallet to a thickness of about 5mm. This process breaks down the muscle fibres, making the meat exceptionally tender and ensuring a quick, even cook in the pan. Work from the centre outwards with firm but gentle strokes to avoid tearing the delicate meat. You will achieve thin, uniformly sized escalopes perfectly prepared for searing. Chef's Tip: Do not skip this step, as pork loin is a lean cut that can easily become chewy if left too thick.
  2. Take 1 tablespoon of green peppercorns and crush them coarsely using a mortar and pestle or the flat side of a heavy chef's knife on a chopping board. This technique allows the essential oils and the fresh, spicy aroma of the pepper to be fully released into the sauce during cooking. Do not grind them into a fine powder; the grains should remain visible to provide a pleasant aromatic note and a slight crunch to the finished dish. The result will be a balanced, intense aroma that complements the natural sweetness of the milk. Chef's Tip: If using green peppercorns in brine, rinse them thoroughly under cold water before crushing to remove excess vinegar sharpness.
  3. Spread 40g of plain flour on a large flat plate and coat each slice of meat, ensuring both sides are completely covered. Press down lightly with your palm to help the flour adhere to the fibres, then shake each slice vigorously between your fingers to remove any excess flour. This thin coating is essential for creating a golden crust and for thickening the milk into a velvety sauce later on. The meat should look slightly matt, without any white clumps that could cause lumps in the gravy. Chef's Tip: Only flour the meat immediately before placing it in the pan to prevent natural moisture from making the coating sticky.
  4. Place 20g of butter in a very large non-stick frying pan over medium heat, allowing it to melt until it just begins to foam. Swirl the pan to distribute the fat evenly across the base, making sure the butter does not burn or turn brown. The butter should be hot and translucent to ensure a clean, aromatic sear without any bitter aftertaste. You will be left with a fragrant cooking base ready for your escalopes. Chef's Tip: If your pan is too small to fit all the meat comfortably, cook in two batches using half the butter for each to avoid dropping the pan temperature too low.
  5. Lay the floured escalopes into the hot pan, being careful not to overlap them so the heat can reach every part of the meat consistently. Increase the heat slightly and sear for about 2 minutes per side until a thin, appetising golden crust forms on the surface. Use kitchen tongs to turn the slices without piercing them, which prevents the internal juices from escaping and keeps the meat moist. The result should be an even, light hazelnut colour on both sides. Chef's Tip: Do not move the meat constantly once it hits the pan; let it sizzle undisturbed for the first minute to encourage the Maillard reaction for better flavour.
  6. Lower the heat to medium-low and gradually pour 200ml of whole milk into the pan, adding the previously crushed green peppercorns at the same time. It is best if the milk is at room temperature or slightly warm to avoid a thermal shock that could instantly toughen the pork fibres. Gently swirl the pan in a circular motion so the milk combines with the butter and the flour residues left on the bottom. You will notice the liquid beginning to cloud slightly as it starts to emulsify with the other ingredients. Chef's Tip: Warm the milk in the microwave for 20 seconds before adding it to keep the cooking process steady and speed up the thickening.
  7. Allow everything to simmer gently for about 5-6 minutes, turning the meat slices halfway through to ensure they absorb the flavours evenly. During this stage, the starch from the flour will react with the fats in the milk and butter, creating a silky, thick cream that completely coats the pork. The sauce should reduce until it reaches the consistency of a light béchamel, without separating or drying out too much. You will have glossy, succulent escalopes bathed in a fragrant and very creamy dressing. Chef's Tip: If the sauce thickens too quickly and becomes sticky, simply add a tablespoon of extra milk to restore the perfect creamy texture.
  8. Turn off the heat and add a pinch of fine salt only at this final stage, distributing it evenly to enhance the flavours without affecting the meat's texture. Let the escalopes rest in the pan for about 30 seconds to allow the internal juices to redistribute and the sauce to set perfectly. Serve immediately, plating the slices and napping them generously with the creamy green peppercorn sauce from the pan. The dish should look elegant, with meat so tender it can almost be cut with a fork. Chef's Tip: Adding salt only at the end is a professional secret to prevent the meat from releasing moisture during cooking, which would make it dry and tough.

Tips

  • Pound the meat between parchment paper to even out the thickness for consistent cooking results.
  • Flour the slices just before searing to prevent the coating from becoming soggy or damp.
  • Crush the green peppercorns fresh to release their full aromatic potential into the sauce.

Variations

  • Use black peppercorns instead of green for a sharper, more pungent and spicy heat.
  • Replace the butter with extra virgin olive oil for a lighter take on the creamy sauce.
