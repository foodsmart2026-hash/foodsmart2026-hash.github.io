Apple and Sunflower Seed Buckwheat Porridge

4 servings · Prep 5 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

A warm and nutritious breakfast that reimagines porridge using whole buckwheat, which is naturally gluten-free. The sweetness of the apples and the crunch of the toasted seeds make it perfect for the whole family.

A warm and cozy breakfast, ideal for winter mornings. This buckwheat cream offers a velvety texture, enriched by the crunch of sunflower seeds and the aroma of cinnamon. Rice milk provides a delicate, natural sweetness.

Ingredients

  • Hulled buckwheat240 g
  • Rice milk (gluten and soy-free)850 ml
  • Golden Delicious apples2 pcs
  • Sunflower seeds40 g
  • Maple syrup40 g
  • Ground cinnamon5 g
  • Organic lemon zest1 pcs
  • Fine salt1 g

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Begin by thoroughly rinsing 240g of hulled buckwheat under cold running water using a fine-mesh sieve for about one minute. Agitate the grains with your hands until the water runs perfectly clear, a crucial step to remove surface saponins responsible for a bitter aftertaste. Once cleaned, drain the cereal well and set it aside for a moment before cooking. Chef's tip: never skip this step, as deep cleaning ensures the aromatic delicacy of the final dish.
  2. Take a heavy-bottomed non-stick pan and pour in 40g of sunflower seeds without adding any fats. Toast them over medium heat for about 3 minutes, tossing the pan constantly to prevent burning, until you see an even light golden colour. You will notice an intense nutty fragrance indicating the release of essential oils, making the seeds crunchier and easier to digest. Chef's tip: the seeds continue to cook from residual heat, so remove them from the pan as soon as they reach an amber hue.
  3. Pour 850ml of rice milk into a large pot along with 1g of fine salt, bringing the liquid almost to a boil over high heat for about 3-4 minutes. Watch the edges of the pot: when the first persistent small bubbles form, the milk is at the ideal temperature to receive the grain. This warm base will allow the buckwheat starch to activate immediately without thermal shock. Chef's tip: keep a close eye on the rice milk as it tends to foam up and overflow quickly once it reaches boiling point.
  4. Add the previously rinsed buckwheat to the hot milk, lower the heat to minimum, and cover the pot with a tight-fitting lid. Let it simmer gently for about 8 minutes, allowing the grains to gradually absorb the liquid and start swelling evenly. During this stage, there is no need to stir, letting the steam complete the internal cooking of the grain. Chef's tip: using the lid is essential to maintain constant hydration and prevent the rice milk from evaporating too much.
  5. While the buckwheat continues its slow cooking, take a moment to prepare the 2 Golden apples: peel them carefully and cut them into regular cubes about 1cm in size. Precision in cutting is important to ensure all pieces cook at the same time, maintaining a melting yet not mushy texture. Keep the apples ready on a chopping board for immediate addition to the pot. Chef's tip: cut the apples only at the last minute to prevent oxidation from browning the flesh, keeping a bright golden colour.
  6. Lift the lid and add the apple cubes, 5g of ground cinnamon, and the grated zest of one organic lemon to the preparation. Stir carefully to distribute the aromas and continue cooking for another 5 minutes, this time without the lid, maintaining a medium-low flame. You will notice the spicy scent spreading intensely and the liquid starting to reduce, thickening around the apples. Chef's tip: grate only the yellow part of the lemon zest, as the white pith would impart an unpleasantly bitter note.
  7. In the last 2 minutes of cooking, use a wooden spoon to stir the cream vigorously and continuously with circular movements. This mechanical technique serves to partially break the outside of the grains and release natural starches, which will act as a binder creating a velvety and coating texture. The visual result should be a thick cream where the grains are still distinguishable but well bound together. Chef's tip: the energy applied while stirring determines the final creaminess, just like in a risotto.
  8. Turn off the heat and fold 40g of maple syrup and the previously toasted sunflower seeds into the cream, stirring one last time to unify the flavour. Cover the pot again and let the preparation rest for about 2 minutes away from direct heat. During this rest, the density will stabilise, reaching the ideal consistency of a warm, soft pudding. Chef's tip: do not add the maple syrup during boiling to preserve its delicate woody aromatic notes intact.
  9. Divide the buckwheat cream into four individual bowls and serve while still hot, checking that the consistency is fluid yet full-bodied. To the bite, the buckwheat should be tender and pleasant, with the apples melting in the mouth contrasted by the crunchiness of the sunflower seeds. You can finish with an extra dusting of cinnamon if you desire an even more persistent aroma. Chef's tip: if the cream appears too thick after resting, add a tablespoon of warm rice milk to bring it back to the desired fluidity.

Tips

  • Toast the sunflower seeds over low heat in a thick-bottomed pan to prevent burning.
  • Rinse the buckwheat repeatedly under running water until the water runs clear.
  • Add the lemon zest only at the end of cooking to preserve its citrusy aroma.

Variations

  • Replace Golden apples with ripe pears for a more intense flavor and a melt-in-the-mouth texture.
  • Use pumpkin seeds instead of sunflower seeds for a different color and a toastier taste.
