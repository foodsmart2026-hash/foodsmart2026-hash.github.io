Rustic Onion and Olive Tart

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 45 min · Medium

Inspired by the French pissaladière but in a vegetarian version, this tart relies on the sweetness of slow-cooked onions.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⬇️ Big saving🌱 Vegan🧒 Kid-friendly

A rustic savory tart perfect for buffets or casual dinners. The sweetness of caramelized onions pairs beautifully with savory black olives, all encased in a golden, crispy puff pastry crust.

Ingredients

  • rectangular puff pastry1 rotolo
  • yellow onions800 g
  • pitted black olives60 g
  • cream cheese100 g
  • brown sugar1 cucchiaino
  • dried thymeq.b.
  • extra virgin olive oil30 ml

Allergens

Gluten, Milk

Method

  1. Begin by prepping 800 g of golden onions, removing the papery outer skin and slicing them very thinly. Use a sharp chef's knife or a mandoline to ensure uniform slices, which are essential for even cooking. Transfer the onions to a wide, deep frying pan along with 30 ml of extra virgin olive oil, a pinch of salt, and 1 teaspoon of brown sugar. The initial volume will be substantial, but don't worry as it will shrink significantly. Chef's tip: slice the onions along the grain (from pole to root) so they maintain some structure without turning into mush.
  2. Turn the heat to the lowest setting, cover the pan with a lid, and let the onions stew for at least 20-25 minutes. Stir frequently with a wooden spoon to prevent sticking and ensure they don't brown too quickly. Towards the end, remove the lid to let any residual moisture evaporate until you achieve a melting, jam-like consistency with a deep amber hue. The result should be a glossy compote free of excess liquid. Chef's tip: if the onions dry out too much before becoming tender, add a tablespoon of warm water to continue cooking without burning them.
  3. While the onions cool slightly, prepare the base by unrolling the rectangular puff pastry sheet directly onto a baking tray, keeping it on its parchment paper. Prick the entire surface of the pastry evenly with the tines of a fork, being careful not to pierce it all the way through. This prevents the pastry from puffing up excessively in the middle during baking, ensuring a flat base for the toppings. You should see regular small holes across the surface. Chef's tip: work with the puff pastry while it's still cold from the fridge to keep the fat firm for the best flaky results.
  4. Take 100 g of cream cheese and spread it using a spatula or the back of a spoon over the pastry base. Make sure to leave a 2 cm border clear on all four sides, which will form the crust of the tart. Spread the cheese in a thin, even layer to act as a protective barrier for the pastry. The result will be a creamy bed ready for the onion filling. Chef's tip: if the cheese is too firm, whip it for a moment in a small bowl with a drop of olive oil to make it smoother and easier to spread.
  5. Distribute the now lukewarm onion compote over the layer of cream cheese, leveling it carefully with a spatula. Always maintain that 2 cm border clean and free of toppings to allow the pastry to rise. The onions should completely cover the cream cheese in a rich, flavorful layer. The appearance should be like an inviting golden carpet. Chef's tip: never add boiling hot onions to the raw pastry, as the heat will melt the fat in the dough, compromising the final crispness.
  6. Take 60 g of pitted black olives, cut them in half lengthways, and arrange them harmoniously over the bed of onions. Sprinkle the entire surface with plenty of dried thyme, scattering it from a height for even coverage. The aroma of the thyme will perfectly complement the sweetness of the onions and the saltiness of the olives. You should see a beautiful visual contrast between the amber onions and the glossy black olives. Chef's tip: lightly press the olives into the onions with your fingers to prevent them from drying out or shifting during baking.
  7. Carefully fold the clean pastry edges inwards, slightly overlapping the filling to create a rustic frame. Lightly press the corners to seal them and prevent them from opening during baking. This frame will hold the filling in place and become the crispiest, most decorative part of the dish. The result will be a tart with a defined, artisanal look. Chef's tip: for an extra golden and professional finish, brush the pastry frame with a little milk or a beaten egg yolk before putting it in the oven.
  8. Place the tray in a preheated conventional oven at 200°C and bake for about 20-25 minutes on the middle rack. Monitor the progress: the pastry should puff up visibly and turn a deep golden-brown, indicating perfect caramelisation. The kitchen will be filled with the scent of toasted bread, sweet onions, and herbs. If the edges seem to be darkening too fast, slightly lower the temperature for the last 5 minutes. Chef's tip: place the tray in the lower part of the oven for the first 10 minutes to ensure the base cooks through and doesn't stay soggy.
  9. Once out of the oven, let the tart rest on the tray for at least 10 minutes before slicing. This resting time is crucial for the onion juices and the cream cheese to stabilise, preventing the filling from running out. Cut the tart into 4 generous portions using a serrated knife to avoid crushing the crispy pastry. Serve the tart warm to best enjoy the contrast between the flaky base and the soft heart. Chef's tip: serve this tart with a small fresh rocket salad to balance the sweetness of the onions with a peppery, refreshing note.

Tips

  • Cook the onions on very low heat to achieve a melt-in-your-mouth texture without burning.
  • Prick the bottom of the puff pastry with a fork before spreading the cream cheese.
  • Let the tart cool slightly before slicing to ensure neat and firm portions.

Variations

  • Replace black olives with Taggiasca olives for a more delicate flavor.
  • Add chopped walnut kernels over the onions for an extra crunchy texture.
