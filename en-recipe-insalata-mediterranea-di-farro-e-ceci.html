Mediterranean Spelt and Chickpea Salad

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 20 min · Medium

A balanced one-dish meal that combines spelt fibre with plant-based chickpea protein, enriched with crisp fresh vegetables. Ideal for a quick and affordable meal, perfect for preparing in advance for the whole family.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🌱 Vegan🧒 Kid-friendly🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free

A fresh and complete single-course meal, ideal for outdoor summer lunches or office breaks. The saltiness of the olives combines with the crispness of cucumber and a bright citrus note from the lemon.

Ingredients

  • Pearl spelt280 g
  • Precooked chickpeas (drained weight)480 g
  • Cherry tomatoes250 g
  • Cucumber1 pz
  • Tropea red onion (a sweet red onion variety from Calabria)60 g
  • Pitted black olives40 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 ml
  • Lemon (juice and zest)1 pz
  • Fresh parsley20 g
  • Fine salt5 g

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Begin by thoroughly rinsing 280g of pearled farro under cold running water using a fine-mesh sieve. Agitate the grains with your hands until the water runs perfectly clear, removing any dust or surface starch. This preliminary step takes only a couple of minutes but is essential to ensure the grains remain distinct and separate after cooking. The final result will be a clean cereal ready for uniform boiling. Chef's tip: Never skip this step, as excess starch would make your salad sticky and unappealing.
  2. Bring a large pot with approximately 2 litres of water to a boil and add 5g of fine salt only once it reaches a vigorous boil. Pour in the farro and let it cook over medium heat for about 20 minutes, maintaining a steady but not overly turbulent simmer. Taste the grain towards the end of the time: the texture should be tender on the outside but with a heart that is still perceptible and pleasantly 'al dente'. Chef's tip: For superior digestibility, you can add a bay leaf to the cooking water to delicately scent the grains.
  3. While the farro is cooking, focus on the Tropea red onion: slice it very finely using a sharp knife or a mandoline to obtain almost transparent slivers. Submerge the slices in a small bowl with cold water and let them rest for about 10-15 minutes. This treatment reduces the bulb's pungency and makes it incredibly crunchy and digestible while maintaining its vivid colour. Chef's tip: Add an ice cube to the soaking water to maximise the onion's crispness.
  4. Drain the 480g of pre-cooked chickpeas from their canning liquid and rinse them thoroughly under fresh running water. Shake the sieve vigorously to remove all excess water and the foamy residue typical of canned legumes. The chickpeas should appear shiny, intact, and free of that slippery coating that would alter the final flavour. Chef's tip: If you have time, gently removing the outer skins of the chickpeas will make the salad even more refined and velvety on the palate.
  5. Prepare the vegetable base by washing 250g of cherry tomatoes and cutting them into four equal parts. Peel the cucumber with a vegetable peeler, cut it in half lengthways to remove the central seeds with a teaspoon, and dice it into small, uniform cubes of about half a centimetre. You will achieve a mix of different colours and textures that will provide freshness and structure to the dish. Chef's tip: If the cucumber is organic and the skin is thin, you can leave some of it on for a more intense dark green chromatic note.
  6. Once the farro has finished cooking, drain it and immediately pass it under a stream of cold water for about 30 seconds. This 'thermal shock' technique instantly stops any residual cooking and sets the starch, ensuring the grains do not stick together while resting. Leave it to drain very well in the sieve, tossing it occasionally so it is perfectly dry before seasoning. Chef's tip: Pat the farro with a clean kitchen towel if you notice it is still holding too much moisture, to avoid watering down the emulsion.
  7. Create the Mediterranean emulsion in a small cup by combining 40ml of extra virgin olive oil, the strained juice of one lemon, and its finely grated zest. Whisk vigorously with a fork or a small whisk until you obtain an opalescent, slightly thick, and highly fragrant liquid. This dressing will bind all the ingredients together, providing an acidic note that balances the sweetness of the chickpeas and farro. Chef's tip: Grate only the yellow part of the lemon zest, as the white pith would impart an unpleasant bitter aftertaste.
  8. In a large bowl, combine the well-dried farro, chickpeas, tomatoes, cucumber, sliced black olives, and the drained, patted-dry onion. Gently mix all the ingredients using two spoons, using upward folding motions so as not to crush the vegetables and legumes. The result should be an explosion of Mediterranean colours evenly distributed throughout the bowl. Chef's tip: Use a previously chilled ceramic or glass bowl to maintain the freshness of the ingredients during preparation.
  9. Complete the dish by finely chopping 20g of fresh parsley and sprinkling it over the salad along with the previously prepared oil and lemon emulsion. Mix once more with care so that every grain and piece of vegetable is glossy and well-coated with the citrus dressing. Allow the salad to rest at room temperature or in the refrigerator for at least 15 minutes before serving, so the flavours can meld harmoniously. Chef's tip: Never add the parsley too far in advance if preparing the dish a day early; add it only at the last minute to preserve its bright green colour and pungent aroma.

Tips

  • Rinse the spelt under cold water immediately after cooking to maintain its texture and prevent overcooking.
  • Let the salad rest in the fridge for 30 minutes before serving to allow the flavors to meld.
  • Add the lemon juice just before serving to keep the vegetable colors vibrant.

Variations

  • Add cubed feta cheese for a creamy and savory touch to the mix.
  • Replace the parsley with fresh mint for an even more refreshing result.
