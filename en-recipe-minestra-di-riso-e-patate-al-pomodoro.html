Rice and Potato Soup with Tomato

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 35 min · Medium

The quintessential comfort food, budget-friendly and filling, where the starch from the potato and rice melt into a creamy embrace. A traditional home-cooked one-pot dish that warms up cold days.

A classic of Italian home cooking, perfect for warming up on colder days. This thick and comforting soup combines the softness of potatoes with the typical texture of soup rice.

Ingredients

  • Originario rice (short-grain rice for soups)280 g
  • Yellow-fleshed potatoes400 g
  • Tomato passata (smooth sieved tomatoes)150 ml
  • Celery1 stalk
  • White onion1 pcs
  • Parmesan rind (optional, if available)1 pcs

Allergens

Celery

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the white onion and celery stalk, washing them thoroughly under cold running water. Finely mince them by hand using a sharp chef's knife to achieve a uniform mixture with pieces about 2-3 millimetres thick, ensuring they almost melt away during cooking. The result should be a fragrant base ready for sautéing. A professional tip: do not blend the vegetables in a food processor, as the heat from the blades can alter their original flavour and make them slightly bitter.
  2. Take the 400g of yellow-fleshed potatoes, peel them with a vegetable peeler, and cut them into cubes roughly 1 centimetre in size. Aim for consistent sizing to ensure every piece cooks at the same rate, avoiding a mix of raw and overcooked bits. Briefly rinse the cubes in cold water to remove excess surface starch, which could otherwise scorch during the browning stage. You will end up with clean, shiny cubes ready for the pot.
  3. Pour one tablespoon of extra virgin olive oil into a high-sided pot and add the minced onion and celery. Allow the vegetables to soften over a very low heat for about 5 minutes, stirring frequently with a wooden spoon until they become translucent and tender without browning. If the vegetables appear to be drying out too quickly, add a tablespoon of hot water to encourage gentle steaming. The base should perfume the oil without overpowering the delicate taste of the rice.
  4. Add the potato cubes to the sautéed vegetables and slightly increase the heat to a medium level. Sauté the potatoes for about 3 minutes, tossing them continuously so they are evenly coated and flavoured by the base. You will notice the edges of the potatoes becoming slightly transparent and starting to seal, a crucial step to help them hold their shape during boiling. Take care not to let them stick to the bottom of the pot by moving them gently.
  5. Pour 150ml of tomato passata into the pot and add approximately 1.5 litres of hot water (or light vegetable stock for more depth). If you have a Parmesan rind available, scrape the outer skin clean and add it whole to provide an extraordinary savoury depth. Bring the liquid to a boil over medium-high heat until you see consistent bubbles rising to the surface. The broth will take on an orange-red hue and release an inviting aroma.
  6. Once boiling, lower the heat and let the potatoes simmer on their own for about 10 minutes, partially covered with a lid. During this stage, the potatoes will begin to soften but remain firm at the core to withstand the subsequent cooking time of the rice. The liquid will reduce slightly, concentrating the natural sugars of the tomato and the aromas of the celery. The potatoes should be parboiled; do not add salt yet to ensure the potatoes don't remain too firm.
  7. Add 280g of 'Originario' or soup rice directly into the pot and stir immediately to distribute the grains evenly. Cook for 15-18 minutes over a gentle heat, stirring every 2-3 minutes to prevent the rice from settling and sticking. The rice will gradually release its starch, naturally thickening the liquid into a dense and velvety soup. If the mixture becomes too dry, add a ladle of boiling water at a time to reach your desired consistency.
  8. When the rice is cooked, turn off the heat and use the back of your spoon to crush 4 or 5 potato cubes against the inside wall of the pot. Stir vigorously for one minute; the mashed potato pulp will create an exceptional creaminess without the need for extra fats. Season with salt to taste and let the soup rest for two minutes with the lid on before serving. The final result should be a thick, glossy, and deeply comforting soup, perfect with a final drizzle of raw olive oil.

Tips

  • Cut the potatoes into uniform cubes to ensure even cooking throughout the pot.
  • If using Parmesan rind, scrape it well with a small knife before adding it to the soup.
  • Gradually add hot water during cooking if you prefer a thinner consistency.

Variations

  • Add smoked pancetta cubes to the initial sauté for a bolder flavor.
  • Replace the rice with small soup pasta such as ditalini rigati.
