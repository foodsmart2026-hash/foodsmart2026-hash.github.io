Lemon chicken breast

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 20 min · Medium

A refined homemade version of the classic Mediterranean main course, where the meat remains very tender thanks to a delicate citrus emulsion. It is an economical, quick, and balanced dish, perfect for a healthy family dinner without sacrificing taste.

Lemon chicken breast is a quick and elegant main course, characterized by a velvety sauce and a citrusy scent. The light coating keeps the meat juicy, while the zucchinis add a fresh touch to the dish.

Ingredients

  • Whole chicken breast or fillets600 g
  • Unwaxed lemons (juice and zest)2 pz
  • Medium seasonal courgettes600 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 g
  • Plain flour (Type 00)40 g
  • Vegetable stock or warm water150 ml
  • Fresh parsley10 g
  • Fine salt4 g
  • Black pepper1 g
  • Garlic1 clove

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Cut 600g of chicken breast into thin, uniform slices, removing any visible cartilage or fat. Place the slices between two sheets of baking paper and lightly pound them with a meat mallet to even out the thickness to about half a centimetre. This step ensures all pieces cook simultaneously, preventing thinner parts from becoming too dry during browning. You will get flat and regular escalopes, ideal for quick and even cooking. Chef tip: avoid hitting too hard so as not to break the muscle fibres, keeping the meat juicy.
  2. On a flat plate, pour 40g of sifted plain flour and add a grind of black pepper and a pinch of salt. Pass each chicken slice through the flour, pressing gently to make it adhere perfectly on both sides, then shake well to remove excess flour. Do this only a few moments before putting the meat in the pan, otherwise the moisture from the chicken will make the coating gummy. The final result should be a thin and homogeneous coating, almost invisible on the surface of the meat. Chef tip: for an even lighter version, you can use cornflour instead of plain flour.
  3. Heat 20g of extra virgin olive oil in a large non-stick pan, maintaining a medium heat until the oil becomes fluid and shimmering. Place the chicken slices in without crowding them, cooking for 2-3 minutes per side until a light and inviting golden crust forms. The meat should not cook through completely at this stage, as it will finish cooking later in the lemon sauce. Once browned, transfer the slices to a plate and cover them with foil to keep them warm and juicy. Chef tip: never pierce the meat with a fork to turn it; use kitchen tongs to preserve the internal juices.
  4. While the chicken rests, grate the yellow zest of 2 unwaxed lemons, avoiding the bitter white pith, and squeeze the juice until you obtain about 80-100 ml. Strain the juice through a fine-mesh sieve to remove any seeds or pulp residue that might disturb the smooth consistency of the final sauce. The scent of fresh citrus will be the main aromatic base of the dish, providing an indispensable note of freshness to balance the protein. You will get a clear and very fragrant liquid, ready for the deglazing phase of the pan. Chef tip: before squeezing the lemons, roll them on the worktop pressing with your palm to break the internal vesicles and get more juice.
  5. Pour the strained lemon juice into the hot pan used for the meat and add 150ml of vegetable stock or hot water, scraping the bottom with a wooden spoon. Maintain a medium heat and let the liquid collect all the caramelised flavours stuck to the bottom of the pan, bringing it to a light boil. Let the liquid base reduce for about 3 minutes, until you see it start to thicken slightly and the volume reduces significantly. The result will be a tasty cooking base, slightly amber and intensely fragrant. Chef tip: if you like a more aromatic taste, you can add a whole sprig of rosemary during the reduction and then remove it.
  6. Return the chicken slices to the pan along with any juices they have released on the plate, also adding the remaining 10g of extra virgin olive oil and the grated zest. Continue cooking over medium-low heat for another 2 minutes, moving the pan often with a circular motion to allow the flour from the chicken to emulsify with the liquids. The sauce should transform into a velvety, glossy, and thick cream that coats each slice like a protective veil. The meat will finish cooking while remaining extremely tender thanks to the moist and acidic environment created by the lemon. Chef tip: if the cream becomes too thick, thin it with just one tablespoon of hot water.
  7. Wash 600g of fresh courgettes, trim them and cut them into regular rounds about 3 millimetres thick to ensure quick cooking. In a separate pan with a drizzle of oil and a whole garlic clove (skin on), sauté the courgettes over high heat for 6-8 minutes, until they are tender but still crunchy. Salt lightly only at the end of cooking to prevent them from releasing too much water and losing their ideal consistency by becoming soft. The courgettes should retain a bright green colour and show a few golden spots from browning. Chef tip: add a few fresh mint leaves to the courgettes with the heat off to enhance their sweetness.
  8. Turn off the heat for the chicken and add 10g of finely chopped fresh parsley, distributing it evenly over the entire surface. Serve the hot chicken slices immediately on flat plates, generousy nappéed with their lemon sauce and with the sautéed courgettes arranged on the side. The presentation will be clean and elegant, with the colour contrast between the yellow of the sauce, the white of the meat, and the bright green of the vegetables. The intense scent of lemon will stimulate the appetite, making the meal light yet satisfying. Chef tip: accompany the dish with a small portion of basmati rice or wholemeal bread to complete the meal with the necessary carbohydrates.

Tips

  • Flour the chicken only an instant before placing it in the pan to prevent the flour from becoming damp.
  • Use untreated lemons to enjoy the zest without any bitter aftertaste.
  • If the sauce thickens too much, add a tablespoon of vegetable broth to restore the right consistency.

Variations

  • Add a few sage leaves during browning for a more herbal and fragrant aroma.
  • For a crunchy touch, sprinkle the finished dish with toasted pine nuts or flaked almonds.
