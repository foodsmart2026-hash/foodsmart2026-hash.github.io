Turkey Stew with Apples and Rosemary

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 30 min · Medium

A light and budget-friendly main course that uses fruit to create a creamy sauce without cream. The apple melts during cooking, creating a delicious sweet and sour contrast.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⚖️ Good value for money🧒 Kid-friendly🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free

An original main course balancing delicate turkey with the sweet touch of apples. Rosemary provides a lasting Mediterranean aroma, while white wine creates a flavorful sauce. The meat remains tender and juicy throughout.

Ingredients

  • Diced turkey breast600 g
  • Apples (Golden Delicious or Reinette types)2 pcs
  • White onion1 pcs
  • Plain flour (Type 00)30 g
  • Dry white wine50 ml
  • Vegetable stock200 ml
  • Extra virgin olive oil20 ml

Allergens

Gluten, Sulphites

Method

  1. Begin by preparing 600g of turkey breast cut into regular 2cm cubes and coat them evenly in 30g of plain flour. Use a sieve or shake the cubes in a colander to thoroughly remove any excess flour, preventing heavy lumps from forming during browning. The aim is to achieve a very thin, transparent veil of flour that protects the meat and helps bind the cooking juices. The chef's tip: do not flour the meat too far in advance, as the turkey's natural moisture will make the flour tacky rather than velvety.
  2. In a large non-stick frying pan, heat 20ml of extra virgin olive oil over a high heat for about a minute. Arrange the turkey cubes without overlapping and let them brown for 5 minutes, turning only when an inviting golden crust has formed. This technique, known as the Maillard reaction, seals the juices inside, keeping the meat tender. The chef's tip: if the pan is too small, brown the meat in two batches to prevent the temperature from dropping and the turkey from boiling in its own liquid.
  3. Once golden, transfer the turkey to a plate and, in the same hot pan, add the finely chopped white onion with a tablespoon of vegetable broth. Let the onion soften over a low heat for about 4-5 minutes, stirring with a wooden spoon to scrape the caramelised meat residues off the bottom. The onion should become translucent and soft, releasing a sweet rather than pungent aroma. The chef's tip: add a pinch of salt to the onion to help it release water and cook evenly without burning.
  4. While the onion is softening, peel the 2 apples, remove the cores, and cut them into small dice of the same size as the meat for even cooking. Add them to the onion in the pan and continue cooking over a medium heat for about 3 minutes, tossing occasionally. The apples should soften slightly on the outside but maintain their internal structure. The chef's tip: use Renetta or Golden apples as they maintain a perfect balance between sweetness and acidity, essential for contrasting the delicacy of the turkey.
  5. Return the turkey to the pan with the apples and onion, increase the heat, and deglaze with 50ml of dry white wine. Allow the alcohol to evaporate completely for about 2 minutes until you no longer smell the sharp scent of wine, but only its fruity aroma. This step serves to cleanse the palate and give a fresh note to the dish. The chef's tip: use a good quality wine, preferably the same one you will serve at the table, to ensure an impeccable harmony of flavours.
  6. Lower the heat to medium-low and pour in 200ml of hot vegetable broth and a whole sprig of fresh rosemary. Cover the pan with a lid and simmer slowly for 15-20 minutes, allowing the flavours to meld and the meat to tenderise further. Check occasionally that the liquid hasn't dried out too much; if necessary, add another splash of broth. The chef's tip: keep the rosemary sprig whole so you can easily remove it at the end of cooking, preventing the needles from getting stuck in your guests' teeth.
  7. A few minutes before the end, remove the lid and gently mash about a third of the apple cubes directly in the pan with a fork. Stir well to mix the apple pulp with the cooking juices, creating a thick and fragrant cream that coats the stew. The result should be a glossy, coating sauce with a beautiful light amber colour. The chef's tip: if the sauce is too thin, increase the heat for the last two minutes without the lid to reduce it quickly.
  8. Turn off the heat, remove the rosemary sprig, and season with salt and freshly ground black pepper to taste. Let the stew rest for two minutes before serving, allowing the meat fibres to relax and better absorb the sauce. Serve the stew piping hot, garnished with a sprig of fresh rosemary and, if desired, accompany with pilau rice or mashed potatoes. The chef's tip: a drizzle of raw olive oil at the end will enhance all the fruity aromas of the apple and rosemary.

Tips

  • Coat the turkey cubes well in flour to achieve a creamy and well-bound cooking sauce.
  • Choose firm-fleshed apples that hold their shape without falling apart while cooking in the pan.
  • Adjust the amount of broth during cooking to keep the stew moist and tender.

Variations

  • Replace the rosemary with fresh sage leaves for a more earthy flavor profile.
  • Add a pinch of cinnamon to the apples to evoke Central European culinary traditions.
