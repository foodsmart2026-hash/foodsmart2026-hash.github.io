Pistachio-crusted squash medallions with a balsamic reduction

🌿 Seasonal recipe

Sicilia · 4 servings · Prep 25 min · Cooking 20 min · Medium

Slices of firm squash, breaded and pan-fried until golden, served with an elegant sweet and sour touch.

Ingredients

  • Delica squash (cut into 1.5cm thick round slices)8 slices
  • Chopped pistachios100g
  • Breadcrumbs50g
  • Medium eggs2
  • Plain flouras required
  • Balsamic vinegar of Modena100ml
  • Acacia honey1 tsp
  • Groundnut oilfor frying
  • Salt and pepperto taste

Allergens

Tree nuts, Gluten, Eggs

Method

  1. Start by preparing the reduction by pouring 100ml of balsamic vinegar and 1 teaspoon of honey into a thick-bottomed stainless steel saucepan. Cook over a very low heat, stirring occasionally with a wooden spoon, until the liquid has reduced by half its original volume. You should achieve a syrupy, thick consistency that 'coats' the back of the spoon, releasing an intense sweet-and-sour aroma and a glossy dark brown colour. My advice: never turn up the heat, otherwise the sugar in the honey and vinegar will burn, making the sauce irreparably bitter.
  2. Take the Delica pumpkin and, after washing it thoroughly, cut 8 circular slices exactly 1.5cm thick, keeping the skin on as it is thin and delicious in this variety. Remove the seeds and internal fibres using a spoon, trying to keep the medallion shape as intact and regular as possible. This precision in cutting ensures uniform cooking of the pumpkin heart without risking burning the outer breading. My advice: use a sharp chef's knife and make firm, parallel movements to prevent the slices from being lopsided.
  3. Set up your breading station by arranging three large bowls in a row on your workspace. In the first, put the plain flour; in the second, vigorously whisk 2 medium eggs with a generous pinch of salt and pepper; and in the third, combine 100g of pistachio kernels with 50g of breadcrumbs. Mix the pistachio and breadcrumb blend well so that the breading is homogeneous and visually appealing due to the contrast between green and golden tones. My advice: always keep one hand for dry ingredients and the other for wet ones to avoid creating 'club fingers' with clumps of batter.
  4. Take each pumpkin slice and carefully dredge it in the plain flour, ensuring every millimetre of the surface is covered, then gently shake it between your hands to remove any excess. Immediately dip the floured medallion into the beaten egg, turning it several times so the flour absorbs the liquid, creating a natural and uniform 'glue'. This step is vital to ensure the pistachio crumbs do not detach during frying. My advice: make sure the pumpkin is perfectly dry before you start flouring, so the flour doesn't turn into a paste.
  5. Transfer the pumpkin into the mix of 100g pistachio kernels and 50g breadcrumbs, pressing firmly with the palm of your hand to make the coating adhere well to both sides and edges. You should obtain a thick, rich, and even crust that completely hides the orange pumpkin flesh underneath. The crumbs must be compactly distributed to guarantee extraordinary crunchiness and a toasted flavour in every bite. My advice: compact the circular edges of the medallion well by rolling it in the mix as if it were a small wheel.
  6. Pour plenty of peanut oil into a large, high-sided frying pan and heat it over medium heat until it reaches the ideal temperature of 170°C. If you don't have a kitchen thermometer, dip the tip of a wooden skewer into the oil: if it sizzles immediately creating lively small bubbles, the oil is ready for the pumpkin. Peanut oil is the best choice due to its high smoke point, which protects the delicate flavour of the pistachios. My advice: never exceed 170-175°C to prevent the pistachios from turning dark and unpleasant before the inside is cooked.
  7. Gently place the medallions into the hot oil, taking care not to overcrowd the pan to prevent the oil temperature from dropping suddenly. Cook for about 4-5 minutes per side, maintaining a medium-low flame, allowing the heat to gently penetrate to the heart of the pumpkin. The medallions should turn golden on the surface, with the pistachios lightly toasted and the inner flesh tender (you can check by piercing them from the side with a toothpick). My advice: use kitchen tongs to turn them gently, strictly avoiding piercing the breading with a fork.
  8. Once cooking is complete, remove the medallions with a slotted spoon and place them on a tray lined with plenty of kitchen paper. Lightly pat the top as well to remove any trace of excess oil and immediately season with salt while they are still piping hot, allowing the crystals to stick to the crust. The surface should appear dry, extremely crispy, and fragrant with toasted nuts. My advice: never stack the medallions on top of each other while resting, otherwise the residual heat will create steam, making the breading soggy.
  9. Assemble the plate by placing two pumpkin medallions for each guest, perhaps overlapping them slightly to add volume and height to the presentation. Take your balsamic and honey reduction and decorate the medallions by drizzling it in a fluid, artistic motion, finally adding a few whole pistachios if available. The colour contrast between the green crust, the orange flesh, and the glossy brown sauce will make your dish look worthy of a Michelin-starred restaurant. My advice: slightly warm the plates before serving to keep the medallions at the ideal temperature for longer.
