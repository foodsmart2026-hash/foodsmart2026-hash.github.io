Baked Banana and Oat Bites

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

These 'mock biscuits' consist only of fruit and cereals, resulting in a soft and chewy texture just right for toddlers to handle. They are extremely budget-friendly and quick to prepare.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🌱 Vegan🧒 Kid-friendly🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🌍 International

Ingredients

  • Very ripe bananas (with dark spots)2 pcs
  • Fine porridge oats100 g
  • Vanilla powder1 g

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Preheat your conventional oven to 180°C (350°F/Gas Mark 4) and line a large baking tray with greaseproof paper to ensure the bites do not stick. Take the 2 very ripe bananas, peel them, and carefully remove any strings or excessively bruised dark spots. This initial step is vital to ensure the oven temperature is stable and consistent by the time you are ready to bake. Your goal is an organised workspace with a hot oven and a prepared tray ready for the mixture. Chef's tip: use bananas with skins that are almost entirely black, as their natural sugar content and creamy texture are the keys to success for this sugar-free recipe.
  2. Place the bananas in a shallow bowl or a large plate and mash them thoroughly using the prongs of a sturdy fork. Work the pulp with vigorous, steady movements until you achieve a semi-liquid, smooth puree free of any large lumps. This process should take about 2 to 3 minutes of manual work to fully release the fruit's natural sweetness. You should aim for a consistency similar to a velvety cream that releases an intense aroma of ripe fruit. Chef's tip: strictly avoid using a hand blender, as it would make the banana too watery, causing the mixture to lose the structural integrity needed to hold the oats.
  3. Pour 100g of small rolled oats (porridge oats) directly onto the banana puree you have just prepared in the bowl. Mix the ingredients with a spoon or a silicone spatula, ensuring that every single oat flake is completely coated and moistened by the fruit pulp. Continue mixing for about a minute until the dough appears uniformly thick and slightly sticky to the touch. The result should be a cohesive mass where no dry or powdery patches of oats are visible. Chef's tip: choose 'small' or 'fine' rolled oats rather than jumbo ones, as they have a much better liquid absorption capacity, leading to a superior texture.
  4. Add 1g of vanilla powder to the mixture and fold it in carefully using circular motions to distribute the flavour perfectly. The vanilla does not just provide fragrance; it acts as a natural sweetness enhancer, making the bites more palatable without the need for sweeteners. Ensure the powder does not form small dark clumps by stirring well from the bottom up to incorporate it into the entire mass. You should immediately notice a sweet, spiced aroma blending harmoniously with the banana. Chef's tip: if you do not have vanilla powder, you can substitute it with grated organic lemon zest or a pinch of cinnamon to vary the flavour profile.
  5. Allow the mixture to rest in the bowl for about 10 minutes at room temperature before you start shaping it on the baking tray. This resting time is a crucial technical step that allows the oats to hydrate deeply, softening through the natural moisture of the banana. During this rest, you will notice the mixture becoming visibly firmer, more elastic, and less runny, making the shaping process much easier. The result will be a perfect consistency that allows the bites to hold their shape without crumbling in the oven. Chef's tip: do not rush to bake; this rest period ensures the bites remain tender on the inside even after cooking.
  6. Scoop a small amount of the dough with a tablespoon and form walnut-sized balls, placing them onto the prepared baking tray. Maintain a distance of about 2 to 3 centimetres between each ball, although these bites tend not to spread much during baking. Once all the balls are positioned, use the back of a spoon slightly dampened with water to gently flatten them into discs about 1 cm thick. You should yield between 12 and 16 bites with an even surface and well-defined edges. Chef's tip: keeping the spoon damp is a professional trick to prevent the dough from sticking to the tool, allowing you to achieve a smooth, professional finish.
  7. Place the tray on the middle shelf of the preheated oven at 180°C and bake for between 12 and 15 minutes. Monitor the baking process closely towards the end of the time; the bites should feel firm and set to the touch but must remain a very pale golden colour. Avoid letting them brown too much, as the oat fibre would become too tough and woody, losing the pleasant soft bite. At the end of the time, the edges should look slightly drier than the centre, which will remain moist. Chef's tip: if using a fan-assisted oven, reduce the temperature to 160°C to prevent the outside from drying out before the inside is cooked.
  8. Remove the tray from the oven and allow the bites to cool completely at room temperature before attempting to lift them from the greaseproof paper. During this cooling phase, which will take about 20-30 minutes, the banana sugars and oat fibres will fully stabilise, creating the final structure of the treat. Once cold, lift the bites gently; if you notice any slightly overcooked or sharp edges, you can trim them by pressing lightly with your fingers. The final result is a soft, naturally sweet bite with a rustic yet velvety texture. Chef's tip: store them in an airtight glass container in a cool place; they will stay perfect and soft for about two days.
