Farmhouse Ricotta and Lemon Sponge

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 40 min · Medium

A moist, fragrant cake that melts in your mouth, typical of Italian rural snacks. Ricotta replaces most of the fats, making the cake light and high in protein.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🥕 Vegetarian🧒 Kid-friendly🇮🇹 Italian

A rustic cake with a light structure, ideal for those seeking a simple yet fragrant dessert. Fresh ricotta provides a natural moisture to the batter, enhanced by organic lemon notes.

Ingredients

  • Fresh cow's milk ricotta (a soft Italian whey cheese)250 g
  • Caster sugar120 g
  • Medium eggs2 pcs
  • Plain flour (extra-fine Italian '00' flour)180 g
  • Baking powder16 g
  • Organic lemon (zest)1 pcs
  • Semi-skimmed milk30 ml

Allergens

Gluten, Eggs, Milk

Method

  1. To begin, preheat your static oven to 180°C and carefully line a 20cm cake tin with damp, well-wrung parchment paper. In a large mixing bowl, combine 250g of fresh cow's milk ricotta with 120g of granulated sugar using a hand whisk or a flexible spatula. You should whisk vigorously for about 2-3 minutes until the sugar granules have partially dissolved and the ricotta has become creamy, smooth, and velvety. The secret is to eliminate any lumps from the ricotta right from this initial stage to ensure a perfect final texture. Chef's tip: if the ricotta is very wet, let it drain in a sieve for 15 minutes before starting to prevent the batter from becoming too runny.
  2. Now incorporate the 2 medium eggs into the ricotta mixture, adding them one at a time and ensuring the first is fully absorbed before proceeding with the second. Use the whisk with firm, circular motions, incorporating air to make the cake lighter and fluffier. Continue working the mix for a couple of minutes until you obtain a thick cream with a consistent pale yellow colour. You will notice the mass becoming more fluid and glossy, a sign that the egg proteins are creating the necessary structure. Chef's tip: use eggs at room temperature, as they bind much better with the fats in the ricotta than cold ones straight from the fridge.
  3. Take your organic lemon and finely grate the zest directly over the bowl, being careful not to include any of the bitter white pith. Stir gently with a spatula to evenly distribute the essential oils, which will release an intense and natural citrus aroma. This operation takes only a few seconds but is fundamental for characterising the 'country-style' flavour of the cake. The result will be a batter speckled with tiny, extremely aromatic yellow fragments. Chef's tip: grate the lemon at the very last moment because the essential oils are volatile and lose intensity if left exposed to the air for too long.
  4. Prepare the dry part by sifting 180g of plain flour together with 16g of baking powder into a separate bowl. Sifting is a crucial step to avoid lumps and to aerate the flour, promoting a more uniform rise in the oven. Once finished, you will have a very fine, impalpable powder ready to be combined with the liquid ingredients. Make sure the baking powder is well distributed in the flour to avoid areas of uneven rising. Chef's tip: hold the sieve about 20cm above the bowl to incorporate even more air as the flour falls.
  5. Begin adding the sifted flour to the ricotta and egg mixture, alternating it with 30ml of semi-skimmed milk poured in a thin stream. Use a spatula and proceed with slow, upward folding motions to avoid deflating the mass and to keep the fluffiness created earlier intact. Continue until all ingredients are used, working the batter for no more than one minute after the last addition. You will get a thick, elastic, and visibly aerated mixture that falls in 'ribbons' from the spatula. Chef's tip: do not overwork the batter after adding the flour, otherwise you will develop too much gluten and the cake will turn out rubbery rather than soft.
  6. Gently pour the resulting batter into the previously prepared tin, using a silicone spatula to scrape every bit from the bowl. Level the surface with the back of a wet spoon or the spatula itself, without pressing too hard so as not to compromise the leavening. The thickness should be even across the entire surface to ensure uniform baking at every point. The 20cm tin will allow the cake to rise well in height while maintaining a moist core. Chef's tip: give the tin a couple of firm taps on the worktop to eliminate any air bubbles trapped at the bottom.
  7. Place the tin on the middle rack of the preheated static oven at 180°C and bake for between 35 and 40 minutes. During baking, absolutely avoid opening the oven door for the first 25 minutes to prevent temperature fluctuations that could cause the cake to deflate. Observe through the glass: the cake should puff up and take on a beautiful, inviting deep golden colour on the surface. The scent of lemon and milk will start to spread throughout the kitchen about halfway through. Chef's tip: if you notice the surface browning too quickly after 25 minutes, cover it with a sheet of foil without touching the batter.
  8. After 40 minutes, perform the skewer test by inserting it into the centre of the cake to check the internal cooking. If the skewer comes out clean and dry, the cake is ready; however, if it shows traces of wet batter, continue baking for another 5 minutes, monitoring closely. The cake should feel firm at the edges but retain a slight elasticity in the centre when pressed with a finger. Once baked, turn off the oven and let it rest inside with the door slightly ajar for about 5 minutes. Chef's tip: don't worry if the cake sinks slightly once out of the oven; this is normal given the high percentage of ricotta.
  9. Remove the cake from the oven and let it cool completely inside the tin before attempting to remove or unmould it. The structure of a ricotta cake is very delicate and unstable when hot; cooling allows the fats and sugars to stabilise correctly. After about an hour, open the springform tin or carefully lift the edges of the parchment paper to transfer the cake onto a serving plate. The final result will be a rustic, fragrant, and incredibly soft cake, perfect for a wholesome breakfast or snack. Chef's tip: enjoy the cake at room temperature or slightly chilled from the fridge to maximise the citric note of the lemon.

Tips

  • Sieve the ricotta before mixing it with sugar for a smooth, lump-free batter.
  • Ensure the eggs are at room temperature to promote perfect emulsification.
  • Do not open the oven during the first twenty minutes of baking to ensure optimal rising.

Variations

  • Enrich the batter with dark chocolate chips for a more indulgent version.
  • Dust the surface with powdered sugar once the cake has completely cooled down.
