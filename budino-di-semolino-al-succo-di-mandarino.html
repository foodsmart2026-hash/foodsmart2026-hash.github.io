Semolina Pudding with Mandarin Juice

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 10 min · Easy

A creamy dessert with a citrus flavour, perfect for winter when mandarins are in peak season. Semolina creates a thick and comforting texture that little ones love.

A delicate and fragrant spoon dessert combining milky creaminess with the citrus notes of winter mandarins. Its velvety texture makes it a light and refined end to any meal.

Ingredients

  • Whole milk300 ml
  • Wheat semolina40 g
  • Strained mandarin juice100 ml
  • Grated mandarin zest1 pcs
  • Egg yolk1 pcs

Allergens

Gluten, Eggs, Milk

Method

  1. Begin by thoroughly washing an organic mandarin under running water, dry it, and finely grate the zest without touching the bitter white pith. Squeeze the juice until you reach 100 ml, filtering it through a fine-mesh sieve to remove any pulp or seeds. This preliminary step takes about 5 minutes and ensures a clean, intense aromatic base for your dessert. You will end up with highly fragrant zest and clear juice ready for use. Chef's tip: use firm mandarins, as their skin is much easier to grate without mashing the fruit.
  2. Take a heavy-bottomed saucepan and pour in 300 ml of whole milk along with the freshly grated mandarin zest. Place the saucepan over a medium-low heat and warm the liquid gradually, being careful never to let it reach a rolling boil. This warm infusion phase extracts the essential oils from the citrus, giving the milk a delicate, natural fragrance. The result should be very hot milk emitting a persistent citrus aroma. Chef's tip: for an even deeper flavour, leave the zest to infuse in the cold milk for 15 minutes before turning on the heat.
  3. As the milk approaches simmering point, slowly rain in the 40 g of wheat semolina while whisking vigorously by hand. It is essential to maintain a constant, circular motion to ensure the grains separate and are evenly hydrated by the hot liquid. Keep stirring until any potential lumps have disappeared and the mixture begins to coat the back of a spoon. You will obtain a fluid, homogeneous base free of any gritty texture. Chef's tip: pour the semolina with one hand while whisking with the other, much like making a quick polenta.
  4. Lower the heat to the absolute minimum and continue cooking the semolina for about 5 minutes, never stopping the whisking or stirring with a wooden spoon. The mixture should progressively thicken until it reaches a dense consistency, similar to a very firm custard. The gentle heat allows the semolina grains to swell without scorching the bottom of the pan. The final result must be a smooth, glossy, and visibly thick mass. Chef's tip: if the mixture seems to thicken too rapidly, lift the pan off the heat for a few moments while continuing to stir.
  5. Once the semolina is cooked, remove the pan from the heat and immediately pour in the 100 ml of filtered mandarin juice. Whisk energetically to incorporate the acidic juice into the milky base until they are perfectly combined. This step will slightly cool the mixture and intensify its colour, making it look more vibrant. You should have a slightly more fluid but well-bound and fragrant cream. Chef's tip: do not add the juice during the boiling stage to preserve the vitamins and the fresh aromatic profile of the mandarin.
  6. Let the cream rest for a couple of minutes off the heat, stirring occasionally to distribute the heat evenly. In a separate small bowl, have the egg yolk ready at room temperature. This pause is crucial to lower the temperature of the mass just enough to welcome the egg without scrambling it instantly. The mixture should feel hot to the touch but not boiling, ready for the emulsification phase. Chef's tip: test the side of the pan; if you can hold your hand against it for a few seconds, the temperature is ideal.
  7. Add the egg yolk to the semolina cream and work quickly with the whisk to distribute it evenly before the heat sets it. Return the saucepan to a very low heat for just one minute, stirring continuously to safely pasteurise the egg. The cream will take on a deeper yellow hue and an even more velvety, rich texture thanks to the fats in the yolk. The result should be a smooth cream that leaves a trail on the surface when the whisk is lifted. Chef's tip: do not exceed one minute for this final cook, otherwise the egg may impart a metallic taste to the pudding.
  8. Immediately divide the hot pudding into four small individual bowls or ramekins, smoothing the surface with the back of a spoon. Take some food-grade cling film and apply it 'in contact' with the surface of each portion, pressing gently to remove any air pockets. This technical trick prevents moisture from evaporating and stops a tough skin from forming on top. Allow the bowls to cool at room temperature for at least one hour. Chef's tip: use ceramic or glass ramekins, as they distribute heat more gently during the cooling process.
  9. Serve the semolina pudding at room temperature to allow the mandarin's aromatic notes to shine fully without being masked by fridge temperatures. Before serving, gently remove the cling film and decorate, if desired, with a little fresh zest or a segment of mandarin. The texture should be soft to the spoon, almost melting, with a perfect balance between the sweetness of the milk and the acidity of the citrus. It is a light and comforting dessert, perfect for an elegant finish to a meal. Chef's tip: for a restaurant-style presentation, you can turn the puddings out onto a plate if you previously moistened the ramekins with a drop of water.

Tips

  • Pour the semolina very slowly while whisking constantly to prevent any unpleasant lumps from forming.
  • Use organic mandarins so you can safely grate the zest and extract its full aroma.
  • For a more elegant presentation, pour the pudding into individual molds and let it set in the refrigerator.

Variations

  • Substitute the mandarin juice with orange or clementine juice for a slightly different citrus profile.
  • Garnish the surface with a drizzle of wildflower honey or citrus marmalade before serving.
