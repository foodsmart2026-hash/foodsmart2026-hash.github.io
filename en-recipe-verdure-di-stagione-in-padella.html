Seasonal Pan-Fried Vegetables

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 20 min · Medium

A colourful and nutritious side dish that combines the crunch of fresh vegetables with the creaminess of legumes. This recipe highlights extra virgin olive oil and fibre, following the pillars of the Mediterranean diet for a balanced meal.

A rustic and colorful side dish, perfect to accompany main courses or as a base for protein bowls. The sweetness of carrots blends with the crunchiness of chickpeas and the Mediterranean scent of fresh rosemary.

Ingredients

  • Dark courgettes400 g
  • Medium carrots300 g
  • Canned precooked chickpeas (drained weight)240 g
  • Tropea red onion (a sweet red onion variety from Calabria)100 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Fresh rosemary5 g
  • Iodised salt3 g
  • Ground black pepper1 g

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Begin by thoroughly washing 400g of dark courgettes and 300g of medium carrots under running water to remove any soil residue. Trim the ends and cut both vegetables into uniform 1.5cm cubes using a sharp chef's knife and a stable chopping board. Maintain a consistent size to ensure every piece cooks at the same rate, preventing some bits from becoming mushy while others stay raw. Your goal is a precise, colourful dice that gives your dish a professional look. Chef's tip: do not peel the courgettes, as the skin contains vital nutrients and helps the cubes hold their shape during frying.
  2. Take 100g of Tropea red onion, peel it and slice it very thinly, almost paper-thin, to make it more digestible and delicate. In a large non-stick frying pan (at least 28cm in diameter), heat 30ml of extra virgin olive oil over a low heat. Add the onion and let it soften gently for about 4 minutes, stirring with a wooden spoon so it becomes translucent without browning. The onion should be soft and fragrant, releasing its natural sweetness. Chef's tip: if the onion starts to colour too quickly, add a tablespoon of warm water to lower the oil temperature and prevent burning.
  3. Now add the 300g of diced carrots to the pan, as they require longer cooking than the courgettes. Increase the heat slightly to medium and sauté for about 5 minutes, turning them often to distribute the heat evenly. To help soften the vegetable fibres without adding extra fat, pour in two tablespoons of hot water and partially cover with a lid. The carrots should begin to tenderise but retain a vivid orange colour. Chef's tip: always use hot water or vegetable stock to avoid a thermal shock that would stop the cooking process in the pan.
  4. It is time to add the 400g of prepared courgettes and raise the heat to medium-high. Cook the vegetables uncovered for about 7-8 minutes, allowing moisture to evaporate quickly and encouraging the Maillard reaction. Stir frequently or toss the pan with a firm flick of the wrist until the courgettes show a light golden char on the surface. You want an 'al dente' texture, where the outside is fragrant and the core remains firm and juicy. Chef's tip: do not overcrowd the pan; if necessary, use two separate pans to ensure the vegetables roast rather than boil in their own steam.
  5. Take 240g of pre-cooked chickpeas, drain them from their canning liquid and rinse them very well under cold running water to remove excess salt and residue. Add them to the pan with the carrots and courgettes, stirring gently to avoid breaking the legumes. Let the flavours meld for about 3 minutes over a medium heat, allowing the chickpeas to warm through and absorb the seasoning. This step turns a simple side dish into a nutritionally complete base thanks to the plant-based proteins. Chef's tip: rinsing canned chickpeas is crucial for improving digestibility and controlling the sodium content of the recipe.
  6. Move to the seasoning phase by finely chopping 5g of fresh rosemary and adding it to the pan along with 1g of freshly ground black pepper. Sauté everything for another 2-3 minutes over a high heat so the heat releases the essential oils of the herb, enveloping the vegetables in an intense Mediterranean scent. You will immediately notice a balsamic, pungent aroma that enhances the sweetness of the onion and carrots. The result should be a fragrant mix where every ingredient is visually distinct. Chef's tip: use fresh rosemary instead of dried, as the latter can be woody and less aromatic in quick pan-fry recipes.
  7. Season the mixture with 3g of iodized salt only at this final stage of preparation. Stir well to distribute the seasoning over all ingredients and perform a final doneness test: the carrots should be easily pierced with a fork while still offering a slight resistance. Salting at the end prevents the courgettes from releasing too much water through osmosis, ensuring they stay crunchy rather than soggy. The dish will look glossy, colourful, and inviting, with the vegetables perfectly intact. Chef's tip: always taste a cube of each vegetable type before turning off the heat to ensure the seasoning is perfectly balanced.
  8. Turn off the heat, cover the pan with a lid, and let the side dish rest for about 2 minutes before serving. This brief rest with the heat off allows internal temperatures to stabilise and the chickpea fibres to become even more tender thanks to the trapped residual moisture. Serve the vegetables hot or warm, perhaps finishing with a drizzle of raw olive oil if you prefer a more intense flavour. The ideal presentation is in a large bowl that showcases the vibrant colours. Chef's tip: this dish is also excellent the next day, as the extra resting time allows the rosemary aromas to penetrate even deeper into the vegetables.

Tips

  • Cut the carrots into small cubes to ensure even cooking alongside the zucchini.
  • Dry the chickpeas thoroughly after rinsing to help them get slightly crispy in the pan.
  • Store any leftovers in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to two days.

Variations

  • Add sliced bell peppers for a bolder flavor and an extra touch of color.
  • Swap the fresh rosemary for thyme for a more delicate aromatic note.
