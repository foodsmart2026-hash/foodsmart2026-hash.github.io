Mezze Maniche with Creamy Rosemary Chickpeas

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

A thick and nutritious pasta dish that uses legumes to create a velvety, flavourful sauce. Fresh rosemary provides an unmistakable Mediterranean aroma that enhances the sweetness of the chickpeas.

Ingredients

  • Mezze Maniche pasta (short, ridged tube-shaped pasta)320 g
  • Canned pre-cooked chickpeas480 g
  • Fresh rosemary2 sprigs
  • Garlic1 clove
  • Tomato pulp100 g

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Start by preparing the pulses: drain 480 g of pre-cooked chickpeas and rinse them thoroughly under cold running water using a sieve. Move them with your hands to remove any traces of brine and preservatives until the water runs clear. This step, which takes about 2 minutes, is essential to ensure a clean flavour and improve digestibility. You will have chickpeas ready for use, vivid in colour and free from the foam typical of the canning liquid. Chef's Tip: do not skip rinsing, as the liquid in the jars is high in sodium which would alter the salty balance of the recipe.
  2. Prepare the aromatic base by finely chopping the needles of 2 sprigs of fresh rosemary and lightly crushing 1 unpeeled garlic clove (clove in its skin). In a large frying pan, heat a generous drizzle of extra virgin olive oil over medium-low heat for about 2-3 minutes. Add the garlic and the chopped rosemary, letting them sizzle gently without browning to release the essential oils. You will smell an intense fragrance spreading through the kitchen, a sign that the seasoning is becoming aromatic and flavorful. Chef's Tip: keep the oil temperature under control; if the rosemary turns dark, it will acquire an unpleasant bitter aftertaste.
  3. Pour the well-drained chickpeas into the pan and add 100 g of tomato pulp, also adding half a glass of warm water. Stir with a wooden spoon and cook over medium heat for about 10 minutes, partially covering with a lid. The chickpeas must soften further and absorb the rosemary flavour, while the tomato must reduce slightly, creating a bound sauce. The result will be a moist and fragrant base, where the flavours have blended harmoniously. Chef's Tip: if the sauce dries out too quickly, add another ladle of hot water to keep the chickpeas well-hydrated.
  4. Transfer about half of the chickpea and tomato mixture into a jug for an immersion blender, leaving the rest in the pan. Blend at maximum power for about a minute until you obtain a thick, smooth, and velvety cream with a uniform orange colour. If the cream is too grainy, add a tablespoon of water and continue blending until it reaches the consistency of a soft purée. This cream will be the key element to wrap the pasta in a silky embrace. Chef's Tip: for a restaurant-quality texture, pass the cream through a fine-mesh sieve to remove any remaining chickpea skins.
  5. While the sauce rests, bring a pot with about 3-4 litres of water to a boil and add a pinch of coarse salt. Toss in 320 g of mezze maniche and cook them according to the times on the package, but make sure to drain them at least one minute before the end of the indicated time for a perfect 'al dente' texture. Stir the pasta occasionally to prevent the shapes from sticking together. The pasta should be firm to the bite but ready to finish cooking in the sauce. Chef's Tip: always save a cup of the cooking water before draining; the released starch is the secret to an impeccable creaminess.
  6. Recombine the chickpea cream in the pan with the remaining whole chickpeas, stirring over very low heat to blend the two components. Adjust the salt if necessary and heat gently without bringing it to a hard boil, to avoid the cream separating or drying out excessively. You will get a rich and layered seasoning, characterised by the contrast between the velvety part and the whole chickpeas that provide structure. The scent of rosemary must remain the protagonist. Chef's Tip: if the cream appears too thick at this stage, thin it with a drop of cooking water to make it fluid.
  7. Drain the mezze maniche and transfer them directly into the pan with the sauce over high heat. Add half a ladle of cooking water and start tossing the pasta with quick movements, ensuring the cream penetrates the mezze maniche and adheres to the external ridges. This 'mantecatura' process takes about a minute and serves to create a perfect emulsion between the oil and the pasta starches. You will notice the sauce becoming glossy and every piece of pasta perfectly coated. Chef's Tip: don't be afraid to use the cooking water; it's the element that transforms a dry sauce into an irresistible cream.
  8. Turn off the heat, plate the mezze maniche immediately, and finish each portion with a final drizzle of raw extra virgin olive oil. Serve the dish very hot, possibly decorating with a small sprig of fresh rosemary to recall the aromas used during cooking. The final consistency should be that of a 'bound' pasta, rich in sauce that doesn't slide to the bottom of the plate. The flavour will be balanced, with the sweetness of the chickpeas contrasted by the aromatic note of the rosemary and a hint of acidity from the tomato. Chef's Tip: consume the dish as soon as it's ready, as the pasta tends to absorb the creaminess quickly once plated.
