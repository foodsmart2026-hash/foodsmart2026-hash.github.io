Golden Roasted Cauliflower with Mustard Breadcrumbs

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

A crunchy transformation of classic boiled cauliflower, enriched with a savoury and slightly spicy breadcrumb coating. Budget-friendly, quick, and loved even by those who don't usually enjoy this vegetable.

A tasty dish that transforms cauliflower into crunchy and flavorful bites. The mustard and garlic breading provides a bold note and an inviting golden crust after baking.

Ingredients

  • Cauliflower800 g
  • Breadcrumbs50 g
  • Mild mustard1 tbsp
  • Fresh parsley1 bunch
  • Garlic powder1 pinch

Allergens

Gluten, Mustard

Method

  1. Start by preparing 800g of white cauliflower: remove the outer leaves and the central core, then cut it into similar-sized florets, about 3-4 cm in diameter. It is vital that the pieces are uniform so they all cook in the same amount of time without burning. Rinse the florets under cold running water to remove any soil residue and drain them thoroughly. You will have clean, compact nuggets ready for parboiling. My tip is not to cut the florets too small, otherwise they will tend to fall apart during boiling.
  2. Fill a large pot with water, add a pinch of salt, and bring it to a rolling boil over high heat. Once boiling, gently drop in the cauliflower florets and blanch them for exactly 4 minutes. This step serves to soften the core of the fibre without cooking it completely, maintaining an 'al dente' structure. Once the time is up, immediately drain the cauliflower using a slotted spoon or a colander. The result should be a floret that is still firm to the touch and bright white. Do not overcook to prevent the cauliflower from absorbing too much water and becoming mushy.
  3. Arrange the freshly drained florets on a clean tea towel or kitchen paper, patting them gently. Let them rest for about 5 minutes so that the residual heat evaporates any excess surface moisture. This step is a chef's secret to achieving a crispy breading: residual water is the enemy of browning and would make the dish soggy rather than roasted. You will see the cauliflower become matte and dry on the surface. Ensure they are well-spaced during this drying phase.
  4. In a very large bowl, prepare the aromatic emulsion by combining 1 tablespoon of sweet mustard, 3 tablespoons of extra virgin olive oil, a pinch of garlic powder, freshly ground black pepper, and a bunch of finely chopped fresh parsley. Whisk vigorously with a hand whisk or a fork for about a minute until you get a thick, velvety sauce with an intense ochre colour. This base will act as a glue for the breading and provide the main aromatic note to the dish. If the emulsion seems too thick, add a teaspoon of hot water to make it more fluid. The chef's tip is to use sweet mustard so as not to overpower the delicate flavour of the cauliflower.
  5. Pour the now warm and dry cauliflower florets into the bowl with the mustard emulsion. Mix extremely gently using your hands or a wooden spoon, ensuring every single crevice of the florets is coated with a thin film of sauce. Act carefully so as not to break the tips of the flowers, which are the most attractive part of the dish. At the end, the cauliflower will appear shiny, fragrant, and slightly yellowish thanks to the mustard. If you see some parts are still white, continue mixing from the bottom upwards with circular motions.
  6. Now sprinkle 50g of breadcrumbs over the seasoned cauliflower, scattering it like rain over the entire surface. Instead of stirring with a spoon, shake the bowl with quick jolting motions so that the breadcrumbs naturally adhere to the moist emulsion without clumping. The breading should be thin and uniform, creating a sandy effect on every piece. You should see the florets dotted with golden crumbs that stay firmly attached. The trick for a perfect breading is not to overdo the breadcrumbs: they should enrich, not hide, the main ingredient.
  7. Prepare a large baking tray by lining it with baking paper and distribute the florets in a single layer, taking care to leave at least a centimetre of space between each one. Never overlap the cauliflower, as the steam released during cooking would prevent the formation of the crispy crust. If necessary, use two separate trays instead of crowding one. Preheat the oven to 200°C (fan-assisted or static) to ensure an immediate heat shock. An orderly arrangement ensures that hot air circulates evenly, browning each side equally.
  8. Place the tray on the middle rack of the oven and bake for about 15-20 minutes, visually monitoring the colour progression. Halfway through, after about 10 minutes, briefly remove the tray and turn the florets with kitchen tongs or shake it gently to expose the base to direct heat as well. The aroma released will be toasted and slightly pungent as the mustard heats up. Towards the end, if you want an even crunchier effect, you can activate the grill function for the last 2 minutes. The final result must be a dark golden, crispy crust covering a tender and juicy heart.
  9. Remove the cauliflower from the oven and let it rest for a couple of minutes before serving, allowing the breading to stabilise and become even more brittle. Transfer the golden florets to a serving platter, perhaps garnishing with a few reserved fresh parsley leaves. The dish should look rustic yet elegant, with a deep golden colour and a texture that invites a bite. Serve it warm to fully appreciate the contrast between the cauliflower's natural sweetness and the mustard's tangy note. A final tip: do not cover the freshly baked dish, otherwise the steam will immediately soften the hard-earned crust.

Tips

  • Thoroughly dry the florets after parboiling to help the mustard emulsion adhere better.
  • Spread the cauliflower on the baking sheet without overcrowding to ensure uniform crunchiness on all sides.
  • You can prepare the breadcrumb and spice mix in advance and store it in a cool, dry place.

Variations

  • Replace the parsley with chopped chives for a fresher and more pungent flavor.
  • Add some grated lemon zest to the breading for a citrusy touch that balances the mustard.
