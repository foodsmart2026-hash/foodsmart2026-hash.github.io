Tuscan Ribollita

Toscana · 6 servings · Prep 30 min · Cooking 90 min · Medium

A rustic peasant soup of cavolo nero, beans, and stale bread, re-boiled before serving.

Ingredients

  • Cooked cannellini beans500 g
  • Cavolo nero300 g
  • Savoy cabbage200 g
  • Potatoes200 g
  • Carrot100 g
  • Celery80 g
  • Red onion100 g
  • Peeled plum tomatoes200 g
  • Stale Tuscan bread250 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil60 ml
  • Water1500 ml
  • Fine salt8 g

Allergens

Gluten, Celery
May contain traces of: Mustard, Soy.

Method

  1. Start by preparing the aromatic base: finely chop 100g of red onion, 100g of carrot, and 80g of celery, then sauté them gently in a large pot with 60ml of extra virgin olive oil for about 8 minutes. The heat should be medium-low so the vegetables release their sugars without burning; you'll know the soffritto is ready when the onion turns translucent and releases a sweet, inviting aroma.
  2. Clean and roughly chop 300g of black kale (cavolo nero) and 200g of savoy cabbage, removing the tougher central ribs, then cut 200g of potatoes into roughly 2-centimeter cubes. Add these vegetables to the soffritto along with 200g of peeled tomatoes crushed coarsely with a fork, stirring well for a couple of minutes so all the ingredients absorb the oil's flavor.
  1. Take 250g of the cooked cannellini beans (half of the total) and blend them with a portion of the 1500ml of water until you get a smooth and velvety cream. This is the true secret of Tuscan tradition: the legume puree will give the soup a natural thickness and an enveloping creaminess without needing any other thickeners.
  2. Pour the freshly prepared bean puree and the remaining water into the pot (for a total of 1500ml), bringing everything to a gentle boil over high heat. Once boiling, lower the heat to minimum, cover with a lid, and let it simmer gently for about 60 minutes, ensuring the liquid doesn't reduce too much and the vegetables become tender.
  3. After the hour of cooking, add the remaining 250g of whole cannellini beans and season with 8g of fine salt. Let it cook for another 10-15 minutes so the whole beans warm up and integrate perfectly; the soup is ready when the potatoes start to fall apart slightly, a sign that the cooking has reached the heart of the vegetables.
  4. Take the stale Tuscan bread and slice it thinly, then arrange it in a soup tureen or a terracotta pot, alternating layers of bread with the still-boiling vegetable soup. It is essential to let the mixture rest for at least 2 hours (or even better, overnight): the bread must absorb all the flavor of the broth, transforming the soup into a compact and succulent mass.
  5. At the moment of serving, as the name suggests, you must 're-boil' (ribollire) the soup: place it in a pan and heat over medium heat for about 10 minutes, stirring often to prevent the bread from sticking to the bottom. Serve your ribollita hot, finishing each plate with a generous drizzle of raw extra virgin olive oil to enhance the earthy and herbal aromas.
