Aubergine Rolls with Ricotta and Basil

4 servings · Prep 25 min · Cooking 20 min · Medium

A Mediterranean main course that smells of summer. The aubergines become delicious parcels enclosing a creamy cheese heart.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🌾 Gluten-free🇮🇹 Italian

Ingredients

  • long aubergines2 pcs
  • cow's milk ricotta300 g
  • tomato passata (sieved tomatoes)250 ml
  • Parmigiano Reggiano (aged Italian hard cheese)40 g
  • fresh basil1 mazzetto
  • extra virgin olive oil20 ml

Allergens

Milk

Method

  1. Begin by thoroughly washing 2 long aubergines, drying them with a cloth, and removing the green stalks with a sharp knife. Place the vegetable on a chopping board and slice it longitudinally into pieces about half a centimetre thick, aiming for uniformity to ensure even cooking. Use a straight-edged knife for a clean cut that won't tear the delicate flesh of the vegetable. The final result should be long, intact slices that will serve as the casing for our filling. The chef's tip is to discard the first and last slices, which are almost entirely skin, to ensure a more refined eating experience.
  2. Heat a cast-iron griddle or grill pan over a high flame until it starts to emit a light smoke, indicating it has reached the ideal temperature. Place the aubergine slices on it without overlapping and grill for about 2 minutes per side, turning them with kitchen tongs. Carefully monitor the flesh: it should become flexible and show the classic brown grill marks without burning. This process partially cooks the vegetable and removes excess water. The chef's tip is not to salt the aubergines at this stage to prevent them from releasing too much water and becoming soggy instead of firm.
  3. While the aubergines cool, focus on the filling by combining 300g of cow's milk ricotta and 20g of grated Parmesan cheese in a large bowl. Add a pinch of black pepper and the bunch of fresh basil, making sure to tear the leaves by hand to preserve their essential oils and prevent them from bruising. Work the mixture with a fork in circular motions until you achieve a smooth cream that still retains the characteristic texture of fresh ricotta. The filling should be fragrant and firm, ready to be handled. The chef's tip is to drain the ricotta in a fine-mesh sieve for half an hour before use if it appears too watery.
  4. Take a grilled aubergine slice and place a generous tablespoon of the ricotta filling at the wider end of the slice. Roll the aubergine up gently but firmly, moving towards the narrower end to form a compact cylinder. Ensure the filling stays inside without leaking from the sides when pressing the seal. You should get about 12-16 uniform rolls, depending on the size of the initial aubergines. The chef's tip is not to overfill the slices: an overstuffed roll risks opening up or becoming difficult to manage during the final baking stage.
  5. Prepare the baking base by pouring about three or four tablespoons of the tomato passata (from the 250ml total) onto the bottom of a baking dish. Season this initial layer with a drizzle of the 20ml extra virgin olive oil and a pinch of salt, spreading it evenly with the back of a spoon. This step will create a moist and flavourful bed that prevents the rolls from sticking to the bottom and provides the necessary steam for the final bake. The bottom of the dish should appear thinly veiled in bright red. The chef's tip is to choose a dish of the right size so that the rolls stay close together, supporting each other.
  6. Arrange the rolls in the dish side by side, ensuring the aubergine seam is facing downwards. This technical step is crucial as the weight of the roll and the oven's heat will seal the joint, avoiding the need for cocktail sticks. Organise them in neat rows so the heat can circulate properly between them. Once finished, the tray should look well-stocked and ready for the final gratin. The chef's tip is to wedge the rolls in so they are stable, but without squeezing them excessively to prevent the ricotta from oozing out.
  7. Pour the remaining tomato passata over the rolls, distributing it with a spoon so that each roll is partially covered by the sauce. Sprinkle the surface with the remaining 20g of Parmesan cheese, focusing on the central areas to encourage the formation of a tasty crust. The vibrant red of the tomato combined with the white of the cheese will create an inviting visual contrast even before cooking. Make sure there are no overly dry areas that might burn in the oven. The chef's tip is to add a final drizzle of raw oil over the surface to provide shine and protect the cheese during the gratin process.
  8. Place the dish in a preheated conventional oven at 190°C and bake for approximately 15-20 minutes. The dish will be ready when you see the tomato sauce bubbling vigorously at the edges and the Parmesan has turned into a golden, fragrant crust. The aroma of basil and tomato will fill the kitchen, an unmistakable sign of a successful recipe. Carefully remove from the oven and let the dish rest for at least 5 minutes before serving. The chef's tip is to resist the temptation to serve them piping hot: resting allows the flavours to meld and the structure of the roll to stabilise, making it perfect for the palate.
