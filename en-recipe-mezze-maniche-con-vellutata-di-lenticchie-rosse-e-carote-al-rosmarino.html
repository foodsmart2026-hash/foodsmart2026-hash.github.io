Mezze Maniche with Red Lentil Velouté and Rosemary Carrots

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

A nutritious and velvety first course that transforms red lentils into an enveloping cream for the pasta. The sweetness of the carrots pairs beautifully with the aroma of rosemary for a complete vegan meal at a very low cost.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⬇️ Big saving🌱 Vegan🧒 Kid-friendly🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🇮🇹 Italian

A creamy and comforting pasta dish, ideal for cooler days. The sweetness of the carrots pairs perfectly with the delicate red lentils, infused with fresh rosemary notes.

Ingredients

  • Mezze maniche rigate (short, ridged tube-shaped pasta)320 g
  • Red split lentils (husked red lentils)150 g
  • Carrots200 g
  • Yellow onion1 pcs
  • Fresh rosemary1 rametto

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Start by peeling and trimming the yellow onion and carrots (200g), then dicing them into very small cubes, a brunoise of about 2-3 mm. Use a stable chopping board and a sharp chef's knife, working steadily to achieve uniform pieces that ensure even cooking. This process takes about 8 minutes and prepares the aromatic base to quickly release its natural sugars. You should aim for a fine, colourful dice, ready for sautéing. Chef's tip: precision in chopping is vital, as equal-sized cubes will cook at the same rate, preventing any firm bits in your final velvet sauce.
  2. In a large pot, heat two tablespoons of extra virgin olive oil and add the diced vegetables along with the fresh rosemary sprig. Sauté over medium heat for about 5-7 minutes, stirring frequently with a wooden spoon to prevent the onion from browning too much. The onion should become translucent and the carrots should soften slightly, releasing a sweet and aromatic scent. When the vegetables begin to 'sizzle' gently without burning, your base is ready. Chef's tip: if the vegetables seem to be drying out too quickly, add a tablespoon of water to encourage a gentle and even sautéing process.
  3. Rinse the 150g of red split lentils under cold running water using a fine-mesh sieve until the water runs clear. Add them to the pot with the sautéed vegetables and toast them over a high heat for about 1 minute, stirring continuously. This technique allows the lentils to absorb the flavours of the base and the rosemary, lightly sealing their surface. You will notice the lentils becoming glossy and starting to smell toasted. Chef's tip: do not skip rinsing the lentils, as it removes surface starch residues that could make the cream too sticky.
  4. Cover the mixture with approximately 800 ml of hot water or light vegetable stock and bring to a boil over high heat. Once boiling, lower the heat and let it simmer gently for 15-20 minutes, partially covered with a lid. The red lentils should begin to break down spontaneously and the liquid should reduce, creating a thick and fragrant soup. The colour will shift from a bright orange to a softer, creamier tone. Chef's tip: always use pre-heated water to avoid a thermal shock to the lentils, which ensures a silkier final texture.
  5. Remove the rosemary sprig from the pot and, using a stick blender, purée about half of the mixture into a velvety cream directly in the saucepan. Use short pulses for a few seconds, making sure to leave some of the lentils and carrots whole to give the dish texture and bite. The final result should be a thick, rustic velvet sauce that will act as the binding agent for the pasta. You will see the consistency transform into an enveloping sauce similar to a white ragù. Chef's tip: blending only a portion is the secret to a 'gourmet' dish that alternates smooth creaminess with the texture of whole pulses.
  6. While the lentil cream finishes cooking, bring a large pot of water (about 3 litres) to the boil. Salt it moderately only once the water reaches a rolling boil, using about 7-8 grams of salt per litre of water. Add the 320g of mezze maniche rigate and stir immediately to prevent them from sticking to the bottom. Cook the pasta for about 2 minutes less than the time indicated on the packet. Chef's tip: the pasta cooking water is 'liquid gold'; do not discard it all when draining, as the starch it contains is essential for the final creaminess.
  7. Drain the mezze maniche while they are very al dente, making sure to reserve at least two ladles of the starchy cooking water. Immediately transfer the pasta into the pot with the lentil and carrot cream. This quick transition prevents the pasta from overcooking and allows it to absorb the seasoning from within. You will notice the pasta immediately beginning to bond with the pulse cream. Chef's tip: draining the pasta directly with a slotted spoon allows you to naturally carry over some cooking water, making it easier to meld the flavours together.
  8. Turn the heat to high under the pot with the pasta and add a ladle of the reserved cooking water. Stir vigorously or 'toss' the pasta for about 2 minutes, using professional 'mantecatura' techniques. The starch released by the pasta will emulsify with the lentil cream, creating a fluid, glossy, and perfectly bound sauce. The dish is ready when every mezze manica is uniformly coated in the velvet sauce and no liquid remains at the bottom. Chef's tip: move the pot with a firm circular motion to encourage the emulsion between the oil fats and the pasta starches.
  9. Turn off the heat, cover with a lid, and let the pasta rest for exactly one minute before serving. This short rest allows the temperatures to stabilise and the pasta to finish absorbing any residual moisture, reaching the perfect al dente texture. Plate the mezze maniche, finishing each portion with a drizzle of raw extra virgin olive oil and, if desired, a dusting of fresh black pepper. The final result is a nutritious, rosemary-scented, and incredibly velvety first course. Chef's tip: serve in warmed bowls to keep the velvet sauce creamy for longer during your meal.

Tips

  • Rinse the red lentils thoroughly under running water to remove any impurities before cooking.
  • Use an immersion blender to achieve a smooth and lump-free vegetable cream.
  • Keep a ladle of pasta cooking water to adjust the final creaminess of the dish.

Variations

  • Add a pinch of fresh chili flakes to give the sauce a spicy kick.
  • Substitute the rosemary with fresh thyme for a more delicate and citrusy aroma.
