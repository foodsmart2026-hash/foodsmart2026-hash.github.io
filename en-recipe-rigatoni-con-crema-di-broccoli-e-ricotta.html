Rigatoni with Cream of Broccoli and Ricotta

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

A thick cream made by blending boiled broccoli with ricotta. A clever way to get children to eat their vegetables.

💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🧒 Kid-friendly🇮🇹 Italian

A comforting pasta where the sweetness of ricotta meets the velvety texture of broccoli. The ridged shape is perfect for catching every drop of this delicate and nutritious cream.

Ingredients

  • Rigatoni (large ridged pasta tubes)320 g
  • Broccoli500 g
  • Cow's milk ricotta (a creamy Italian whey cheese)150 g
  • Garlic1 spicchio
  • Chilli flakes (optional)1 pizzico
  • Grated Parmesan (an aged Italian hard cheese)20 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil20 ml

Allergens

Gluten, Milk

Method

  1. Start by preparing 500g of broccoli: separate the florets from the central stalk using a small sharp knife and wash everything thoroughly under cold running water. Do not discard the stem; instead, peel it with a vegetable peeler to remove the fibrous outer layer and cut it into small, uniform cubes. This technique minimises waste and makes use of the sweetest part of the vegetable, ensuring even cooking. You should end up with clean vegetables ready for boiling. Chef's tip: cut larger florets in half to ensure they cook in the same time as the diced stems.
  2. Fill a large pot with approximately 4 litres of water, bring to a rolling boil over high heat, and add a generous pinch of sea salt. Gently submerge the prepared broccoli and boil for about 8-10 minutes without fully covering the pot to preserve the vibrant colour. The goal is to make them very tender, almost falling apart when pressed with a fork. You will notice the colour turning a brilliant green and the water becoming intensely fragrant. Chef's tip: do not over-salt at this stage, as the water will be used for the pasta and the Parmesan will add saltiness later.
  3. Once the broccoli is well cooked, use a slotted spoon to carefully lift it out, being careful not to discard the cooking water as it will be used for the rigatoni. Transfer the steaming broccoli directly into a tall blender jug or immersion blender container. It is vital to perform this step while the vegetables are still hot to help create a stable emulsion with the dairy. You should have the broccoli in the jug, steaming and still slightly coated in water. Chef's tip: keep the jug close to the pot to avoid dripping boiling water across your work surface.
  4. Add 150g of cow's milk ricotta, 20g of grated Parmesan, and a small ladle of the broccoli cooking water to the jug. Use an immersion blender on medium speed, moving it up and down to incorporate air and lighten the mixture. Continue for about 1-2 minutes until you achieve a smooth, silky, lump-free cream with an inviting pastel green colour. The final consistency should resemble a thick, velvety béchamel sauce. Chef's tip: if the cream looks too grainy, add another tablespoon of cooking water, but do so gradually to avoid making it too runny.
  5. Take a large frying pan, pour in 20ml of extra virgin olive oil, and add a crushed garlic clove and a pinch of chili flakes if desired. Turn the heat to medium-low and sauté gently for about 2-3 minutes until the garlic becomes golden and the oil is infused with the aromatics. Now, pour the broccoli and ricotta cream into the pan, stirring with a spatula to combine it with the scented oil. You should have a warm, homogeneous base ready for the pasta. Chef's tip: remove the garlic clove as soon as it begins to brown too much to prevent a bitter aftertaste in the cream.
  6. Bring the broccoli water back to a boil and drop in the 320g of rigatoni, adjusting the salt if necessary. Cook the pasta according to the package instructions, but set a timer to drain it exactly 2 minutes before the full cooking time. By cooking in this water, the pasta will absorb the residual broccoli flavour, making it far tastier than if cooked in plain water. The rigatoni should be very "al dente," with the centre still slightly firm to the bite. Chef's tip: never discard all the cooking water until the dish is finished; you will need it for the final tossing.
  7. Drain the rigatoni using a slotted spoon or colander, transferring them directly into the pan with the broccoli cream over medium-high heat. Add half a ladle of the cooking water and begin to toss the pasta, moving the pan vigorously to bind the pasta starches with the ricotta fats. This technique will transform the cream into a silky coating that perfectly envelops every single rigatone, even filling the ridges. The result should be glossy, moist, and perfectly coated pasta. Chef's tip: turn off the heat a few seconds before the sauce dries out completely, as residual heat will continue to thicken it.
  8. Let the rigatoni rest in the pan for 30 seconds before plating, allowing the flavours to settle and the temperature to become ideal for eating. Serve the 4 portions in warmed shallow bowls, finishing with a final drizzle of raw olive oil to enhance the aromas and, if you like, a crack of fresh black pepper. The dish will present a vibrant green colour, an enveloping texture, and a balanced scent between the sweetness of the ricotta and the character of the broccoli. Chef's tip: slightly warm your serving bowls beforehand to keep the delicate ricotta cream creamy for longer during the meal.

Tips

  • Use the broccoli boiling water to cook the pasta to enrich its final flavor.
  • If the cream is too thick while blending, add a tablespoon of the cooking water.
  • Remove the garlic from the pan before tossing the rigatoni to maintain a more balanced taste.

Variations

  • Add coarsely chopped walnuts before serving to provide a crunchy note to the dish.
  • Add anchovies dissolved in hot oil for a stronger and saltier flavor contrast.
