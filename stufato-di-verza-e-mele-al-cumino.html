Savoy Cabbage and Apple Stew with Cumin

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 35 min · Medium

Inspired by Alpine cuisine, this side dish pairs winter Savoy cabbage with the sweetness of apples and the digestive aroma of cumin. Perfect for accompanying roasts or matured cheeses.

A typical Central European side dish combining the bold taste of savoy cabbage with the aromatic sweetness of Golden apples. Slow cooking and cumin seeds create a perfect balance of enveloping flavors and soft textures.

Ingredients

  • Savoy cabbage1 pcs
  • Golden Delicious apples2 pcs
  • White onion1 pcs
  • Cumin seeds1 tsp
  • White wine vinegar20 ml
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Vegetable stock100 ml

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Start by removing the tough outer leaves of the Savoy cabbage and wash it thoroughly under cold running water. Cut the head into quarters, remove the fibrous central core with a sharp knife, and shred the leaves into thin strips about half a centimetre wide. This process takes about 10 minutes and ensures the vegetable cooks evenly. The result should be a voluminous pile of crisp, green ribbons. The chef recommends: do not cut the strips too short, as cabbage tends to shrink drastically during cooking and would lose its texture.
  2. Take the white onion, peel it, and slice it very thinly with a sharp knife to obtain almost transparent semi-circles. Then proceed with the two Golden Delicious apples: peel them, remove the core, and cut them into regular 1.5 cm cubes. Keep the apple cubes in a separate bowl to prevent them from getting squashed prematurely. You will have ingredients ready to be sautéed in an orderly sequence. The chef recommends: keep the apples submerged in water with a squeeze of lemon if not using immediately to prevent browning, though the cooking process will resolve this aesthetically.
  3. In a wide casserole or a deep frying pan, pour 30 ml of extra virgin olive oil and add one teaspoon of cumin seeds and the sliced onion. Turn the heat to medium-low and let them sauté gently for about 5-7 minutes, stirring regularly with a wooden spoon. You must wait until the onion becomes translucent and the cumin begins to release its characteristic earthy and pungent aroma. The result will be a fragrant, unburnt base. The chef recommends: be careful not to burn the cumin seeds, otherwise they will release a bitter aftertaste that would mask the sweetness of the apples.
  4. Slightly increase the heat to medium and add all the shredded cabbage to the pan; it will initially seem too much for the pot, but it will lose volume quickly. Sauté the vegetable for about 5 minutes, turning it continuously with kitchen tongs to flavour it evenly with the cumin-scented oil. The cabbage should begin to wilt noticeably and show the first shiny reflections on the leaf surfaces. You will get a reduced volume of vegetables well-distributed in the pan. The chef recommends: if the cabbage seems excessively dry at this stage, add just one tablespoon of water to prevent it from sticking to the bottom.
  5. Now pour 20 ml of white wine vinegar directly onto the cabbage while the heat is still lively. Let the liquid reduce for about a minute without covering with the lid, allowing the pungent smell of the alcohol and the sharpest part of the vinegar to evaporate completely. You will smell an intense aroma that perfectly balances the sweetness of the onion and the apple you will soon add. The result will be a complex and slightly acidic flavour base that will help preserve the cabbage's colour. The chef recommends: use a good quality vinegar to avoid an excessively aggressive note in the final dish.
  6. Add the previously prepared Golden Delicious apple cubes and pour 100 ml of hot vegetable stock into the pan. Stir very gently to distribute the apples evenly among the cabbage strips, trying not to break the cubes with the spoon. The stock will create the necessary steam for the gentle, deep stewing that characterises this recipe. You will see the colours of the dish blend harmoniously between the bright green of the cabbage and the pale yellow of the apples. The chef recommends: always heat the stock before adding it so as not to drop the cooking temperature and to keep the fibres tender.
  7. Lower the heat to the absolute minimum, cover the casserole with a tight-fitting lid, and let it cook for about 25 minutes. During this time, check and stir occasionally to ensure there is always a thin layer of moisture at the bottom of the pan. The cabbage must become extremely tender, almost melting in the mouth, and the cooking liquid should be almost completely absorbed. You will notice that the apples have become soft but still retain their original cubic shape. The chef recommends: if you see the liquid evaporating too quickly before the end of the cooking time, add another splash of stock to maintain a moist environment.
  8. Once cooked, turn off the heat and season with salt and freshly ground black pepper, tasting carefully to find the perfect balance between sweet and savoury. Let the stew rest for a couple of minutes with the lid on before serving, allowing the flavours to stabilise and the fibres to relax further. Serve the side dish hot in large shallow bowls or as an ideal accompaniment to roast meat dishes or mature cheeses. The final result is a dish with a spicy aroma and an irresistible sweet-and-sour flavour. The chef recommends: a final dusting of high-quality coarsely ground black pepper incredibly enhances the sweetness of the apples and cabbage.

Tips

  • Slice the cabbage into uniform strips to ensure even cooking throughout the pan.
  • Add the vegetable broth gradually to maintain the right moisture without diluting the flavor.
  • Store the stew in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to two days.

Variations

  • Replace white wine vinegar with apple cider vinegar for an even more delicate note.
  • Use red onion instead of white for a sweeter taste and a vibrant color contrast.
