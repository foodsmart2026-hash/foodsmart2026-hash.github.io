Roasted potatoes and onions

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 40 min · Medium

A classic Mediterranean diet side dish that enhances the sweetness of red onions and the crispness of yellow-fleshed potatoes. Thanks to a brief initial parboil and the use of wholemeal breadcrumbs, a tasty dish with optimal plant-based fat distribution is achieved.

A home-cooking classic where the sweetness of red onions blends with the rustic texture of yellow potatoes. Whole wheat breadcrumbs and aromatic herbs envelop the ingredients in a golden and tasty crust.

Ingredients

  • Yellow-fleshed potatoes800 g
  • Red onions400 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 ml
  • Wholemeal breadcrumbs30 g
  • Fresh rosemary1 sprig
  • Dried oregano1 tsp
  • Iodised salt5 g
  • Ground black pepper2 g

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Start by thoroughly washing 800g of yellow potatoes under running water to remove any soil, then peel them and slice into even rounds about 5mm thick. Use a sharp chef's knife or a mandoline to ensure identical thickness for every slice, which is crucial for even cooking. As you slice, submerge them in a bowl of cold water to prevent browning and remove excess starch. You will have clean, calibrated slices ready for pre-cooking. My advice is to pat them slightly dry before boiling so you don't drop the water temperature too significantly.
  2. Fill a large pot with water, add a small portion of the 5g iodised salt, and bring to a rolling boil over a high heat. Carefully drop in the potato slices and blanch them for exactly 4 minutes from the moment the water returns to the boil: this technique breaks the surface starch, creating a perfect foundation for crispiness. After the time is up, the potatoes should look translucent at the edges but remain firm at the core. Be careful not to overcook them at this stage, or they will fall apart later. The secret is to start timing only when the water starts bubbling again after adding the potatoes.
  3. Drain the potatoes using a slotted spoon to avoid breaking them and arrange them in a single layer on a clean cotton tea towel or kitchen paper. Gently pat them until they are perfectly dry, removing every trace of residual moisture that would cause a soggy 'boiled' effect in the oven. Let them rest for about 5 minutes at room temperature so the remaining steam can fully evaporate. The final result should be a potato slice that feels dry and lukewarm to the touch. Remember, water is the enemy of a crisp roast: the drier they are, the better they will be.
  4. While the potatoes rest, turn your attention to the 400g of red onions, removing the dry, papery outer skin. Slice them crosswise into rings about 1cm thick, slightly thicker than the potatoes as they tend to shrink more during roasting. Do not separate the rings too much, as this helps them maintain a meaty texture after baking. You should end up with vivid, fragrant onion discs. If you prefer a milder flavour, you can soak the rings in ice-cold water for a few minutes before using them.
  5. Take a very large bowl and transfer both the lukewarm potatoes and the sliced onions into it. Pour over 40ml of extra virgin olive oil and add the rest of the salt and 2g of freshly ground black pepper. Mix everything with clean hands, using gentle circular motions from the bottom upwards to coat every single surface without breaking the vegetables. The slices should look glossy and uniformly seasoned, almost shimmering. A common mistake is using a spoon: your hands are the best tools to feel the texture and ensure the ingredients aren't damaged.
  6. Prepare the herb mix by finely chopping the sprig of fresh rosemary and adding it to the bowl along with the teaspoon of dried oregano and 30g of wholemeal breadcrumbs. Give the bowl a vigorous shake or stir gently until the breadcrumb coating sticks to the oil film created earlier. Wholemeal breadcrumbs are not only more nutritious but also provide a toasted note and a rustic texture. The result will be a fragrant, grainy coating enveloping the vegetables. Make sure no excess breadcrumbs are left at the bottom of the bowl; if they are, you might need a tiny drop more oil.
  7. Line a large baking tray with parchment paper and spread the potato and onion mix out in a single layer. Strictly avoid overlapping the slices, as trapped steam would prevent the golden crust from forming; use two trays if necessary. Space between the ingredients allows hot air to circulate freely and dry the vegetables evenly. You will see the contrasting colours of the yellow potatoes and purple onions harmoniously distributed. If the tray looks too crowded, bake in two batches for a restaurant-quality result.
  8. Place the tray on the middle rack of a static oven, preheated to 200°C, and set the timer for 30 minutes. During this stage, the sugars in the onions will begin to caramelise and the potatoes will finish cooking through, becoming tender. Around the 15-minute mark, take a look: you should smell the intense aroma of herbs and sweet onions filling the kitchen. Avoid opening the oven door too often to keep the heat constant for browning. If you notice one side browning faster, rotate the tray 180 degrees halfway through.
  9. For the finishing touch, switch the oven to grill mode and cook for a further 5-7 minutes, placing the tray slightly higher up. Watch closely through the glass: the edges of the potatoes should turn amber and the breadcrumbs should become a deep golden brown and crunchy. Remove the tray when the onions are softened and slightly charred at the tips, creating an irresistible contrast of textures. Let the dish rest for 2 minutes out of the oven before serving; this allows the flavours to settle. Do not cover the tray once out of the oven, or the steam will immediately soften that hard-earned crunch.

Tips

  • Parboil the potatoes for a few minutes before baking to achieve a creamy interior and a crispy surface.
  • Cut the onions into wedges of the same size as the potatoes to ensure even cooking of all ingredients.
  • Let the tray rest out of the oven for two minutes before serving to let the flavors stabilize.

Variations

  • Increase the amount of dried oregano and decrease the rosemary for a more typical Mediterranean aromatic note.
  • Cut the onions into thin slices instead of wedges if you want them to become almost caramelized and crispy.
