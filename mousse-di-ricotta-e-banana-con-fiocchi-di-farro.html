Ricotta and Banana Mousse with Spelt Flakes

4 servings · Prep 25 min · Cooking 0 min · Easy

A quick, no-cook cold breakfast, rich in calcium and potassium. The spelt flakes, left to rest in the cream, soften and become perfect for little ones to chew.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⬇️ Big saving🥕 Vegetarian🧒 Kid-friendly🇮🇹 Italian

A fresh and light spoon dessert that combines the creaminess of ricotta with the sweetness of a ripe banana. Spelt flakes add a rustic note that completes the tasting experience.

Ingredients

  • Fresh cow's milk ricotta (a creamy Italian whey cheese)200 g
  • Ripe banana1 pcs
  • Small wholegrain spelt flakes40 g
  • Orange juice10 ml

Allergens

Milk, Gluten

Method

  1. Start by peeling a ripe banana (approx. 120g) and slice it thinly into a glass or ceramic bowl. Immediately pour 10ml of freshly squeezed orange juice over the fruit to prevent enzymatic browning that would darken it. Use a tight-tined fork or a manual potato masher to reduce the banana into a very fine and completely smooth purée. The final result should be a fluid, fragrant cream with a uniform pale yellow colour. The chef's tip is to choose a banana with a skin that is slightly spotted with black: this is the secret to achieving natural sweetness without adding refined sugars.
  2. Take 200g of very fresh cow's milk ricotta and transfer it to a separate bowl to begin the aeration phase of the dairy product. Use an electric whisk or a vigorous hand whisk, working the ricotta at medium speed for exactly 2 minutes. This movement will incorporate tiny air bubbles, transforming the grainy texture of the ricotta into a frothy and velvety structure. You will obtain a shiny white base that almost resembles whipped cream in its lightness. Ensure you thoroughly drain any excess whey from the ricotta before starting, to prevent the mousse from becoming too watery at the bottom of the dish.
  3. Add the previously prepared banana purée to the bowl with the whipped ricotta, pouring it in a thin stream. Incorporate the two elements using a silicone spatula with slow, circular movements, moving from the bottom upwards so as not to deflate the mass. Continue mixing for about 30-40 seconds until the two mixtures are perfectly blended into a single cream nuance. The result should be a soft, streak-free blend with a delicate aroma of milk and fruit. My advice is not to use an immersion blender at this stage, as it would break down the ricotta's proteins, making the mixture too fluid.
  4. Precisely weigh 40g of small wholemeal spelt flakes and sprinkle them over the surface of the freshly created mousse. Use a metal spoon to distribute the flakes evenly throughout the mixture, ensuring every part of the cream is covered. Mix gently for about 20 seconds, avoiding crushing the flakes too much as they must remain intact. You will obtain a mousse enriched with a visible texture that is still slightly firm to the touch. The chef recommends using 'small' or 'mignon' flakes because they have a larger surface area and soften more uniformly than large ones.
  5. Cover the bowl with cling film in direct contact or an airtight lid and place it in the middle part of the refrigerator at approximately 4°C. Let the preparation rest for a minimum of 15-20 minutes; this interval is technical, not just for cooling, as it allows the spelt flakes to hydrate. During this rest, the flakes will absorb the natural moisture from the ricotta and orange juice, becoming tender and digestible. The result after resting will be a more compact and structured mousse, with the grains perfectly integrated. Never skip this step: raw wholemeal spelt flakes would be too tough and unpleasant on the palate, especially for children.
  6. Remove the mousse from the refrigerator about 10 minutes before actual consumption, leaving it at room temperature to take the chill off. This waiting time allows the fats in the ricotta to soften and the banana aromas to be fully released, ensuring a better perception of taste. The mousse should appear thick but not excessively stiff, ready to be worked one last time. The ideal result is a serving temperature of around 12-14°C, which is optimal for palatability. A professional secret is to avoid thermal shock: a mousse that is too cold numbs the taste buds and hides the natural sweetness of the fruit.
  7. Before proceeding to the final portioning, perform a quality test by picking up a single spelt flake between your thumb and index finger. Squeeze it gently: it should give way without resistance, proving it has reached perfect softness through hydration. If the texture satisfies you, give it one last energetic stir with a spoon for 10 seconds to restore creaminess and shine to the mousse that had stabilised in the cold. The mixture should return to a soft foam that 'writes' when dropped from the spoon. If the flakes are still too hard, extend the rest for another 5 minutes, adding a teaspoon of milk or water.
  8. Divide the ricotta and banana mousse into 4 small individual bowls or transparent dishes, levelling the surface with the back of a teaspoon. For a presentation dedicated to adults, you can decorate the surface with a pinch of reserved spelt flakes or a thin slice of fresh banana cut at the last moment. Serve immediately to enjoy the freshness of the ricotta and the airy texture of the foam. The visual result should be simple, rustic but extremely inviting, with the flakes well distributed. A little chef's touch: a very light dusting of ground cinnamon (if liked) will incredibly enhance the banana-ricotta pairing without adding calories.

Tips

  • Use very ripe bananas with black-spotted peels to achieve maximum natural sweetness.
  • Let the mixture rest in the fridge so the spelt flakes soften slightly.
  • Serve the mousse in single-portion glasses for a more refined and elegant presentation.

Variations

  • Replace the orange juice with lemon juice if you prefer a more citric note.
  • Add oat flakes instead of spelt for an even softer texture.
