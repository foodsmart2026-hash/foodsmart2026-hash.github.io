Sautéed Green Beans with Cherry Tomatoes and Capers

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

A classic Mediterranean summer side dish, where the freshness of the tomato meets the character of the capers. Ideal for those looking for a light but flavourful dish.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🌱 Vegan🌾 Gluten-free🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🇮🇹 Italian

A Mediterranean side dish highlighting the freshness of summer green beans. Withered cherry tomatoes and savory capers create a flavorful dressing, perfect alongside fish or meat mains.

Ingredients

  • Fresh green beans600 g
  • Cherry tomatoes250 g
  • Salted capers (capers preserved in salt, common in Italian pantries)15 g
  • Garlic1 clove
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Fresh basil5 leaves

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Start by preparing 600g of fresh green beans: trim them by snapping or cutting off the stem ends and wash them thoroughly under cold running water. If the beans are particularly long, cut them in half to make them easier to handle in the pan and more pleasant to eat. Ensure they are firm and have an even green colour before proceeding. Chef's tip: don't remove too much of the bean when trimming; only the fibrous tip needs to go.
  2. Bring a large pot of water to the boil, add a pinch of rock salt, and plunge in the green beans. Let them cook over a medium heat for about 8 minutes, checking the texture with a fork so they remain slightly 'al dente' at the core. This pre-cooking is vital to preserve their bright colour and ideal texture. Chef's tip: add a tiny pinch of bicarbonate of soda to the water if you want the beans to maintain a brilliant emerald green colour.
  3. While the beans are boiling, prepare the aromatics: rinse 15g of salted capers to remove excess salt and cut 250g of cherry tomatoes into halves or quarters. Peel the garlic clove but leave it whole so it can be easily removed later. This organisation, known as 'mise en place', will allow you to manage the next steps without stress. Chef's tip: lightly crush the garlic with the flat side of your knife to help release its essential oils into the dish.
  4. In a large non-stick frying pan, pour 30ml of extra virgin olive oil and add the prepared garlic clove. Heat over a very low flame for 2 minutes, letting the garlic turn slowly golden and infuse the oil without burning. When the garlic is pale gold and starts to sizzle gently, your aromatic base is ready. Chef's tip: tilt the pan slightly to pool the oil in one spot, allowing the garlic to fry more evenly.
  5. Add the chopped tomatoes and rinsed capers to the pan, turning the heat up to medium. Cook for about 5 minutes, stirring occasionally with a wooden spoon until the tomatoes begin to soften. You should achieve a light, colourful, and fragrant sauce created by the release of the tomatoes' juices. Chef's tip: do not add salt at this stage, as the capers will already release a significant amount of saltiness during cooking.
  6. Drain the green beans after 8 minutes of boiling and transfer them directly into the pan with the tomato and caper mixture. Increase the heat to high and sauté the vegetables for 2-3 minutes, moving the pan with a fluid wrist motion. The beans should coat perfectly in the sauce, becoming glossy and flavourful. Chef's tip: if the sauce dries out too quickly, add a tablespoon of the bean cooking water to keep the dish succulent.
  7. Taste a bean to check the seasoning and final texture, adjusting the salt only if necessary. Stir carefully one last time to ensure the capers and tomatoes are evenly distributed among the beans. The result should be a balanced dish where each ingredient retains its visual identity while being well combined. Chef's tip: a grind of fresh black pepper at the end can add a spicy note that enhances the tomato flavour.
  8. Turn off the heat, remove the garlic clove, and add 5 fresh basil leaves, tearing them roughly by hand. This avoids the basil oxidising and turning black, allowing the residual heat to release its Mediterranean aroma. Serve the sautéed green beans while still hot or at room temperature as a healthy and wholesome side dish. Chef's tip: tear the basil at the very last second to keep the aromatic volatile oils intact.

Tips

  • Be sure to rinse the capers thoroughly under running water to remove excess salt.
  • To keep the green beans crunchy and bright green, drain them immediately after eight minutes of boiling.
  • You can store the dish in the refrigerator for one day in an airtight container.

Variations

  • Add pitted Taggiasca olives for an even more intense savory note.
  • Substitute fresh basil with dried oregano for a more rustic aroma.
