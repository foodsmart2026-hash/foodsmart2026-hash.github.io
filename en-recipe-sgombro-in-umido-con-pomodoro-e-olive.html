Stewed Mackerel with Tomato and Olives

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 35 min · Medium

A flavourful and nutritious oily fish main course, perfect for making mackerel enjoyed by the whole family thanks to the creamy sauce. The combination of tomato and black olives tones down the intense flavour of the fish, making the recipe balanced and extremely economical.

A flavorful and rustic fish main course where mackerel is enhanced by a rich tomato and black olive sauce. The mackerel meat remains juicy thanks to the slow stewing process, making it perfect for serving with toasted bread.

Ingredients

  • Whole fresh mackerel (cleaned)800 g
  • Tomato pulp (chopped tomatoes)400 g
  • Pitted black olives80 g
  • Golden onion60 g
  • Dry white wine50 ml
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Garlic1 pz
  • Dried oregano5 g
  • Fresh parsley10 g
  • Fine salt4 g
  • Black pepper2 g

Allergens

Fish

Method

  1. Carefully wash 800 g of already cleaned mackerel under cold running water, removing any internal residue and patting it well with kitchen paper. Use a sharp knife to make two or three deep diagonal cuts on each side of the fish, reaching down to the central bone. This operation, to be done with a steady hand, will allow the heat and flavours of the sauce to penetrate evenly during cooking. You will have fish ready for the pan, dry and regularly scored. Chef's tip: if you prefer faster serving for children, you can fillet the mackerel before cooking, but reduce the final cooking time to just 5 minutes in total.
  2. Finely chop 60 g of golden onion and lightly crush a clove of garlic with the back of a heavy knife to facilitate the release of essential oils. Pour 30 ml of extra virgin olive oil into a very large non-stick pan and heat over medium-low heat for about a minute before adding the aromatics. Let them soften gently for 5-6 minutes, stirring often with a wooden spoon to prevent them from browning too quickly. The onion should become translucent and release a sweet scent, without any burnt parts which would make the dish bitter. Chef's tip: add a tablespoon of water during the sautéing to keep the temperature low and make the onion more digestible.
  3. Slightly increase the heat and pour 50 ml of dry white wine into the pan, letting the alcohol evaporate completely for about 2 minutes. Shake the pan with circular movements to blend the fats of the oil with the acidity of the wine until you no longer smell the pungent odour of alcohol rising from the steam. This deglazing serves to cut through the base and prepare the aromatic foundation needed to balance the natural fattiness of the oily fish. You will have a fragrant, slightly reduced base free of alcoholic sharpness. Chef's tip: use a good quality wine, the same one you will serve at the table, to avoid transferring metallic or overly acidic notes to the final sauce.
  4. Add 400 g of tomato pulp and 80 g of pitted black olives, whole or sliced according to your aesthetic preference. Adjust with salt and black pepper, also adding 5 g of dried oregano to recall the classic flavours of Mediterranean cuisine. Mix well with a wooden spoon and bring the mixture to a light boil while maintaining a medium heat. The sauce will start to take shape, blending the sweetness of the tomato with the savouriness of the olives. Chef's tip: if the tomato tastes too acidic, add a pinch of sugar (less than half a teaspoon) to perfectly balance the flavour of the seasoning.
  5. Lower the heat to minimum, cover the pan with a lid and let the sauce cook for about 10 minutes, stirring occasionally to check its density. The consistency should become slightly thicker and the tomato should lose its raw vegetable note, acquiring an intense and inviting red colour. If you notice the sauce drying out too quickly, add a couple of tablespoons of hot water to keep it fluid and creamy. You will get a rich and flavourful base, perfect for welcoming the fish. Chef's tip: do not over-salt at this stage, as the olives and the sauce reduction will naturally increase the savouriness of the dish during the rest of the preparation.
  6. Gently place the four mackerels in the pan, arranging them in a single layer so they are partially submerged in the hot tomato sauce. Cover again with the lid and maintain the heat at medium-low intensity, letting the fish cook for about 7 minutes without touching it. It is essential to maintain the integrity of the fish by avoiding unnecessary movement, so that the skin remains intact and the flesh cooks evenly thanks to the trapped steam. You will notice the fish starting to change colour, moving from translucent grey to an opaque and compact white. Chef's tip: if the pan isn't large enough, arrange the fish in an alternating head-to-tail pattern to optimise space and ensure all receive equal heat.
  7. Turn the mackerels very gently using a wide spatula or two spoons, taking care not to break the skin or tails. Continue cooking for another 6-7 minutes without the lid, occasionally spooning some sauce from the bottom of the pan over the back of the fish. The mackerel flesh should be tender to the touch and the skin should appear slightly coated by the tomato, a sign of perfect osmosis between the ingredients. The sauce will have reduced further, becoming glossy and enveloping thanks to the fusion with the Omega-3 fats released by the fish. Chef's tip: strictly avoid overcooking beyond the indicated times; mackerel is a fish with lean fibres that quickly becomes dry and fibrous if overdone.
  8. Turn off the heat and sprinkle the dish with 10 g of fresh parsley coarsely chopped at the moment to preserve all its freshness and volatile oils. Let everything rest for 2-3 minutes in the turned-off pan before proceeding to plate, allowing the flavours to stabilise and the fish fibres to fully relax. Distribute one mackerel per plate, drizzling generously with the tomato sauce and black olives. The final result will be a colourful, fragrant main course with the intense flavour typical of the 'cucina povera' maritime tradition. Chef's tip: accompany the mackerel with slices of toasted crusty bread or boiled potatoes, ideal for soaking up all the precious sauce left in the pan.

Tips

  • Let the white wine evaporate completely over high heat before adding the tomato pulp.
  • Avoid turning the fish too often during cooking to prevent the whole fillets from breaking.
  • Serve the dish very hot, finishing with a drizzle of raw extra virgin olive oil and fresh parsley.

Variations

  • Add a pinch of fresh chili flakes for a spicier and more intense version.
  • Replace black olives with desalted capers for an even more savory flavor profile.
