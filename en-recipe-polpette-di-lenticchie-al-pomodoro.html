Lentil meatballs in tomato sauce

💰 Save Euro

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 40 min · Medium

A plant-based alternative to classic meatballs, rich in fibre and high-quality proteins thanks to the combination of pulses and grains. An affordable and nutritious recipe that even children love thanks to its soft texture and tasty sauce.

Ingredients

  • Small dried lentils (mignon type)250 g
  • Wholewheat breadcrumbs80 g
  • Egg1 pz
  • Tomato passata (smooth sieved tomatoes)500 ml
  • Golden onion100 g
  • Carrot80 g
  • Celery50 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 ml
  • Dried oregano2 g
  • Fine salt5 g

Allergens

Gluten, Eggs, Celery

Method

  1. Start by rinsing the lentils under running water and boil them in plenty of unsalted water for about 20-25 minutes: they should be tender but not mushy, ready when they mash easily with a fork.
  2. While the lentils are cooking, finely chop the onion, carrot and celery; heat a tablespoon of extra virgin olive oil in a pan and soften half of the chopped vegetables for 5 minutes until the onion is translucent.
  3. Drain the lentils well and let them cool slightly, then transfer to a large bowl and mash about half of them with a fork to create a natural binding base.
  4. Add the sautéed vegetables, egg, wholewheat breadcrumbs and a pinch of salt to the lentils; knead with your hands until you obtain a firm and manageable mixture that is not too sticky.
  5. Form meatballs the size of a walnut (about 30-35 g each), compacting them well between your palms to prevent them from breaking during the braising process.
  6. In a large pan, heat the remaining oil and brown the rest of the chopped vegetables for 3 minutes, then pour in the tomato passata, oregano and a pinch of salt, bringing to a gentle simmer.
  7. Gently place the meatballs in the sauce, cover with a lid and cook over low heat for 15 minutes; halfway through, gently shake the pan instead of using a spoon to avoid breaking them.
  8. Remove the lid for the final 5 minutes to let the sauce reduce until thick and velvety, a sign that the flavours have perfectly blended.
  9. Serve the meatballs hot, drizzled with their sauce, preferably accompanied by a slice of toasted wholewheat bread to complete the amino acid profile.
