Lentil meatballs in tomato sauce

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 40 min · Medium

A plant-based alternative to classic meatballs, rich in fibre and high-quality proteins thanks to the combination of pulses and grains. An affordable and nutritious recipe that even children love thanks to its soft texture and tasty sauce.

A tasty comfort food that turns legumes into soft meatballs, slowly cooked in an oregano-scented tomato sauce. The dense texture of small lentils pairs perfectly with the sweetness of the classic carrot, celery, and onion mirepoix.

Ingredients

  • Small dried lentils (mignon type)250 g
  • Wholewheat breadcrumbs80 g
  • Egg1 pz
  • Tomato passata (smooth sieved tomatoes)500 ml
  • Golden onion100 g
  • Carrot80 g
  • Celery50 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 ml
  • Dried oregano2 g
  • Fine salt5 g

Allergens

Gluten, Eggs, Celery

Method

  1. Begin by thoroughly rinsing 250g of small dried lentils under cold running water to remove any impurities, then place them in a pot with plenty of cold, unsalted water. Bring to a boil and cook over medium heat for about 20-25 minutes, testing their consistency with a fork: they should be tender but still hold their shape without falling apart. Once cooked, drain the lentils using a fine-mesh sieve and let them cool completely so they lose any excess moisture. Chef's tip: do not add salt to the cooking water, otherwise the skins will become tough and won't absorb the subsequent flavours.
  2. While the lentils are resting, prepare the aromatic base by finely mincing 100g of onion, 80g of carrot, and 50g of celery with a sharp knife to achieve a uniform soffritto. Heat about 10ml of extra virgin olive oil in a non-stick frying pan and add half of the chopped vegetables, letting them soften over low heat for 5 minutes with a tablespoon of water if necessary. The vegetables should become translucent and fragrant without browning or burning. Chef's tip: cut the vegetables into tiny, regular cubes so they blend perfectly into the meatball mixture, improving the overall texture.
  3. Transfer the now-lukewarm lentils into a large bowl and, using a fork or a potato masher, firmly mash about half of them until you obtain a coarse puree. This step is essential because the starch released from the lentils will act as a natural binder, reducing the need for excessive amounts of other binding agents. Mix the mashed portion well with the whole lentils to maintain a pleasant variety of textures in every bite. Chef's tip: avoid using an immersion blender, as this would create a paste that is too smooth, making the meatballs soft and difficult to handle.
  4. Add the previously sautéed vegetables, the whole egg, 80g of wholemeal breadcrumbs, and about 2g of fine salt to the bowl. Mix vigorously with clean hands, incorporating all ingredients until the mixture is firm, homogeneous, and easily mouldable between your palms. If the mixture feels too wet, add an extra tablespoon of breadcrumbs, but be careful not to overdo it to prevent them from becoming too hard. Chef's tip: let the mixture rest in the fridge for 15 minutes before shaping; as the breadcrumbs hydrate, the mixture will become much more stable.
  5. Take a small portion of the mixture at a time and shape into walnut-sized meatballs, weighing approximately 30-35g each, by rolling them between your palms with light pressure. Ensure the surface is smooth and free of obvious cracks, compacting them well to prevent them from breaking apart during the subsequent simmering in the sauce. Arrange them neatly on a tray lined with baking paper as you go. Chef's tip: lightly dampen your hands with water or oil before shaping to prevent the mixture from sticking to your skin, resulting in a smoother finish.
  6. Prepare the base for the final cooking phase by heating the remaining 30ml of extra virgin olive oil in a wide, high-sided pan. Add the remaining half of the chopped onion, carrot, and celery and let it sauté over medium heat for about 3 minutes, stirring frequently with a wooden spoon. Once the soffritto releases its aroma, you will have created the ideal savoury base for the tomato sauce. Chef's tip: add a tiny pinch of salt to the vegetables at this stage to help them release their juices and intensify the flavour of the base.
  7. Pour 500ml of tomato puree into the pan with the vegetables, add 2g of dried oregano and the remaining 3g of salt, bringing it to a gentle boil over high heat. Once the sauce begins to simmer, turn the heat down to low to stabilise the cooking temperature. Stir well to distribute the oregano, which will provide an irresistible Mediterranean aroma to the dish. Chef's tip: if the tomato sauce tastes too acidic, add a splash of water or a tiny pinch of sugar to balance the pH of the sauce.
  8. Place the lentil meatballs very gently into the tomato sauce, trying not to overlap them to ensure even cooking for each one. Cover the pan with a tight-fitting lid and let them cook over very low heat for about 15 minutes, avoiding touching the meatballs with utensils during the first few minutes. To move or turn them, gently shake the pan horizontally by the handle to protect their shape. Chef's tip: slow, covered cooking allows the steam to cook the centre of the meatball evenly without the outside drying out or crumbling.
  9. Remove the lid during the last 5 minutes of cooking to allow the sauce to reduce and concentrate the flavours until it reaches a velvety consistency that coats the meatballs perfectly. Turn off the heat and let the dish rest for a couple of minutes before serving, allowing the structure of the meatballs to stabilise further. Serve by generously spooning the sauce over the meatballs and accompany with toasted wholemeal bread. Chef's tip: a final drizzle of raw oil and a grating of lemon zest can provide an unexpected freshness that balances the earthiness of the lentils.

Tips

  • If the mixture is too moist, add an extra tablespoon of whole wheat breadcrumbs to firm it up.
  • Let the meatballs rest in the sauce for a few minutes with the heat off before serving to let the flavors meld.
  • You can store leftover meatballs in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to two days.

Variations

  • Replace the dried oregano with fresh thyme for a more pungent aromatic note.
  • Add a pinch of chili flakes to the tomato purée if you prefer a more lively and spicy version.
