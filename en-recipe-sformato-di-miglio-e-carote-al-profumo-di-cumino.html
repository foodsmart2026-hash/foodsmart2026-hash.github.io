Cumin-scented Millet and Carrot Bake

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 45 min · Medium

A gluten-free single-course dish using millet, an inexpensive cereal rich in minerals. The sweetness of the carrots pairs perfectly with the exotic aroma of cumin.

A nutritious flan that combines the sweetness of grated carrots with the spicy character of cumin. Millet offers a pleasant texture on the palate, enriched by the crunchy touch of sunflower seeds on top.

Ingredients

  • Hulled millet200 g
  • Carrots400 g
  • Red onion1 pcs
  • Ground cumin5 g
  • Sunflower seeds20 g
  • Vegetable stock
    Make it at home: Vegetable broth ›    500 ml
  • Extra virgin olive oil20 ml

Allergens

Celery

Method

  1. Start by thoroughly rinsing 200g of hulled millet under cold running water using a fine-mesh sieve to remove any bitter saponins. In a heavy-bottomed pot, heat one teaspoon of the 20ml extra virgin olive oil and toast the millet over medium heat for about 2-3 minutes, stirring frequently with a wooden spoon. This process should continue until you notice an intense nutty aroma, signaling that the grains are ready. Be careful not to burn them by keeping the heat steady but not excessive.
  2. Pour 500ml of boiling vegetable broth over the toasted millet, cover with a lid, and lower the heat to minimum for a gentle absorption cooking method. Let it simmer for about 15-20 minutes without ever stirring, allowing the grains to swell naturally and remain intact. The final result should be a cereal that has absorbed all the liquid, being tender yet still slightly al dente. The chef recommends not lifting the lid during the first 15 minutes to maintain constant steam inside the pot.
  3. While the millet is cooking, prepare the aromatic base: peel and grate 400g of carrots using the coarse holes of a grater and finely slice the red onion. In a large non-stick pan, heat the remaining olive oil and add the onion, letting it soften over low heat for 5 minutes until translucent. Then add the carrots and 5g of cumin powder, stirring well to distribute the spices. Cook for about 10 minutes until the carrots are tender and fragrant.
  4. Once the millet is ready, turn off the heat and fluff the grains gently using a fork, making upward motions to incorporate air. Transfer the millet to a large bowl and let it cool slightly for a few minutes so the flavours can settle and the texture becomes less sticky. You will notice the grains separating easily without breaking. A little secret: if the millet seems too compact, add a drop of raw oil while fluffing.
  5. Combine the fluffed millet with the carrot and onion mixture seasoned with cumin in the bowl, stirring with a spatula to achieve an even distribution of the orange colour. At this stage, add the 20g of sunflower seeds, which will provide a pleasant crunchy note and beneficial fats to the dish. Mix everything well until you have a homogeneous and compact yet workable dough. Ensure the seeds are distributed so every bite is balanced.
  6. Take a baking dish and lightly grease it with a pastry brush to prevent the flan from sticking to the sides during baking. Pour the mixture into the dish and use the back of a slightly dampened spoon to level the surface evenly, pressing gently. The surface must appear smooth and the thickness consistent throughout the dish to ensure even baking. The chef suggests not pressing too hard to avoid the flan becoming overly rubbery.
  7. Preheat your conventional oven to 190°C and place the dish on the middle rack for about 25 minutes. During this time, the heat will dry out residual moisture and allow the flavours to meld perfectly, while the edges will begin to pull away slightly from the sides of the dish. The texture will become firmer and more compact, ideal for cutting into slices or cubes. Check that the surface doesn't brown too quickly; if it does, cover with foil for the final minutes.
  8. To finish and achieve that irresistible professional crust, set the oven to the grill function for the last 3 minutes of cooking. Closely monitor the browning through the oven glass: the surface should turn a beautiful golden hazelnut colour and the sunflower seeds on top should be toasted. This final step not only improves aesthetics but also incredibly intensifies the cumin aroma. Once out of the oven, let the flan rest for at least 10 minutes before cutting.
  9. After resting, cut the flan into regular cubes or slices, depending on the occasion, and serve it warm to best appreciate the aromatic nuances. Accompany each portion with a fresh seasonal salad dressed with a hint of lemon, which will cleanse the palate from the richness of the cumin. The result will be a balanced, nutritious, and visually appealing dish thanks to the colour contrast between the orange and the green of the salad. Remember that post-cooking rest is crucial so the flan doesn't crumble when cut.

Tips

  • Rinse the millet thoroughly under running water to remove impurities and any bitter aftertaste.
  • Fluff the grains well with a fork after cooking to prevent them from compacting too much.
  • Be sure to grease the baking dish well to make it easier to remove the portions after baking.

Variations

  • You can enrich the top by adding sesame seeds along with the sunflower seeds for extra crunch.
  • Replace the cumin with turmeric powder for a different aromatic profile and a more intense color.
