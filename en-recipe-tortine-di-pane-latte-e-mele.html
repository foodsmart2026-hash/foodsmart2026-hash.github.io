Bread, Milk, and Apple Mini Cakes

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 35 min · Medium

An anti-waste recovery recipe that transforms stale bread into a delicious and nutritious breakfast. It is an economical, family-style version of the classic English bread pudding, revisited with Mediterranean ingredients.

A traditional recipe that transforms stale bread into small baked treats. The sweetness of apples combines with lemon zest, offering a moist texture reminiscent of old-fashioned snacks.

Ingredients

  • Stale bread (Italian 'pane comune' style)200 g
  • Whole milk400 ml
  • Eggs2 pcs
  • Sugar70 g
  • Apples2 pcs
  • Lemon zest1 pcs
  • Butter10 g

Allergens

Gluten, Milk, Eggs

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the base of the dessert by cutting 200g of stale bread into cubes of approximately 1.5 cm, ensuring uniformity for even liquid absorption. Transfer the cubes into a large glass or ceramic mixing bowl that provides ample space for mixing in the later stages. Ensure the bread is completely dry; the drier it is, the better it will soak up the liquids, turning into a velvety custard-like base. Chef's tip: If the bread crust is excessively hard or burnt, partially remove it to achieve a more refined, pudding-like final texture.
  2. Warm 400ml of whole milk in a small saucepan over low heat until it reaches a lukewarm temperature, roughly 40-45°C, ensuring it does not boil. Pour the warm milk directly over the bread cubes in the bowl, pressing down gently with the back of a spoon so all the bread is submerged. Let the mixture rest for about 15-20 minutes at room temperature, stirring occasionally to encourage deep hydration of the fibres. The result should be completely softened bread that begins to break apart easily. Chef's tip: Cover the bowl with a plate or cling film to trap moisture and residual heat during the soaking process.
  3. In a separate bowl, crack 2 medium eggs and add 70g of granulated sugar along with the grated zest of one unwaxed lemon, taking care not to include the bitter white pith. Use a hand whisk or electric beaters to cream the mixture for about 3-4 minutes, working vigorously until you achieve a pale, frothy mass that has slightly increased in volume. This step is crucial for incorporating air, which will keep the cakes light despite the dense bread base. Chef's tip: Always use room-temperature eggs to encourage a more stable and voluminous foam.
  4. Return to the bowl with the now-softened bread and work it with a fork or manual whisk, mashing the cubes against the sides until they turn into a thick pulp free of large lumps. Now, fold the egg and sugar mixture into the bread, pouring it in a thin stream while stirring with bottom-to-top movements to avoid deflating the air bubbles. You should obtain a fluid, creamy batter with a very intense citrus aroma. Chef's tip: If you prefer a perfectly smooth texture, you can use an immersion blender for a few seconds, but do not over-process or the cake may become rubbery.
  5. Wash and peel the 2 apples, removing the cores and cutting them first into quarters and then into very thin slices, about 2-3 mm thick. Incorporate the apple slices into the bread and egg batter, stirring gently with a spatula to distribute them evenly without breaking them. The apples will release moisture and natural sweetness during baking, melting into the dessert base. The visual result should be a rich mixture where the slices are well-coated by the bread cream. Chef's tip: Add a pinch of cinnamon if you desire a warmer, spiced aroma that pairs perfectly with the apples.
  6. Prepare 4 individual 10cm ramekins by thoroughly greasing the insides with 10g of softened butter, paying close attention to the edges and the bottom to ensure easy removal or cleaning. If preferred, you can use a single small baking tin, but individual portions ensure faster cooking and a more elegant presentation. Divide the batter equally among the containers, filling them up to about 1 cm from the rim to prevent overflow during the natural rise in the oven. Chef's tip: Sprinkle the bottom of the buttered ramekins with a light dusting of sugar to create a slightly caramelised base.
  7. Level the surface of the puddings with the back of a spoon and, if desired, add an extra pinch of sugar or small dots of butter on top to help form a golden crust. Preheat your oven to 180°C (fan-assisted or static), ensuring the temperature is stable before placing the ramekins inside. Place the ramekins on the middle rack for optimal heat distribution and bake for 30-35 minutes. The final result should be a well-browned, almost crisp surface that contrasts with the soft heart. Chef's tip: If you notice the tops browning too quickly, cover them with a sheet of foil for the final 10 minutes.
  8. At the end of the baking time, check the consistency by lightly tapping the centre of the cake: it should feel firm and offer slight resistance, similar to a flan or a baked pudding. Carefully remove from the oven and let the ramekins cool for at least 10-15 minutes before serving, as this resting period allows the structure to set and the flavours to meld. Serve the cakes directly in their containers while still warm to fully enjoy the internal softness and the aroma of bread and apple. Chef's tip: Serve with a scoop of vanilla ice cream or a dollop of unsweetened whipped cream for an exceptional contrast in temperature and texture.

Tips

  • Cut the bread into uniform cubes to allow the milk to be absorbed evenly.
  • Let the bread soak in the milk for at least ten minutes before mashing it with a fork.
  • Brush the surface with a thin layer of melted butter before baking for a golden crust.

Variations

  • Add a pinch of cinnamon to the batter to enrich the apple fragrance.
  • Replace lemon zest with orange zest for a more intense aroma.
