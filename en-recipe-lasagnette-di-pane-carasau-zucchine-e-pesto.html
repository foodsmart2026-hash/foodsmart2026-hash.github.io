Pane Carasau, Courgette and Pesto Lasagnette

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 20 min · Medium

A 'lazy' and light version of traditional lasagna that replaces pasta with Sardinian flatbread. A fresh, summery and very impressive dish.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🇮🇹 Italian

Ingredients

  • Pane Carasau (traditional Sardinian crispy thin flatbread)200 g
  • medium courgettes3 pcs
  • ready-made Béchamel sauce400 ml
  • Pesto alla Genovese (basil and pine nut sauce)100 g
  • pizza mozzarella125 g
  • Parmigiano Reggiano (aged Italian hard cheese)30 g

Allergens

Gluten, Milk, Tree nuts

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the 3 medium courgettes: wash them thoroughly and slice them into very thin, uniform rounds, ideally about 2 millimetres thick. Heat a drizzle of extra virgin olive oil in a large frying pan and sear the courgettes over a high heat for about 5 minutes, adding a pinch of salt halfway through. The vegetables should be lightly golden on the surface but still crisp at the core, maintaining a vibrant green colour. My advice is not to cover the pan, so the moisture evaporates immediately, preventing the courgettes from turning mushy.
  2. Now focus on the creamy base of your lasagne by mixing the 400 ml of ready-made béchamel sauce with 100 g of basil pesto in a large bowl. Use a hand whisk or a wooden spoon to combine the two with circular motions until you achieve a smooth cream with a uniform pastel green hue. Ensure the pesto is perfectly distributed to guarantee a balanced flavour in every layer of the dish. If the béchamel seems too stiff, you can add a tablespoon of milk to make it thinner and easier to spread.
  3. Prepare the setup for hydrating the pane carasau by lightly warming some vegetable broth or salted water in a wide, shallow bowl. The liquid should be lukewarm, not boiling, to prevent the bread from disintegrating instantly on contact. Keep the bowl close to the baking dish you will be using to make the assembly quick and tidy. A chef's trick: add a drop of oil to the water to prevent the pieces of bread from sticking to each other once they are wet.
  4. Take a rectangular baking dish and lightly butter the bottom and sides, or spread a thin layer of the pesto béchamel to create a non-stick base. Quickly dip a piece of pane carasau (about 40-50 g per layer) into the lukewarm liquid for no more than one second per side, just to soften it. Arrange the bread on the bottom of the dish, trying to cover the entire surface and slightly overlapping the pieces if necessary. The bread should be flexible but must absolutely not fall apart between your fingers.
  5. Spread a couple of generous tablespoons of the pesto béchamel over the first layer of bread, smoothing it out evenly with the back of a spoon. Add about a quarter of the sautéed courgettes, distributing them well, and top with a handful of diced pizza mozzarella (about 30 g). Pizza mozzarella is ideal because it releases less moisture during cooking, preventing the lasagne from becoming too watery. Try to maintain an even thickness to ensure uniform cooking throughout the dish.
  6. Continue by repeating the process for another 3 or 4 layers, alternating the soaked bread, béchamel cream, courgettes, and mozzarella until almost all ingredients are used. As you assemble the layers, press down lightly with the palm of your hand to compact the lasagne and remove any air bubbles. This step is essential for obtaining a stable, well-defined slice when served. Remember to save a portion of the béchamel and the parmesan for the top finish.
  7. Complete the preparation by covering the final layer of bread with the remaining pesto béchamel, making sure to seal the corners well to prevent the bread from drying out too much in the oven. Evenly sprinkle the surface with the 30 g of grated Parmigiano Reggiano, which will create a savoury crust. The surface should look rich and well-covered, ready for browning. An extra touch? A pinch of freshly ground black pepper over the parmesan to enhance the aroma of the pesto.
  8. Preheat your conventional oven to 200°C and, once up to temperature, place the dish on the middle shelf for about 15-18 minutes. During this time, the pane carasau will absorb the liquids from the béchamel and courgettes, transforming into a soft sheet similar to fresh pasta. Check that the edges are starting to bubble slightly, a sign that the heat has reached the core of the lasagne. If you see the surface browning too quickly, cover it temporarily with a sheet of foil.
  9. For the finishing touch, switch the oven to grill mode at maximum power for the last 2-3 minutes of cooking. Constantly monitor the lasagne until classic golden bubbles and a crispy crust form on the surface. Remove the dish from the oven and let it rest for at least 5-10 minutes before slicing; this allows the bread fibres to stabilise and the flavours to meld perfectly. Resting is the secret to serving a perfect slice that doesn't fall apart on the plate.
