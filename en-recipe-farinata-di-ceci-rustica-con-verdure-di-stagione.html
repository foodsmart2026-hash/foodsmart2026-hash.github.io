Rustic Chickpea Farinata with Seasonal Vegetables

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

A naturally gluten-free and vegan one-dish meal. Chickpea farinata (a traditional Italian savoury pancake) is cheap and high in protein, ideal for a nutritious and quick meal.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🌱 Vegan🌾 Gluten-free🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🇮🇹 Italian

Ingredients

  • Chickpea flour (gram flour)300 g
  • Water900 ml
  • Extra virgin olive oil50 ml
  • Fresh or frozen spinach200 g
  • Fresh rosemary1 rametto
  • Salt and pepperq.b.

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Begin by pouring 300g of chickpea flour into a large mixing bowl and slowly adding 900ml of room-temperature water in a steady stream. Use a hand whisk with constant circular motions, starting from the centre to dissolve every lump and prevent the flour from settling at the bottom. This process requires about 5 minutes of focused attention to achieve a liquid mixture that is perfectly smooth and homogeneous. The result should resemble a thin chickpea milk without any floury bits; my chef's tip is to sift the flour beforehand to make this initial stage significantly easier.
  2. Allow the batter to rest for between 2 and 4 hours at room temperature, or even overnight in the fridge if you prefer superior digestibility. During this time, the flour fibres will fully hydrate and the enzymes will begin to break down the complex starches. You will notice a thin white foam forming on the surface: gently remove it with a slotted spoon to ensure a clear and clean finish. The chef's secret is not to rush, as adequate resting prevents that unpleasant 'raw bread' sensation on the palate.
  3. While the batter rests, turn your attention to the 200g of spinach; if fresh, wash thoroughly and chop coarsely with a sharp knife. If using frozen spinach, let it defrost completely and squeeze it with extreme force between your hands or inside a clean tea towel to remove every trace of residual moisture. The spinach must feel almost dry to the touch so as not to compromise the consistency of the farinata during baking. Excess water would make the base soggy rather than pleasantly firm.
  4. Preheat your oven to its maximum possible power, ideally between 220°C and 250°C, setting it to static mode or a specific pizza setting if available. Take a large round or rectangular baking tray, preferably made of tinned copper or heavy aluminium, and grease it evenly with a portion of the 50ml extra virgin olive oil. The tray must be well-oiled to allow the farinata to release easily and to encourage the formation of a crisp bottom crust. The chef recommends placing the empty tray in the oven for a couple of minutes before pouring in the mixture to create an immediate thermal shock.
  5. Take the batter again, add salt to taste, and incorporate the remaining 50ml of extra virgin olive oil (accounting for what was used for the tray) by whisking energetically. Now fold in the previously prepared spinach and the needles from a sprig of fresh rosemary, finely chopped just before adding to release all their essential oils. Stir gently with a spatula to distribute the vegetables and aromatics evenly throughout the liquid. The scent of fresh rosemary combined with quality oil will be the olfactory indicator of a dish that is about to become excellent.
  6. Pour the mixture into the hot tray, being careful not to burn yourself, aiming for a liquid depth of about 1 centimetre. If the tray is the correct size, the mixture will naturally spread towards the edges without needing much intervention. Ensure the spinach doesn't clump in one spot but is scattered homogeneously across the surface. The visual result should be a golden bed dotted with the bright green of the spinach and the dark green of the rosemary. Remember: a farinata that is too thick will be rubbery, while one that is too thin will become dry.
  7. Bake immediately in the middle or upper-middle part of the oven for approximately 20-25 minutes, constantly monitoring the surface colour. The farinata is ready when the edges begin to pull away slightly from the tray and a crackled, amber crust forms on top. If after 20 minutes the surface still appears pale, activate the grill function for the final 2-3 minutes to achieve that characteristic rustic browning. Do not overcook, or the interior will lose its pleasant moisture and become too tough.
  8. Remove the farinata from the oven and let it rest in the tray for at least 5 minutes before cutting; this allows the structure to set perfectly. Dust the surface with plenty of freshly ground black pepper, which completes the aromatic profile with a pungent, warm note. Cut it into irregular slices or diamonds, as per Ligurian and Tuscan tradition, and serve it while still hot and steaming. My final chef's tip is to enjoy it alongside a fresh tomato or rocket salad, as the acidity of raw vegetables beautifully balances the richness of the chickpea flour.
