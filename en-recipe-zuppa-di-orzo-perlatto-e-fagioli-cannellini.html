Pearl Barley and Cannellini Bean Soup

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 45 min · Medium

A perfect comfort food for cold days, rich in complex carbohydrates and protein. It is an affordable dish as it uses dried or canned pulses and budget-friendly grains.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🌱 Vegan🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🇮🇹 Italian

Ingredients

  • Pearl barley200 g
  • Cooked cannellini beans400 g
  • Carrot, celery, onion (soffritto mix)150 g
  • Tomato passata (sieved tomatoes)100 ml
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Bay leaves2 foglie
  • Water or stock1.5 l

Allergens

Gluten, Celery

Method

  1. Begin by thoroughly rinsing the 200g of pearl barley under cold running water using a fine-mesh sieve. Agitate the grains with your fingers until the water runs completely clear, which removes any dust or surface starch residues. Let the barley drain well for a few minutes before moving to the next stage. You will have clean, translucent grains ready for even cooking. Chef's tip: Do not skip this step, as removing excess starch prevents the soup from becoming overly gluey.
  2. Take a deep, heavy-bottomed pot, pour in half of the extra virgin olive oil (about 15ml), and heat over a medium flame. Add the 150g of finely chopped mirepoix (carrot, celery, and onion) and sauté gently for about 8-10 minutes. Stir frequently with a wooden spoon so the vegetables soften without browning, releasing all their natural sweetness. The result should be translucent, fragrant, and lightly golden vegetables. Chef's tip: If the vegetables start to stick, add a tablespoon of water to encourage gentle, even sweating.
  3. At this point, add the 100ml of tomato purée and the 2 whole bay leaves to the sautéed base. Let the mixture simmer for about 2 minutes over medium-low heat, stirring carefully to slightly reduce the sauce. This phase allows the tomato to lose its initial acidity and meld with the aromatic base. You will notice a deep red colour and a rich, concentrated aroma. Chef's tip: Lightly rub the bay leaves between your palms before adding them to release more of their essential oils.
  4. Pour the previously rinsed barley into the pot with the aromatic base. Slightly increase the heat and toast the grains for 1-2 minutes, stirring constantly and vigorously. This technique seals the surface of the grain, ensuring it remains 'al dente' even after long boiling. The grains will appear shiny and thoroughly coated in the seasoned oil. Chef's tip: Toasting is essential to prevent the barley from becoming mushy on the outside while remaining undercooked at the core.
  5. Add 1.5 litres of hot water or boiling vegetable stock, pouring it in gradually to maintain the simmer. Bring to a boil, then reduce the heat to low and simmer for approximately 30 minutes, covering the pot with a slightly ajar lid. Stir occasionally to ensure nothing sticks to the bottom and check that the liquid consistently covers the grains. The barley will begin to swell, and the liquid will start to thicken. Chef's tip: Always keep some extra hot liquid nearby in case the barley absorbs more than expected during this phase.
  6. After the first 30 minutes, thoroughly rinse the 400g of pre-cooked cannellini beans and add them to the pot. Continue simmering for another 10 minutes, allowing the flavours of the pulses and the cereal to blend perfectly. Keep the heat low and stir gently to avoid breaking the beans at this stage. The soup should look rich and well-balanced. Chef's tip: If using canned beans, rinse them very well under cold water to remove the canning liquid, which can negatively affect the final flavour.
  7. To achieve an irresistibly velvety consistency, take a small portion of the beans and crush them against the inner side of the pot with the back of a spoon, or use a ladle to remove a small amount and blend roughly before stirring back in. Mix everything well to distribute this creaminess throughout the soup. You will see the texture transform instantly, becoming thicker and more comforting. The result is a creamy soup that beautifully binds the whole barley grains. Chef's tip: Do not blend the entire soup; the appeal of this dish lies in the contrast between the creamy base and the bite of the whole grains.
  8. Turn off the heat, remove the bay leaves, and let the soup rest in the covered pot for at least 5 minutes before serving. This resting period is crucial for the temperatures to stabilise and for the flavours to reach their peak. Serve in deep bowls, finishing each portion with the remaining 15ml of raw extra virgin olive oil and a generous crack of fresh black pepper. The soup will be thick, aromatic, and deeply satisfying. Chef's tip: Serve the soup warm rather than piping hot to fully appreciate the aromatic complexity of the raw oil and bay leaves.
