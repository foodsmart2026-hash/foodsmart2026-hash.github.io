White Cauliflower Risotto with a Mild Melting Cheese Heart

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 20 min · Medium

A risotto where the cauliflower disappears into the creamy base, making the dish very sweet and nutritious. Ideal for encouraging children to enjoy cruciferous vegetables.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⬇️ Big saving🌱 Vegan🧒 Kid-friendly🌾 Gluten-free🇮🇹 Italian

This white risotto is a prime example of delicate cooking, where the cauliflower nearly melts to create a creamy base. The heart of mild caciotta cheese adds a gooey, delicious element to every spoonful. It is a dish with neutral tones and an enveloping flavor.

Ingredients

  • Risotto rice (Originario type - a short-grain Italian rice)160 g
  • Cauliflower (florets only)250 g
  • Mild caciotta cheese (a soft, semi-cooked Italian cheese)40 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil15 ml
  • Small brown onion1/2 pc

Allergens

Milk

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the vegetables: thoroughly wash the 250g of cauliflower florets and use a sharp knife to mince them into tiny crumbs, almost the size of rice grains. Finely chop the half golden onion, ensuring the pieces are uniform for even cooking. This process takes about 5-7 minutes and is essential to ensure the cauliflower breaks down during cooking to create a natural cream. The final result should be a fine mince that releases a fresh, vegetal aroma. Chef's tip: use a food processor with pulse mode if you prefer an even smoother texture and want to save time, but don't turn it into a raw purée.
  2. Place a heavy-based saucepan over medium-low heat and add the chopped onion along with two tablespoons of water. Let it sweat gently for about 3-4 minutes, stirring with a wooden spoon until the liquid has almost completely evaporated. No oil is used at this stage to maintain the delicate flavour and digestibility required for young children. The onion should become translucent and soft without browning or burning. Chef's tip: if the onion starts to stick, add another splash of water to prevent it from turning bitter.
  3. Add the 160g of Originario rice and the cauliflower crumbs to the saucepan with the softened onion. Slightly increase the heat to medium and toast everything for about 2 minutes, stirring continuously to prevent the grains from burning. You will notice a faint nutty aroma and see the rice grains becoming shiny and semi-transparent at the edges. This step is vital to seal the grain and allow a gradual release of starch during cooking. Chef's tip: dry-toasting with the vegetables helps the cauliflower release its natural sugars immediately.
  4. Cover the rice and cauliflower with boiling water (or a very light, salt-free vegetable stock) until it rises about two centimetres above the ingredients. Lower the heat to medium-low and start stirring regularly to stimulate the starch release, which creates the creamy sauce. The liquid should simmer steadily but not violently, maintaining even heat throughout the pan. You will notice the water becoming pleasantly cloudy as the cauliflower starts to soften. Chef's tip: always keep a small pot of boiling water on the hob next to your saucepan to ensure the rice's boil is never interrupted.
  5. Continue cooking for approximately 15-18 minutes in total, adding a ladle of hot water whenever the bottom of the pan starts to look dry. Stir with slow, circular motions, observing how the cauliflower progressively disintegrates into a white purée that coats every grain of rice. The flame must remain gentle to ensure the rice cooks evenly from the core outwards without breaking. The result will be soft rice immersed in a velvety, sweet-scented cauliflower cream. Chef's tip: in the last 5 minutes of cooking, reduce the amount of water added to better control the final thickness.
  6. While the rice finishes cooking, prepare the creamy element: take the 40g of mild caciotta cheese and cut it into tiny 2-3mm cubes or grate it coarsely. This small size is crucial so the cheese melts instantly upon contact with the residual heat of the rice without creating rubbery lumps. Ensure the cheese is at room temperature to encourage perfect melting. You will have a cheese mince ready to release all its milky sweetness. Chef's tip: choose a very young caciotta to guarantee optimal melting properties.
  7. Once the rice is cooked and the texture is creamy, turn off the heat completely and move the saucepan away from the hob. Add the caciotta cubes and the 15ml of extra virgin olive oil, drizzling it over the hot mixture. Begin the 'mantecatura' stage by stirring vigorously with a wooden spoon, using quick movements to incorporate air and emulsify the fats with the remaining starch. The risotto will transform, becoming glossy, well-combined, and incredibly inviting. Chef's tip: creaming off the heat is the secret to preserving the fruity aroma of the extra virgin olive oil.
  8. Cover the saucepan with a lid and let the risotto rest for exactly 2 minutes before serving. This short rest allows the flavours to settle and the rice structure to stabilise, achieving that 'rippling' consistency typical of the best risottos. When plating, the rice should glide gently onto the plate without resistance. The final aroma will be a perfect balance between the sweetness of the cauliflower and the milky note of the cheese. Chef's tip: if the rice seems too stiff after resting, add one final tablespoon of hot water and stir once more before serving.

Tips

  • Finely mince the cauliflower so it almost completely dissolves during the rice cooking process.
  • Add the cheese only after turning off the heat for a perfect mantecatura that doesn't separate.
  • If the rice gets too dry, always keep some extra boiling water handy.

Variations

  • You can use Taleggio cheese instead of caciotta for a bolder, more aromatic taste.
  • Substitute the extra virgin olive oil with a small knob of butter during the final mixing for extra glossiness.
