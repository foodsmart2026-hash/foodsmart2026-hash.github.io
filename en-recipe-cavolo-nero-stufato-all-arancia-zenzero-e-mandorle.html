Braised cavolo nero with orange, ginger and almonds

🌿 Seasonal recipe

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 20 min · Easy

An aromatic and healthy side dish that balances the earthy notes of cavolo nero with the freshness of orange and a spicy touch of ginger.

Ingredients

  • Cavolo nero600 g
  • Orange (juice and zest)1
  • Freshly grated ginger15 g
  • Flaked almonds40 g
  • Garlic1 clove
  • Extra virgin olive oil3 tbsp
  • Salt and chilli flakesto taste

Allergens

Tree nuts

Method

  1. Start by cleaning 600 g of cavolo nero (black kale): remove the tough central rib from each leaf and cut the tender part into pieces about 4 cm wide. Wash the leaves thoroughly in plenty of cold water and drain them without drying too much, as the residual moisture will be essential for cooking. The result should be clean, uniformly chopped greens that are still slightly wet. Chef's tip: don't throw away the ribs; you can boil and blend them to enrich a vegetable soup or minestrone.
  2. Take a small non-stick pan and add 40 g of flaked almonds to dry-toast them without adding any fats. Heat over medium-low heat for about 2-3 minutes, constantly tossing the pan to prevent the almonds from burning against the heat. When you see the flakes have taken on a uniform golden colour and release an intense nutty aroma, remove them from the heat immediately and place them in a small bowl. Chef's tip: almonds continue to cook from residual heat, so remove them a moment before they reach the desired shade.
  3. In a very large frying pan or sauté pan, heat 3 tablespoons of extra virgin olive oil along with one 'clothed' garlic clove (crushed but with the skin on) and a pinch of chilli flakes to taste. Maintain a medium heat for about a minute, tilting the pan slightly so the garlic is submerged in the oil, releasing its full aroma without burning. You will achieve a highly fragrant oil and garlic that is lightly golden on the surface. Chef's tip: leaving the skin on the garlic makes the flavour more delicate and prevents the inner core from becoming too pungent or bitter.
  4. Add the 600 g of cavolo nero to the pan, being careful of any oil splashes caused by the residual water on the leaves. Season immediately with a pinch of salt to encourage the release of vegetable juices and cover instantly with a tight-fitting lid. Use medium heat to generate the steam necessary for braising the kale. You will see the volume of the leaves begin to reduce significantly within a couple of minutes. Chef's tip: if the leaves seem too dry at the start, add a couple of tablespoons of water to create the perfect steam chamber.
  5. Let the cavolo nero braise for about 8-10 minutes, keeping the lid closed and the heat at a moderate intensity. During this time, the kale fibres will soften thanks to the combined action of heat and trapped steam, while maintaining a bright green colour. Shake the pan occasionally to redistribute the ingredients without lifting the lid too often. By the end, the kale should be tender to the bite but not mushy. Chef's tip: do not overcook during this stage to avoid losing nutritional properties and colour vibrancy.
  6. Remove the lid and discard the garlic clove, then add 15 g of freshly grated ginger and the grated zest of one unwaxed orange. Mix well with a wooden spoon so the new aromatics are evenly distributed among the hot kale leaves. You will immediately smell a fresh, pungent, and citrusy fragrance that transforms the character of the dish. The texture will remain moist and inviting. Chef's tip: grate only the orange part of the zest, as the white pith underneath is bitter and would ruin the balance of the dish.
  7. Pour the juice of one squeezed orange into the pan and slightly increase the heat to medium-high. Continue cooking without a lid for another 5 minutes, stirring often to encourage the evaporation of the water content in the juice. The liquid should reduce until it forms a sort of light, fragrant glaze that completely coats the kale leaves. The final result will be glossy, flavourful greens with almost all the cooking liquid evaporated. Chef's tip: this step is crucial to concentrate the orange sugars and provide the right acidic punch to the recipe.
  8. Turn off the heat, transfer the braised cavolo nero to a large serving plate, and scatter the 40 g of previously toasted almonds over the top. Serve the dish hot, perhaps accompanying it with braised pulses or a white meat main for a balanced and refined meal. You will notice the perfect contrast between the softness of the leaves and the crunchiness of the almonds, enhanced by the citrusy aftertaste. Chef's tip: add the almonds at the very last second before serving to prevent the moisture from the kale from making them lose their crispness.
  9. Taste a piece of kale before serving to check if an extra pinch of salt or fresh chilli is needed. The presentation should be neat, with the almond flakes clearly visible, adding light to the dark green leaves. The ginger and orange scent should be balanced, without one overpowering the other. You will have a modern, healthy side dish that will amaze your guests with its complexity of flavours. Chef's tip: a final drizzle of raw extra virgin olive oil will further enhance all the citrusy aromas of the dish.
