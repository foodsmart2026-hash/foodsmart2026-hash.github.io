Sautéed chicory with pine nuts, sultanas and black olive powder

Lazio · 4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

An autumn side dish that balances the bitterness of the chicory with the sweetness of the sultanas and the savoury note of the black olive powder.

🌿 Seasonal recipe🌱 Vegan🌾 Gluten-free🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🇮🇹 Italian

This recipe transforms a classic side dish into a sophisticated course thanks to the play of sweet and savory flavors. Raisins and pine nuts contrast the chicory's bitterness, while black olive powder adds intensity. The texture is soft yet punctuated by crunchy notes.

Ingredients

  • Wild chicory800 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 ml
  • Garlic2 cloves
  • Sultanas30 g
  • Pine nuts25 g
  • Pitted black olives50 g
  • Fresh chilli1
  • Fine saltto taste

Allergens

Tree nuts

Method

  1. Begin by cleaning 800g of wild chicory: remove the tough ends of the stalks and wash repeatedly in cold water until every trace of soil is gone. Simultaneously, place 30g of raisins in a bowl with lukewarm water for about 15 minutes to rehydrate them properly. This ensures clean vegetables and plump, sweet raisins; remember to squeeze the raisins dry before use. Chef's tip: change the washing water at least three times to ensure all grit is removed from the chicory clusters.
  2. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil and plunge the chicory in for a quick cook of about 6 minutes over high heat. Use a carving fork to submerge it completely and check the consistency of the stalk, which should be tender but still 'al dente'. Short cooking preserves the nutritional properties and the structure of the fiber. Chef's tip: do not cover the pot during boiling to allow volatile acids to escape, thus maintaining a more vibrant colour.
  3. Prepare a bowl with cold water and ice cubes, then drain the chicory and immediately plunge it in to stop the cooking. This thermal shock fixes the chlorophyll, making the green of the vegetable bright, and prevents it from becoming too mushy. Leave it in the water for a couple of minutes, then drain again and squeeze it very firmly between your hands to remove every trace of liquid. Chef's tip: the chicory must be as dry as possible, otherwise it will boil in the pan instead of browning properly.
  4. While the vegetable rests, prepare the garnish: finely chop 50g of pitted black olives with a knife into a coarse powder and let them dry on paper towels. In a hot non-stick frying pan, toast 25g of pine nuts over low heat for 2-3 minutes, moving them constantly. You should achieve golden and fragrant pine nuts, being careful not to burn them as they would become bitter. Chef's tip: do not use oil to toast pine nuts; their natural fats are enough for perfect browning.
  5. In a large, high-sided frying pan, pour 40ml of extra-virgin olive oil and add the two crushed garlic cloves and the sliced fresh chili. Heat over medium-low heat for about 4 minutes, allowing the aromas to infuse the oil without the garlic turning dark. This aromatic base is fundamental to giving character to the final dish. Chef's tip: tilt the pan slightly to create a 'puddle' of oil where the garlic can fry evenly without burning in contact with the dry bottom.
  6. Increase the heat to medium-high and add the well-squeezed chicory to the pan with the flavoured oil. Sauté the vegetable for about 5 minutes using kitchen tongs to separate the bunches and ensure every leaf is coated by the seasoning. The result should be a glossy, flavourful vegetable with a slight crispy note from the direct heat. Chef's tip: if you see the vegetable tends to stick too much, add a tablespoon of reserved cooking water instead of more oil.
  7. Halfway through the sautéing process, add the well-squeezed raisins and the previously toasted pine nuts, adjusting the salt to your taste. Continue to sauté for another 2-3 minutes over medium heat to allow the sweetness of the raisins and the toasted note of the pine nuts to merge with the natural bitterness of the chicory. You will obtain a balance of contrasting flavours and a varied texture. Chef's tip: always taste a leaf and a stalk before turning off the heat to check that the saltiness is balanced, considering that the final olives will add further salt.
  8. Remove the garlic cloves and arrange the sautéed chicory on individual plates, trying to give volume to the serving. Complete each dish by generously distributing the previously prepared black olive powder over the entire surface. The powder will add a savory note and an interesting texture, as well as an elegant chromatic contrast with the dark green of the chicory. Chef's tip: serve the dish immediately while the vegetable is very hot, so as to release the essential oils of the chili and garlic to the maximum.

Tips

  • Submerge the chicory in ice water after boiling to maintain the bright green color of the leaves.
  • Mince the olives very finely and let them air-dry to achieve a more consistent powder.
  • Squeeze the boiled chicory well before sautéing to prevent it from releasing too much water.

Variations

  • Add desalted capers along with the olives to intensify the dish's Mediterranean and savory character.
  • Replace pine nuts with chopped walnut halves for a more earthy taste and a different crunch.
