Roasted Hasselback Broccoli with Chimichurri Sauce

🌿 Seasonal recipe

4 servings · Prep 25 min · Cooking 40 min · Medium

A visually striking technique applied to whole broccoli, slow-roasted in the oven and served with an aromatic and punchy chimichurri sauce.

Ingredients

  • Whole medium broccoli heads2
  • Fresh parsley1 bunch
  • Garlic2 cloves
  • Dried oregano1 tablespoon
  • Dried chilli flakes1 pinch
  • Red wine vinegar30ml
  • Extra virgin olive oil100ml
  • Coarse saltto taste

Allergens

Sulphites

Method

  1. Start by preparing the 2 medium broccoli: remove any outer leaves and the woody base of the stalk, keeping the head intact. Using a very sharp knife, make deep vertical incisions every centimetre across the entire surface, stopping just before reaching the base so the slices stay attached. You want to achieve a fan-like structure that allows heat and seasoning to penetrate the core. Chef's tip: place two wooden spoons on either side of the broccoli while slicing; they will act as a 'stop' for your blade, preventing you from cutting all the way through by mistake.
  2. Bring a large pot of water to the boil and add the rock salt. Submerge the broccoli upside down and blanch them over a high heat for exactly 5 minutes. This parboiling is essential to soften the tougher fibres of the stalk without overcooking the florets. Once ready, drain them gently and pat them thoroughly dry with a clean tea towel: they must be completely dry to roast properly in the oven. Chef's tip: do not skip this step, otherwise the core of the broccoli will remain too hard compared to the outer tips which cook much faster.
  3. Prepare the sauce by finely chopping a bunch of fresh parsley and the 2 garlic cloves with a knife. Avoid using an electric mixer because the high-speed blades heat the herbs, oxidising them and ruining their vibrant aroma. Work patiently until you have a consistent mince that still retains a rustic, visible texture. The result should be a fragrant mixture with a bright green colour. Chef's tip: remove the inner germ of the garlic if you prefer a less pungent and more digestible flavour for your guests.
  4. In a medium bowl, combine the chopped herbs with 1 tablespoon of dried oregano, a pinch of dried chilli, 30ml of red wine vinegar, and 100ml of extra virgin olive oil. Whisk vigorously with a fork or a small whisk to emulsify the liquids and distribute the spices evenly. Let the sauce rest for at least 10 minutes so the flavours can meld perfectly, creating a savoury and balanced dressing. Chef's tip: prepare the sauce slightly in advance to allow the vinegar to mellow the intensity of the parsley and garlic, making everything more harmonious.
  5. Prepare a baking tray lined with greaseproof paper and place the two whole broccoli on it with the sliced side facing up. Using a pastry brush, generously distribute about half of the chimichurri sauce over the entire surface, making sure to get into the crevices you created earlier. Ensure the dressing slides inside the cuts to season the broccoli right down to the heart of the stalk. The visual result will be a vegetable completely veiled in green and Mediterranean scents. Chef's tip: use your hands to gently spread the broccoli slices apart as you brush, so the sauce reaches everywhere.
  6. Carefully cover the tray with a sheet of aluminium foil, sealing the edges tightly to prevent steam from escaping. Place in a preheated static oven at 200°C and bake for exactly 20 minutes. During this stage, the moisture trapped under the foil will cook the broccoli evenly, making it as tender as if it were steamed but infused with the sauce's flavour. You will get a cooked but still firm vegetable that has already begun to absorb the hints of chimichurri. Chef's tip: make sure the foil does not touch the surface of the broccoli directly to avoid 'tearing' away the seasoning when you remove it.
  7. Carefully remove the aluminium foil, watching out for the burst of hot steam, and continue roasting in the oven for a further 15-20 minutes. The direct heat will start to brown the tips of the florets and crisp up the edges of the Hasselback slices. You are looking for a well-browned surface with almost caramelised highlights, while the aroma of roasted garlic and oil fills the kitchen. Chef's tip: for the final 5 minutes, you can switch on the grill function to achieve extra crunch and an even more inviting colour, but keep a close eye on the oven to prevent burning.
  8. Remove the broccoli from the oven and let them rest for 5 minutes directly on the tray; this short rest allows the internal juices to redistribute, making the flesh more succulent. Transfer them to a serving platter, either whole for a dramatic effect or sliced in half to show the unique cross-section. Finally, drizzle each piece with the remaining fresh chimichurri sauce you set aside to add a final note of acidity and freshness. Chef's tip: serve the dish warm rather than piping hot to fully enhance the aromatic complexity of the fresh herbs and the textural contrast of the broccoli.
