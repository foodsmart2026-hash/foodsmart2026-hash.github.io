Caldo verde

Portugal · 4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 30 min · Easy

Portuguese potato and kale soup with slices of chouriço.

Ingredients

  • Potatoes600 g
  • Kale250 g
  • Onion100 g
  • Garlic6 g
  • Chouriço120 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 ml
  • Water1300 ml
  • Fine salt8 g

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.
May contain traces of: Gluten, Mustard, Sulphites.

Method

  1. Start by finely chopping 100g of onion and 6g of garlic. In a large pot, heat 30ml of extra virgin olive oil over medium-low heat and let them soften for about 4 minutes until they are translucent and fragrant, without browning. This step creates the sweet aromatic base essential for balancing the soup's overall flavor.
  2. Add 600g of potatoes, previously peeled and cut into uniform 2cm cubes. Immediately pour in 1300ml of water and add 8g of fine salt, turning up the heat to bring everything to a boil. Cutting the potatoes into small pieces reduces cooking time and encourages the release of starches necessary for the soup's density.
  3. Once the boil is reached, lower the heat and let it simmer gently for about 20 minutes, covering with a lid. You will know the potatoes are ready when, after piercing them with a fork, they feel very tender and begin to fall apart slightly, a sign they are ready to be creamed.
  4. Use an immersion blender directly in the pot to turn the potatoes and broth into a smooth, velvety cream free of lumps. This dense and pale base is the 'heart' of the Caldo Verde and will support the texture of the kale strips you will add shortly.
  5. While the base cooks, take 250g of kale, remove the tougher central ribs, and stack the leaves to slice them into very thin strips, almost like hair. The finer the cut, the more pleasant the kale will be on the palate, blending perfectly with the potato cream without overpowering it.
  6. Bring the potato cream back to a gentle boil and drop in all the sliced kale, letting it cook uncovered for only 5 minutes. The strips should soften but maintain a bright green color and a slight resistance to the bite, preventing them from becoming too dark or mushy.
  7. In a separate small pan, heat the remaining 10ml of oil and sauté 120g of thinly sliced chouriço for about 2 minutes over high heat. The heat will release the savory and slightly spicy fat of the sausage, making the slices crispy and turning the oil red and very fragrant.
  8. Pour the piping hot soup into individual bowls and place the sautéed chouriço slices on top of each portion, finishing with the spicy cooking juices from the pan. Serve immediately to enjoy the contrast between the sweetness of the potato cream and the bold saltiness of the typical Portuguese sausage.
