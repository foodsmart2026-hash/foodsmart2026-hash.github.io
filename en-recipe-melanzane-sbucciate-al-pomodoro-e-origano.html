Peeled Aubergines with Tomato and Oregano

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

Aubergines cooked for a long time until they almost become a sauce, peeled to avoid any swallowing discomfort. The tomato and oregano recall the Mediterranean flavours of pizza.

A rustic dish that recalls Mediterranean flavors, where peeled eggplant becomes tender and juicy in the tomato sauce. Dried oregano provides an intense aroma reminiscent of summer dinners.

Ingredients

  • Large aubergines350 g
  • Sweet tomato passata (smooth tomato purée)150 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil15 g
  • Dried oregano1 pinch
  • Water50 ml

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Start by completely peeling 350g of large aubergines using a standard vegetable peeler to remove every trace of the purple skin. Place the vegetable on a stable chopping board and peel with smooth, downward strokes until the silky white flesh is fully exposed. This preliminary step is essential for achieving a final velvety texture, free from any tough parts that might be unpleasant on the palate. You will end up with perfectly cleaned aubergines, ready to be diced into small pieces that will blend perfectly with the sauce. Chef's tip: do not discard the skins; you can cut them into thin strips and deep-fry them for a few seconds to create a crispy garnish.
  2. Take the aubergine flesh and cut it first into thick slices, then into strips, and finally into very small cubes about half a centimetre per side. Use a sharp chef's knife to ensure clean, uniform cuts, which are vital for even cooking in a short space of time. Ensure the cubes are as regular as possible to prevent smaller pieces from breaking down too much while larger ones remain undercooked. By the end of the dicing, you should have a fine mixture that visually resembles the texture of a vegetable ragù. Chef's tip: keep the tip of the knife fixed on the board and move only the handle in a guillotine motion to get perfect cubes effortlessly.
  3. Take a heavy-based non-stick frying pan and pour in 150g of sweet tomato purée, 50ml of water, and 15g of extra virgin olive oil. Stir the liquids thoroughly with a wooden spoon or silicone spatula to create a well-distributed liquid emulsion before turning on the heat. Keep the flame at a medium-low intensity to warm the base gently, preventing the tomato from splashing or reducing too quickly. You should obtain a bright red, fluid cooking base, lightly scented by the fresh oil. Chef's tip: adding water to the cold base is the secret to allowing the aubergine to stew evenly without frying in the oil.
  4. Add the previously prepared aubergine cubes into the pan with the still-lukewarm tomato base. Distribute the cubes evenly across the entire surface of the pan, using the spatula so that every piece is coated in the red liquid. Slightly increase the heat for a few moments until you see the first bubbles appear, then immediately turn it back down to minimum. You will notice that the aubergine will start to absorb some of the sauce straight away, becoming heavier and darker. Chef's tip: avoid adding salt at this stage, as it would cause the aubergine to release its water too quickly, altering its natural spongy texture.
  5. Cover the pan with a well-fitting lid and let the mixture cook for about 20-25 minutes over a very low heat. During this stage, the aubergine must stew gently thanks to the steam trapped by the lid, softening its fibres without ever sticking to the bottom of the pan. Stir gently every 5 or 6 minutes to check that the heat is correctly distributed and that the cooking is progressing evenly. The visual result will be a sauce that gradually becomes thicker and darker as the liquids reduce. Chef's tip: if your lid has a steam vent, block it with a small piece of paper towel to retain all the steam necessary for the stewing process.
  6. While cooking, carefully observe the thickness of the sauce: if you see it reducing too much before the aubergine has become tender, add a small splash of hot water. Using hot water avoids thermal shocks that could toughen the aubergine fibres, keeping the simmering point constant. The aubergine should appear glossy and almost translucent, a clear sign that the cell structure is breaking down correctly and integrating with the tomato. At the end of cooking, the texture should be almost "melted", forming a thick cream where the aubergine cubes are still visible but extremely soft. Chef's tip: for an even finer consistency, you can lightly mash some of the cubes with the back of the spoon during the final minutes.
  7. Once the aubergine has reached the desired softness, turn off the heat and add a generous pinch of dried oregano. Rub the herb between your palms directly over the hot pan to crush the leaves and instantly release the most fragrant essential oils. Stir one last time with circular motions to distribute the herbal, Mediterranean aroma throughout the seasoning. You will smell an intense and fresh fragrance that perfectly balances the natural sweetness of the tomato and aubergine. Chef's tip: never add oregano at the start of cooking because prolonged heat will make it bitter, compromising the delicacy of the dish.
  8. Let the preparation rest in the covered pan for at least 5 minutes before final plating. This resting time is crucial for allowing the temperatures to stabilise and the flavours to meld into a perfect, enveloping balance. Serve the dish in individual bowls or as a side dish, ensuring the temperature is lukewarm, which is ideal for enhancing the aromatic notes of the oil and oregano. The final texture on the palate should be silky, almost like a savoury compote, very easy to spread or enjoy with a spoon. Chef's tip: this dish is extraordinary if prepared a few hours in advance, as the prolonged resting period accentuates its depth of flavour.

Tips

  • Peel the eggplants carefully to get a uniform consistency that blends with the tomato.
  • Cut the cubes into similar sizes to ensure even cooking throughout the pan.
  • If the tomato becomes too thick during simmering, dilute it with a small amount of warm water.

Variations

  • Add a few fresh basil leaves at the end of cooking for a more fragrant version.
  • Use cherry tomato purée for an even sweeter and more intense base.
