Roasted Carrots with Paprika and Oregano

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 20 min · Medium

Carrots, one of the most affordable ingredients in the supermarket, take centre stage thanks to bold seasoning. Roasting them in the oven concentrates their natural sugars, creating a lovely bittersweet contrast.

A tasty and colorful way to enjoy carrots, made tender by a brief boiling and then crispy in the oven. The combination of paprika and oregano creates an enveloping and slightly smoky aromatic profile. Ideal as an original side dish or to enrich grain bowls.

Ingredients

  • Carrots800 g
  • Sweet paprika1 cucchiaino
  • Dried oregano1 cucchiaino
  • Extra virgin olive oil20 ml
  • White wine vinegar10 ml

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Begin by prepping 800 g of fresh carrots, trimming the ends and peeling them thoroughly with a professional vegetable peeler. Cut them into uniform sticks about 1 cm thick and 6-7 cm long, aiming for consistent sizing to ensure even roasting throughout the tray. You should have vibrant orange, neatly shaped batons that will all cook at the same rate. My professional tip is to avoid cutting them too thin, as they tend to shrink significantly in the oven, losing their satisfying, meaty texture.
  2. Bring a large pot of lightly salted water to a rolling boil over high heat. Submerge the carrot sticks and blanch them for exactly 3 minutes from the moment the water returns to the boil. This pre-cooking technique softens the inner fibres without overcooking the exterior, ensuring a melt-in-the-mouth core after roasting. The result should be a carrot that is still firm but has lost its raw rigidity. Chef's advice: do not exceed 3 minutes, otherwise the carrots will become too mushy and break during the subsequent steps.
  3. Drain the carrots carefully and spread them out over a clean kitchen towel or absorbent paper, patting them gently to remove every trace of surface moisture. Let them cool slightly for about 5 minutes so that any remaining humidity evaporates through the residual heat. This stage is vital to allow the oil to adhere perfectly and to achieve a crisp crust rather than a 'boiled' effect. You will notice the surface becomes matte and dry to the touch. The secret to a perfect roast is always the absence of free water on the food's surface.
  4. While the carrots are drying, prepare the aromatic emulsion in a large bowl by combining 20 ml of extra virgin olive oil, 10 ml of white wine vinegar, 1 teaspoon of sweet paprika, and 1 teaspoon of dried oregano. Add a pinch of salt and a grind of black pepper, then whisk vigorously with a fork or a small whisk for about a minute. You will obtain a thick, fragrant emulsion with an inviting brownish-red hue. Chef's tip: the vinegar is the secret ingredient here, as its acidity perfectly balances the natural sweetness of the caramelised carrots.
  5. Transfer the dry carrot sticks into a large mixing bowl and pour the prepared emulsion over them, tossing with your hands or two spoons for about two minutes. Massage every piece well so the seasoning penetrates the fibres and coats every side of each baton evenly. The carrots should appear glossy and completely covered in the paprika and oregano powder, with no excessive oil pooling at the bottom. Ensure there are no bare patches, as the spices protect the flesh from the oven's direct heat. My advice is to use your (clean!) hands to feel if the coating is truly uniform.
  6. Line a large baking tray with parchment paper and arrange the carrots in a single layer, making sure to leave at least half a centimetre of space between each stick. Never overlap the carrots, or the steam released will prevent the Maillard reaction and the formation of a crust. The tray should look tidy and colourful, ready for the heat. Chef's tip: if you have a lot of carrots, use two separate trays rather than crowding one, as space is the best friend of crispiness.
  7. Preheat your oven to 210°C (fan-assisted ovens to 190°C) and, once reached, place the tray on the middle shelf for about 15 minutes. During this time, the natural sugars in the carrots will begin to caramelise thanks to the combined action of the paprika and the high heat. Towards the end of the time, check the consistency with the tip of a knife: it should slide in without resistance. The scent of toasted oregano will begin to fill the kitchen. Chef's tip: do not open the oven for the first 10 minutes to avoid sudden temperature drops that would hinder the cooking process.
  8. After the initial 15-18 minutes, switch the oven to the grill function at maximum power for the final 3 minutes, moving the tray to a slightly higher shelf. Watch closely through the glass: the tips of the carrots should turn slightly charred and the surface should look a bit wrinkled but well-browned. This final step adds that irresistible 'roasted' touch and a slightly chewy exterior texture. Be careful not to burn them, as paprika can turn bitter if it gets too dark. My advice is to stay right by the oven during these critical final moments.
  9. Remove the tray from the oven and let the carrots rest for 2-3 minutes before touching them, allowing the fibres to stabilise and the flavours to concentrate. Transfer them to a serving platter or a rustic bowl, serving them hot as a side dish or at room temperature as a healthy snack. The final result will be a baton that is soft inside, savoury and aromatic outside, with a beautiful amber colour. Chef's tip: serve them with a dollop of Greek yoghurt or some hummus for a truly professional contrast in temperatures and textures.

Tips

  • Drain the carrots while they are still al dente to prevent them from falling apart during roasting.
  • Ensure the oil and spice emulsion evenly coats each stick for perfectly uniform browning.
  • Let the carrots cool for a few minutes out of the oven to stabilize their crunchy texture.

Variations

  • Add a tablespoon of white wine vinegar to the emulsion for an acidic note that balances the carrots' sweetness.
  • Replace sweet paprika with hot paprika if you prefer a more vibrant and bold aftertaste.
