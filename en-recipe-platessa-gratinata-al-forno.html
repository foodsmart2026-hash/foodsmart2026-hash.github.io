Baked Gratin Plaice

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

A light and crispy main course that transforms affordable and delicate plaice fillets into a gourmet dish thanks to an aromatic breading and a bed of flavourful vegetables. This recipe perfectly balances the lean protein of the fish with the complex carbohydrates of the potatoes, ensuring a complete meal for the whole family.

A light and crispy baked dish where plaice fillets rest on a bed of thinly sliced potatoes and zucchini. The aromatic lemon and parsley breading provides a fresh note and a pleasant golden crust on top.

Ingredients

  • Plaice fillets (fresh or frozen)600 g
  • Yellow-fleshed potatoes400 g
  • Medium courgettes300 g
  • Fine breadcrumbs80 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 ml
  • Organic lemon (zest and juice)1 pz
  • Fresh parsley20 g
  • Garlic1 spicchio
  • Fine salt5 g
  • Ground black pepper2 g

Allergens

Gluten, Fish

Method

  1. Start by preparing the vegetable base: peel 400 g of potatoes and cut them into very thin slices of about 2 mm using a mandoline. Season them in a bowl with 10 ml of oil and a pinch of salt, then arrange them on a baking tray lined with greaseproof paper, overlapping them slightly. Bake at 200°C for 12 minutes until they become tender but not yet golden. This step prevents the potatoes from remaining raw under the fish, which cooks much faster. Chef's tip: dry the potato slices well with kitchen paper before oiling them to make them crispier.
  2. While the potatoes are cooking, wash 300 g of courgettes and cut them into very thin rounds of the same thickness as the potatoes. Season them with a drizzle of oil and set them aside in a large bowl. Ensure the slices are uniform to guarantee even cooking in the oven with the rest of the ingredients. The courgettes will release moisture during cooking, keeping the fish juicy. Chef's tip: do not salt the courgettes now, otherwise they will release too much water, making the base soggy.
  3. Prepare the aromatic breading (panure) by finely chopping 20 g of fresh parsley and one clove of garlic with the germ removed. In a small bowl, combine the chopped herbs with 80 g of breadcrumbs, the grated zest of a lemon, and a grind of black pepper. Mix with a fork, adding 10 ml of oil to obtain a sandy but slightly moist mixture. The breading should smell intensely of lemon and parsley. Chef's tip: rub the garlic clove directly onto the sides of the bowl before chopping it for a more delicate and digestible aroma.
  4. Take the 600 g of plaice fillets (if frozen, ensure they are perfectly thawed and dry) and pat them carefully on both sides. Squeeze half of the lemon over the fillets and let them flavour for a couple of minutes before proceeding. The acidity of the lemon will begin to denature the proteins, making the flesh firmer during cooking. The fillets must be very dry so that the breading adheres perfectly. Chef's tip: use plenty of kitchen paper, as plaice tends to retain a lot of water which would compromise the gratin.
  5. Remove the tray with the potatoes from the oven and distribute the prepared courgette rounds over them. Create an even layer that covers the potatoes almost entirely, leaving only the edges free to become crispy. Lightly salt the courgette layer only at this moment. You will notice that the heat from the tray will already start to soften the courgettes. Chef's tip: do not create too many layers of courgettes; a single or double layer is ideal for quick cooking.
  6. Lay the plaice fillets over the bed of courgettes, trying not to overlap them to allow the heat to circulate freely. If the fillets are very long or thin, you can fold them in half to even out the thickness of the dish. Lightly press the fish onto the vegetables so that the flavours meld during baking. The plaice must cover the vegetables well to protect them from direct heat. Chef's tip: position the thickest part of the fillet towards the outside of the tray where the heat is higher.
  7. Distribute the aromatic breading over each plaice fillet, pressing with the back of a spoon or your fingers to make it adhere well. The layer of breadcrumbs should be generous and entirely cover the surface of the fish to create a protective crust. Finally, drizzle the remaining 20 ml of extra virgin olive oil over everything, trying to moisten all the breadcrumbs well. You will see that the breadcrumbs will change colour, becoming more golden under the oil. Chef's tip: add a teaspoon of cornmeal to the breading for extra crunchiness that lasts longer.
  8. Place the tray back in the middle of the oven, maintaining the temperature at 200°C in fan mode, for about 12-15 minutes. Towards the end of the time, activate the grill function for the last 2-3 minutes to perfectly brown the surface. You should obtain a brown and crispy crust, while the fish underneath should be opaque white and tender. The scent of lemon and parsley will spread through the kitchen, indicating that cooking is complete. Chef's tip: do not overcook or the plaice will become tough.
  9. Remove the tray from the oven and let the dish rest for about 2-3 minutes before portioning and serving. This short rest allows the juices from the fish and vegetables to redistribute, making the fillets easier to lift without breaking them. Serve each portion by lifting both the fish and the underlying bed of potatoes and courgettes with a wide spatula. The final result will be a colourful, balanced, and very fragrant dish. Chef's tip: garnish with fresh lemon wedges to squeeze at the last moment to enhance the freshness of the white fish.

Tips

  • Use a mandoline to slice the potatoes and zucchini very thinly so they cook quickly.
  • Thoroughly dry the plaice fillets with paper towels before placing them on the vegetables for optimal browning.
  • If using frozen fish, ensure it is completely thawed and patted dry before use.

Variations

  • Add halved cherry tomatoes among the zucchini for a touch of color and moisture.
  • Replace breadcrumbs with crushed almonds for a more rustic and crunchy crust.
