Small Pasta Shells with Red Lentils and Mantuan Squash

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

An enveloping cream that hides split lentils, perfect for children's digestion. The squash provides a natural sweetness that makes the dish very popular.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⬇️ Big saving🌱 Vegan🧒 Kid-friendly🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🇮🇹 Italian

A comforting first course featuring red lentils and the natural sweetness of Mantuan pumpkin. The small pasta shells perfectly hold the dense cream created by slow-cooking the legumes and vegetable.

Ingredients

  • Small pasta shells (conchigliette)120 g
  • Split red lentils80 g
  • Cleaned squash (Mantuan squash is a sweet, firm Italian variety)200 g
  • Small golden onion1/2 pc
  • Extra virgin olive oil15 ml

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Begin by thoroughly rinsing the 80g of split red lentils under cold running water using a fine-mesh sieve to remove any dust or excess starch. Clean and dice 200g of Mantuan squash into very small, 1cm cubes, and finely chop the half golden onion until it is almost a paste. This preparation takes about 10 minutes and ensures that all ingredients cook evenly in the subsequent stages. The visual result should be a uniform dice of squash and shiny lentils free from surface impurities. The chef's tip is to rinse the lentils until the water runs clear to make the final cream more digestible.
  2. Take a large, heavy-based saucepan and add the squash cubes, chopped onion, and well-drained red lentils. Cover everything with approximately 500ml of cold water, ensuring the liquid level is at least two or three fingers above the ingredients. Place the pan over a high heat until it reaches a boil, then immediately reduce the heat to the lowest setting. You want a liquid base that gently simmers, allowing the flavours to meld slowly without burning. Starting with cold water allows the cell walls of the squash to break down more effectively, releasing all its natural sweetness.
  3. Allow the mixture to cook over a gentle heat for about 20 minutes, keeping the pan partially covered with a lid to limit evaporation. During this time, check that the water does not dry out too much and that the ingredients remain submerged in the cooking liquid. The squash should be very tender when touched with a fork, and the lentils should almost fall apart, losing their original shape in favour of a soft texture. You will smell a sweet, earthy aroma filling the kitchen, indicating the squash sugars have fully released. The chef's tip is to avoid adding salt at this stage to prevent the lentil skins from toughening.
  4. Once the base is cooked, turn off the heat and prepare to transform the mixture into a smooth, thick velouté. Use an immersion blender directly in the pan, operating it at medium speed with circular motions to incorporate a bit of air and make it fluffy. If you don't have a blender, you can vigorously mash the ingredients with a fork or pass them through a sieve until you achieve a homogenous pulp. The result should be a bright orange cream, free of large lumps and with a consistency similar to thick yoghurt. Ensure there are no chunks left; if the cream seems too dry, add a small ladle of hot water.
  5. Bring the squash and lentil cream back to a gentle simmer over a medium heat and add the 120g of small conchigliette pasta directly into the mixture. This 'risotto-style' cooking technique allows the pasta to absorb the nutrients and flavour of the base instead of losing them in boiling water. Stir frequently with a wooden spoon, paying close attention to the bottom of the pan as the density of the squash makes it prone to sticking. The pasta should cook for the time indicated on the packet, usually about 8-10 minutes, absorbing the liquid gradually. The chef suggests keeping a small pot of boiling water nearby to adjust the fluidity during cooking.
  6. Closely monitor the texture of the pasta during the final two minutes of cooking, tasting to ensure the conchigliette are very tender. For this specific recipe, it is preferable to cook for one minute beyond the 'al dente' stage, making the dish perfect for easy and safe swallowing. The final result should be a 'bound' dish, where each pasta shell is generously coated in the lentil cream without floating in liquid. You will notice the cream becomes more opaque and thicker as the pasta releases its starch. The tip is not to leave the stove during this crucial phase to guarantee even cooking without burning.
  7. When the cooking is finished, turn off the hob and move the pan away from the residual heat to finish the dish. Pour in 15ml of raw extra virgin olive oil and stir vigorously with a circular motion to create a silky emulsion. This cold 'mantecatura' process preserves the antioxidant properties of the EVO oil and enhances its fruity aroma. The result will be a glossy, fragrant, and incredibly velvety dish, with a natural sapidity balanced between the ingredients. The chef's secret is to let the pasta rest for a minute covered with a clean tea towel before serving, allowing the temperatures to stabilise.
  8. Serve the conchigliette in pre-warmed shallow bowls, distributing the cream and pasta equally among the four portions. Check the final temperature with a small taste: the dish should be warm but not scalding to fully appreciate the sweetness of the Mantuan squash. The consistency should appear as a thick, rich soup, ideal for being easily scooped up with a spoon. Before serving, you can add a final drizzle of oil if you desire an even more intense aroma. The final advice is to consume the dish immediately, as the pasta will continue to absorb the moisture from the cream, losing its ideal texture over time.

Tips

  • Cut the pumpkin into small, regular cubes to ensure even cooking alongside the lentils.
  • Rinse the red lentils under running water until the water runs clear before starting the cooking process.
  • If the cream thickens too much while the pasta is cooking, add a ladle of hot water.

Variations

  • Replace the small shells with ditalini rigati for a different but equally binding texture.
  • You can use Butternut squash instead of Mantuan for a slightly nuttier flavor.
