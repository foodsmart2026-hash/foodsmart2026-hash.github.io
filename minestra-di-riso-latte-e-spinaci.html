Rice, Milk and Spinach Soup

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 20 min · Easy

A nostalgic dish, delicate and nutritious, which uses milk to make the rice incredibly creamy. Spinach adds iron and colour to this economical and balanced meal.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⬇️ Big saving🥕 Vegetarian🧒 Kid-friendly🌾 Gluten-free🇮🇹 Italian

A delicate and nourishing soup, typical of rural traditions, where milk gives a unique creaminess to the rice. Fresh spinach adds a touch of color and freshness, making the dish balanced. The final nutmeg completes the aromatic profile with its unmistakable touch.

Ingredients

  • Originario rice (a short-grain rice variety ideal for soups)280 g
  • Whole or semi-skimmed milk500 ml
  • Fresh or frozen spinach300 g
  • Nutmeg1 pinch
  • Water500 ml

Allergens

Milk

Method

  1. Start by preparing the 300g of spinach: if using fresh leaves, wash them thoroughly under running water, dry them, and chop them coarsely with a knife; if using frozen, let them thaw completely and squeeze them firmly to remove all excess water. This step takes about 5 minutes and ensures the greens integrate into the cream without making it watery. You will have prepared leaves with a vibrant green colour and uniform texture. Chef's tip: squeeze frozen spinach very well to prevent its metallic flavour and excess water from diluting the delicate taste of the milk.
  2. Take a large, heavy-based saucepan and pour in 500ml of water and 500ml of milk, adding a pinch of salt. Place the pan over medium heat and bring the liquid almost to a boil, monitoring closely to prevent the milk from overflowing. You should see light steam rising and tiny bubbles forming around the edges of the pan. Chef's tip: do not let the milk boil vigorously, as the proteins can burn on the bottom, creating a bitter aftertaste; a temperature just below boiling is ideal.
  3. Once the liquid is hot, pour the 280g of soup rice directly into the pan and stir immediately with a wooden spoon. Maintain a gentle heat to allow the grains to gradually release their starch, which will act as a natural thickener for your soup. During this initial stage, stir frequently for about 2 minutes to ensure the rice doesn't settle and stick to the base. Chef's tip: use a variety like 'Originario' or pudding rice for its ability to release plenty of starch, ensuring that traditional creamy texture.
  4. Continue cooking over very low heat for about 8-10 minutes, allowing the rice to absorb some of the liquid. Do not cover the pan completely; leave a small gap if using a lid to maintain constant humidity. You will see the rice increase in volume and the liquid start to coat the spoon, becoming slightly more opaque. Chef's tip: if you notice the liquid reducing too quickly, lower the heat further; the rice must simmer 'swimming' in the milk, not dry out like a risotto.
  5. Halfway through cooking, add the 300g of prepared spinach to the rice and milk mixture, blending with circular motions. The spinach will wilt instantly upon contact with the heat, distributing evenly throughout the soup. Cook for another 8-10 minutes, or until the rice is very tender, almost melting on the palate. Chef's tip: add the spinach only halfway through to preserve its bright green colour and nutritional properties, preventing it from turning too dark.
  6. As the soup finishes cooking, grate a generous pinch of nutmeg directly over the pan. This spice is essential as it creates a perfect aromatic bridge between the sweetness of the milk and the earthy notes of the spinach. Stir vigorously for a minute to distribute the aroma and help form the final cream. Chef's tip: always use whole nutmeg to grate fresh; the pre-ground version loses most of the essential oils that make this dish special.
  7. Turn off the heat and assess the consistency: the soup should be thick and velvety but still fluid. If it seems too dense, add a splash of warm milk (about 30-50ml) and stir well until you reach the desired fluidity. Let the dish rest for a minute with the lid on to allow the flavours to settle. Chef's tip: remember that rice continues to absorb liquid even after the heat is off, so it's better to leave it slightly looser than the desired final result.
  8. Taste to adjust the salt and ladle the soup into large bowls while still hot. Complete each portion with a drizzle of extra virgin olive oil to add shine and a fresh note to the palate. The result will be a comforting dish with a milky, spiced aroma and a perfect balance between cereal and vegetables. Chef's tip: serve the soup warm rather than piping hot; your taste buds will better perceive the sweetness of the milk and the complexity of the nutmeg.

Tips

  • Use a heavy-bottomed pot to prevent the milk from sticking or burning during slow cooking.
  • Stir the rice frequently, just like a risotto, to encourage the release of starches into the milk base.
  • If the soup dries out too much, add a splash of hot water to restore the correct fluidity.

Variations

  • You can replace spinach with tender chard for a slightly more earthy flavor.
  • For an even more aromatic result, add a piece of organic lemon zest to the milk during the first minutes of cooking.
