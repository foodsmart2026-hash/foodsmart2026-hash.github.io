Homemade Honey and Seed Granola

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

A crunchy mix of cereals and seeds made at home, much cheaper and healthier than industrial versions rich in hydrogenated fats. Perfect for dipping into yogurt or cold milk.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🥕 Vegetarian🧒 Kid-friendly🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🌍 International

A crunchy homemade granola, perfect to pair with yogurt or cold milk. The comforting taste of honey blends with toasted seeds, creating a fragrant and rich texture.

Ingredients

  • Whole grain rolled oats250 g
  • Sunflower seeds40 g
  • Honey60 g
  • Sunflower oil30 ml
  • Raisins40 g
  • Salt1 g

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Begin by preheating your oven to 160°C (320°F) using the static setting, placing the rack in the middle position to ensure consistent heat distribution. In a very large mixing bowl, combine 250g of wholemeal oat flakes, 40g of sunflower seeds, and one gram of fine salt. Stir thoroughly with a wooden spoon or spatula to distribute the seeds and salt evenly throughout the oat fibres. You should aim for a dry mix where every component is well-distributed, preventing the salt from settling at the bottom. My chef's tip is to use a bowl larger than strictly necessary so you can toss the ingredients freely without spilling them.
  2. Take a small non-stick saucepan and pour in 60g of honey and 30ml of sunflower oil. Warm the mixture over a very low heat for about 2 to 3 minutes, stirring continuously with a hand whisk until the two liquids merge into a fluid, slightly cloudy emulsion. There is no need to bring it to a boil; the goal is simply to reduce the honey's viscosity so it pours easily. The result will be a shiny golden syrup with a sweet, delicate aroma. Be careful not to overheat the honey, as this can alter its nutritional properties and flavour profile.
  3. Pour the still-warm honey and oil emulsion directly over the dry oat and seed mixture in the large bowl. Use a rubber spatula or a spoon to combine everything using circular motions from the bottom upwards, making sure to scrape the sides of the bowl. Continue stirring for about 2 minutes until every single oat flake looks glossy and slightly sticky, indicating it has been perfectly coated by the liquid. You will notice the flakes starting to clump together into small, fragrant clusters. The secret to a crunchy granola lies in this thorough coating: do not leave any dry spots in the bowl.
  4. Prepare a large baking tray and line it with baking parchment, then tip the mixture onto the centre of the surface. Use the back of a wet spoon or a spatula to level the granola, spreading it into a thin and as even a layer as possible across the entire tray. Avoid piling the product too high in the middle; the edges tend to cook faster, so try to keep a consistent thickness of about one centimetre. The granola should look like a uniform carpet ready for toasting. A professional trick: press the mixture down firmly onto the tray if you prefer larger 'clusters' after baking.
  5. Place the tray in the preheated oven at 160°C and set a timer for the first 12 minutes of baking. During this initial stage, the honey will begin to caramelise and the moisture from the oats will slowly evaporate, releasing an irresistible toasted aroma. Keep a close eye on the tray, as the browning speed varies significantly depending on your oven's accuracy. By the end of this time, the edges of the granola will start to take on a slightly darker colour compared to the centre. I recommend not opening the oven before 10 minutes to avoid a sudden drop in the internal temperature.
  6. Briefly remove the tray from the oven and, using a spatula, gently stir the granola, bringing the outer pieces towards the centre and vice versa. Flatten the mixture back into an even layer and return it to the oven for approximately another 10-13 minutes. Monitor the colour closely: the granola should turn a beautiful deep golden blonde, but never dark brown, as burnt honey takes on a bitter aftertaste. When the surface looks uniform and the sunflower seeds appear slightly puffed and toasted, it is time to remove it from the oven. Remember that the granola will still feel slightly soft to the touch, which is perfectly normal.
  7. Remove the tray from the oven and, while the granola is still piping hot, scatter 40g of raisins over the surface. Do not stir them in immediately; let the residual heat gently soften the raisins without drying them out or making them hard, which would happen if they were baked. Allow the granola to cool completely directly on the tray, untouched, for at least 40-60 minutes at room temperature. During cooling, the sugars in the honey crystallise, creating the signature crunchy and crisp texture. My tip is to be patient: if you move it while warm, you will lose the chance to form those delicious crunchy clusters.
  8. Once the granola is perfectly cold and firm to the touch, use your hands to break it into coarse chunks and peel it away from the parchment paper. Transfer the resulting pieces into a clean glass jar with an airtight seal to preserve its fragrance and protect it from ambient humidity. You can store it in a cool, dry place away from heat sources, where it will maintain its crunchiness for up to two weeks. The final result will be a healthy, balanced breakfast that is far tastier than shop-bought versions. For a professional touch, shake the jar before each use to redistribute the raisins and seeds that tend to settle at the bottom.

Tips

  • Let the granola cool completely in the tray to achieve crunchy clusters.
  • Store the product in an airtight glass jar to maintain its freshness.
  • Add the raisins only after baking to prevent them from hardening too much in the oven.

Variations

  • Replace raisins with dried cranberries for a tarter note.
  • Use pumpkin seeds instead of sunflower seeds for a bolder flavor.
