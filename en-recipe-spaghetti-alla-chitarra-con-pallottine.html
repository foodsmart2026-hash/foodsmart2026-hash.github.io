Spaghetti alla chitarra with tiny meatballs

Abruzzo · 4 servings · Prep 40 min · Cooking 50 min · Medium

Egg pasta from Teramo with tomato sauce and tiny meatballs.

Ingredients

  • Egg spaghetti alla chitarra360 g
  • Minced veal250 g
  • Egg50 g
  • Grated Abruzzese Pecorino40 g
  • Tomato passata700 ml
  • Onion60 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 ml
  • Fine salt6 g

Allergens

Gluten, Milk, Eggs
May contain traces of: Celery, Soy.

Method

  1. In a large bowl, combine 250g of minced veal with 50g of whole egg, 20g of grated Abruzzese pecorino (half of the total dose), and a pinch of the 6g of salt. Knead vigorously with your hands until you obtain a homogeneous, smooth, and well-bound mixture; the meat must be compact to prevent the meatballs from falling apart during the long cooking process in the sauce.
  2. Take small portions of the mixture and manually shape tiny meatballs about the size of a small hazelnut, approximately 3g each, carefully rolling them between your palms. This operation requires patience and precision, as the small size is the hallmark of the Teramo tradition and ensures a perfect balance between pasta and meat in every bite.
  3. Finely chop 60g of onion and heat 40ml of extra virgin olive oil in a large casserole over low heat. Let the onion sauté for about 3 minutes until it becomes translucent and tender; ensure it doesn't brown too much to maintain a sweet aromatic base that doesn't overpower the delicate flavor of the veal.
  4. Pour 700ml of tomato passata into the casserole and add about half of the remaining salt. Cover with a lid and let it simmer over medium-low heat for 20 minutes; the sauce will be ready for the next step when it begins to reduce slightly, turning a deeper, more fragrant red.
  5. Gently drop the raw meatballs directly into the boiling sauce, distributing them evenly without overlapping them too much. It is crucial not to stir with a spoon at this stage to avoid breaking the still-tender meat; simply shake the casserole lightly to allow them to sink completely into the tomato.
  6. Continue cooking the sauce with the meatballs for another 20 minutes over a very low and constant flame. You will know the preparation has reached ideal cooking when the meatballs are firm and the sauce appears thick and velvety, having absorbed all the juices and flavors of the veal.
  7. Bring a pot with plenty of water to a boil, add the remaining salt, and cook 360g of spaghetti alla chitarra for about 3-4 minutes. Being egg pasta, it cooks very quickly: drain it while it is still very 'al dente' to allow it to finish absorbing the flavors directly in the sauce.
  8. Transfer the pasta to the casserole with the sauce, stirring extremely gently to coat every strand of spaghetti, then plate and garnish with the remaining 20g of grated pecorino. Serve immediately while the heat of the pasta releases the aroma of the cheese, creating an irresistible creaminess that celebrates the authentic flavors of Abruzzo.
