Crispy cavolo nero and pecorino fritters

🌿 Seasonal recipe

Toscana · 4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

Delicious golden fritters where cavolo nero meets the saltiness of pecorino in a light and crispy batter.

Ingredients

  • Cavolo nero (trimmed)250 g
  • Plain flour150 g
  • Cold sparkling water200 ml
  • Grated Pecorino Romano50 g
  • Egg1
  • Baking powder5 g
  • Groundnut oil for frying500 ml
  • Salt and pepperto taste

Allergens

Gluten, Milk, Eggs
May contain traces of: Peanuts.

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the 250g of cavolo nero: wash the leaves thoroughly, remove the tough white central ribs, and stack the leaves to slice them into very fine ribbons. This task, performed with a sharp knife, ensures the kale cooks evenly and blends perfectly with the batter. You will end up with vibrant green strands ready for the next stage. Chef's tip: do not discard the removed ribs; you can boil and blend them to create a flavourful base for a vegetable soup.
  2. Bring a pot of lightly salted water to a boil and blanch the kale ribbons for exactly 2 minutes over a high heat. Drain them immediately and plunge them into a bowl of ice water to stop the cooking process and preserve the bright green colour. Once cold, squeeze them forcefully between your hands or inside a clean tea towel until they are completely dry. Chef's tip: if the kale retains water, it will dilute the batter excessively, and the fritters will turn out soggy instead of crispy.
  3. In a large bowl, carefully sift the 150g of plain flour together with the 5g of instant savoury baking powder to remove any lumps. Create a small well in the centre and add the whole egg, beginning to whisk it lightly by hand. This stage prepares the dry structure that will incorporate the liquid elements. Chef's tip: sifting the flour doesn't just remove lumps; it incorporates air, making the fritters much lighter and more aerated.
  4. Pour the 200ml of ice-cold sparkling water in a thin stream over the flour, whisking continuously from the centre outwards. Work the mixture for a couple of minutes until you obtain a smooth, velvety, and lump-free batter with a semi-fluid consistency. The thermal shock between the icy water and the hot oil is the technical secret to a perfect fry. Chef's tip: keep the water in the freezer for 10 minutes before use, as the colder it is, the less oil the batter will absorb during cooking.
  5. Add the 50g of grated Pecorino Romano, a generous grind of black pepper, and the well-dried kale ribbons to the batter. Mix everything together using a spoon or spatula with gentle movements, ensuring the vegetable is evenly distributed throughout the mixture. The final batter should appear rich and thick, holding its shape on the spoon. Chef's tip: do not add extra salt at this stage, as the Pecorino is already very salty and you risk over-seasoning the dish.
  6. Prepare the frying oil by pouring 500ml of peanut oil into a deep pan and heating it over a medium flame until it reaches a temperature of 170-180°C. If you don't have a kitchen thermometer, dip a wooden skewer into the oil: if you see many small bubbles rising immediately around it, the temperature is perfect. It is vital not to exceed the smoke point to maintain the quality of the fry. Chef's tip: use peanut oil because it has high heat stability, ensuring a healthier result and a neutral flavour.
  7. Use two spoons to scoop a small portion of the mixture and gently slide it into the hot oil, attempting to form a rounded shape. Fry no more than 4 or 5 pieces at a time to prevent the oil temperature from dropping sharply, which would compromise the crispness. You will see the fritters puff up almost instantly and float happily on the surface. Chef's tip: do not rush; frying too many pieces at once will cause the fritters to absorb oil, making them heavy and greasy.
  8. Let the fritters cook for about 3 minutes in total, turning them halfway through with a slotted spoon to brown them on both sides. The final result should be a firm outer crust with a beautiful golden-amber colour, hiding a soft and fragrant heart. Ensure the heat reaches the centre thoroughly before removing them. Chef's tip: if you notice them darkening too quickly (in less than a minute), lower the heat slightly as the inside might remain raw.
  9. Remove the fritters with a slotted spoon and place them on a plate lined with plenty of kitchen paper to remove any excess grease. Pat them gently without crushing them and let them rest for a few seconds so the outer structure stabilises and becomes even crispier. Avoid covering them, otherwise the steam will soften them immediately. Chef's tip: never stack hot fritters on top of each other, as the released moisture will make them rubbery in an instant.
  10. Transfer the fritters to a serving plate, add a pinch of salt over the surface while they are still piping hot, and serve immediately. The combination of melted Pecorino, the slightly bitter note of the kale, and the crunchy batter is best enjoyed straight from the pan. You can accompany them with a lemon wedge or a tart yoghurt dip to contrast the fried richness. Chef's tip: salt should only be added at the very last second, as it attracts moisture and ruins the crispy texture if added too early.
