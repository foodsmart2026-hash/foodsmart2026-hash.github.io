Turkey Strips with Peppers and Sweet Paprika

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

A quick recipe that uses the sweetness of peppers to balance the neutral flavour of turkey breast. Paprika provides a vibrant colour and deep aroma without making the dish heavy.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⚖️ Good value for money🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free

A vibrant and colorful main dish where tender turkey meets the sweetness of sautéed peppers. Sweet paprika and a splash of white wine create a flavorful, coating sauce. Ideal for a quick weekday lunch that doesn't compromise on taste.

Ingredients

  • Turkey breast slices600 g
  • Mixed peppers (yellow and red)2 pcs
  • Red onion1 pcs
  • Sweet paprika1 cucchiaio
  • Dry white wine50 ml
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Plain flour (Type 0 flour)20 g

Allergens

Gluten, Sulphites

Method

  1. Start by cutting 600g of turkey breast into thin, uniform strips about 1cm wide. Place the strips in a bowl with 20g of plain flour and mix by hand until each piece is lightly coated. Use a sieve or a large-mesh strainer to shake off any excess flour, which is crucial to prevent lumps in your sauce. You should achieve matte pieces of meat that are well-separated from one another. Chef's tip: do not flour the meat too far in advance, otherwise the turkey's moisture will create a sticky paste instead of a crisp coating.
  2. Thoroughly wash the 2 mixed peppers, halve them, and carefully remove the white seeds and internal pith, which can be hard to digest. Cut them into strips about 5-6cm long, trying to maintain a similar thickness to the turkey strips for even cooking. Peel the red onion and slice it thinly with a sharp knife or a mandoline. The result should be a mix of colourful, uniform vegetables ready for sautéing. Chef's tip: cutting vegetables to the same size as the meat ensures each mouthful is balanced in flavour and texture.
  3. Pour 30ml of extra virgin olive oil into a very large non-stick frying pan or a wok and heat it over a medium flame. Add the sliced red onion and let it soften slowly for about 3-4 minutes, stirring frequently with a wooden spoon. The onion shouldn't burn but should become translucent and soft, releasing its sweet aroma without any bitter notes. You will smell a delicate fragrance that forms the aromatic base of your dish. Chef's tip: if you notice the onion browning too quickly, add a tablespoon of water to lower the temperature and stew it properly.
  4. Increase the heat to high and add the pepper strips to the sautéed onion. Toss them for about 5 minutes, moving the pan frequently so the skins start to brown while the flesh remains firm and crisp. This step seals the vegetable juices and caramelises the natural sugars in the peppers. You will notice the colours becoming more vibrant and the tips of the peppers turning slightly brown. Chef's tip: do not salt the peppers at this stage, as salt would draw out the water and make them soggy instead of seared.
  5. Slightly move the peppers to the edges of the pan and add the floured turkey strips to the centre. Sear the meat over high heat for 3-4 minutes, turning frequently until it takes on a uniform golden-white colour on all sides. The flour in contact with the oil will create a very thin protective barrier that keeps the turkey juicy inside. The meat should look well-sealed and shouldn't release any liquid into the pan. Chef's tip: avoid overcrowding the pan; if necessary, sear the turkey in two batches to keep the temperature high.
  6. Once the meat is well browned, pour in 50ml of dry white wine, distributing it evenly over all the ingredients. Keep the heat high and allow the alcohol to evaporate for about 1-2 minutes until the sharp smell of the wine disappears, leaving only a fragrant note. Use the wooden spoon to scrape the bottom of the pan (deglazing), dissolving the small savoury crusts that have formed. You will see a slightly velvety sauce forming thanks to the flour. Chef's tip: use a good quality wine, the same one you would drink at the table, so as not to compromise the final flavour of the sauce.
  7. Slightly lower the heat and sprinkle the dish evenly with 1 heaped tablespoon of sweet paprika. Pour in half a glass of hot water (about 100ml) to hydrate the spice and create the necessary cooking liquid for the final stage. Cover the pan with a lid and let it simmer gently for another 5 minutes, allowing the flavours to meld perfectly. The paprika will give the dish an intense red-orange colour and an inviting, slightly smoky aroma. Chef's tip: make sure the water is hot so as not to stop the meat cooking, which could otherwise make it tough.
  8. Remove the lid and check the consistency of the sauce: it should be fluid but able to 'nappe', meaning it should evenly coat both the meat and the vegetables. If the sauce is too thin, increase the heat for a minute to reduce it; if it's too dry, add a splash of water. Season with salt and freshly ground black pepper to taste, stirring one last time gently. The turkey will be very tender and the peppers soft but still intact. Chef's tip: serve the dish piping hot, accompanied by basmati rice or boiled potatoes to help mop up every drop of the delicious paprika cream.

Tips

  • Lightly flour the turkey strips before cooking to achieve a creamier texture and keep the meat moist.
  • Cut the peppers into thin, uniform strips to ensure they cook evenly alongside the meat.
  • For the best results, use a very hot pan to sear the turkey and lock in its juices.

Variations

  • Use chicken breast instead of turkey for an even milder flavor profile.
  • Add a handful of Taggiasca olives at the end of cooking for a savory, Mediterranean touch.
