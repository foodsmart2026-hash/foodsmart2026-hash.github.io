Creamy Leek and Turmeric Risotto

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 20 min · Easy

A risotto with an intense golden colour and a delicate flavour. Turmeric adds antioxidant properties without weighing down the family budget.

💰 Save Euro🌾 Gluten-free🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🌍 International

Ingredients

  • Originario rice (an affordable short-grain variety for risotto)320 g
  • Large leeks2 pcs
  • Turmeric powder1 cucchiaino
  • Vegetable stock1.2 L
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Nutritional yeast or Parmesan20 g
  • Salt and pepperq.b.

Allergens

latte_(se_usato_parmigiano)

Method

  1. Start by carefully cleaning the 2 large leeks: remove the roots and the tough dark green parts, then slice them into thin rings and rinse thoroughly. In a high-sided saucepan, heat 30 ml of extra virgin olive oil over a low heat and add the leeks with a pinch of salt. Let them sweat, covered, for about 10 minutes, adding a splash of water if necessary, until they become tender and almost creamy. The result should be a sweet and fragrant base, without the leeks browning or burning. The chef's tip is to slice the leeks very evenly to ensure uniform cooking and a velvety texture in the final dish.
  2. Pour the 320 g of Originario rice directly into the saucepan with the stewed leeks, slightly increasing the heat to a medium level. Stir continuously with a wooden spoon for about 2 or 3 minutes, ensuring that every grain is well coated with the oil and seasoning. You will notice the rice becoming glossy and the edges of the grains turning translucent, a sign that toasting is happening correctly. This stage is crucial to seal the grains, allowing them to maintain their bite during cooking. Be careful not to burn the leeks during this step; if you hear them sizzling too loudly, move immediately to the next step.
  3. Add 1 teaspoon of turmeric powder to the toasted rice and stir vigorously to distribute the golden colour evenly throughout the base. Immediately after, pour in the first two ladles of boiling vegetable broth (part of the 1.2 litres total) and continue stirring until the liquid is almost completely absorbed. Maintain a medium heat so the risotto simmers gently and constantly. The rice will instantly take on a stunning ochre-yellow hue and release a spicy, earthy aroma. Chef's tip: always keep the broth at a light simmer in a separate small pot so as not to drop the rice's temperature each time you add it.
  4. Continue cooking by adding one ladle of broth at a time, taking care to wait until the previous one has been almost entirely absorbed before adding more. Stir the risotto very often, almost non-stop: this mechanical movement allows the starch-rich Originario rice to release it gradually, creating the dish's signature creaminess. Adjust the heat if you see the broth evaporating too quickly or if the simmer stops. After about 10-12 minutes of constant additions, the rice will begin to swell and the cooking liquid will become visibly thicker. A chef's secret is to "caress" the rice with the spoon in circular motions, without crushing it, to preserve the integrity of the grains.
  5. Keep adding broth for another 6-8 minutes, constantly monitoring the texture of the grain by tasting it regularly. The rice should be "al dente", meaning tender on the outside but with a central core that still offers a slight resistance to the bite. Towards the end of cooking, reduce the amount of broth added to prevent the risotto from being excessively liquid when the heat is turned off. The colour should remain a vibrant yellow and the leek fragrance should be well-balanced with the turmeric. Remember that the rice will continue to cook from residual heat even while resting, so keep the grains slightly firmer than you wish to serve them.
  6. When there are about two minutes left of the expected cooking time, taste the risotto and adjust the salt and freshly ground black pepper to your personal preference. Do not over-salt initially, as the reduction of the broth and the final addition of cheese or yeast will increase the overall savouriness. Stir well to incorporate the flavours and ensure the consistency is still fluid ("all'onda"), adding a final tablespoon of broth if needed. Black pepper not only enhances the flavour of the turmeric but also aids its nutritional absorption. The chef's tip is to use high-quality black pepper for a superior aromatic note.
  7. Turn off the heat and move the saucepan away from the hob to stop direct cooking. Add the 20 g of grated Parmesan (or nutritional yeast for a vegan version) and stir vigorously with quick circular motions for about one minute. This step, known as "mantecatura", creates a perfect emulsion between the rice starch and the added fats, making the dish silky. The risotto should appear incredibly creamy and glossy, with a structure that moves fluidly if you gently shake the pan. Never skip this step with the heat off, as the cheese should only melt harmoniously without becoming stringy or separating.
  8. Cover the saucepan with a lid and let the risotto rest for exactly 1 minute before serving. This brief rest allows the flavours to stabilise and the temperature to distribute evenly, improving the mouthfeel of the grains. Once the time is up, give it one last quick stir and distribute the risotto into shallow bowls, lightly tapping the bottom of the plate with your palm to spread it evenly. The finished dish should be warm, comforting, and visually inviting thanks to its sunny colour. The chef's final touch is to garnish with a few very finely sliced raw leek rings or a small sprig of fresh thyme.
