Bread and Milk Soup with Strawberry Puree

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 3 min · Easy

A reimagining of a classic old-fashioned snack, made creamy and colourful thanks to fresh fruit. The bread crumb soaks completely, becoming a cream that is easy to swallow even for the little ones.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⬇️ Big saving🥕 Vegetarian🇮🇹 Italian

A traditional comfort food that recalls flavors of the past, enriched by the freshness of strawberries. The softness of the bread soaked in milk creates an enveloping and pleasant texture.

Ingredients

  • Soft white sandwich bread or white bread crumb100 g
  • Whole milk350 ml
  • Fresh strawberries150 g

Allergens

Gluten, Milk

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the aromatic base by thoroughly cleaning 150g of fresh strawberries under a gentle stream of cold running water. Carefully remove the green calyx and pat them dry with kitchen paper to avoid adding excess moisture. Place them on a chopping board and check that they are ripe and free from blemishes. The result should be clean, intact, and fragrant fruit. My chef's advice is not to soak them, as they would absorb too much water, losing flavour intensity.
  2. Transform the strawberries into a velvety pulp using a stick blender or, for a more rustic texture, mashing them vigorously with a fork in a bowl. If you desire an extremely refined soup, pass the resulting pulp through a fine-mesh sieve, pressing with the back of a spoon to remove the seeds. You should obtain approximately 140-150g of smooth, bright red purée. Chef's tip: keep the pulp at room temperature to avoid excessive thermal shock when you combine it with the warm milk.
  3. Now turn your attention to the bread, taking 100g of white sandwich bread or fresh white breadcrumbs. Use a serrated knife to precisely remove every trace of the outer crust, isolating only the soft, white inner part. This step is crucial because the crust has different absorption times and would ruin the homogeneity of the soup. You will obtain a soft, white, and light heart of bread. Ensure the bread is not excessively dry, otherwise it will not transform into the desired cream.
  4. Shred the obtained bread crumb into small, irregular pieces, crumbling it directly with your hands over a large ceramic or glass bowl. Try to maintain a uniform size for the flakes, about 1-2 centimetres, to ensure the liquid penetrates every part simultaneously. This operation takes only a couple of minutes and prepares the base for hydration. The result will be a bowl full of fluffy bread clouds ready to receive the milk. A little secret: the more finely you shred the bread, the more the final texture will resemble a custard.
  5. Pour 350ml of whole milk into a heavy-bottomed stainless steel saucepan and place it over a medium-low flame. Heat the liquid while monitoring it constantly: it must become quite hot, around 50-60°C, but absolutely must not reach boiling point. If you see small bubbles forming at the edges, it is time to turn off the heat. You will get warm, steaming milk that releases a sweet, clean aroma. Avoid boiling it so as not to alter the milk proteins and prevent that annoying skin from forming on the surface.
  6. Proceed with the soaking phase by slowly pouring the hot milk over the shredded bread pieces in the bowl. Use a spoon to gently press the bread down, ensuring every piece is completely submerged in the liquid. Let the mixture rest for about 2-3 minutes without stirring excessively at this stage. You will notice the bread absorbing almost all the milk, swelling up and becoming a soft, uniform mush. The chef's trick lies in patience: giving the bread time to fully regenerate.
  7. It is time to unite the flavours by adding the 150g of previously prepared strawberry pulp to the bread and milk mixture. Use a spatula or a wooden spoon to incorporate the fruit with slow, circular movements, starting from the centre and moving outwards. The milky white of the bread will gradually turn an inviting, natural pastel pink. Continue stirring until the strawberry purée is perfectly and evenly distributed. If the consistency seems too thick, you can adjust the fluidity by adding an extra tablespoon of milk at room temperature.
  8. Finish the preparation by working the mixture with a spoon until you achieve a creamy consistency free of large lumps. Divide the soup into four individual small bowls, levelling the surface with the back of the spoon. The dish should present as a velvety, lukewarm cream with a fresh spring scent. Serve immediately to enjoy the contrast between the sweetness of the milk and the natural acidity of the strawberry. A final professional touch: serve the soup in pre-warmed bowls to maintain the ideal temperature for longer.

Tips

  • Use slightly stale bread to allow for optimal absorption of the hot milk.
  • Sieve the strawberry pulp if you prefer an even smoother texture without seeds.
  • Consume the soup freshly made to enjoy the right temperature and creaminess.

Variations

  • You can use an almond vegetable drink instead of cow's milk for a different taste.
  • Replace the strawberries with fresh raspberries for a more woodland flavor.
