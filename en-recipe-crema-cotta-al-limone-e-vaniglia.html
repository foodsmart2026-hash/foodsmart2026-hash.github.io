Lemon and Vanilla Cooked Cream

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 10 min · Easy

A silky and refreshing spoon dessert made with basic pantry staples. Very budget-friendly, it is inspired by the tradition of homemade Italian custard.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🥕 Vegetarian🧒 Kid-friendly🌾 Gluten-free🇮🇹 Italian

A staple of Italian home baking, this cooked cream combines the zest of lemon with the smooth sweetness of vanilla. Its silky and velvety texture makes it ideal as a standalone dessert or a rich filling for cakes and tarts.

Ingredients

  • Whole milk500 ml
  • Egg yolks4 pcs
  • Caster sugar100 g
  • Cornflour (Maizena)40 g
  • Lemon (zest)1 pcs
  • Vanilla essenceq.b.

Allergens

Eggs, Milk

Method

  1. Begin by pouring 500 ml of whole milk into a heavy-bottomed saucepan, adding the zest of one whole lemon and vanilla essence to taste. Heat the liquid over medium heat until it is just about to boil, which is when you see the first small bubbles rising to the surface. Stir occasionally with a whisk to evenly distribute the heat and the essential oils from the aromatics. The final result should be a very hot and intensely fragrant milk. Chef's tip: use a vegetable peeler to remove the zest, taking extreme care not to include the spongy white pith which would give your custard a bitter aftertaste.
  2. While the milk is heating, carefully separate 4 egg yolks into a large bowl and add 100 g of granulated sugar. Use a hand whisk to whip the mixture with vigorous and constant movements for about two minutes. You must achieve a light, frothy, and slightly aerated mass that falls in a 'ribbon' from the whisk. Working this phase well ensures a much silkier final texture. Chef's tip: do not leave the sugar in contact with the yolks without stirring, as the sugar will chemically 'cook' the yolk, creating small solid lumps that are impossible to dissolve later.
  3. Carefully sift 40 g of cornflour (cornstarch) directly over the egg and sugar mixture, incorporating it with the whisk. Mix from the centre outwards until the powder has been completely absorbed without leaving any white traces. You should obtain a thick, smooth, and perfectly homogeneous batter. This phase is crucial for the stability of the custard during the subsequent cooking stage. Chef's tip: sifting the starch is a mandatory step to avoid the formation of annoying lumps that would compromise the delicacy of the dessert.
  4. Retrieve the hot milk from the heat and remove the lemon zests using a slotted spoon or a fine-mesh sieve. Pour just one ladle of hot milk into the bowl with the yolks and starch, whisking quickly to temper the mixture. This step serves to gradually balance the temperatures, preventing the sudden heat from turning the eggs into an omelette. You will notice the mixture becoming more fluid and glossy. Chef's tip: pour the milk in a thin stream along the edge of the bowl while continuing to move the whisk for total temperature control.
  5. Now transfer all the diluted egg mixture back into the saucepan where the rest of the milk remains, scraping the sides of the bowl well with a spatula. Return the saucepan to the heat, setting the flame to a medium-low intensity. Start stirring without ever stopping, drawing 'eights' on the bottom of the pan to cover the entire cooking surface. This constant movement ensures that the custard does not stick or burn on the bottom. Chef's tip: use a stainless steel whisk with flexible wires to perfectly reach every corner of the saucepan during this delicate phase.
  6. Continue cooking for about 4-5 minutes, carefully observing the change in density that will happen almost suddenly. The custard is ready when it begins to 'coat the back of a spoon' and the first thick, slow boiling bubbles appear. Remove the pan from the heat immediately as soon as the consistency becomes thick and velvety, similar to a soft pudding. Prolonged cooking beyond this point would ruin the delicate egg aroma. Chef's tip: if you notice the custard thickening too quickly, lift the pan off the heat while continuing to whisk vigorously to better manage the residual heat.
  7. Immediately divide the still-hot cooked custard into 4 individual bowls or dessert glasses, distributing it in equal parts. Cover each portion immediately with cling film, making it adhere directly to the surface of the custard (contact film). This precaution prevents the air from drying out the top, avoiding the formation of that hard and unpleasant skin. You should obtain a perfectly smooth surface under the film. Chef's tip: if you want a custard with impeccable shine, you can pass it through a very fine-mesh sieve before portioning it.
  8. Let the bowls rest at room temperature until they are no longer hot to the touch, then transfer them to the refrigerator. The ideal cooling time is at least 2 hours, so that the cornstarch structure stabilises correctly, giving the right consistency. Serve the custard well chilled, perhaps decorating it at the last moment with a fine grating of fresh lemon zest or a vanilla pod. The result will be a fresh, balanced, and incredibly creamy spoon dessert. Chef's tip: prepare the custard a few hours in advance, as the cold of the fridge significantly enhances the aromatic notes of the lemon.

Tips

  • To prevent lumps, pour the warm milk slowly into the egg mixture while whisking continuously.
  • Place plastic wrap directly on the surface of the cream to stop a skin from forming as it cools.
  • Store the cream in the refrigerator for up to two days in an airtight container.

Variations

  • Replace the lemon zest with orange zest for a deeper and more aromatic citrus flavor.
  • Serve the cream in individual ramekins accompanied by dry biscuits for a crunchy contrast.
