Creamy Ditalini with Baby Peas and Fresh Ricotta

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

A delicate pasta dish where the peas are blended for a velvety consistency suitable for little ones. The ricotta adds creaminess without the need for heavy fats.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⬇️ Big saving🧒 Kid-friendly🇮🇹 Italian

A simple and creamy first course, ideal for a quick yet satisfying lunch thanks to the freshness of baby peas. Fresh ricotta cheese coats the ditalini pasta, creating a velvety texture enriched by Parmigiano Reggiano. The taste is mild and delicate, making it perfect for the whole family.

Ingredients

  • Ditalini pasta (small tube-shaped pasta)160 g
  • Frozen baby peas200 g
  • Cow's milk ricotta100 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil20 ml
  • Grated Parmigiano Reggiano (aged Italian hard cheese)20 g

Allergens

Gluten, Milk

Method

  1. Fill a large pot with approximately 2 litres of water and bring it to a boil over high heat, avoiding salt if the dish is intended for young children to keep it naturally low in sodium. You should achieve a steady, vigorous boil before proceeding with the cooking process. Chef's tip: cover the pot with a lid during this stage to speed up the process and save energy.
  2. Pour the 200g of fine frozen peas into the boiling water and cook them for about 8-10 minutes over medium heat. The legumes must become extremely tender, almost falling apart when pressed with a fork, to ensure a velvety base for your sauce. Chef's tip: do not overcook the peas beyond the suggested time, or they will lose their bright green colour and turn a dull grey.
  3. Drain the peas using a slotted spoon, making sure to keep the cooking water in the pot, and transfer them to a tall blending beaker. Blend them with an immersion blender, adding a tablespoon of the cooking water, until you obtain a perfectly smooth, thick cream without any visible skins. Chef's tip: for an even more refined texture for toddlers, pass the cream through a fine-mesh sieve to remove any remaining fibre.
  4. Add the 160g of ditalini pasta directly into the water where the peas were boiled, which is now rich in flavour and nutrients, and cook for the time indicated on the packet. I suggest cooking it for an extra minute beyond 'al dente' to achieve a softer texture that is safer for easy chewing. Chef's tip: stir the pasta frequently during the first few minutes to prevent the small shapes from sticking to the bottom of the pot.
  5. While the pasta is cooking, place the 100g of cow's milk ricotta in a small bowl and soften it with a tablespoon of hot cooking water taken from the pot. Work it with a spoon or a small whisk until it transforms into a fluid and velvety cream, removing the typical graininess of fresh ricotta. Chef's tip: ensure the ricotta is at room temperature before mixing to achieve a smoother emulsion without thermal shock.
  6. Drain the ditalini once the desired softness is reached, reserving a small ladle of water, and return them to the pot away from the heat. Immediately add the pea cream and the prepared ricotta cream, beginning to mix with circular motions. Chef's tip: adding the dressing off the heat preserves the delicacy of the ricotta and the freshness of the peas' colour.
  7. Add the 20ml of raw extra virgin olive oil and the 20g of grated Parmigiano Reggiano to the pasta, continuing to stir vigorously to distribute the flavours. You should achieve a creamy and glossy result where every single ditalino is evenly coated in the green and white sauce. Chef's tip: raw oil is essential to keep the properties of essential fatty acids intact and provide a fruity note that balances the sweetness of the peas.
  8. Serve the pasta lukewarm in shallow bowls, checking that the consistency is moist and not too dry; if necessary, add the last tablespoon of reserved cooking water. The dish should look inviting, with a delicate scent of milk and fresh vegetables, perfect for easy digestion. Chef's tip: avoid serving the dish piping hot, as the perception of the delicate flavours of ricotta and young peas is enhanced at moderate temperatures.

Tips

  • Remember to thin the ricotta with a little pasta cooking water to achieve a smooth, lump-free cream.
  • Use the same water used for boiling the peas to cook the pasta for extra flavor.
  • Serve immediately to enjoy the maximum creaminess of the ricotta.

Variations

  • For a more intense flavor, you can substitute the cow's milk ricotta with sheep's milk ricotta.
  • Add a sprinkle of freshly ground black pepper for a final spicy note.
