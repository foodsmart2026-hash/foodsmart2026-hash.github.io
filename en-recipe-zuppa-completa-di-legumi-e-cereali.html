Complete Legume and Grain Soup

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 55 min · Hard

A thick and comforting preparation that perfectly balances plant proteins and complex carbohydrates. The addition of anchovies, a typical affordable oily fish, provides a unique depth of flavour and precious essential fats without increasing the cost of the dish.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free

Ingredients

  • Pearl spelt200 g
  • Small dried lentils (no soaking required)200 g
  • Anchovy fillets in oil20 g
  • Carrots150 g
  • Celery (stalks)100 g
  • Golden onion100 g
  • Cavolo nero (Tuscan kale, or chard in season)250 g
  • Tomato purée (concentrate)30 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil50 ml
  • Hot water or vegetable stock1600 ml
  • Whole sea salt5 g
  • Ground black pepper2 g

Allergens

Gluten, Fish, Celery

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the aromatic base (soffritto) by finely chopping 150g of carrots, 100g of celery, and 100g of yellow onion. Use a very sharp knife to achieve a dice of just a few millimetres, which is essential for the vegetables to melt harmoniously into the soup during cooking. This task requires about 10 minutes of manual precision. The final result should be a uniform, colourful mix, ready to release all its essential oils. My advice is to avoid using a blender at high speed to prevent oxidising or overheating the vegetables.
  2. Thoroughly rinse 200g of pearl spelt and 200g of small lentils under cold running water using a fine-mesh sieve. Move the grains with your hands until the water runs perfectly clear and free of starch or residual impurities. This cleaning phase ensures the soup doesn't become overly cloudy and the flavours remain clean. You will have shiny grains and pulses ready for toasting. A little secret: rinsing lentils well significantly improves their digestibility.
  3. In a large, heavy-bottomed pot, pour 30ml of extra virgin olive oil and add 20g of anchovy fillets. Heat over a very low flame and use a wooden spoon to gently press the anchovies against the bottom, facilitating their complete dissolution. In about 2-3 minutes, the anchovies will transform into a savoury, fragrant, and almost invisible paste. The result will be an incredibly deep umami base. Be careful not to burn the oil; if you see smoke, remove the pot from the heat immediately.
  4. Add the previously prepared chopped vegetables to the oil and anchovy base. Allow the soffritto to soften over medium-low heat for about 8 minutes, stirring occasionally for even cooking. The vegetables should become tender and the onion should appear almost translucent without browning or burning. You will smell a sweet and inviting aroma releasing from the pot. Chef's tip: a pinch of salt now will help the vegetables release their moisture, improving the texture of the soffritto.
  5. Stir 30g of tomato paste into the soffritto and toast it for about 2 minutes, stirring vigorously. This is a technical step: direct heat caramelises the sugars in the tomato, removing acidity and giving the soup a deep, brownish-red colour. You will see the paste darken slightly and stick to the vegetables. You will achieve a concentrated and powerful aromatic base. Do not skip this step, as it provides the soup with that 'old-fashioned' depth typical of professional cooking.
  6. Pour the spelt and lentils into the pot and let them infuse for 3 minutes over medium heat, continuing to stir. This dry toasting allows the grains to absorb the base flavours and partially seals the pulse surfaces, helping them maintain their shape during cooking. You will notice a slight nutty aroma and see the grains become translucent at the edges. The result is a well-coated and vibrant mix. Remember to stir constantly to prevent the spelt from sticking to the bottom of the pot.
  7. Add 1600ml of hot water (or vegetable stock) and bring to a boil. Once boiling, lower the heat to minimum, cover with a lid slightly ajar, and simmer gently for 30 minutes. The heat must be constant but never violent to allow the lentils to cook without breaking apart. The soup will begin to thicken, and the aromas will blend perfectly. The advice is to use already hot water to avoid a thermal shock to the pulses, keeping them more tender.
  8. While the soup cooks, prepare 250g of black kale by removing the woody central ribs by hand and cutting the leaves into 1cm strips. After the first 30 minutes of cooking the soup, add the kale directly to the pot and continue cooking for another 10-15 minutes. The leaves should wilt and become tender but maintain a vibrant dark green colour. The soup will now be rich, thick, and complete. If you prefer a creamier texture, you can mash a few spoonfuls of lentils against the side of the pot.
  9. Turn off the heat and balance the flavours by adding 5g of sea salt and 2g of freshly ground black pepper. Stir thoroughly and let the soup rest in the covered pot for 5 minutes before serving. This resting period is vital as it allows the fibres to relax and the temperature to drop slightly, making the flavours more discernible to the palate. The result will be a balanced, fragrant soup with a silky texture. The chef suggests always tasting before salting, as the anchovies are already naturally salty.
  10. Serve the soup in warmed bowls, finishing each portion with a generous spoonful of the remaining extra virgin olive oil (20ml total across 4 servings). The oil added at the end will enhance the herbaceous notes of the kale and the rusticity of the spelt, creating a perfect contrast with the heat of the dish. The visual result will be glossy and inviting, with a scent of fresh olives. A final professional touch? A grate of extra black pepper directly at the table for an immediate aromatic kick.
