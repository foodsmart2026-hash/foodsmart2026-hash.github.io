Roasted chicken thighs with potatoes

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 55 min · Hard

A great Mediterranean classic that combines the juiciness of chicken thighs with the crunchiness of roast potatoes. This dish represents the perfect balance between economy and nutrition, ideal for Sunday lunch or a hearty dinner.

A timeless family classic pairing crispy chicken skin with tender, golden roasted potatoes. Fresh rosemary and sage release an inviting aroma throughout the baking process. This dish is the ideal choice for a flavorful and comforting Sunday lunch.

Ingredients

  • Chicken thighs with skin800 g
  • Yellow-fleshed potatoes800 g
  • Carrots200 g
  • Red onion150 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 ml
  • Dry white wine100 ml
  • Garlic2 cloves
  • Fresh rosemary and sage4 sprigs
  • Fine salt8 g
  • Black pepper2 g

Allergens

Sulphites

Method

  1. Dry 800 g of chicken thighs well with kitchen paper to remove all traces of surface moisture. Massage each piece with some of the salt and pepper, ensuring the seasonings adhere well to both the skin and the underside of the meat. This operation takes about 3 minutes and serves to ensure an optimal Maillard reaction during cooking. You will get a surface ready to become crispy and tasty, avoiding a boiled effect. Chef's tip: never wash chicken under water to avoid splashes that could spread bacteria on kitchen surfaces.
  2. Peel 800 g of yellow-fleshed potatoes and 200 g of carrots, then cut the potatoes into regular wedges and the carrots into rounds about 2 cm thick. Use a sharp kitchen knife and a stable cutting board to obtain pieces of uniform size, thus ensuring simultaneous cooking of all ingredients. Devote 5 minutes to this step, trying not to make the wedges too thin to prevent them from falling apart. The result should be a rustic but proportioned mix of vegetables. Chef's tip: leave the cut potatoes to soak in cold water for 10 minutes to remove excess starch and make them much crispier in the oven.
  3. Prepare a coarse chop with the rosemary needles, sage leaves, and 2 garlic cloves with the central germ removed. In a large bowl, mix these herbs with 40 ml of extra virgin olive oil, creating an immediate aromatic infusion. Let the oil rest for 5 minutes so that the essential oils of the aromatic plants are extracted by the vegetable fat. You will get a very fragrant seasoning that will distribute the flavour evenly across the dish. Chef's tip: if you prefer a more delicate taste, leave the garlic whole and 'in camicia' (unpeeled), only crushing it slightly with the palm of your hand.
  4. In a very large bowl, combine the chicken, potatoes, carrots, and the red onion cut into 8 thick wedges. Pour the flavoured oil over all the ingredients and mix vigorously with your hands or two large spoons for about 2 minutes. Ensure every single piece of meat and every vegetable cube is perfectly glossy and coated by the seasoning. You will see the colours of the vegetables become bright and the meat will begin to smell of the woods. Chef's tip: massaging the seasoning directly with your hands allows the aromas to penetrate better under the chicken skin.
  5. Preheat the oven to 200°C (static) and line a large baking tray or roasting tin with greaseproof paper. Arrange the chicken thighs with the skin side up and distribute the vegetables in the free spaces, trying not to overlap them excessively. Leaving space between the pieces is essential to allow the hot air to circulate and roast rather than stew the ingredients. The tray should look tidy, with colours well distributed over the entire surface. Chef's tip: place the potatoes closer to the edge of the tray, where the heat is usually more intense, for extra browning.
  6. Place the tray on the middle shelf of the oven and cook for the first 20 minutes at 200°C without covering. During this initial phase, the intense heat will sear the meat and begin to melt the subcutaneous fat of the chicken, which will naturally flavour the potatoes below. You will notice the skin starting to tighten and take on a slightly golden colour, while the onion begins to caramelise. The scent of rosemary will become the main indicator that cooking has started correctly. Chef's tip: do not open the oven during this phase to avoid sharp drops in temperature that would compromise crispness.
  7. Quickly remove the tray from the oven and deglaze the bottom with 100 ml of dry white wine, taking care not to wet the chicken skin directly so as not to compromise its crunchiness. Tilt the tray slightly to distribute the liquid among the vegetables, then reinsert it into the oven and continue cooking for another 25 minutes at 190°C. As the wine evaporates, it will create the necessary moisture to make the internal meat very tender and melt-in-the-mouth. The potatoes will absorb some of the wine, acquiring an acidic note that balances the chicken fat. Chef's tip: use a wine you would happily drink at the table; the quality of the wine directly affects the goodness of the final sauce.
  8. Activate the fan function or the grill for the last 5-7 minutes of cooking to finalise the surface browning. Monitor closely through the oven glass: the chicken skin must become brown and crispy like a wafer, and the potatoes must show well-toasted edges. This step is crucial for the final texture of the dish, transforming it from a simple roast into a gourmet homemade meal. The result will be a sharp contrast between the external crunchiness and the internal softness of the ingredients. Chef's tip: if you notice the carrots or onions darkening too much, cover that area with a small piece of foil.
  9. Remove the tray from the oven and let the chicken and potatoes rest for at least 5 minutes before plating. During this waiting time, the meat fibres will relax and the internal juices will redistribute, making every bite incredibly succulent. The potatoes will finish stabilising their consistency, becoming less fragile to the touch. You will notice that the cooking juices have thickened slightly, forming a tasty sauce ideal for drizzling over the meat. Chef's tip: never skip the rest; serving the chicken piping hot straight from the oven would cause all the precious juices to disperse on the plate.
  10. Place one chicken thigh in the centre of each flat plate and surround it with a generous mix of potatoes, carrots, and red onion wedges. Recover the cooking juices left on the baking paper with a spoon and pour it over the meat to enhance its gloss and flavour. Serve the dish very hot, perhaps accompanying it with an additional sprig of fresh rosemary for decoration. The final appearance must be that of a rich, homemade, and inviting dish, with warm colours and enveloping scents. Chef's tip: slightly warm the serving plates before serving to keep the chicken at temperature for longer during the meal.

Tips

  • Pat the chicken skin completely dry to ensure it becomes perfectly golden and crispy.
  • Cut the potatoes and carrots into similar sizes to ensure they cook evenly together.
  • Spread the ingredients across the tray without overlapping to achieve a better roast.

Variations

  • Replace the white wine with lemon juice for a more intense citrus finish.
  • Add Taggiasca olives during the last ten minutes of roasting for an extra savory kick.
