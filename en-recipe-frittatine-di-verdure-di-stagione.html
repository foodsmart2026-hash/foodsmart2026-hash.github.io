Seasonal Vegetable Mini-Frittatas

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

A nutritious and versatile starter that combines the freshness of courgettes with the savouriness of chickpeas. These small golden frittatas are ideal for introducing pulses and fibre into the family diet in a tasty and economical way.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🧒 Kid-friendly

Small vegetable-rich omelets, perfect for a lunch on the go or a colorful buffet. The soft texture is enriched by the presence of chickpeas and the bold note of Parmigiano Reggiano.

Ingredients

  • Large eggs4 pz
  • Courgettes200 g
  • Carrots100 g
  • Canned chickpeas150 g
  • Wholemeal wheat flour40 g
  • Grated Parmigiano Reggiano (a hard, aged Italian PDO cheese)30 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil25 ml
  • Unrefined sea salt5 g
  • Black pepper2 g
  • Fresh parsley10 g

Allergens

Gluten, Eggs, Milk

Method

  1. Start by preparing the vegetable base: thoroughly wash 200g of courgettes and 100g of carrots, trim them, and coarsely grate them using the large holes of a box grater to preserve the fibre. This technique allows the vegetables to maintain an 'al dente' bite even after cooking, avoiding a mushy texture. Place the grated mix in a large colander over a bowl, sprinkle with a small portion of the 5g of salt, and mix by hand. My advice: let the veg rest for at least 10 minutes, then squeeze them firmly to remove all excess water, ensuring crispy rather than soggy fritters.
  2. Move on to the pulses by taking 150g of pre-cooked chickpeas: drain them from their canning liquid and rinse well under cold running water to remove excess sodium. Transfer them to a chopping board and mash about half of them with the tines of a fork until you get a coarse paste, leaving the rest whole. This contrast in textures is essential: the mashed chickpeas act as a natural binder, while the whole ones provide structure to the bite. Chef's tip: avoid using a blender; the irregular hand-mashed texture is the secret to a rustic, artisanal feel.
  3. Prepare the protein base by cracking 4 large eggs into a large, clean bowl. Add 30g of grated Parmesan cheese, the black pepper (2g), and 10g of fresh parsley that you have previously finely chopped with a knife. Whisk vigorously with a hand whisk for about 2 minutes until the mixture is slightly frothy and well combined. My advice: use room-temperature eggs, as they bind more effectively with the fats in the cheese, creating a more stable emulsion.
  4. Integrate the dry ingredients by sifting 40g of wholemeal flour directly over the egg and cheese mixture. Fold gently using a spatula or whisk with upward motions, ensuring no visible lumps of flour remain. You will obtain a thick, fragrant batter with the amber hue characteristic of wholemeal grain. Chef's tip: sifting wholemeal flour is a golden rule to prevent lumps that would stay raw inside during the quick pan-frying process.
  5. Now combine the squeezed vegetables and the chickpeas (both mashed and whole) with the egg and flour batter. Mix thoroughly with a wooden spoon or spatula until every fragment of vegetable is perfectly coated by the protein mix. The result should be a very rich, compact mixture where the vegetables are the absolute stars. My advice: do not let the final batter sit for too long, as any residual salt in the vegetables might draw out more moisture, making the mixture too runny.
  6. Move to the cooking stage by heating a 28-30cm non-stick frying pan with 15ml of extra virgin olive oil over a medium heat. Tilt the pan to distribute the oil evenly across the surface; when the oil begins to shimmer slightly, it is ready. Do not wait for it to smoke, to avoid altering the oil's nutritional properties. Chef's tip: test the temperature by dropping a tiny bit of batter into the pan; if it sizzles immediately without browning too fast, it is perfect.
  7. Use a tablespoon to scoop portions of the batter and drop them into the hot pan, forming small discs spaced apart. Lightly flatten each fritter with the back of the spoon to achieve a thickness of about 1.5cm, allowing the heat to reach the core quickly. Cook over medium heat for about 4 minutes without moving them. My advice: do not overcrowd the pan; cook in batches to prevent the oil temperature from dropping, which would compromise the golden crust.
  8. Flip the fritters with a thin spatula only when the edges appear well-browned and the base releases easily from the bottom of the pan. Continue cooking for another 4 minutes on the second side, maintaining a steady flame to achieve an even golden-brown colour. To the touch, the fritters should feel firm with an inviting outer crust. Chef's tip: if you notice them browning too quickly, lower the heat slightly; the centre must cook through without the exterior burning.
  9. Once ready, remove the fritters and place them on a plate lined with kitchen paper to drain any excess oil while maintaining their crispness. Proceed in the same way with the rest of the batter, adding the remaining 10ml of oil if necessary. Serve them lukewarm to best appreciate the sweetness of the carrots and the bold flavour of the Parmesan. My advice: serve with a squeeze of fresh lemon or a light yoghurt dip to balance the richness and cleanse the palate.
  10. Present your seasonal fritters slightly stacked or arranged in a circle on a serving platter, garnishing with a sprig of fresh parsley. A standard portion is about 3-4 fritters per person, depending on the size made. The final result will be a balanced, colourful, and fibre-rich dish, perfect as a light main or a nutritious starter. Chef's tip: these fritters are also excellent cold the next day, making them ideal for a packed lunch or the office.

Tips

  • Use a high-quality non-stick pan to successfully flip the small omelets without breaking them.
  • Ensure the vegetables are well-dried before adding them to the eggs to avoid thinning the mixture too much.
  • You can bake the mixture in muffin tins at 180°C for a more uniform and regular shape.

Variations

  • Replace Parmigiano with aged pecorino cheese for a sharper and more traditional taste.
  • Try using peas instead of chickpeas for a sweeter-flavored variation.
