Spaghetti with sardines and breadcrumbs

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

A healthy reimagining of Sicilian tradition, affordable and rich in precious Omega-3 thanks to the fresh oily fish. The use of wholewheat pasta and toasted breadcrumbs ensures a controlled glycaemic index and an irresistible crunchy texture.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free

A simple yet characterful seafood dish where fresh sardine saltiness meets bright citrus notes. Toasted breadcrumbs provide a delightful crunch that contrasts beautifully with the soft sauce.

Ingredients

  • wholewheat spaghetti320 g
  • fresh sardines (cleaned and butterflied)300 g
  • wholewheat breadcrumbs60 g
  • extra virgin olive oil40 ml
  • cherry tomatoes200 g
  • garlic2 spicchi
  • organic lemon (zest)1 pz
  • fresh parsley20 g
  • fresh chilli1 pz

Allergens

Gluten, Fish

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the aromatics: wash 20g of fresh parsley, dry it thoroughly, and finely chop it with a knife along with one fresh chilli pepper (seeds removed). Wash 200g of cherry tomatoes and carefully cut them into quarters to facilitate the release of their juices during cooking. Place everything on a clean chopping board, keeping the ingredients separate but ready for quick use. The final result should be a fragrant mix and uniform tomatoes that will ensure even cooking. Chef's tip: chop the parsley only at the last moment to prevent it from oxidising and losing its bright green colour.
  2. Take a small non-stick frying pan and pour in one teaspoon of extra virgin olive oil taken from the 40ml total, then add 60g of wholemeal breadcrumbs. Toast the breadcrumbs over medium heat for about 3-4 minutes, constantly moving the pan or stirring with a wooden spoon to avoid local burning. You should obtain breadcrumbs with a beautiful dark amber colour, a crunchy texture, and a very intense aroma of toasted grain. As soon as they reach the desired browning, transfer them immediately to a cold bowl to stop residual cooking. Chef's tip: watch the colour closely, as breadcrumbs can turn from golden to burnt in a matter of seconds.
  3. Fill a large pot with approximately 4 litres of water and bring it to a rolling boil over high heat. Add a moderate amount of coarse salt, bearing in mind that the sardines have a natural saltiness that will be released into the sauce. The water temperature must remain constant to receive the 320g of wholemeal spaghetti, which requires even heat to cook the fibres properly. The result should be clear, slightly salted water, ready to transform the pasta. Chef's tip: do not add oil to the pasta water, as it would create a film that prevents the sauce from adhering correctly to the spaghetti.
  4. In a very wide frying pan or sauté pan, heat the remaining extra virgin olive oil with the two previously crushed garlic cloves and the chopped chilli. Maintain a gentle heat for about 2 minutes, allowing the oil to become infused without the garlic browning excessively, which would make the dish bitter. The oil will begin to sizzle slightly, releasing the typical scents of Mediterranean cuisine in a delicate way. You will obtain a perfect aromatic base, warm and fragrant, ready to receive the other ingredients. Chef's tip: crush the garlic 'in camicia' (with the skin on) for a more delicate aroma, or peeled for a bolder flavour, but remember to remove it after browning.
  5. Pour the 200g of cut cherry tomatoes into the pan with the aromatic oil and slightly increase the heat to medium intensity. Cook for about 5 minutes, using a fork or a wooden spoon to gently crush the tomatoes as they soften, encouraging the creation of a moist base. The tomatoes should visibly soften, creating a light but tasty sauce that will act as a binder for the entire dish. The scent will become sweeter and the consistency of the base will be slightly creamy thanks to the released pulp. Chef's tip: if the tomatoes seem too dry, add half a ladle of hot water to keep the sauce fluid.
  6. Add the 300g of fresh sardines, already cleaned and butterflied, carefully arranging them over the prepared tomato sauce. Cook over medium heat for only 2-3 minutes, turning them gently halfway through with kitchen tongs so as not to break them too much. The oily fish will change colour, becoming white and opaque, while remaining tender and juicy at the core. It is vital not to overcook to prevent the sardines from becoming dry or falling apart completely in the sauce. Chef's tip: place the sardines skin-side down for the first 60 seconds to preserve their structural integrity.
  7. Submerge the 320g of wholemeal spaghetti in the boiling water and set the timer for 2 minutes less than the time indicated on the package. Stir immediately with a pasta fork to separate the strands well and ensure even cooking at every point. While the pasta cooks, take a ladleful of the starchy cooking water and set it aside in a cup. The spaghetti must still be quite firm ('al dente') when you drain them, as they will finish cooking in the pan. Chef's tip: wholemeal pasta has different hydration times than white pasta, so taste it a minute early to monitor the texture.
  8. Transfer the spaghetti directly into the pan with the sardine and tomato sauce, immediately adding the reserved ladle of cooking water. Sauté the pasta over high heat for the final 2 minutes, moving the pan with a circular motion to encourage emulsion between the water starch and the oil. You will see a thick and glossy cream form, perfectly coating every single strand of spaghetti and binding it harmoniously to the sauce. The result should be a moist dish, not dry, where the flavours of the sea and the land blend. Chef's tip: use pasta tongs to lift the spaghetti and incorporate air, making the emulsion even lighter and fluffier.
  9. Turn off the heat and proceed with finishing the dish by adding the grated zest of one organic lemon, the chopped parsley, and half of the 60g of toasted breadcrumbs. Stir one last time gently to evenly distribute the fresh aromas and the first part of the crunchy component. The citrus scent of the lemon will perfectly balance the richness of the sardines, giving immediate freshness to every bite. You will obtain a balanced whole where the citric note enhances the saltiness of the fish and the toasted bread. Chef's tip: only grate the yellow part of the lemon zest, avoiding the white pith which would be unpleasantly bitter.
  10. Plate immediately by distributing the pasta into four soup or dinner plates, making sure to gather the sardine and tomato sauce from the bottom of the pan. Complete each portion by sprinkling the rest of the crunchy breadcrumbs over the top, which will add a fundamental texture contrast. Serve the dish very hot, so that the heat releases all the essential oils from the lemon and parsley. The final result is a balanced first course, rich in Omega-3 and fibre, with an inviting and artisanal appearance. Chef's tip: serve in pre-warmed plates to maintain the optimal temperature of the pasta throughout the meal.

Tips

  • Toast the breadcrumbs over low heat, stirring constantly to prevent them from burning and turning bitter.
  • Add the fresh sardines only in the last few minutes of cooking to keep them from falling apart too much.
  • Drain the spaghetti very al dente and finish cooking them in the pan with a ladle of starchy pasta water.

Variations

  • Add some desalted capers to enrich the sauce with a bolder savory touch.
  • Replace fresh chili with orange zest for an even more citrusy and less spicy version.
