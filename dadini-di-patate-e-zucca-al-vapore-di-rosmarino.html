Steamed Potato and Squash Cubes with Rosemary

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 18 min · Medium

An autumn classic with soft pieces that melt in the mouth, steamed to maintain nutrients and natural sweetness. The rosemary delicately scents the cooking water.

A classic garden pairing prepared using the gentle technique of steam cooking. The sweetness of the squash and the texture of the potatoes blend beautifully, scented by the timeless aroma of fresh rosemary.

Ingredients

  • Mantovana or Butternut squash (Mantovana is a sweet, floury Italian pumpkin)250 g
  • Yellow-fleshed potatoes250 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil15 g
  • Fresh rosemary1 sprig

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the pumpkin (Mantovana or Butternut): carefully remove the outer skin with a sharp knife, scoop out the seeds and fibrous strands with a spoon, then cut 250g of the flesh into neat 1cm cubes. Precision in dicing is essential to ensure every piece cooks at the same rate without falling apart. You should aim for uniform, bright orange cubes ready for consistent steaming. The chef's tip is to use a stable chopping board and a very sharp knife to prevent the blade from slipping on the tough pumpkin skin.
  2. Move on to the yellow-fleshed potatoes: peel 250g with a vegetable peeler and dice them into cubes exactly the same size as the pumpkin, 1cm per side. Rinse them quickly under cold running water to remove excess surface starch, then pat them dry with a clean tea towel. The visual result should be a balanced mix of yellow and orange cubes of equal proportions. The chef's tip is not to leave the cut potatoes exposed to air for too long to prevent oxidation, which can cause them to turn a dull grey colour.
  3. Prepare the aromatic base by pouring about two fingers of water into the bottom of a large pot and adding a well-washed sprig of fresh rosemary. Turn the heat to medium and bring the water to a gentle simmer, allowing the herb's essential oils to infuse into the rising steam. You should notice a very pleasant, balsamic aroma filling the kitchen without being overpowering. The chef's tip is to lightly bruise the rosemary between your hands before adding it to the water to break the cell membranes and release more fragrance.
  4. Place the steamer basket inside the pot, ensuring the bottom of the basket does not touch the rosemary-scented water. Distribute the pumpkin and potato cubes evenly inside the basket, trying not to overcrowd them so the steam can circulate freely between the pieces. Cover the pot with a tight-fitting lid to prevent any heat or steam from escaping. The chef's tip is to place the potatoes at the bottom of the basket and the pumpkin on top, as pumpkin tends to cook slightly faster than potato.
  5. Maintain a medium heat for approximately 15-18 minutes, timing from the moment you see steady wisps of steam escaping from the lid. Do not lift the lid during this process, as this will drop the internal temperature and interrupt the steaming. By the end of the cooking time, the vegetables should be tender but still hold their shape, with colours slightly more vivid than when raw. The chef's tip is to adjust the flame so the simmer is constant but not aggressive, preventing the water from evaporating completely.
  6. Check the exact cooking point by gently piercing a potato cube with the tines of a fork: there should be minimal resistance, and the potato should feel soft all the way to the centre. If the potato is done, the pumpkin (which has a less dense structure) will certainly be ready too. Carefully remove the steamer basket from the pot using oven gloves to avoid being scalded by the hot steam. The chef's tip is to test the vegetables at exactly 15 minutes to prevent the pumpkin from becoming too watery and losing its creamy texture.
  7. Transfer the cooked cubes into a large bowl and, while still steaming hot, use a fork to roughly mash about a quarter of the vegetables. This creates a creamy 'binder' that coats the remaining whole cubes, making the dish more palatable and easier to scoop. The final texture should be a delightful contrast between the smooth puree and the structured whole cubes. The chef's tip is to avoid using a blender, as manual mashing ensures a superior, rustic, and artisanal texture.
  8. Finish the dish by drizzling 15g of extra virgin olive oil over the vegetables and folding it in very gently with a spoon. Adding raw oil at the end not only enhances the natural flavour of the vegetables but also helps the absorption of fat-soluble vitamins and gives the dish an inviting sheen. You will notice the subtle rosemary aroma, infused during steaming, blending perfectly with the olive oil's herbaceous notes. The chef's tip is to add a pinch of freshly ground white pepper if you want an extra aromatic touch without masking the pumpkin's natural sweetness.

Tips

  • Cut the squash and potatoes into equal-sized cubes so they reach the perfect cooking point simultaneously.
  • Ensure the water in the pot never touches the steamer basket to guarantee true steam cooking.
  • Season with extra virgin olive oil only after cooking to preserve its full flavor.

Variations

  • Add a whole garlic clove to the cooking water to infuse the cubes with a subtle, pungent aroma.
  • Replace the rosemary with fresh thyme for a more delicate and floral herbal note.
