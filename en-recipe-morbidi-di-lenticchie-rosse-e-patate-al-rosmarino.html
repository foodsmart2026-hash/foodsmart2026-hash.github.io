Soft Red Lentil and Potato Bites with Rosemary

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

A nutritious and creamy plant-based main course, where split red lentils blend with the sweetness of the potato. The consistency is similar to a solid mash, perfect for baby-led weaning and safe to swallow.

A nutritious and fragrant plant-based alternative, perfect for those seeking the cozy texture of legumes paired with potatoes. Rosemary adds a Mediterranean touch to this rustic and wholesome dish.

Ingredients

  • Split red lentils120 g
  • Yellow-fleshed potatoes250 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil15 ml
  • Fresh rosemary1 sprig
  • Water400 ml

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Begin by thoroughly rinsing the 120g of split red lentils under cold running water using a fine-mesh sieve. Move the pulses with your fingers until the water running through them is completely clear and free of foam or impurities. This process takes about 2 minutes and is crucial for ensuring even cooking and a clean flavour. The final result should be a mass of shiny, dust-free lentils. Chef's tip: Do not skip this step, as removing excess starch prevents the lentils from becoming overly gluey during cooking.
  2. Take the 250g of yellow potatoes, peel them carefully, and cut them into very small cubes, roughly 1cm in size. Aim for a consistent size for each cube, using a sharp knife on a stable chopping board. This precision allows the potatoes to cook in the same timeframe as the lentils, preventing any hard bits. You will have uniform cubes that smell of fresh earth. Chef's tip: Cutting the potatoes into small pieces is the secret to achieving a velvety purée without having to overwork the mixture later.
  3. Transfer the rinsed lentils and potato cubes into a large, heavy-bottomed saucepan, adding the whole sprig of fresh rosemary. Pour in 400ml of cold water until the ingredients are completely covered, making sure no debris is stuck to the sides. Place the pot over a high heat and wait for the liquid to reach a boil. You will see the water turn slightly cloudy and start to smell the aromatic fragrance released by the rosemary. Chef's tip: Keep the rosemary sprig whole rather than chopped so you can easily remove it later, preventing needles from being unpleasant in the final texture.
  4. As soon as the water boils, reduce the heat to minimum and cover the pot with a lid, leaving just a small gap for steam. Let it simmer gently for about 20 minutes, checking occasionally to ensure the liquid isn't evaporating too quickly. The potatoes should become extremely tender and the lentils should start to break down naturally, creating almost a cream. You will notice the colour of the lentils shift from bright orange to a delicate pale yellow. Chef's tip: If you notice the water drying up before the 20 minutes are up, add a tablespoon of hot water to maintain the cooking process.
  5. After 20 minutes, turn off the heat and gently remove the rosemary sprig using kitchen tongs. If there is any excess water at the bottom of the pot, drain it carefully, but ensure the mixture remains visibly moist and not dry. The mixture should appear thick and fragrant, ready to be mashed. The ideal consistency is that of a very thick soup or a dry stew. Chef's tip: Do not over-drain, as the residual moisture is necessary to obtain soft medallions that won't crack in the oven.
  6. Use a fork or a potato masher to work the lentil and potato mix directly in the still-warm pot. Mash with firm movements until you have achieved a homogenous consistency free of lumps or whole potato pieces. The mass should be easily mouldable, similar to a very compact mash that is velvety to the touch. You should have a thick cream with a uniform colour and an inviting rosemary scent. Chef's tip: Avoid using an immersion blender, as it would make the mixture too elastic and difficult to shape by hand.
  7. Take a small amount of the mixture, roughly the size of a child's palm, and form small balls with slightly dampened hands. Flatten them gently to create medallions or small flat patties of uniform thickness, about 1.5cm. Place each piece onto a baking tray previously lined with parchment paper, spacing them slightly apart. You will get about 12-16 small, rustically shaped and inviting medallions. Chef's tip: Moistening your hands with cold water prevents the mixture from sticking to your skin, allowing for a smoother surface.
  8. Preheat your oven to 180°C (static mode) and bake the tray for just 5 minutes. This step is not for cooking but solely to set the structure of the medallions using dry heat. Remove the tray as soon as the edges seem slightly firmer, but before any dark or hard crusts form. The result should be a bite-sized piece that remains tender in the middle and easy to chew. Chef's tip: Do not exceed 5-6 minutes in the oven, otherwise the lentils will dry out too much, losing their characteristic softness.
  9. Let the medallions cool slightly to room temperature directly on the tray for a few minutes. Before serving, season raw with the 15ml of extra virgin olive oil, distributing it equally over each portion. The oil will add shine, softness, and a fruity flavour that enhances the sweetness of the potatoes. You will see the medallions glisten and release their full herbal aroma. Chef's tip: Dressing the dish raw preserves all the nutritional properties of the EVO oil and ensures a superior mouthfeel compared to cooked oil.

Tips

  • Cut the potatoes into very small cubes to ensure even cooking along with the lentils.
  • Mash the mixture while still warm to get a smoother and more workable puree.
  • If the mixture is too wet, let it rest for a few minutes so the lentils can absorb the liquid.

Variations

  • Enrich the dough with a tablespoon of nutritional yeast for a cheesy flavor note.
  • Replace rosemary with sage for a different but equally autumnal aroma.
