Red Lentil, Carrot and Ginger Soup

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 20 min · Easy

A protein-rich and warming smooth soup with a vibrant colour. Red split lentils cook in just a few minutes and create a thick consistency without the need for potatoes.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🥕 Vegetarian🌾 Gluten-free🌍 International

A warm, comforting soup with a vibrant color, ideal for chilly days. The fresh touch of ginger balances the creaminess of the red lentils, offering an exotic yet delicate flavor.

Ingredients

  • red split lentils250 g
  • carrots300 g
  • fresh ginger20 g
  • brown onion1 pcs
  • vegetable stock1 L
  • Greek yoghurt100 g

Allergens

Milk, Celery

Method

  1. Begin by carefully preparing the aromatic base: peel the golden onion and finely dice it, then peel the carrots and slice them into thin rounds about 2mm thick. Use a sharp chef's knife to ensure precise cuts, which will guarantee the vegetables cook quickly and evenly. This preparation should take about 5 minutes and ensures all ingredients are ready for the cooking stages. You are aiming for a mix of fresh, fragrant vegetables ready for sautéing. My professional tip is to keep the carrot slices very thin, almost translucent, so they melt perfectly into the lentils later on.
  2. Place a large, heavy-based pot over medium heat, add a drizzle of extra virgin olive oil, and wait for it to shimmer before adding the diced onion. Sweat the onion gently for about 4-5 minutes, stirring frequently with a wooden spoon until it becomes translucent and soft without browning. Once the onion is tender, add 20g of fresh ginger that you have peeled and finely grated using a Microplane or fine grater. You should immediately smell a pungent, citrusy aroma filling the kitchen. Chef's tip: if the onion starts to colour too quickly, add a tablespoon of water to lower the pan temperature.
  3. At this stage, add the sliced carrots to the pot and let them cook with the onion and ginger for about 3 minutes over a medium-high flame. Stir constantly to ensure the carrots are coated in the aromatic oil and begin to slightly caramelise. This step is crucial for developing the natural sweetness of the carrots, which perfectly balances the spicy heat of the ginger. You will see the carrots turn a more vivid, glossy orange. The secret to a great soup is not to rush this initial sautéing phase, as it builds the foundational flavour profile.
  4. Take 250g of split red lentils and rinse them thoroughly under cold running water in a fine-mesh sieve until the water runs clear. Add them directly into the pot with the vegetables and toast them for 1-2 minutes, stirring vigorously to coat every grain with the seasoned oil. Toasting helps the lentils maintain some structure during the initial boil, preventing them from turning into an indistinct mush too quickly. You will notice the lentils change hue slightly, becoming almost shiny. Be careful to keep them moving so they do not stick to the bottom of the pot.
  5. Pour one litre of hot vegetable stock over the lentil and vegetable mixture, ensuring everything is completely submerged. Increase the heat to bring it to a boil, then immediately turn the flame down to the lowest setting to maintain a constant, gentle simmer. Partially cover the pot with a lid to prevent excessive evaporation while allowing steam to escape gradually. This slow-cooking phase will last roughly 15-20 minutes, allowing the flavours to meld harmoniously. Chef's tip: always use hot stock to avoid shocking the lentils, which can result in a grainy texture.
  6. Monitor the soup as it cooks: split red lentils are ready when they begin to break down naturally, losing their distinct shape and creating a thick, hearty consistency. The soup's colour will shift towards an inviting creamy orange, and the carrots should be soft enough to crush easily with a fork. If the soup appears too thick, feel free to add an extra ladle of stock to reach your preferred fluidity. The aroma should be a perfect balance between earthy lentils and fresh ginger. Avoid adding salt until this point to ensure the pulses remain tender.
  7. Turn off the heat and proceed to create the texture: use an immersion blender directly in the pot, but only blend about half of the soup. Move the blender in quick, intermittent bursts to achieve a 'mixed' consistency—partially velvety and smooth, partially rustic with whole carrot pieces. This contrast in textures makes the dish far more engaging on the palate than a completely smooth purée. The soup will look sophisticated yet home-cooked. My advice is not to over-blend; you want to retain some of the soul and structure of the ingredients.
  8. Taste the soup and season with salt and freshly cracked black pepper according to your preference. Serve the soup piping hot in large bowls or warmed stoneware to retain the heat. In the centre of each portion, gently place a generous dollop (about 25g) of cold Greek yoghurt without stirring it in immediately. The yoghurt provides a stunning visual contrast of white against orange and adds a refreshing acidity that cuts through the sweetness of the carrots. Final tip: finish with a tiny drizzle of raw olive oil and some fresh coriander or parsley for a pop of colour.

Tips

  • Rinse the lentils thoroughly under cold water until the water runs clear.
  • Add the Greek yogurt only when serving to maintain its freshness and thermal contrast.
  • Grate the fresh ginger at the very last moment to keep its pungent aroma intact.

Variations

  • Use coconut milk instead of yogurt for a dairy-free version.
  • Add a pinch of turmeric during cooking for an even more intense color.
