Semolina Milk Cream with Dried Apricots

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 5 min · Easy

A warm and velvety breakfast that uses the natural sweetness of dried apricots rehydrated in milk. The semolina ensures a smooth texture perfect for the transition to solid foods.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🥕 Vegetarian🧒 Kid-friendly

Ingredients

  • Wheat semolina (finely milled durum wheat)60 g
  • Whole milk500 ml
  • Dried apricots (no added sugar)6 pcs

Allergens

Gluten, Milk

Method

  1. Begin by soaking the 6 dried apricots in a small bowl with a little warm milk for about 15 minutes. This preliminary step allows the fruit fibres to fully rehydrate, making them soft and easy to process for a perfect final texture. Ensure they are completely submerged so that rehydration occurs evenly throughout the fruit. By the end of this time, the apricots should appear plump and yield easily to finger pressure. My professional tip is to use a portion of the 500 ml of milk specified in the recipe to avoid diluting the fruit's natural sweetness.
  2. Take the rehydrated apricots, drained of excess liquid, and chop them very finely on a board with a sharp knife or use a small stick blender with a tablespoon of the soaking milk. Process the mixture patiently until you achieve a smooth, thick paste, ensuring it is completely free of coarse pieces that might ruin the cream's silkiness. This puree will release a sugary aroma and a vibrant orange hue that will define the entire dish. The chef's secret is to pass the puree through a fine-mesh sieve if you desire a gourmet result, removing any remaining bits of skin.
  3. Pour the remaining 500 ml of whole milk into a heavy-bottomed saucepan, which ensures even heat distribution, and place it over a medium heat. Heat the liquid slowly, stirring occasionally with a wooden spoon to prevent a protein skin from forming on the surface. Turn off the heat or reduce it to the absolute minimum as soon as you notice the first small bubbles rising along the edges of the pan, indicating the milk is nearly at boiling point. You should have a hot, steaming liquid ready for the thickening stage. Take great care not to let the milk boil vigorously, as this can alter its delicate, sweet flavour.
  4. Take the 60 g of semolina and begin pouring it in a steady stream into the hot milk, keeping the heat very low for total control. As you pour with one hand, use the other to whisk vigorously, creating a constant small vortex in the centre of the pan. This rotational movement is essential for distributing the tiny semolina grains instantly, preventing the formation of lumps that are difficult to dissolve later. Initially, you will have a liquid mixture, but it will already be slightly clouded by the presence of the flour. My advice is to pour the semolina very slowly: the finer the 'rain', the smoother and more pleasant the cream will be.
  5. Continue cooking the semolina over low heat for about 4-5 minutes, never stopping the stirring motion in a 'figure-eight' pattern to reach every part of the pan's bottom. During this time, the starch in the semolina will hydrate and swell, transforming the milk into a thick, enveloping cream. The final result must be a soft, smooth structure with a density similar to a perfectly made béchamel sauce. Do not rush the process by raising the heat, as the semolina risks sticking and burning, which would impart a bitter taste. If you notice the cream thickening too quickly, momentarily lift the pan away from the heat source.
  6. Once the desired creaminess is achieved, remove the pan from the heat and immediately fold in the apricot paste you prepared earlier. Use a spatula or whisk to blend the fruit puree, distributing it evenly throughout the hot cream mass. You will notice the colour of the preparation shift from milk-white to a delicate and inviting ivory or peach shade. Continue stirring until you no longer see any streaks of different colours and the cream appears perfectly homogeneous. The chef's advice is to always add the fruit with the heat off to preserve its fresh, natural aroma without further cooking the sugars.
  7. Before plating, carefully assess the final consistency of your preparation by lifting a small amount with a spoon. If it appears excessively firm or stiff, add a tiny splash of warm milk and stir firmly to restore the desired fluidity. The ideal cream should be soft and easy to swallow, with a texture reminiscent of a freshly made tender pudding. Keep in mind that as semolina cools, it tends to set and solidify further, so it is preferable to leave it slightly more fluid at this stage. Ensure there is no separation between the liquid and the denser semolina parts.
  8. Carefully divide the still-warm semolina cream into four single-portion bowls, smoothing the surface with the back of a teaspoon slightly moistened with water or milk. Allow the dessert to rest at room temperature for at least 5-10 minutes before serving, letting the flavours settle and the temperature reach an ideal level for tasting. The surface of the cream will set, creating a matte and silky finish that is very visually appealing. For a touch of class, you can decorate each portion with a thin dusting of cinnamon or a small fruit garnish. The chef's final touch: if you want to prevent a skin from forming on the surface during resting, cover the bowls with cling film placed directly in contact with the cream.
