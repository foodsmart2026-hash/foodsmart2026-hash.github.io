Linguine with Savoy Cabbage and Toasted Breadcrumbs

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 20 min · Easy

A humble peasant dish from the rural tradition that transforms simple ingredients into an explosion of flavours. The sweetness of the braised cabbage pairs perfectly with the crunch of the seasoned bread.

A rustic first course that highlights the simplicity of winter ingredients. Stewed cabbage pairs perfectly with the crunchiness of toasted breadcrumbs, creating a satisfying balance of textures.

Ingredients

  • Linguine320 g
  • Savoy cabbage (Verza)400 g
  • Coarse breadcrumbs or 'mollica' (the soft inner part of a loaf of bread)50 g
  • Garlic2 cloves
  • Dried chilli1 g

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Start by thoroughly cleaning 400g of Savoy cabbage: remove the tough outer leaves, cut it in half to excise the central core, and slice it into strips about one centimetre wide. Wash the vegetable under cold running water and drain well in a colander to remove excess moisture. This preparatory step takes about 5 minutes and the result should be clean cabbage ready for cooking. My chef's tip is to cut the strips uniformly to ensure even cooking in the pan later.
  2. Prepare the crispy base by heating a large frying pan with a drizzle of extra virgin olive oil and adding 50g of coarse breadcrumbs. Stir continuously with a wooden spoon over medium heat for about 3-4 minutes, taking extreme care not to burn the crumbs. Once the breadcrumbs have reached a golden-brown colour and an intense toasted aroma, transfer them immediately to a small cold bowl to stop the cooking process. A professional trick: add a pinch of salt to the crumbs while toasting to enhance their natural savouriness.
  3. In the same pan used for the breadcrumbs, add two tablespoons of oil, the 2 crushed garlic cloves, and 1g of dried crushed chili flakes. Let them sauté over very low heat for about 2 minutes, swirling the pan so the oil infuses without the garlic turning dark and bitter. Once the garlic is lightly golden, releasing its characteristic aroma, you can choose to leave it in or remove it for a milder taste. Absolutely avoid letting the oil smoke, otherwise, the chili flavor will become acrid and unpleasant.
  4. Add the previously prepared cabbage to the pan, add a pinch of salt, and pour in a ladle of hot water to aid the cooking process. Cover with a lid and let it stew over medium heat for about 12-15 minutes, stirring occasionally to prevent the vegetables from sticking to the bottom. The cabbage will be ready when it is tender to the bite but not completely mushy, maintaining a vibrant green colour. If you notice the vegetables drying out too quickly, add another splash of water to maintain the necessary moisture.
  5. Meanwhile, bring a large pot with about 4 litres of water to a boil and, as soon as it reaches the boil, add coarse salt. Pour in 320g of linguine and stir immediately with a pasta fork to prevent the pasta from sticking to itself or the bottom of the pot. Cook over high heat following the times indicated on the package, but be ready to drain it about two minutes before the ideal 'al dente' point. Remember that the cooking water is rich in starch and precious for the final binding, so do not discard it all.
  6. About two minutes before the end of the cooking time, reserve a couple of ladles of cooking water and drain the linguine directly into the pan with the stewed cabbage. Turn the heat up to medium-high to begin the final process of flavouring the pasta with the dressing. This step requires manual attention to correctly balance the liquids and ensure the pasta absorbs the cabbage flavour. Do not drain the pasta too dry, as it will continue to absorb moisture in the following minutes.
  7. Toss the linguine vigorously with the cabbage for one minute, adding the reserved cooking water a little at a time as needed. Stir with a fluid, circular motion to create a silky emulsion between the oil, water, and starch released by the pasta, which should coat every single strand. The final result should be glossy, creamy, and well-bound pasta where the dressing adheres perfectly without sliding off. The secret is to keep the heat high during this phase to achieve a perfect 'mantecatura' or emulsification.
  8. Turn off the heat, add two-thirds of the toasted breadcrumbs, and give a final quick stir to distribute the crunchiness evenly. Plate the linguine in a nest shape using tongs and a ladle to give volume, decorating each portion with the remaining crumbs to preserve the textural contrast. Serve immediately while the dish is piping hot and the toasted bread aroma is still fragrant. Chef's tip: serve in pre-warmed plates to prevent the emulsion from thickening too quickly during the meal.

Tips

  • Toast the breadcrumbs until golden brown, being careful not to burn them to avoid bitter notes.
  • Drain the linguine very al dente to finish cooking directly in the pan with the cabbage.
  • Use firm, fresh cabbage leaves to achieve the best result in terms of flavor.

Variations

  • Add anchovies in oil to the garlic sauté for an extra savory boost.
  • Replace the breadcrumbs with toasted pine nuts for a more refined version.
