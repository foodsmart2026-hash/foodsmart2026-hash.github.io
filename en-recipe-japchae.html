Japchae

South Korea · 4 servings · Prep 25 min · Cooking 15 min · Medium

Korean sweet potato glass noodles stir-fried with vegetables, beef, soy sauce and sesame.

Ingredients

  • Sweet potato glass noodles (dangmyeon)250 g
  • Beef strips200 g
  • Spinach200 g
  • Carrot100 g
  • Onion100 g
  • Shiitake mushrooms100 g
  • Soy sauce50 ml
  • Sesame oil25 ml
  • Sugar15 g
  • Garlic8 g
  • Sesame seeds8 g
  • Vegetable oil20 ml

Allergens

Gluten, Sesame, Soy

Method

  1. Start by cutting the 200g of beef into thin strips and place them in a small bowl with 15ml of soy sauce, 4g of minced garlic (half the dose), and 5g of sugar. Mix well and let it marinate for about 15-20 minutes; this will allow the meat to tenderize and absorb the deep umami flavors of the marinade.
  2. Bring plenty of water to a boil and cook the 250g of sweet potato starch noodles for about 6 minutes until they become translucent and chewy to the bite. Drain them, rinse under cold water to stop the cooking process, cut them roughly with scissors, and season immediately with 10ml of sesame oil to prevent them from sticking together.
  3. Submerge the 200g of fresh spinach in the same boiling water for just 30 seconds, just long enough to wilt them while maintaining a bright green color. Drain them immediately, squeeze firmly with your hands to remove any residual water, and set aside; this step is crucial to avoid watering down the final seasoning.
  4. In a large skillet or wok, heat half of the 20ml of seed oil over high heat and add the 100g of carrot and 100g of onion cut into julienne strips. Sauté the vegetables for 2-3 minutes until they become tender yet still slightly crunchy, releasing a sweet and inviting aroma.
  5. Add the remaining seed oil to the pan and cook the 100g of sliced shiitake mushrooms for about 3 minutes over medium heat. You should see them slightly golden and smell their typical earthy aroma, a sign that they have lost excess moisture and are ready to be combined with the other ingredients.
  6. In the same wok, sear the previously marinated beef strips for about 3 minutes over high heat until they are well browned and caramelized. The reaction of intense heat with the sugar in the marinade will create a delicious crust that forms the savory heart of the dish.
  7. Prepare the final seasoning by mixing the remaining 35ml of soy sauce, 10g of sugar, the remaining 4g of minced garlic, and 15ml of sesame oil in a small cup. This mixture perfectly balances the saltiness of the soy with the toasted note of the sesame and a delicate aromatic sweetness.
  8. Combine the noodles, sautéed vegetables, spinach, and beef in a very large bowl, pouring the freshly prepared seasoning over them. Mix vigorously with tongs so that every single noodle appears shiny, amber-colored, and uniformly coated with all the flavors from the cooking juices.
  9. Complete your Japchae by sprinkling the 8g of sesame seeds over the surface to add a crunchy note and an elegant visual touch. Serve the dish warm or at room temperature, just as Korean tradition dictates to best enhance the complexity of all the aromas.
