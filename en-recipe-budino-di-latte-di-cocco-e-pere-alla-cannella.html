Coconut Milk and Cinnamon Pear Pudding

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

A creamy vegan dessert that requires no eggs or gelatine. The natural pectin in the pears helps give texture to this fragrant end-of-meal treat.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🌱 Vegan🧒 Kid-friendly🌾 Gluten-free🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free

A delicate and velvety spoon dessert where the natural sweetness of pears meets the exotic aroma of coconut milk. Cinnamon wraps the palate in a warm, spicy note.

Ingredients

  • Coconut milk (carton drink type)500 ml
  • Ripe pears (Abate or Kaiser varieties common in Italy)2 pcs
  • Brown sugar60 g
  • Cornflour40 g
  • Ground cinnamon3 g
  • Lemon juice10 ml

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Start by preparing the aromatic base: peel the 2 ripe pears, remove the core and cut them into very small, regular cubes, about 5-8 mm each. Transfer the cubes to a bowl and immediately drizzle with 10 ml of lemon juice, tossing gently to prevent oxidation from browning the fruit. You will have bright fruit dice ready for cooking. The chef's secret is to keep the cube size uniform to ensure even cooking and a perfect distribution in every spoonful.
  2. Take a non-stick saucepan and add the prepared pears, along with one tablespoon of the 60 g total brown sugar and 3 g of ground cinnamon. Cook over medium-low heat for about 5 minutes, stirring with a wooden spoon until the fruit is tender and coated in a fragrant syrup, similar to a compote. The result should be soft fruit that still holds its shape without falling apart completely. Avoid high heat so as not to burn the sugar and cinnamon, which would turn bitter.
  3. While the fruit rests, prepare the thickening agent: pour 100 ml of cold coconut milk (taken from the 500 ml total) into a small bowl and add 40 g of cornflour. Whisk vigorously with a small hand whisk or a fork until the starch is completely dissolved, removing any lumps. You will get a white, dense, and perfectly smooth liquid. This step is crucial: cornflour must always be dissolved in a cold liquid before being added to a hot mixture to avoid unpleasant lumps in the pudding.
  4. In a heavy-bottomed saucepan, pour the remaining 400 ml of coconut milk and the remaining brown sugar, then heat over gentle heat. Stir occasionally with a whisk to help the sugar crystals dissolve and bring the liquid almost to boiling point. You will notice the milk starting to steam slightly and release an intense tropical scent. Ensure it does not reach a rolling boil, as excessive heat could alter the fat component of the coconut milk and compromise its silkiness.
  5. As soon as the milk is hot, slowly pour in the cold starch and milk mixture, stirring constantly with the hand whisk. Keep the heat very low and cook for about 2-3 minutes, moving the whisk in constant circular motions that also touch the sides of the pan. You will see the mixture quickly turn from liquid to creamy, becoming glossy and visibly thick. The chef advises never to stop stirring at this stage: the precision of the movement guarantees a texture as velvety as silk.
  6. Once you have a thick cream, turn off the heat and immediately fold in the cooked pears along with all their spiced syrup. Use a silicone spatula to gently fold the fruit into the coconut cream, ensuring the cubes are evenly distributed throughout the mixture. The result will be a fragrant cream, speckled with cinnamon and rich with fruit pieces. Be careful not to stir too forcefully to avoid breaking the pear cubes, which should remain intact and pleasant to the bite.
  7. Prepare 4 individual moulds or glass bowls and pour the still-hot mixture divided into equal parts, using a small ladle to avoid messy edges. Lightly tap the bottom of each mould on the work surface to settle the pudding and eliminate any trapped air bubbles. The surface should appear smooth and level. A professional trick is to lightly wet the inside of the moulds with a drop of cold water before filling if you wish to unmould the pudding onto a serving plate.
  8. Let the puddings cool at room temperature for about 30 minutes, then cover them with cling film in direct contact with the surface to prevent a hard skin from forming. Transfer them to the refrigerator and let them set for at least 3 hours, preferably overnight, so that the starch completes its gelation process. The pudding will be ready when it feels firm to the touch but still yielding and creamy at the heart. Do not rush: cold resting is the only way to achieve the perfect consistency without adding too many thickeners.
  9. When ready to serve, remove the puddings from the fridge and gently peel off the protective film. You can choose to serve them directly in the bowl or flip them onto a saucer, decorating the top with a freshly sliced pear slice or a final dusting of cinnamon. The contrast between the freshness of the pudding and the aromatic sweetness of the pear will delight your guests. The chef's final tip: serve the dessert with a plain biscuit or some crushed hazelnuts to add a crunchy note that balances the softness of the dessert.

Tips

  • Choose very ripe pears to get a juicier pulp that caramelizes easily in the pan.
  • Thoroughly dissolve the cornstarch in cold milk to prevent any lumps from forming in the pudding.
  • Serve the dessert well-chilled from the fridge to best appreciate its texture and flavor.

Variations

  • Garnish the top with dried coconut flakes to add a crunchy element to the dessert.
  • Replace the brown sugar with maple syrup for a version with a slightly caramelized aftertaste.
