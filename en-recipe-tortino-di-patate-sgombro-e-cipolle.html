Potato, Mackerel and Onion Bake

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 60 min · Hard

Canned mackerel is an excellent source of Omega-3 and costs less than tuna. In this rustic bake, it pairs perfectly with potatoes to create a hearty dish.

A hearty single-course dish combining soft potatoes with savory grilled mackerel. Golden onions add a sweet, aromatic note, making every bite balanced and rustic.

Ingredients

  • Floury potatoes800 g
  • Canned grilled mackerel (drained)250 g
  • Golden onions2 pcs
  • Breadcrumbs40 g
  • Eggs1 pcs
  • Parsley1 mazzetto

Allergens

Gluten, Eggs, Fish

Method

  1. Begin by washing 800g of old potatoes under running water to remove any soil residue. Place them in a large pot, cover with plenty of cold water, and bring to a boil; cook for about 30-40 minutes over medium heat until tender when pierced with a fork. Cooking them in their skins is essential to protect the starch and prevent the flesh from absorbing too much water, ensuring a firm, non-watery tart. Chef's tip: add a pinch of rock salt to the water only once it starts boiling to avoid delaying the boiling point.
  2. While the potatoes are cooking, peel the 2 golden onions and slice them thinly with a sharp knife to achieve uniform rings. Transfer them to a non-stick pan with a drizzle of oil and a pinch of salt, then stew over low heat for about 15 minutes until they become translucent and lightly caramelised. It is vital to cook them slowly to develop the onions' natural sweetness without burning them. The final result should be a soft, fragrant onion compote that is almost melting. Chef's tip: if the onions dry out too quickly, add a tablespoon of hot water to ensure even cooking.
  3. Drain the potatoes and, while still hot, peel them quickly using a tea towel or a fork to protect your fingers. Immediately pass them through a potato ricer directly into a large bowl, working firmly to obtain a fluffy, lump-free texture. Let the mash rest for about 10 minutes to allow excess steam to evaporate, concentrating the potato flavour and improving the structure of the mix. You should have a smooth, slightly dry mass, perfect for combining with the other ingredients. Chef's tip: never use an immersion blender for this task, as it breaks the starch molecules too much, making the mixture gluey.
  4. Prepare the flavour components by finely chopping a bunch of fresh parsley with a knife to release its essential oils. Take the 250g of grilled canned mackerel, drain the preserving oil thoroughly, and break it into coarse chunks using your hands or a fork. In a separate small bowl, lightly beat the whole egg to make it easier to incorporate later. These ingredients will provide the aromatic notes, the savoury sea flavour, and the protein structure needed for the tart. Chef's tip: ensure the mackerel is well-drained so it doesn't compromise the final consistency of the mixture.
  5. In the bowl with the lukewarm potato mash, add the flaked mackerel, stewed onions, beaten egg, and chopped parsley. Mix everything with a wooden spoon or a spatula, using slow, circular motions to distribute the flavours evenly without turning the mash into a shapeless cream. The mixture should appear well-blended, with visible pieces of mackerel and streaks of onion providing different textures. Chef's tip: taste the mixture before adding the egg to adjust the seasoning with black pepper or salt, bearing in mind that canned mackerel is already naturally salty.
  6. Take a circular baking tin roughly 22-24cm in diameter and grease the entire inner surface thoroughly with a drizzle of extra virgin olive oil. Pour in about 20g of breadcrumbs (half of the total amount) and swirl the tin to coat both the bottom and sides evenly, tapping out any excess. This step creates a thin protective layer that prevents the tart from sticking and encourages the formation of a crisp outer crust. Chef's tip: you can rub a halved garlic clove on the bottom of the tin before greasing for a subtle, appetising aroma.
  7. Transfer the potato and fish mixture into the prepared tin, spreading it out evenly with a spatula. Use the back of a wet spoon to level the surface, pressing down slightly towards the edges to remove any internal air pockets. Sprinkle the remaining 20g of breadcrumbs over the top and finish with a generous spiral of extra virgin olive oil. The tart should have a consistent thickness of about 3-4cm to ensure perfect browning. Chef's tip: create small grooves on the surface with the tines of a fork to catch the oil and breadcrumbs for a more rustic gratin effect.
  8. Place the tin in a preheated conventional oven at 200°C and bake for approximately 20 minutes on the middle shelf. During baking, the egg will set to provide structure, and the breadcrumbs will turn into a golden, crunchy crust. You are ready to take it out when the edges of the tart begin to pull away slightly from the sides of the tin and the surface is a beautiful amber colour. The aroma of grilled fish combined with sweet onions will signal it is perfectly cooked. Chef's tip: if the top isn't dark enough in the final minutes, switch to the grill function for 120 seconds for a professional finish.
  9. Once removed from the oven, let the tart rest in the tin for at least 10 minutes before slicing and serving. This resting period is crucial for the potato starch and egg proteins to set, allowing you to cut perfect, firm slices that don't crumble. The result will be a balanced dish where the soft heart contrasts pleasantly with the outer crunch. Chef's tip: serve the tart warm rather than piping hot, accompanying it with a light lamb's lettuce or cherry tomato salad to refresh the palate between bites.

Tips

  • Mash the potatoes while they are still hot to achieve a smoother and more consistent texture.
  • Let the potato cake rest outside the oven for about ten minutes to help the slices firm up.
  • Carefully drain the mackerel from its canning oil to prevent the mixture from becoming too greasy.

Variations

  • You can substitute the mackerel with canned grilled sardines for an even bolder seafood flavor.
  • Add some chopped Taggiasca olives to the mixture for an additional savory Mediterranean touch.
