Broad Bean Purée and Chicory

Puglia · 4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 120 min · Easy

Dried broad bean purée with sautéed chicory: a rustic, vegan dish from Puglia.

Ingredients

  • Dried hulled broad beans300 g
  • Chicory800 g
  • Potato100 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil60 ml
  • Garlic4 g
  • Fine salt6 g

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Start the night before by soaking 300 g of dried split broad beans in plenty of cold water for about 12 hours. This step is essential to rehydrate the legumes, soften the fibers, and ensure a uniform, velvety cook. Once the time has passed, drain and rinse them thoroughly under running water until the water runs clear.
  2. Transfer the beans to a high-sided pot and add 100 g of potato, previously peeled and cut into small cubes. Cover everything with fresh water, exceeding the level of the ingredients by about two fingers, and bring to a boil over medium heat. The potato will release its natural starch during cooking, helping you achieve an even denser and more cohesive final consistency.
  3. As soon as the water reaches a boil, lower the heat to minimum and let it simmer gently for about 120 minutes with the lid slightly ajar. During the first half hour, use a skimmer to remove the white foam that forms on the surface: this will eliminate residual impurities, making the dish lighter. The beans will be ready when they start to break apart spontaneously, having absorbed almost all the liquid.
  4. Once cooked, remove the pot from the heat and vigorously work the beans and potato with a wooden spoon, mashing them firmly against the sides of the pot. Add 3 g of fine salt and the first 30 ml of extra virgin olive oil, stirring until you obtain a thick, smooth, and light cream-colored puree. Avoid using an immersion blender to preserve the rustic texture and the artisanal soul of this ancient peasant recipe.
  5. While the puree rests, clean 800 g of chicory by removing the toughest stems and washing it well, then boil it in salted boiling water with the remaining 3 g of salt for about 8 minutes. The vegetable should be tender to the bite but maintain a vibrant green color, showing it has preserved most of its properties. Once ready, drain it carefully and squeeze it slightly to remove excess water that could water down the dish.
  6. In a large skillet, heat the remaining 30 ml of extra virgin olive oil along with 4 g of garlic (you can leave it whole for a delicate aroma or mince it if you prefer a bold flavor). When the garlic begins to turn slightly golden and scent the air, add the chicory and sauté over high heat for 3 minutes. This step is essential for the vegetable to absorb the oil's flavors and to balance its natural bitter note.
  7. Now it is time to plate: place a generous portion of broad bean puree on one side of a shallow bowl and position the sautéed chicory next to it, creating a chromatic contrast between the cream and dark green. Complete the presentation with a final drizzle of raw oil if desired, serving the dish very hot. This perfect pairing between the sweetness of the beans and the bitterness of the chicory represents the essence of Apulian tradition.
