Grandma's Baked Apple Fritters

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 18 min · Medium

A lighter and healthier version of the classic fried fritters. The apple remains soft inside a fragrant batter, making them perfect for children.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⬇️ Big saving🥕 Vegetarian🧒 Kid-friendly🇮🇹 Italian

Grandma's apple fritters are a timeless homemade dessert, presented here in a baked version for a lighter result. The apple rings stay tender under a cinnamon-scented batter. They are best enjoyed while still slightly warm with the family.

Ingredients

  • Large apples (Fuji or Gala type)2 pcs
  • Plain flour (Type 00, a finely ground Italian flour)150 g
  • Semi-skimmed milk120 ml
  • Medium egg1 pcs
  • Sugar30 g
  • Baking powder (Italian 'lievito per dolci')5 g
  • Cinnamonq.b.

Allergens

Gluten, Eggs, Milk

Method

  1. Start by preheating your oven to 190°C (fan-assisted) and line a large baking tray with high-quality parchment paper. This preliminary step ensures that the heat is evenly distributed and that the fritters do not stick during cooking without the need for extra fats. Make sure the oven reaches the set temperature before baking, which should take about 10 minutes. The result will be an organised workspace and an oven ready to instantly stabilise the batter. Chef's Tip: for extra crunch, sprinkle a pinch of granulated sugar directly onto the parchment paper before placing the apples.
  2. In a large mixing bowl, crack the room-temperature egg and add the 30g of granulated sugar. Use a hand whisk or electric beaters to beat the mixture vigorously for about 3 minutes until it becomes pale and slightly frothy. This step is essential for incorporating air and making the fritters light despite not being deep-fried. You will achieve a pale yellow, fluid cream that serves as a binding base. Chef's Tip: do not over-whisk the egg as if making a sponge cake; stop as soon as the sugar has dissolved.
  3. Gradually pour the 120ml of semi-skimmed milk into the egg and sugar mixture, continuing to stir gently. The milk must be added slowly to avoid deflating the volume created earlier and to encourage a smooth emulsion. Work for 1 minute with the whisk at low speed until the liquids are perfectly combined. The result should be a liquid mixture, free of separation and with a delicate aroma. Chef's Tip: use room-temperature milk to avoid thermal shock that could cause the fats in the egg to clump.
  4. Carefully sift the 150g of plain flour together with the 5g of baking powder and the cinnamon to taste directly over the liquids. Incorporate the dry ingredients gradually using circular motions with the whisk, starting from the centre towards the edges of the bowl to prevent lumps from forming. Continue working the mixture for about 3-4 minutes until you obtain a smooth, thick, and velvety batter. The perfect consistency is reached when the batter 'ribbons', meaning it falls back onto the mass leaving a visible trace for a few seconds. Chef's Tip: sifting the dry ingredients is not optional; it is the secret to a fine texture without unsightly flour specks.
  5. Wash the 2 large apples (Fuji or Gala), dry them well, and peel them with a vegetable peeler to remove only the outer skin rich in tannins. Use a dedicated apple corer to pierce the centre of the apples from top to bottom, removing seeds and tough parts. Cut the apples horizontally into rings exactly 1cm thick, trying to be as precise as possible to ensure simultaneous cooking. You will have regular, fragrant rings ready to be coated. Chef's Tip: if you don't have a corer, cut the slices first and use a small metal cap or a piping nozzle to punch out the centre.
  6. Take each apple ring and dip it completely into the prepared batter, ensuring every centimetre is covered. Lift the apple with a fork or a hook, letting the excess batter drip off for about 2 seconds over the bowl before moving it. This step must be quick to prevent the apple from releasing too much water into the batter. The result will be a perfectly 'coated' ring, where the white of the apple is no longer visible under the creamy layer. Chef's Tip: do not leave the apples soaking in the batter, otherwise the structure will become soggy instead of fluffy.
  7. Arrange the apple rings on the lined tray, maintaining a distance of about 2-3cm from each other to allow hot air to circulate. Do not crowd the tray, as the baking powder will cause the batter to puff up during cooking and the rings might touch. This orderly arrangement takes only a couple of minutes and ensures each fritter cooks with the same intensity. Visually, you should have a grid of golden rings ready for the heat. Chef's Tip: if you have leftover batter, you can pour a teaspoon into the centre of each apple to fill the core hole and create a 'full fritter'.
  8. Place the tray on the middle rack and bake at 190°C for 15-18 minutes, monitoring the colour closely. Halfway through (after about 9 minutes), gently flip the fritters using a spatula if you notice the base is browning faster than the top. The fritters are ready when the batter appears puffed up, a beautiful golden blonde colour, and the edges start to get slightly crispy. You will smell an intense aroma of baked apple and cinnamon spreading through the kitchen. Chef's Tip: avoid opening the oven in the first 10 minutes to avoid compromising the baking powder's action.
  9. Remove the tray from the oven and let the fritters rest for at least 3-5 minutes before touching or serving them. This resting time is crucial so that the apple's residual moisture stabilises and the batter acquires the right structural consistency. Serve the fritters while still warm, finishing with a dusting of icing sugar or a very light drizzle of honey to enhance their natural sweetness. The final result will be a dessert soft on the inside and slightly fragrant on the outside, perfect for a healthy snack. Chef's Tip: do not cover them while they are hot, otherwise the steam will make them rubbery.

Tips

  • Choose firm apples like Fuji so they hold their circular shape well during the baking process.
  • Dust the apple slices with a thin layer of flour before the batter to help the liquid mixture adhere better.
  • Place the fritters well-spaced on the baking sheet to prevent them from sticking together while baking.

Variations

  • Add a pinch of lemon zest to the batter to give a touch of freshness and acidity to the dessert.
  • You can dust the fritters with powdered sugar immediately after baking for a more finished look.
