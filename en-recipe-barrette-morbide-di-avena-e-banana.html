Soft Oat and Banana Bars

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

A healthy snack, without added refined sugars, which exploits the natural sweetness of ripe bananas. Excellent to prepare in advance for the whole week.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🌱 Vegan🧒 Kid-friendly🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free

A practical and tasty snack, ideal for a boost during the day. The base of ripe bananas creates a soft texture that holds the crunchiness of walnuts and chocolate chips.

Ingredients

  • Rolled oats200 g
  • Very ripe bananas3 pcs
  • Dark chocolate chips40 g
  • Chopped walnuts30 g
  • Peanut butter (optional)20 g
  • Salt1 pizzico

Allergens

Gluten, Tree nuts, Peanuts

Method

  1. Preheat your oven to 180°C (Fan 160°C/Gas Mark 4) and peel the 3 very ripe bananas, ensuring any stringy bits are removed. In a large mixing bowl, thoroughly mash the fruit with a sturdy fork until it turns into a nearly liquid purée with no large lumps remaining. This creamy base is the core of the recipe as it acts as a natural binder, replacing eggs and added fats. You should aim for a smooth, glossy, and highly aromatic cream. Chef's tip: The riper and more 'spotted' the bananas, the sweeter and softer your bars will be without the need for refined sugar.
  2. Pour 200g of rolled oats into the bowl with the banana purée and stir with a flexible spatula for about a minute. It is vital that every single oat flake comes into contact with the fruit's moisture, starting the hydration process that makes the snack pleasant to bite. Continue working the mixture until it is densely combined and no dry residue remains at the bottom of the bowl. You should have a sticky, uniform mass that easily pulls away from the sides. Chef's tip: Use jumbo oats for a rustic, chewy texture, or porridge oats for a more compact and uniform bar.
  3. Now add the 40g of dark chocolate chips, 30g of roughly chopped walnuts, and a pinch of salt to balance the sweetness. Incorporate these ingredients into the dough with circular motions, ensuring they are evenly distributed to guarantee flavor in every single bite. The salt won't make the bars salty but will enhance the toasted notes of the nuts and the intensity of the chocolate. The mixture will now appear textured while maintaining its malleable nature. Chef's tip: Lightly toast the walnuts in a dry pan for two minutes before chopping them to release their essential oils and depth of flavour.
  4. For an extra touch of creaminess and to increase the satiety factor, fold in the 20g of peanut butter by drizzling it from the spoon over the mixture. Work the dough vigorously for a couple of minutes until the peanut butter is perfectly integrated and no shiny streaks are visible. This optional ingredient provides further binding and gives a deep, nutty undertone to the bars. The final result will be a heavy, fragrant, and very cohesive dough. Chef's tip: If your peanut butter is too firm, warm it slightly in the microwave for 10 seconds to make it fluid and easier to mix.
  5. Prepare a rectangular baking tin (approx. 20x20 cm) by lining it with baking parchment that has been slightly dampened and squeezed to fit snugly into the corners. Transfer the entire mixture to the centre of the tin and spread it towards the edges using the back of a slightly wet spoon. Aim for a uniform thickness of about 1.5 to 2 cm throughout to ensure even baking. The surface should look flat and well-distributed across the whole tin. Chef's tip: Ensure the tin is the correct size; if it is too large, the bars will be too thin and may dry out in the oven.
  6. This is the crucial step for structural integrity: use the back of the spoon or a spatula to compact the dough with firm, constant pressure. Press down across the entire surface, paying particular attention to the corners and edges to eliminate any internal air pockets. Proper compression ensures the bars hold their shape after cutting and don't crumble in your hands once baked. The mixture should look solid, smooth, and perfectly levelled. Chef's tip: Place a second sheet of parchment paper over the dough and use a flat-bottomed glass to press down professionally and evenly.
  7. Place the tin on the middle rack of the preheated oven at 180°C and set a timer for 20-25 minutes. During baking, the banana's moisture will slowly evaporate and the oats will toast, releasing an irresistible aroma of freshly baked biscuits. Visually check the progress: the edges should appear golden and slightly darker than the centre. To the touch, the surface should be firm but still slightly springy when pressed gently. Chef's tip: Do not overbake, as the bars tend to harden significantly once cooled; if they look too dark, take them out immediately.
  8. Remove the tin from the oven and, despite the tempting smell, let it rest on a wire rack without touching the mixture for at least 30-40 minutes. This waiting time is essential for the oat fibres and banana sugars to stabilise, creating the final structure of the bar. If you try to cut them now, the mixture will be too soft and will break irreparably. The block must be completely cool and stable to the touch before proceeding. Chef's tip: For a perfect cut, place the tin in the fridge for 15 minutes after it has cooled to room temperature to make the mixture even firmer.
  9. Gently lift the parchment paper to remove the compact block from the tin and place it on a clean wooden chopping board. Using a very sharp straight-edged knife, divide the block into 8 or 10 rectangular bars with clean, decisive strokes, avoiding dragging the blade. Each bar should weigh roughly 50-60 grams, the ideal portion for a balanced snack. You will achieve bars that are soft in the centre with a delicious slight crust on the outer edges. Chef's tip: Clean the knife blade with a damp cloth between cuts to achieve precise, professional-looking edges.
  10. Store the bars in an airtight glass container, placing them in a single layer or separated by small squares of parchment paper. At room temperature, they will keep for 2-3 days, while in the fridge, they maintain their freshness for up to 5 days. They are the ideal snack to take to the office or school thanks to their durability and convenience. The final result is a healthy product with no added sugar, rich in slow-release energy. Chef's tip: If you prefer a crispier texture, you can briefly reheat individual bars in a toaster or a non-stick pan for a minute before eating.

Tips

  • Use bananas with very dark skin to achieve maximum natural sweetness and ease of processing.
  • Press the mixture firmly into the pan with the back of a spoon to prevent the bars from crumbling.
  • Allow the oat block to cool completely before cutting to get clean, sharp edges.

Variations

  • Replace walnuts with chopped toasted hazelnuts for a more intense and aromatic flavor.
  • Omit the peanut butter if you prefer a version with a purer and lighter banana taste.
