Grated Baby Courgettes with Basil and Parmesan

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 10 min · Easy

A quick preparation that transforms courgettes into an almost velvety texture, perfect for children who struggle to chew large pieces. Fresh basil stimulates the sense of smell without being heavy.

A quick and flavorful side dish highlighting the freshness of young zucchini. Grating allows for fast cooking, keeping the vegetables moist and enriched by the bold taste of Parmigiano and basil.

Ingredients

  • Medium courgettes400 g
  • Grated Parmigiano Reggiano (a hard, aged Italian PDO cheese)20 g
  • Fresh basil4 leaves
  • Extra virgin olive oil10 g
  • Water20 ml

Allergens

Milk

Method

  1. Begin by thoroughly washing 400g of baby courgettes under cold running water to remove any traces of soil. Place the courgettes on a chopping board, trim the ends, and grate them entirely using the large holes of a classic box grater. This process should produce uniform courgette petals rather than a watery mush. The visual result will be a soft heap of green and white flakes. My chef's tip is to grate with long, firm strokes to keep the courgette fibre intact, preventing it from releasing too much water prematurely.
  2. Take a heavy-bottomed, wide non-stick frying pan to ensure even cooking. Pour in 20ml of still water and 10g of extra virgin olive oil, stirring slightly with a wooden spoon. Turn the hob to a very low heat to warm the liquid without bringing it to a violent boil. You should see tiny bubbles appearing on the surface, a sign that the stewing base is ready for the vegetables. Never let the oil start to smoke: we want a gentle and healthy cooking process.
  3. Spread the 400g of grated courgettes evenly into the pan, trying to cover the entire available surface. Use a silicone spatula or a wooden spoon to level the mixture without pressing down too hard. Maintain the low heat to allow the courgette's natural moisture to emerge gradually and combine with the oil and water emulsion. The courgettes will start to smell fresh and change hue slightly. Avoid adding salt at this stage to prevent the vegetables from losing water too quickly.
  4. Cover the pan with a correctly sized lid to trap the steam inside. Let it stew for about 8-10 minutes, checking the cooking halfway through to ensure the bottom isn't drying out too much. The courgettes should become translucent and very soft, almost melting, without taking on any colour or browning. The steam will make the fibres easy to chew and digest, perfect for younger palates. The chef's secret is to shake the pan occasionally without removing the lid to redistribute the heat.
  5. While the courgettes are cooking, take a moment for the aromatic preparation by washing 4 fresh basil leaves and drying them with extreme care. Stack the leaves on top of each other, roll them into a cigar shape, and finely chop them with a sharp knife until you get a fragrant green dust. Do not use a blender as it heats the leaves and turns them black, oxidising them and ruining the flavour. The basil's aroma should be released only at the last moment. Ensure the chop is very fine to integrate perfectly into the final cream.
  6. After 10 minutes, remove the lid and observe the consistency of the courgettes: the added water should have almost completely evaporated. If you still notice a lot of liquid at the bottom, turn up the heat for just one minute while stirring constantly. The courgettes should appear wilted and concentrated, ready to be transformed into a light cream. Turn off the heat as soon as you are satisfied with the consistency and the pan is almost dry. Remember that cooking continues through thermal inertia even after the hob is off, so do not overdo it.
  7. With the heat off, sprinkle the chopped basil and 20g of grated Parmesan cheese evenly over the courgette base. The residual heat from the vegetables will be enough to melt the cheese without making it rubbery or overly stringy. Start stirring with quick, circular motions, incorporating the ingredients together. You will immediately smell the cheese melting with the fresh aroma of the basil. If the preparation seems too dry, you can add a teaspoon of warm water to help the emulsion form.
  8. Continue to stir vigorously for about 30 seconds to create that creamy bond that characterises this dish. The Parmesan, joining with the residual moisture and oil, should create a sort of natural velvety sauce that coats every flake of courgette. The final result will be a soft, tasty, and visually inviting side dish, where the green of the basil stands out against the white of the cheese. Ensure there are no unmelted cheese lumps. My professional touch is to serve the dish lukewarm, the ideal temperature to enhance the sweetness of the baby courgettes.
  9. Before plating the 4 portions, perform a final visual check on the length of the courgette flakes for child safety. If you notice any strands that are too long, quickly run the tip of your spatula over them to break them up. Serve the preparation in small bowls, smoothing the surface for a neat and elegant presentation. Every spoonful should be a concentrate of nutrients and natural flavour. To avoid the most common mistake, do not add extra salt: the Parmesan already provides the necessary savouriness to balance the sweetness of the vegetables.

Tips

  • Use a coarse grater to prevent the zucchini from releasing too much water and becoming too soft.
  • Keep the lid tightly closed during the first few minutes to use steam for even cooking.
  • Add the Parmigiano only after turning off the heat to let it melt gently without clumping.

Variations

  • Replace the basil with fresh mint for an even more refreshing Mediterranean twist.
  • Use grated Pecorino Romano instead of Parmigiano for a saltier and more rustic flavor.
