Roasted Fennel with Orange and Black Olives

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

A light and elegant side dish that enhances fennel through caramelisation in the oven. The Sicilian pairing with orange and olives makes it perfect for fish dishes.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🌱 Vegan🧒 Kid-friendly🌾 Gluten-free🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🇮🇹 Italian

A refined recipe that transforms fennel into a citrusy and savory side dish through the combination with orange and black olives. Oven roasting gently caramelizes the fennel's natural sugars, enhancing its sweetness.

Ingredients

  • Large fennel bulbs3 pcs
  • Orange (juice and zest)1 pcs
  • Pitted black olives50 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Dried thyme1 tsp
  • Saltq.b.

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the 3 large fennel bulbs: trim off the green fronds and set them aside in a small bowl of cold water for later garnish, remove the tougher outer layers, and slice off the base. Use a sharp chef’s knife to slice the bulbs vertically lengthways into uniform slices exactly 1 centimetre thick. This preparation takes about 5 minutes and ensures the fennel retains its structure during roasting. You should aim for intact slices that showcase the beautiful internal section of the bulb. Chef's tip: don't discard the tough outer layers; they are perfect for adding depth to a homemade vegetable stock.
  2. Prepare the aromatic emulsion by squeezing and straining the juice of 1 orange into a small bowl, then adding 30 ml of extra virgin olive oil, a pinch of salt, and 1 teaspoon of dried thyme. Use a small whisk or a fork to beat the ingredients vigorously for about 30 seconds until a temporary suspension is formed. The goal is to obtain a slightly thick, opaque liquid where the juice and oil are well combined. The result will be a fragrant dressing that balances the richness of the oil with the acidity of the citrus. Chef's tip: rub the dried thyme between your palms before adding it to instantly release the plant's essential oils.
  3. Take a large baking tray and line it with baking parchment to prevent the natural sugars in the orange juice from sticking to the bottom. Arrange the fennel slices on the tray in a single, tidy layer, making sure they do not overlap. This spacing is crucial as it allows the hot air to circulate freely, ensuring an even roast on every slice. The tray should look full but not crowded, ready for the seasoning. Chef's tip: if your fennel bulbs are particularly large, use two separate trays rather than overcrowding one, otherwise the fennel will steam instead of roasting.
  4. Use a silicone pastry brush to generously coat each fennel slice on the tray with the orange emulsion. Brush carefully, making sure the liquid penetrates the natural fibres of the bulb and gets into the edges of the slices. This process takes about 2 minutes and helps to seal the surface, keeping the interior juicy. The fennel should appear glossy and evenly moistened by the citrus dressing. Chef's tip: pay extra attention to the leafy tops of the slices, as they tend to dry out much faster than the denser heart of the bulb.
  5. Scatter the 50 g of pitted black olives across the tray, tucking them into the spaces between the fennel slices. You can leave them whole for a meatier texture or halve them if you want their salty flavour to infuse more deeply into the cooking juices. The olives add a dark visual contrast to the white fennel and orange juice. At this stage, the scent of thyme and citrus will already be noticeable even before cooking. Chef's tip: rinse the olives well under cold water and pat them dry before adding to the tray to better control the overall saltiness of the final dish.
  6. Preheat your oven to 190°C (375°F/Gas Mark 5) and, once it reaches temperature, place the tray on the middle shelf. Bake for the first 15 minutes without opening the door to maintain a constant internal humidity. During this time, the heat will soften the fennel's fibres while the orange sugars begin to concentrate. You should start to see the first signs of softening and a light release of fragrant steam. Chef's tip: if using a fan-assisted oven, lower the temperature to 175°C to prevent the edges from charring before the centre is tender.
  7. Carefully remove the tray from the oven and, using a spatula or kitchen tongs, gently flip each fennel slice to expose the other side to the direct heat. Take this opportunity to baste the slices with the citrusy cooking juices that have pooled on the parchment paper, using a spoon. This 'nappage' technique ensures the fennel stays hydrated and becomes intensely flavoured. Roast for a final 10 minutes. The fennel is ready when it is tender when pierced with a fork, with beautifully caramelised, sweet, dark edges. Chef's tip: if the juices have evaporated too much, add a tablespoon of water or extra orange juice before returning the tray to the oven.
  8. Remove the tray from the oven and finish the dish: grate the fresh orange zest over the steaming fennel and garnish with the reserved green fronds. The residual heat will release the volatile oils from the zest, creating an immediate and irresistible citrus aroma. Transfer the fennel to a serving platter, making sure to include the olives and the syrupy glaze from the tray. The dish should look vibrant, with golden highlights and a fresh citrus scent. Chef's tip: only grate the zest at the very last second and only the orange part, as the white pith (albedo) is bitter and would spoil the sweetness of the roasted fennel.

Tips

  • Cut the fennel into slices of similar thickness to ensure they cook in the same amount of time.
  • Use an untreated orange to grate the zest for maximum aromatic intensity.
  • Spread the emulsion well over each slice to prevent the fennel from drying out too much in the oven.

Variations

  • Add desalted capers along with the olives to intensify the savory note of the dish.
  • Sprinkle with toasted pine nuts before serving to add a crunchy component.
