Spaghetti Carbonara with Peas and Crispy Pancetta

4 servings · Prep 5 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

An affordable twist on the classic carbonara that incorporates peas for a touch of freshness and volume. The egg creates a delicious sauce that pairs perfectly with the savouriness of the browned pancetta.

A spring-inspired take on a classic, enriched by the sweetness of peas and the saltiness of crispy bacon. A balanced dish that plays with contrasting textures and bold flavors.

Ingredients

  • Spaghetti320 g
  • Medium eggs2 pcs
  • Frozen peas150 g
  • Smoked pancetta cubes (cured Italian pork belly)60 g
  • Small onion1/2 pcs

Allergens

Gluten, Eggs

Method

  1. Start by bringing a large pot with approximately 4 litres of water to a boil, adding a tablespoon of rock salt only once it reaches a rolling boil. Meanwhile, finely chop the half onion with a sharp knife until it is reduced to almost imperceptible pieces that will melt during cooking. This initial preparation takes about 3-4 minutes and ensures the onion flavours the dish without creating an unpleasant texture. You should achieve a consistent, fragrant mince. Chef's tip: keep the tip of the knife fixed on the board and move the handle in a fan motion for a perfect chop.
  2. Take a wide non-stick frying pan (about 28-30 cm) and add the 60g of smoked cubed pancetta without adding any oil or butter. Turn the heat to medium and let it sauté for about 5 minutes, stirring occasionally with a wooden spoon. The heat should render the fat from the pancetta, making the lean parts crispy and golden while releasing an intense smoky aroma. The ideal result is browned but not burnt bacon. Chef's tip: always start with a cold pan to allow the fat to render gradually without burning the meat fibres.
  3. In the same pan with the pancetta, add the previously chopped onion and the 150g of frozen peas. Pour in half a glass of water (about 100ml) and immediately cover with a lid, maintaining medium heat for about 8-10 minutes. The peas should soften and absorb the flavours of the pan juices, while the onion becomes translucent and sweet. By the end, the water should be almost completely evaporated, leaving the peas bright and tender. Chef's tip: do not salt the peas at this stage, as the pancetta is already very savoury and you risk over-salting.
  4. While the sauce is cooking, crack the 2 medium eggs into a glass or ceramic bowl and add a pinch of salt and a generous grating of fresh black pepper. Use a fork or a small whisk to beat the eggs vigorously for about 1 minute until they are slightly frothy and perfectly combined. It is vital that the yolk and white are fully integrated to ensure a final cream without white lumps. You should have a pale yellow, fluid mixture speckled with pepper. Chef's tip: use room temperature eggs to help create a better emulsion with the pasta cooking water.
  5. Submerge the 320g of spaghetti into the boiling water and cook them for exactly the time indicated on the packet for 'al dente' results. Stir immediately with a pasta fork to prevent the strands from sticking together or to the bottom of the pot. Before draining, use a ladle to set aside about 200ml of the starchy cooking water, which is essential for the final creaminess. The pasta should be firm to the bite but flexible. Chef's tip: do not add oil to the pasta water; a large pot and the right amount of water are enough.
  6. Drain the spaghetti and toss them directly into the pan with the pea and pancetta mixture, turning the heat to high. Sauté the pasta for about 60 seconds, moving the pan with firm tosses or using tongs so that every strand is coated in the flavours of the pancetta and peas. During this phase, the spaghetti finishes absorbing the flavours from the pan. You should hear the sizzle of the pasta as it becomes evenly seasoned. Chef's tip: if the pasta seems too dry at this stage, add a tablespoon of the reserved cooking water.
  7. Turn off the heat completely and move the pan to a cold hob; this is the most critical step to avoid a 'scrambled egg' effect. Pour the beaten egg mixture over the spaghetti and immediately begin mixing with kitchen tongs, gradually adding some of the reserved cooking water. The residual heat from the pasta and the pan must cook the egg very slowly, turning it into a fluid and velvety cream. The result should be a glossy sauce coating every single strand. Chef's tip: do not rush; add the water a little at a time until you reach your desired consistency.
  8. Serve the spaghetti immediately, distributing them into 4 plates or bowls, ensuring you evenly divide the peas and crispy pancetta cubes left at the bottom of the pan. The cream should not pool at the bottom of the plate but should cling to the pasta, giving it an inviting and professional look. The aroma of pepper and pancetta should stand out clearly. Garnish with an extra dusting of pepper if desired. Chef's tip: slightly warm the serving plates to keep the carbonara creamy for longer while eating.

Tips

  • Sauté the bacon over medium heat until it becomes golden and perfectly crispy.
  • Mix the eggs with the pasta away from the heat to ensure a smooth and creamy texture.
  • Use small frozen peas to guarantee quick and even cooking throughout the process.

Variations

  • Try replacing the smoked bacon with guanciale for an even more intense flavor.
  • Add some freshly ground black pepper for an extra aromatic touch.
