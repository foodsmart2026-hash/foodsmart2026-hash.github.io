Vegetarian Peanut Mafe

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 40 min · Medium

A typical West African stew that uses peanut butter to create a velvety, protein-rich sauce. A surprising recipe that transforms common vegetables into an exotic dish.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🌱 Vegan🧒 Kid-friendly🌾 Gluten-free🌍 International

Inspired by West African traditions, this vegetarian stew combines creamy peanut butter with a base of flavorful vegetables. Served with basmati rice, it offers a contrast of velvety textures and intense aromas.

Ingredients

  • Peanut butter (100% peanuts)100 g
  • Yellow-fleshed potatoes400 g
  • Carrots3 pcs
  • Tomato passata (smooth sieved tomatoes)200 g
  • Brown onion1 pcs
  • Vegetable stock600 ml
  • Vegetable oil20 ml
  • Basmati rice (for serving)200 g

Allergens

Peanuts

Method

  1. Start by rinsing 200g of basmati rice under cold running water until the water runs clear to remove excess starch. Place it in a pot with 400ml of lightly salted water, bring to the boil, then cook on a very low heat, covered, for about 10-12 minutes until all the water is absorbed. Let it rest for 5 minutes off the heat before fluffing with a fork for perfectly separated, aromatic grains. Chef's tip: never lift the lid during cooking to ensure the steam stays trapped inside.
  2. Prepare the vegetable base by peeling the onion, the 3 carrots, and 400g of yellow potatoes. Finely dice the onion with a sharp knife, while cutting the carrots and potatoes into large, uniform chunks of about 3cm. Consistent sizing ensures all vegetables cook at the same rate without falling apart. Chef's tip: keep the cut potatoes in cold water until ready to use to prevent oxidation and keep them bright.
  3. Heat 20ml of vegetable oil in a large saucepan or heavy-based casserole pot over a medium heat. Add the chopped onion and sauté gently for 4-5 minutes, stirring frequently with a wooden spoon until translucent and golden. It should soften and release its natural sweetness without browning. Chef's tip: if the onion starts to dry out or brown too quickly, add a tablespoon of hot water to keep the sauté soft and digestible.
  4. Add the potato and carrot chunks along with 200g of tomato passata to the pan with the sautéed onions. Increase the heat slightly and roast the vegetables for 5 minutes, stirring constantly to coat them well and allow the tomato to lose its initial acidity. You will notice the vegetable colours intensify and the passata starting to thicken. Chef's tip: this roasting stage is crucial for developing a deep, lingering flavour profile.
  5. Pour 600ml of hot vegetable stock into the pan until all ingredients are fully submerged and bring to a gentle boil. Reduce the heat to maintain a constant simmer, which is ideal for softening root vegetable fibres without breaking them. The temperature should be gentle to allow for an optimal flavour exchange between the vegetables and the liquid. Chef's tip: always use hot stock to avoid a thermal shock that could toughen the carrots.
  6. While the stew is simmering, ladle out about 150ml of the hot broth into a small bowl containing 100g of pure peanut butter. Whisk vigorously with a fork or mini-whisk until you achieve a smooth, velvety cream with no lumps. This pre-emulsion is the secret to a homogenous sauce that won't stick to the bottom of the pot. Chef's tip: ensure the peanut butter is at room temperature before diluting to make the process easier.
  7. Pour the peanut cream into the saucepan with the vegetables and stir gently to incorporate it fully into the cooking liquid. Cover with a lid and continue to cook over a low heat for approximately 25 minutes, stirring occasionally to prevent the cream from catching. The sauce will begin to thicken and release the signature toasted aroma of West African cuisine. Chef's tip: keep the heat at the lowest setting, as the density of the peanuts causes them to catch heat very quickly.
  8. Check for doneness: the potatoes should be tender enough to break apart with a fork, and the sauce should show a slight oily separation on the surface. This glossy film is the hallmark of a perfectly cooked Mafe, indicating the sauce has reached the right density. If it is too thin, simmer for another 5 minutes without the lid. Chef's tip: taste and adjust the salt only at this stage, as both the peanut butter and stock are naturally savoury.
  9. Serve by placing a generous portion of basmati rice at the bottom of each shallow bowl, creating a well in the centre for the stew. Ladle the vegetarian Mafe over the rice, ensuring an even distribution of carrots, potatoes, and plenty of creamy sauce. Serve steaming hot to fully enjoy the aromatic complexity of the peanuts combined with the sweetness of the root vegetables. Chef's tip: garnish with some crushed toasted peanuts for a crunchy texture that contrasts with the softness of the stew.

Tips

  • Dissolve the peanut butter in some warm broth before adding it to prevent lumps from forming.
  • Use yellow-fleshed potatoes as they hold their shape well even after long simmering.
  • Serve the dish hot to fully enjoy the toasted aroma of the peanuts.

Variations

  • Instead of basmati rice, you can pair the stew with durum wheat couscous.
  • For a final touch of color, garnish the dish with coarsely chopped roasted peanuts.
