Mediterranean Potato, Mackerel and Vegetable Dish

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

A nutritionally balanced proposal that combines high-quality oily fish protein with the complex carbohydrates of potatoes and legume fibre. A quick and affordable one-dish meal, a pillar of the modern Mediterranean diet for the whole family.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🌾 Gluten-free🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free

Ingredients

  • Yellow-fleshed potatoes600 g
  • Mackerel fillets in brine (sustainably caught)250 g
  • Cooked cannellini beans (drained)240 g
  • Medium courgettes400 g
  • Cherry tomatoes200 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 ml
  • Red onion60 g
  • Dried oregano and salt5 g

Allergens

Fish

Method

  1. Begin by preparing 600g of yellow potatoes: wash them thoroughly, peel, and cut into uniform 2cm cubes. Place the cubes in a pot of cold, lightly salted water and bring to the boil, simmering for about 5-6 minutes from the start of the boil. You should achieve a tender texture when tested with a fork, but the core must remain firm so they don't break apart in later stages. Chef's tip: starting potatoes in cold water ensures a gradual release of starch, guaranteeing even cooking from the outside to the centre.
  2. While the potatoes are boiling, prepare the vegetable base by finely slicing 60g of red onion and cutting 400g of courgettes into 0.5cm thick rounds. Use a sharp knife for precise cuts, which will help all ingredients cook evenly in the pan. Keep the vegetables separate on your chopping board until needed. Chef's tip: for better digestibility, soak the sliced onion in cold water for a few minutes before cooking.
  3. Take a very large non-stick frying pan, pour in 40ml of extra virgin olive oil, and add the sliced onion. Turn the heat to low and let the onion sweat for about 3-4 minutes, stirring occasionally with a wooden spoon. The onion should become glossy, translucent, and release its sweet aroma without browning or burning. Chef's tip: if you notice the onion colouring too quickly, add a tablespoon of warm water to steam it gently instead of frying.
  4. Carefully drain the parboiled potatoes and add them to the pan along with the 400g of courgette rounds. Increase the heat to medium-high and sear the vegetables for about 10 minutes, tossing them frequently to distribute the heat evenly. The goal is to achieve an inviting golden crust on both the potatoes and courgettes while keeping the inside soft and juicy. Chef's tip: do not overcrowd the pan; if necessary, use two separate pans to allow every potato cube to touch the hot surface and brown properly.
  5. Prepare 240g of cannellini beans by rinsing them thoroughly under cold running water to remove the canning liquid and excess salt. Once drained, add them to the pan with the now-golden potatoes and courgettes, stirring gently to avoid breaking the pulses. This stage takes only 2 minutes over medium heat to allow the beans to absorb the flavours and heat through evenly. Chef's tip: rinsing canned pulses well drastically reduces sodium content and improves the clarity of flavour in the finished dish.
  6. Halve 200g of cherry tomatoes and drain 250g of canned mackerel fillets in brine, breaking them into large chunks with your hands or a fork. Add both ingredients to the pan, scattering them over the hot vegetable and bean mix. Continue cooking for a further 4 minutes over medium heat, using a lid for just the first two minutes to help the tomatoes release their juices. Chef's tip: avoid over-stirring at this stage to prevent the mackerel from breaking up too much; it should remain in visible, succulent chunks.
  7. Turn off the heat and proceed with the final seasoning using 5g of dried oregano and a pinch of fine salt, adjusting to your taste. Distribute the herbs evenly over the surface and give one final gentle toss from the bottom up. The aroma of the oregano will be released immediately by the residual heat of the pan, coating all the ingredients. Chef's tip: rub the dried oregano between your palms as you sprinkle it to reactivate the essential oils and release a much more intense fragrance.
  8. Arrange the Mediterranean dish into four large shallow bowls, ensuring each portion has a balanced mix of mackerel, potatoes, and vegetables. Serve the dish hot, noting how the cherry tomatoes have just wilted but kept their shape, providing freshness and colour. You can finish with a drizzle of raw olive oil if you prefer a bolder flavour. Chef's tip: this dish is also excellent served lukewarm, allowing the flavours to meld further for a quick lunch or a gourmet lunchbox.
