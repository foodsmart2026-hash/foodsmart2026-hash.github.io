Soft Pearl Barley with Pumpkin and Potato Cream

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 40 min · Medium

When cooked for a long time, barley becomes tender and pleasant. Paired with pumpkin, it creates a sweet and comforting first course, perfect for autumn.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🌱 Vegan🧒 Kid-friendly🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free

This first course combines the rustic texture of pearl barley with the sweet creaminess of Mantuan pumpkin and potatoes. It is a comforting dish, ideal for autumn days with a naturally balanced flavor.

Ingredients

  • Pearl barley140 g
  • Cleaned pumpkin (Mantovana type - a dense, sweet Italian squash)300 g
  • Potato100 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil20 ml
  • Sage1 leaf

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the vegetables: wash and peel 100g of potato and 300g of Mantovana pumpkin, then cut them into uniform 1cm cubes using a sharp chef's knife. This small size is crucial because the vegetables need to almost completely dissolve during cooking to create the creamy base. Arrange the cubes on a cutting board, ensuring they are of similar size to guarantee even cooking. Chef's tip: Do not worry if the cubes are not aesthetically perfect, as they will be mashed, but try not to make them too large or the barley will overcook before the vegetables are tender.
  2. Take 140g of pearl barley and rinse it thoroughly under cold running water using a fine-mesh sieve. Move the grains with your fingers until the water runs perfectly clear, thereby removing impurities and excess surface starch. This step ensures a creamy final dish that isn't gummy, keeping the grains well-defined. Chef's tip: Rinsing the barley also reduces foam formation during boiling, making the cooking process cleaner and more controlled.
  3. Place the diced pumpkin and potato into a large, heavy-bottomed pot along with the rinsed barley and one fresh sage leaf. Do not add any fats or salt at this stage, as we want the vegetable fibres to soften gently in contact with the water. Stir briefly with a wooden spoon to distribute the ingredients evenly across the bottom of the pot. Chef's tip: Using a heavy-bottomed pot is ideal for spreading heat steadily and preventing the barley from sticking to the bottom during the long cooking time.
  4. Pour approximately one litre of cold water into the pot, ensuring it rises several inches above the level of the ingredients. Turn the heat to medium-high and bring to a boil; using cold water allows for a gradual release of the natural flavours and sugars from the pumpkin and potato. As soon as the water begins to boil, you will notice the first aromas of sage releasing into the air. Chef's tip: If you prefer a more intense flavour, you can replace the water with a light vegetable stock, but plain water perfectly highlights the natural sweetness of the Mantovana pumpkin.
  5. Reduce the heat to low, partially cover with a lid, and let it simmer gently for about 35-40 minutes. During this time, the barley will absorb the liquid and gradually swell, while the vegetables will become extremely tender, almost falling apart. Check the density occasionally: the result should be a very thick soup, with the barley having doubled its initial volume. Chef's tip: Keep a small pot of hot water nearby; if you see the liquid drying up too quickly before the barley is cooked, add a ladleful at a time.
  6. Once the 40 minutes have passed, turn off the heat and remove the sage leaf with kitchen tongs. Use a fork or a potato masher directly inside the pot to press any remaining visible pieces of pumpkin and potato against the sides or the bottom. This operation will instantly transform the cooking liquid into a velvety cream that envelops the barley grains. Chef's tip: Mash firmly but try not to break the barley grains, which should remain whole to provide a pleasant chewy texture to the dish.
  7. Stir the mixture vigorously with a wooden spoon for one minute, simulating the 'mantecatura' technique typical of risotto. This mechanical movement encourages the release of the final starches and the perfect union between the vegetable purée and the cereal, bringing the consistency to a 'wavy' (all'onda) density. The aroma will now be intense and the colour a warm, inviting orange. Chef's tip: If the consistency seems too dry, add a splash of hot water at this stage to achieve the desired creaminess.
  8. Complete the dish by adding the 20ml of extra virgin olive oil raw and stirring one last time to emulsify everything. Adding the oil off the heat preserves its nutritional properties and gives a golden sheen to the cream. Serve the dish lukewarm in large bowls, letting it rest for a minute before eating. Chef's tip: A pinch of freshly ground black pepper over each serving will magnificently enhance the sweetness of the pumpkin and potato without masking their delicate flavour.

Tips

  • Rinse the barley thoroughly under running water to remove impurities before cooking.
  • Cut the pumpkin and potatoes into small, uniform cubes to ensure even cooking.
  • If the cream becomes too thick, add a little hot water at the end.

Variations

  • Add a sprig of rosemary during cooking for a more intense aroma.
  • Replace pumpkin with carrots for a version with equally vibrant color and flavor.
