Rustic Apple and Raisin Tart with Oil Pastry

4 servings · Prep 25 min · Cooking 35 min · Medium

A 'povera' shortcrust pastry without butter, crunchy and light, enclosing a juicy filling of seasonal apples. Ideal for those looking for a home-made dessert low in saturated fats.

Ingredients

  • Type 1 or wholemeal flour (semi-refined Italian flour)250 g
  • Sunflower oil70 ml
  • Brown sugar80 g
  • Medium egg1 pcs
  • Apples (Golden Delicious or Russet types)3 pcs
  • Raisins30 g
  • Ground cinnamon1 tsp

Allergens

Gluten, Eggs

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the liquid emulsion that will serve as the base for your oil-based pastry. In a large mixing bowl, crack the medium egg and combine it with 65g of brown sugar (reserve the rest for the apples) and 70ml of sunflower oil. Whisk vigorously by hand for about 2 minutes until the mixture appears pale, thick, and perfectly smooth. Chef's tip: ensure the egg is at room temperature to encourage a stable emulsion that won't separate during baking.
  2. Proceed by incorporating the 250g of Type 1 or wholemeal flour into the liquid mixture. Pour it in gradually, starting to mix with a fork and then moving to kneading quickly with your fingertips so as not to overheat the oil. You should obtain a dough that is elastic, compact, and does not stick to your hands; if the dough feels too crumbly, add a tablespoon of cold water. Chef's tip: do not overwork the oil pastry, as unlike butter pastry, excessive kneading will make it tough rather than crumbly.
  3. Wrap the resulting dough in cling film and let it rest in the refrigerator for 30 minutes. This time is necessary for the fibers in the wholemeal flour to hydrate properly and for the lipid structure of the oil to stabilise, making the pastry easier to roll out. The final result will be a firm and malleable dough. Chef's tip: flatten the dough into a disc shape before putting it in the fridge to ensure it cools evenly and quickly.
  4. While the pastry rests, take care of rehydrating the 30g of raisins. Submerge them in a small bowl with lukewarm water for at least 10 minutes, allowing the berries to plump up and become tender. Afterwards, drain them and squeeze them thoroughly between your hands or using a clean tea towel to remove any excess moisture. Chef's tip: you can flavour the soaking water with orange zest to give the raisins a deeper citrus note.
  5. Prepare the fruit filling by peeling the 3 apples and cutting them into cubes of about 1.5 cm. Heat a non-stick frying pan over medium heat and add the apples with the remaining 15g of brown sugar, the squeezed raisins, and the teaspoon of cinnamon. Sauté everything for about 5 minutes: the apples should soften slightly but retain their shape, releasing an intense, spicy aroma. Chef's tip: avoid cooking them too long at this stage, as they will finish softening and caramelising during baking.
  6. Retrieve the pastry from the fridge and divide it into two parts, one about double the size of the other (two-thirds and one-third). Roll out the larger portion with a rolling pin between two sheets of baking parchment to a thickness of about 4-5 mm. Transfer the sheet into a tart tin, lining the bottom and sides well and trimming any excess with a small knife. Chef's tip: oil pastry is more fragile than the classic version; if it breaks during transfer, don't worry, you can 'patch' it by pressing small pieces of dough into the gaps.
  7. Uniformly distribute the apple, raisin, and cinnamon mixture over the tart base, levelling it with the back of a spoon without pressing too hard. Take the remaining pastry and roll it out to cut strips about 2 cm wide using a smooth or fluted pastry wheel. Arrange the strips over the filling to create the classic lattice pattern and seal the edges well by pressing with the tines of a fork. Chef's tip: for a more rustic look, fold the edges of the base inwards over the ends of the strips.
  8. Bake the tart in a preheated static oven at 180°C for approximately 30-35 minutes. Place the tin on the middle-lower rack of the oven to ensure the base cooks perfectly without burning the top strips. The tart will be ready when the surface appears golden brown and the edges start to pull away slightly from the sides of the tin. Chef's tip: if you notice the edges browning too quickly, cover the tart with a piece of foil for the last 10 minutes of baking.
  9. Once out of the oven, it is essential to let the tart cool directly in the tin for at least 20-30 minutes before removing it. This rest allows the apple juices to set and the oil pastry to acquire the correct biscuity consistency. The scent will be that of typical homemade bakes, with warm notes of cinnamon and whole grain. Chef's tip: enjoy this tart lukewarm to enhance the creaminess of the filling, perhaps paired with a cup of black tea.
