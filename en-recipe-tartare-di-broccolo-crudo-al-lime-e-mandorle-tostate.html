Raw broccoli tartare with lime and toasted almonds

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 5 min · Easy

A fresh and surprising starter where raw broccoli reveals an unexpected crunch, enhanced by lime and toasted almonds.

🌿 Seasonal recipe🌱 Vegan🌾 Gluten-free🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free

Ingredients

  • Fresh broccoli500g
  • Flaked almonds40g
  • Lime (juice and zest)1
  • Extra virgin olive oil3 tablespoons
  • Dijon mustard1 teaspoon
  • Fine saltto taste
  • Ground black pepperto taste
  • Fresh chives1 bunch

Allergens

Tree nuts, Mustard
May contain traces of: Sesame.

Method

  1. Start by thoroughly washing 500g of fresh broccoli under cold running water, then dry them perfectly by patting them with a clean kitchen towel or paper towels. It is essential to eliminate every trace of moisture so that the dressing adheres well and the tartare does not become watery on the plate. You should end up with florets and stalks that are completely dry to the touch and bright green. Chef's advice: if you have time, let them air-dry for a few minutes after patting them down to ensure total removal of water residue.
  2. Take the broccoli stalks, remove the tougher, fibrous outer layer using a vegetable peeler, and cut the tender heart into tiny cubes, creating a 'brunoise' of about 2 millimetres. Use a sharp chef's knife and work calmly to achieve uniform pieces that will provide a crunchy and sweet note to the dish. The result should be a precise and regular dicing, almost like a stalk grit. Chef's advice: do not throw away the outer peel scraps; you can use them in the future to enrich a vegetable stock or a creamy soup.
  3. Separate the florets from the stalks and finely chop them with a knife on a stable cutting board until you reach a grainy consistency similar to coarse couscous. I strongly advise against using an electric mixer because the blades heat the fibres and turn the broccoli into a damp mush, whereas manual cutting preserves the freshness and structure of the vegetable. Work a little at a time with a fan-like motion until all the green is reduced to small grains. Chef's advice: keep the tip of the knife firm on the board with one hand and move the handle with the other for a safer and more consistent chop.
  4. Heat a non-stick pan over medium heat and pour in 40g of flaked almonds without adding any fats or oils. Toast them for about 3 minutes, shaking the pan frequently so the browning is uniform and the almonds do not burn, as they turn from golden to burnt in just moments. When they release an intense nutty aroma and turn a beautiful amber colour, remove them from the heat immediately. Chef's advice: almonds continue to cook with the residual heat of the pan, so transfer them immediately to a cold saucer to stop the cooking process.
  5. In a small bowl, grate the lime zest avoiding the bitter white pith and squeeze all its juice, then add 3 tablespoons of extra virgin olive oil and a teaspoon of Dijon mustard. Add a pinch of fine salt and a grind of black pepper, then use a small whisk or a fork to mix vigorously. You must obtain a thick, glossy, and well-bound emulsion with a fresh and pungent scent. Chef's advice: make sure the lime is at room temperature before squeezing it, as it will release a greater amount of juice and essential oils from the peel.
  6. Combine the chopped florets, the stalk brunoise, and the bunch of chives—which you should have previously finely snipped with kitchen scissors—into a large bowl. Pour the freshly prepared lime emulsion over them and stir gently from bottom to top using a metal spoon. Every grain of broccoli must be evenly coated in the dressing, looking glossy and inviting. Chef's advice: use a previously chilled glass or ceramic bowl to keep the vibrancy of the broccoli's natural green colour intact.
  7. Cover the bowl with cling film and let the mixture rest in the refrigerator for about 10 minutes. This short resting time allows the lime acids and salt to slightly penetrate the broccoli fibres, softening it just enough without losing its characteristic raw crunchiness. At the end, the tartare will have developed a more harmonious and deep aromatic profile. Chef's advice: do not exceed 15 minutes of resting, otherwise the acidity of the citrus will begin to chemically 'cook' the broccoli, causing it to change colour to a dull green.
  8. Take the bowl out of the fridge and only now add 3/4 of the toasted flaked almonds, keeping the rest aside for the final decoration. Mix briefly and with extreme care to integrate the almonds without breaking them excessively, trying to distribute their crunchy note well. The tartare should present a pleasant contrast between the softness of the dressing and the fragility of the almond. Chef's advice: adding the almonds only at the very last moment is the fundamental secret to prevent them from absorbing moisture from the dressing and turning soft.
  9. For a professional restaurant-style plating, place a metal ring mould in the centre of a flat plate and fill it with a quarter of the tartare. Press down lightly with the back of a spoon to compact the ingredients well, ensuring there are no air gaps inside the circle. Gently slide the ring upwards to reveal a perfect cylindrical shape that maintains its structure. Chef's advice: if you do not own a ring mould, you can create elegant 'quenelles' (oval scoops) using two tablespoons moistened with a drop of water.
  10. Complete the dish by decorating the surface of the tartare with the reserved toasted almonds and a final drizzle of raw extra virgin olive oil. Serve immediately while the preparation is still fresh and releases the full bouquet of lime and chive scents. The final result should be an elegant starter, vibrant in colour and capable of surprising with its incredible lightness. Chef's advice: add a pinch of Maldon salt or sea salt flakes on top just before serving to provide an extra savoury boost and a crystalline texture on the palate.
