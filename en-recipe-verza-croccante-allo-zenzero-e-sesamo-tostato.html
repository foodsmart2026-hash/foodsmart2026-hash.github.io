Crunchy Savoy Cabbage with Ginger and Toasted Sesame

🌿 Seasonal recipe

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 8 min · Easy

An Asian-inspired side dish where Savoy cabbage is quickly stir-fried at high temperatures to preserve its texture and vibrant flavour.

Ingredients

  • Savoy cabbage500 g
  • Fresh ginger (root)20 g
  • Soy sauce2 tbsp
  • White sesame seeds1 tbsp
  • Groundnut oil3 tbsp
  • Fresh chilli1/2
  • Sesame oil (optional)1 tsp

Allergens

Peanuts, Sesame, Soy

Method

  1. Start by carefully cleaning 500g of savoy cabbage: remove the damaged outer leaves and the tough white central ribs, then slice the leaves into strips about 1.5cm wide. Use a sharp chef’s knife and a stable cutting board to achieve uniform cuts that will cook at the same rate. The final result should be a heap of fresh, clean, and above all, perfectly dry cabbage strips. My tip is to dry the cabbage with a salad spinner: any residual water would create steam in the pan, preventing you from achieving that typical stir-fry crunchiness.
  2. Now focus on the aromatics that will give character to the dish: peel 20g of fresh ginger root with the back of a teaspoon and cut it into very thin matchsticks, then finely mince half a fresh chilli after removing the internal seeds. Work precisely on the board to release the essential oils without crushing the fibres of the ingredients. You will immediately notice a pungent and fresh scent that will form the base of your preparation. Be careful not to touch your eyes after handling the chilli; it is a common mistake that can be very painful.
  3. Take a small non-stick pan and dry-toast one tablespoon of white sesame seeds over medium heat for about 2-3 minutes. Constantly move the pan with circular wrist motions to ensure an even golden brown on every single seed. When you see the seeds turn slightly amber and smell an intense nutty aroma, they are ready to release all their crunch. Transfer them immediately to a small cold bowl to stop the cooking process; leaving them in the hot pan would burn them and make them bitter in seconds.
  4. Heat a wok or a very large non-stick frying pan over the most powerful burner in your kitchen, pouring in 3 tablespoons of peanut oil. Wait until the oil becomes very fluid and almost begins to emit a thin wisp of smoke, a sign it has reached the ideal smoke point for stir-frying. High temperature is essential to sear the cabbage quickly without letting it lose its natural juices. Remember that oil that is too cold would boil the vegetables instead of frying them, irreparably ruining the final texture of the dish.
  5. Carefully pour the ginger and chilli into the boiling oil, letting them sizzle over high heat for no more than 30 seconds. Use a wooden spoon or a spatula to move them quickly across the bottom, preventing the tips of the ginger from darkening too much. You should obtain a highly fragrant and aromatic oil, with the spices becoming slightly crispy but remaining vibrant in colour. Never step away from the stove during this phase, as thinly sliced ginger can burn in an instant, compromising the flavour of the entire dish.
  6. Add the 500g of prepared cabbage into the wok and stir-fry over very high heat for about 5 continuous minutes. Use kitchen tongs to lift and toss the cabbage, ensuring every strip comes into contact with the intense heat of the pan's base. The cabbage will start to reduce slightly in volume and become glossy, while maintaining a firm texture and a bright green colour. If you notice the pan is getting too crowded and the temperature drops, cook the cabbage in two separate batches to maintain the desired 'crunch' effect.
  7. When the cabbage is almost ready but still 'al dente', deglaze the pan with 2 tablespoons of soy sauce and, if desired, a teaspoon of sesame oil. Keep the heat on maximum to allow the watery part of the soy to evaporate instantly, leaving only its savoury flavour and umami note. The cabbage will take on a light caramel hue and release an irresistible toasted aroma. Do not add extra salt, as the soy sauce is already sufficiently salty and perfectly balances the natural sweetness of the cabbage.
  8. Turn off the heat immediately and scatter the previously toasted sesame seeds over the top, stirring one last time to incorporate them evenly. Serve the cabbage piping hot in individual dishes, ensuring it reaches the table while it is still vibrant and crunchy to the bite. The final result should be a vibrant, fragrant side dish with a bold Oriental character that will impress your guests. The chef's secret is to serve instantly: if you leave the cabbage in the hot pan or cover it with a lid, the residual steam will make it soggy within moments.
