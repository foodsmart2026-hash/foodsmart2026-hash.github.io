Mediterranean-style Potato and Pea Curry

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 30 min · Medium

A simplified version of Indian Aloo Matar, adapted with ingredients easily found in any European supermarket. A nutritious and spicy one-pot meal.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🌱 Vegan🌾 Gluten-free🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🌍 International

A fragrant and creamy one-pot dish combining the sweetness of peas with the velvety texture of potatoes. Coconut milk and curry provide an exotic touch, balanced by Mediterranean tomato pulp.

Ingredients

  • Potatoes600 g
  • Frozen peas300 g
  • Tomato pulp (tinned crushed tomatoes)200 g
  • Brown onion1 pcs
  • Fresh ginger10 g
  • Curry powder10 g
  • Coconut milk (canned)200 ml
  • Extra virgin olive oil20 ml

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Start by rinsing 600g of potatoes under cold running water to remove any soil, then peel them with a vegetable peeler and cut into uniform 2cm cubes. Use a stable chopping board and a sharp chef's knife to ensure regular pieces for even cooking. Place the cubed potatoes in a bowl of cold water to prevent browning while you prepare the other ingredients. You should achieve a clean, consistently sized dice. Chef's tip: do not cut the cubes too small, or they may break apart during the long simmering process.
  2. Prepare the aromatic base by finely chopping one golden onion and grating 10g of fresh ginger, previously peeled with the back of a teaspoon. In a large saucepan or wok, heat 20ml of extra virgin olive oil over medium heat, then add the onion, ginger, and 10g of curry powder. Sauté gently for about 4-5 minutes, stirring frequently with a wooden spoon to prevent the spices from burning. You want to achieve a translucent, highly fragrant, and lightly golden base. Chef's tip: heating spices in oil first 'blooms' them, activating the essential oils to release their full flavour bouquet.
  3. Drain the potato cubes well and add them to the pan with the aromatic base, slightly increasing the heat. Toss the potatoes for about 3 minutes, ensuring every cube is thoroughly coated in the spiced oil. This 'toasting' technique partially seals the surface and deepens the overall flavour. The potatoes will turn a vibrant, uniform yellow. Chef's tip: do not rush this stage, as the direct contact with the pan's heat creates a fundamental flavour foundation for the entire dish.
  4. Pour in 200g of tomato pulp and add about 200ml of hot water (just under a cup) to partially cover the potatoes. Reduce the heat to medium-low, cover with a lid, and simmer for 15 minutes. Stir occasionally to prevent the tomato from sticking to the bottom, checking that the liquid doesn't evaporate too quickly. The tomato will begin to reduce into a thick orange-red sauce. Chef's tip: use pre-heated water or a light vegetable stock to avoid stopping the boil and to keep the potato temperature constant.
  5. Add 300g of frozen peas to the pan, folding them in gently with an upward motion. Continue cooking on low heat for another 5 minutes with the lid on, using the internal steam to cook the legumes. The peas should cook just enough to be tender while retaining their bite and a bright green colour. You will see the sauce become even richer and more varied in texture. Chef's tip: adding the peas while still frozen prevents them from becoming mushy and preserves their natural sweetness.
  6. Pour 200ml of canned coconut milk into the pan, stirring thoroughly until the liquid is perfectly incorporated with the other ingredients. The coconut milk will transform the sauce into a velvety, apricot-coloured cream, balancing the pungency of the ginger and curry. Keep the heat low to prevent the fat in the coconut milk from separating excessively. The result should be a smooth, rich, and coating consistency. Chef's tip: shake the coconut milk tin vigorously before opening to mix the solid cream and liquid water components.
  7. Allow the curry to reduce over low heat without a lid for the final 5 minutes, or until it reaches your desired thickness. Check the potatoes by piercing them with a fork: they should be tender in the middle but still hold their shape perfectly. Season with salt and pepper to taste if necessary. The sauce should 'coat' the back of a spoon, appearing thick rather than watery. Chef's tip: if the sauce is too thin, mash two or three potato cubes against the side of the pan to release starch and instantly thicken the base.
  8. Turn off the heat and let the curry rest for a couple of minutes before serving to allow the flavours to settle and the temperature to reach its ideal eating point. Serve the curry in wide bowls, ideally accompanied by steamed basmati rice or toasted rye bread slices. The final dish will appear as a harmonious blend of golden cubes and green pearls immersed in a creamy sauce. Chef's tip: add a few fresh coriander leaves or chopped parsley at the last second for a Mediterranean touch of freshness to contrast the richness of the coconut.

Tips

  • Cut the potatoes into small, equal cubes so they cook evenly along with the peas.
  • Grate the fresh ginger just before using to release its full, pungent aroma.
  • Stir the canned coconut milk well before pouring to properly combine the solid and liquid parts.

Variations

  • For extra color and flavor, add a handful of cherry tomatoes halfway through the cooking process.
  • You can enhance the base by toasting cumin seeds in the oil to intensify the overall spicy notes.
