Meatballs in Tomato Sauce

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 45 min · Hard

A timeless and nutritious version of classic homemade meatballs, made with a mix of white and pork meat to ensure tenderness and savings. The secret of this recipe lies in the slow cooking in tomato, which transforms simple ingredients into a succulent main course loved by adults and children alike.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🧒 Kid-friendly

Meatballs in tomato sauce are the ultimate Italian comfort food, tender on the inside and smothered in rich sauce. The blend of chicken and pork provides a perfect balance of lightness and flavor. A generous recipe that pairs perfectly with crusty bread.

Ingredients

  • Mixed minced meat (chicken and pork)400 g
  • Tomato passata (sieved tomatoes)700 g
  • Stale bread (crumb only)100 g
  • Semi-skimmed milk50 ml
  • Egg1 pz
  • Grated Grana Padano or Parmesan (hard Italian cheeses)30 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil25 ml
  • Brown onion50 g
  • Fine salt5 g
  • Fresh parsley10 g

Allergens

Gluten, Eggs, Milk

Method

  1. Start by soaking 100 g of crumbled stale bread crumb with 50 ml of semi-skimmed milk in a small bowl. Press the pieces well with a fork so the liquid is absorbed evenly and let it rest for about 10 minutes. The bread should be soft to the touch but not excessively watery or mushy. At the end of the time, squeeze the bread firmly with your hands to remove excess milk. The chef's tip is to use bread with a dense crumb to obtain a firmer and more pleasant final texture.
  2. In a large bowl, combine 400 g of mixed minced meat, the whole egg, and 30 g of grated cheese. Add a pinch of salt, pepper, and the finely chopped fresh parsley to provide an aromatic note. Work the mixture with your hands for about 5 minutes, carefully blending all the ingredients until the mixture is perfectly homogenous. You will get a compact mass, slightly sticky but easy to shape. The chef's tip is not to warm the meat too much with your hands; if you feel the mixture becoming too shiny, let it rest in the fridge for 15 minutes.
  3. Take a small portion of the mixture, about 30-35 g, and roll it between the palms of your hands to form a perfect sphere the size of a walnut. Repeat the operation until the ingredients are finished, placing the meatballs on a tray without overlapping them. Try to maintain a constant size for all pieces to ensure even cooking during the next phase. You should get about 16-18 meatballs with a smooth and regular surface. The chef's tip is to slightly moisten your hands with a drop of cold water to prevent the meat from sticking to your skin during shaping.
  4. Take 50 g of brown onion, peel it and chop it finely with a sharp knife. Pour it into a high-sided non-stick pan together with 20 ml of extra virgin olive oil. Turn on the heat to medium intensity and let it sauté gently for 4-5 minutes, stirring often with a wooden spoon. The onion should become transparent and release a sweet scent, without ever darkening too much or burning. The chef's tip is to add a tablespoon of hot water to the sauté to keep the onion soft and digestible without exceeding the fat intake.
  5. Pour 700 g of tomato passata into the pan with the sautéed onion and adjust the salt according to your personal taste. Bring the sauce to a light boil maintaining medium heat, then lower the intensity and partially cover with a lid. Let the sauce base cook for about 10 minutes, allowing the flavours to integrate and the passata to thicken slightly. The result will be a vibrant red sauce, fragrant and ready to welcome the meat. The chef's tip is to add a pinch of sugar to balance any natural acidity of the tomato.
  6. Place the meatballs in the hot sauce one at a time, arranging them gently so they are partially submerged in the tomato. Cover the pan with the lid and cook over medium-low heat for about 12 minutes without turning or touching the meat. In this phase, the heat of the sauce will begin to cook the external proteins of the meatball, sealing the juices inside to keep them tender. You will notice that the meatballs start to change colour, turning towards a lighter and more compact shade. The chef's tip is to resist the temptation to stir immediately, as raw meatballs are very fragile and risk breaking.
  7. After the initial time, use a wooden spoon to gently turn each meatball in the simmering sauce. Continue cooking for another 15-20 minutes over very low heat, leaving the lid slightly ajar to allow moisture to evaporate. The sauce must reduce slowly, gradually becoming darker and glossier thanks to the union with the juices released by the meat. At the end, the meatballs will be well cooked even at the core but will remain incredibly juicy. The chef's tip is to lightly shake the pan by the handles instead of using a spoon, so as to move the meatballs without risking accidentally cutting them.
  8. Turn off the heat and let the dish rest for 5 minutes inside the pan before plating. This short rest allows the meat fibres to relax and the sauce to adhere perfectly to the surface of each meatball. Distribute the meatballs into individual plates, drizzling them generously with the tomato sauce remaining at the bottom. The final dish must appear rich and inviting, with an enveloping scent reminiscent of home cooking. The chef's tip is to serve this main course with toasted wholemeal bread to avoid losing even a single drop of the delicious cooking sauce.

Tips

  • Squeeze the milk from the bread crumbs thoroughly to prevent the mixture from becoming too soft.
  • Shape the meatballs with slightly damp hands to achieve a smooth and consistent surface.
  • Simmer gently and avoid stirring too often to keep the meatballs from breaking apart in the sauce.

Variations

  • Place a small cube of cheese in the center of each meatball for a delicious melted core.
  • Add some grated lemon zest to the meat mixture for an unexpected and fresh citrus note.
