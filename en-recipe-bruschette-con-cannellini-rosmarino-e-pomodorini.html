Bruschetta with Cannellini Beans, Rosemary and Cherry Tomatoes

💰 Save Euro · about €0.69 per person

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 10 min · Easy

A traditional Tuscan 'cucina povera' starter revisited to ensure a balanced intake of fibre and healthy fats. This recipe uses affordable ingredients always available in any supermarket to create a perfect contrast between the creaminess of the pulses and the crunch of wholemeal bread.

Ingredients

  • Rustic wholemeal bread200 g
  • Canned cannellini beans240 g
  • Cherry tomatoes200 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Fresh rosemary2 sprigs
  • Garlic1 clove
  • Fine salt2 g
  • Black pepper1 pinch

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Drain the cannellini beans from their liquid and rinse them thoroughly under running water to remove excess sodium and impurities.
  2. In a non-stick frying pan, heat 10 ml of extra virgin olive oil with the whole garlic clove and one sprig of rosemary for 2 minutes over low heat until the garlic is lightly golden.
  3. Pour the beans into the pan with a tablespoon of water and let them infuse for 5 minutes over medium heat, allowing them to absorb the aromas and further softening the skins.
  4. Remove the garlic and, using a fork, coarsely mash about half of the beans directly in the pan to obtain a texture that is part creamy and part whole beans.
  5. Wash the cherry tomatoes, cut them into small pieces and season them in a bowl with a pinch of salt and the needles from the second rosemary sprig, finely chopped.
  6. Slice the wholemeal bread into 8 slices about 1 cm thick and toast them on a very hot griddle pan for 2-3 minutes per side, aiming for a firm texture and slightly charred edges.
  7. Very gently rub the previously cooked garlic clove onto the surface of the hot toasted bread to release the essence without being too overpowering.
  8. Spread the warm cannellini bean base over the bread slices, pressing lightly so the mixture sticks and doesn't fall off while eating.
  9. Top each bruschetta with the seasoned cherry tomatoes and finish with the remaining 20 ml of raw extra virgin olive oil to maximise the intake of vitamin E and polyphenols.
