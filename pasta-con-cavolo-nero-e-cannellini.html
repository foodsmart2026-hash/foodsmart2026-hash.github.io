Pasta with cavolo nero and cannellini beans

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

A complete main course that marries the rustic nature of cavolo nero with the creaminess of pulses, perfect for the cold season. This recipe balances complex carbohydrates, fibre and plant-based proteins in a single affordable and flavourful dish.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🌱 Vegan🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free

A rustic kitchen classic combining whole wheat pasta texture with the creaminess of legumes. Lacinato kale provides a bold character, while the cannellini bean puree wraps each bite in a velvety embrace.

Ingredients

  • Wholewheat short pasta (e.g. fusilli or rigatoni)320 g
  • Cavolo nero (Italian black kale, cleaned weight)300 g
  • Pre-cooked cannellini beans (drained weight)240 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 ml
  • Garlic cloves1 pz
  • Dried chilli1 g
  • Wholegrain sea salt5 g
  • Black pepper1 g

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Clean and prepare the cavolo nero: start by removing the tough central rib from each leaf of 300g of cavolo nero and cut the leaves into strips about 2 cm wide. Wash them thoroughly in cold water to remove any traces of soil or grit. This process takes about 5 minutes, and the result should be a pile of tender, clean strips ready for even cooking. My tip: don't throw away the ribs; if sliced very thinly, they can enrich a vegetable soup, avoiding unnecessary food waste.
  2. Prepare the water and start blanching: bring about 4 litres of water to a boil in a large pot and add 5g of unrefined sea salt. Drop the cavolo nero strips into the boiling water and cook for 5 minutes over medium heat without covering the pot completely. The leaves should soften significantly while maintaining a deep, vibrant green colour. Be careful not to overcook them at this stage, as they will continue cooking with the pasta.
  3. Cook the wholemeal pasta: in the same water where the kale is boiling, add 320g of short wholemeal pasta, ensuring the water returns to a vigorous boil immediately. Set a timer for 2 minutes less than the package instructions, stirring occasionally with a wooden spoon to prevent the pasta from sticking. The result should be very 'al dente', ready for the final tossing stage in the pan. The chef's tip: always taste the pasta one minute before the timer goes off to check the bite of the whole grain.
  4. Prepare the aromatic base in the pan: while the pasta cooks, heat 40ml of extra virgin olive oil in a large frying pan with a crushed garlic clove and 1g of dried chilli. Keep the heat low for about 3 minutes, letting the garlic turn golden without burning, so it releases its full aroma into the oil. The oil should be fragrant and slightly spicy, creating the perfect base for the sauce. Do not raise the heat too high to avoid degrading the polyphenols in the extra virgin oil.
  5. Season the pulses: thoroughly rinse 240g of pre-cooked cannellini beans under running water to remove the canning liquid and add them to the pan with the garlic. Pour in a ladle of pasta cooking water (about 80ml) and let it simmer over medium heat for 3 minutes. The beans should heat through and begin to release some of their starch into the cooking base. Use a wooden spoon to stir them gently without breaking them prematurely.
  6. Create the natural cannellini cream: using a fork, mash about a third of the beans directly onto the bottom of the pan until they become a pulp. Mix vigorously with the cooking water already in the pan to form a thick, velvety emulsion that will bind the dish together. The result should be a rustic, fragrant cream that coats the pasta without the need for added fats. This is the secret to a creamy yet light and balanced dish.
  7. Sauté the pasta and kale: drain the pasta and cavolo nero using a slotted spoon and transfer them directly into the pan with the cannellini cream. Turn the heat to high and sauté everything for 2 minutes, adding another half ladle of cooking water if the sauce becomes too dry. The pasta must finish cooking enveloped in the sauce, becoming glossy and perfectly bonded with the other ingredients. Toss the pan with a circular wrist motion to encourage the creaminess to form.
  8. Finishing and serving: turn off the heat, remove the garlic clove, and finish with a grind of fresh black pepper (1g). Let the pasta rest for 30 seconds before serving, allowing the flavours to blend harmoniously. The final dish should feature the wholemeal pasta well-coated by the bean cream with the dark green of the kale providing contrast. The chef's touch: a final tiny drizzle of raw oil on the plate will enhance the herbal notes of the kale and the delicacy of the cannellini.

Tips

  • Always remove the central rib of the kale as it remains tough and woody even after cooking.
  • Mash some of the beans with a fork directly in the pan to create a natural cream without added fats.
  • Cook the pasta and kale in the same water to save time and retain the nutrients from the leaves.

Variations

  • Add a tablespoon of tomato paste to the garlic base for a more colorful and savory sauce.
  • Replace dried chili with freshly ground black pepper for a more aromatic and less intense heat.
