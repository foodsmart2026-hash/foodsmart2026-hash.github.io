Cannellini Bean Bites with Lemon and Sage

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 20 min · Medium

Light, protein-rich bites made with humble larder ingredients. The lemon and sage provide a freshness that makes them perfect served both hot and cold.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🌱 Vegan🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free

Ingredients

  • Cooked cannellini beans500 g
  • Boiled potato150 g
  • Breadcrumbs60 g
  • Fresh sage6 foglie
  • Lemon (zest and juice)1 pcs
  • Nutritional yeast flakes15 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil20 ml

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Start by thoroughly rinsing 500g of pre-cooked cannellini beans under running water to remove the canning liquid and excess sodium. In a large mixing bowl, combine the beans with 150g of boiled potato (previously mashed) and work them with a fork or a potato masher until you achieve a coarse, thick purée. Do not use an electric mixer, as this would make the mixture too elastic or gummy; instead, aim for a rustic texture with some bean fragments still visible. The result should be a solid mass that pulls away easily from the sides of the bowl. A chef's tip: pat the beans dry with kitchen paper after washing to prevent adding unnecessary moisture.
  2. Prepare the aromatics by finely mincing 6 fresh sage leaves with a sharp knife and grating the zest of a whole organic lemon, taking care not to include the bitter white pith. Add these fragrances to the bowl along with 15g of nutritional yeast flakes, which will provide a savoury, complex note similar to aged cheese. Mix vigorously with a wooden spoon to evenly distribute the essential oils of the lemon and sage throughout the mixture. You will immediately notice a fresh, balsamic aroma rising from the bowl. The advice here is to gently rub the chopped sage between your fingers before adding it to release its full aromatic potential.
  3. Pour one tablespoon of lemon juice (about 10-12 ml) into the mixture and begin incorporating 60g of breadcrumbs, adding them gradually to monitor the absorption. Work the dough with your hands: it should transform into a compact, non-sticky, and easily mouldable mass, similar to the consistency of shortcrust pastry. If the mixture still feels too soft, add a tiny pinch of extra breadcrumbs, but proceed with extreme caution to avoid making the meatballs too hard during cooking. The ideal texture is reached when you can form a smooth ball without it leaving residue on your fingers. Avoid adding too much lemon juice to keep the acid balance in check.
  4. Preheat the oven to 200°C (static) and line a large baking tray with parchment paper, ensuring it is laid flat. Prepare a small cup with 20ml of extra virgin olive oil and keep a pastry brush (silicone or natural bristle) within reach. It is vital that the oven reaches the set temperature before baking to ensure an immediate thermal shock that seals the surface of the meatballs. If using a fan oven, lower the temperature to 180°C to prevent the crust from drying out before the centre is hot. Ensure the rack is positioned in the middle of the oven for even heat distribution.
  5. Take about 35-40g of the mixture at a time and roll it between your palms to form a perfect walnut-sized sphere. Once the ball is formed, apply light pressure with your palm to flatten it slightly, creating a small disc about 1.5 cm thick. Place the meatballs on the tray, maintaining a distance of about 2 cm from each other to allow hot air to circulate evenly on all sides. You should get about 16-18 meatballs of identical size to ensure simultaneous cooking. Try not to create cracks on the edges when pressing down to prevent them from splitting in the oven.
  6. Dip the brush into the 20ml of extra virgin olive oil and carefully brush only the top surface of each meatball. This film of oil is the secret to achieving a golden-brown finish and a crispiness similar to frying, while maintaining the lightness of oven baking. Proceed with gentle strokes so as not to deform the discs and ensure that the oil does not drip excessively onto the parchment paper below. The heat will transform this thin layer of fat into a tasty, fragrant crust. If you don't have a brush, you can use your fingertips, but the result will be less uniform.
  7. Place the tray in the preheated 200°C oven and set the timer for the first 10 minutes of cooking without ever opening the door. During this phase, the starches from the potato and breadcrumbs will begin to cook, stabilising the internal structure and crisping the base. You will see the surface start to turn matte and the scents of sage and lemon intensify throughout the kitchen. The heat must act constantly to create a solid base that allows you to flip the meatballs without breaking them. The chef's tip is to look through the glass: if the meatballs start to sizzle slightly at the base, the temperature is perfect.
  8. After 10 minutes, remove the tray and, using a thin spatula, quickly flip each meatball onto the opposite side to ensure even browning. Bake for another 8-10 minutes, or until the surface appears dry, a deep golden colour, and shows very fine surface cracks. Be careful not to exceed 20 minutes of total cooking time, otherwise the inside will lose its pleasant creaminess and become floury. The secret is visual: the meatballs are ready when they feel firm to the touch but still slightly yielding if pressed with a finger. Don't wait for them to turn brown, as residual heat will continue to cook them.
  9. Once removed from the oven, let the meatballs rest directly on the tray for about 5-7 minutes at room temperature before serving. This resting stage is mandatory: it allows the legume fibres to fully set, preventing the meatball from being too fragile when eaten. You will notice the texture change from soft to perfectly elastic and compact, ideal for dipping into a sauce. Serve them warm to maximise the citrus note of the lemon and the herbaceous scent of the sage. Final tip: pair them with a vegan mayo or a Greek yoghurt and chive dip for a sublime temperature contrast.
