Chickpea and Sweet Paprika Shakshuka

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 30 min · Medium

A nutritious and filling version of the Middle Eastern classic, enriched with pulses for a complete meal. Perfect for a convivial family dinner using simple cupboard staples.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🥕 Vegetarian🌾 Gluten-free🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🌍 International

A nutritious and spiced version of traditional shakshuka, enhanced by the texture of pre-cooked chickpeas. It is a comforting one-pan meal, perfect for a hearty brunch or a cozy shared dinner. The red sauce, scented with cumin, cradles the eggs in a bold flavor combination.

Ingredients

  • Large eggs4 pcs
  • Canned chickpeas400 g
  • Tomato pulp (chopped canned tomatoes)500 g
  • Brown onion1 pcs
  • Red pepper1 pcs
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Sweet paprika10 g
  • Ground cumin5 g
  • Salt and pepperq.b.

Allergens

Eggs

Method

  1. Start by preparing the vegetables: finely slice the yellow onion and dice the red pepper into uniform 1cm cubes. Use a sharp chef's knife to ensure regular cuts, which are essential for even cooking and a harmonious texture in the sauce. You should achieve a clean, consistent dice. Chef's tip: slice the pepper skin-side down for better grip and precision.
  2. Heat 30ml of extra virgin olive oil in a large, heavy-based frying pan over medium heat. Once the oil is shimmering, add the onion and pepper, sautéing them gently for about 8 minutes. Stir frequently until the onion is translucent and the pepper is tender to the bite. Chef's tip: if the vegetables seem to dry out too quickly, add a tablespoon of water to help them soften without browning.
  3. Now it's time to release the aromatics: add 10g of sweet paprika and 5g of ground cumin to the vegetables. Toast the spices for about 1 minute, stirring continuously with a wooden spoon so the heat activates their essential oils without burning them, which would cause bitterness. You should notice a deep, enveloping fragrance filling the kitchen. Chef's tip: do not skip this step, as dry-toasting is the secret to the Shakshuka's aromatic depth.
  4. Incorporate the 500g of tomato pulp and 400g of pre-cooked chickpeas, ensuring you have rinsed the chickpeas thoroughly under cold water to remove the canning liquid. Season with salt and black pepper to taste, stirring well to combine. You will have a thick, vibrant base where the chickpeas begin to absorb the spiced tomato flavours. Chef's tip: use high-quality tomatoes, as they provide the backbone of the dish's structure.
  5. Reduce the heat and let the mixture simmer on low for about 15 minutes, preferably uncovered. This allows the sauce to reduce and thicken, concentrating the tomato sugars and creating the perfect consistency to cradle the eggs. The sauce should look rich with small bubbles breaking the surface. Chef's tip: if the sauce thickens too much, keep the heat very low to prevent the bottom from sticking.
  6. Using the back of a large spoon, create four small wells (or 'nests') in the chickpea and tomato sauce, spacing them out evenly. This creates a stable pocket for the eggs, preventing the whites from spreading across the surface. The result will be an organised pan ready for the final touch. Chef's tip: make sure the wells reach almost to the bottom of the pan for faster egg cooking.
  7. Carefully crack one egg into each well, being very gentle to keep the yolk intact. I suggest cracking each egg into a small bowl first before sliding it into the sauce: this allows you to check for freshness and avoid any shell fragments. The egg should look like a bright spotlight against the deep red sauce. Chef's tip: work quickly so that all the eggs start cooking at roughly the same time.
  8. Cover the pan with a lid and continue cooking on low heat for 5-7 minutes. The goal is to have whites that are opaque and set, while the yolks remain covered by a thin white film but stay creamy and runny inside. Turn off the heat as soon as the egg no longer 'jiggles' when the pan is lightly shaken. Chef's tip: use a glass lid to monitor progress without losing the heat and steam required to cook the top of the whites.

Tips

  • Rinse the chickpeas thoroughly from their preservation liquid before adding them to the tomato pulp.
  • Create the egg nests only when the sauce has thickened slightly to prevent them from spreading out.
  • Cover the pan with a lid during the last minutes to set the whites while keeping the yolks creamy.

Variations

  • Swap sweet paprika for smoked paprika for a deeper, more intense flavor in the sauce.
  • Add cubes of feta cheese over the hot shakshuka before serving for a salty, Mediterranean twist.
