Red Lentil and Courgette Burgers

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 30 min · Medium

Red split lentils are the most digestible legumes for young children as they are hull-free. These burgers have a melting texture that requires no excessive chewing.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🌱 Vegan🧒 Kid-friendly🌾 Gluten-free🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free

Ingredients

  • Dried red split lentils120 g
  • Courgettes150 g
  • Fine cornmeal (fioretto) [A very finely ground corn flour common in Italy for a smooth texture]30 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil10 g
  • Water300 ml

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Start by thoroughly rinsing 120g of split red lentils under cold running water and placing them in a pot with 300ml of water. Bring to a boil over medium heat, then lower the heat to minimum and simmer for about 15 minutes until the liquid is completely absorbed. You should achieve a thick, soft, and smooth puree with no standing water at the bottom. Chef's tip: avoid salting the water at this stage to keep the lentil fibres tender and encourage the formation of a natural cream.
  2. While the lentils are cooking, wash 150g of courgettes and grate them finely using the coarse side of a box grater. Collect the pulp in a clean tea towel or squeeze it firmly between your hands to remove as much vegetable water as possible. This step is vital to prevent excess moisture from ruining the final consistency of your burgers. Chef's tip: squeeze the courgettes until they feel almost dry to the touch to ensure perfect structural integrity during cooking.
  3. Transfer the still-warm lentil puree into a large mixing bowl and add the previously grated and squeezed courgettes. Stir with a wooden spoon or spatula, blending the two ingredients with circular motions until you achieve an evenly coloured mixture. The result should be a soft dough, already partially structured by the residual heat of the lentils. Chef's tip: work the mixture while it is still warm to promote the release of starches which act as a natural binding agent.
  4. Gradually add 30g of fine cornmeal (fioretto) to the lentil and courgette mixture, distributing it evenly. Continue stirring to incorporate the flour well, then let the dough rest for about 5 minutes at room temperature. During this time, the cornmeal will hydrate by absorbing residual moisture, making the mixture much easier to handle. Chef's tip: do not be tempted to add more flour, as resting is the key to achieving the right firmness without making the burger heavy.
  5. Preheat your oven to 180°C (static) and prepare a baking tray by lining it with a sheet of high-quality parchment paper. Prepare a small bowl of water to dampen your hands, which is necessary to handle the dough without it sticking to your skin. Ensure the oven is fully at temperature before shaping to guarantee proper heat distribution from the start. Chef's tip: use a metal tray that conducts heat well to encourage a crispy crust on the bottom.
  6. With slightly damp hands, take a small portion of the dough and shape it into circular medallions about 4cm in diameter and 1.5cm thick. Place the burgers on the baking tray, spaced well apart to allow hot air to circulate evenly around each piece. You will get about 12-16 small burgers, all similar in size to ensure even cooking. Chef's tip: try to compact the edges well with your fingers to prevent them from cracking during the browning phase in the oven.
  7. Use a pastry brush to evenly distribute 10g of extra virgin olive oil over the surface of each medallion. Bake on the middle rack for a total of 15 minutes, making sure to flip the burgers halfway through using a thin spatula. The burgers should appear lightly golden on the outside while maintaining a tender, non-dry core. Chef's tip: flip the medallions gently only once the base has slightly hardened to avoid breaking them.
  8. Once removed from the oven, let the burgers rest directly on the tray for at least 5-10 minutes before handling them. During this cooling phase, the fibres stabilise and the cornmeal completes its structure, making the burger compact and easy to serve. You will notice the texture change from fragile to pleasantly firm and manageable. Chef's tip: serve these burgers with a yogurt sauce or fresh lemon to balance the natural sweetness of the red lentils.
