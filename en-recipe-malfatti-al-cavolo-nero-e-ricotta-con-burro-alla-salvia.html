Cavolo nero and ricotta malfatti with sage butter

Lombardia · 4 servings · Prep 40 min · Cooking 10 min · Medium

Naked gnocchi made with a base of cavolo nero and ricotta, soft and flavourful, finished with brown butter.

🌿 Seasonal recipe🇮🇹 Italian

Malfatti are rustic, irregularly shaped dumplings, here reinvented with the deep, earthy taste of Tuscan kale. The creaminess of ricotta and the aroma of sage butter create a comforting first course with a soft texture.

Ingredients

  • Cavolo nero (leaves only)500 g
  • Well-drained cow's milk ricotta250 g
  • Grated Parmigiano Reggiano100 g
  • Plain flour80 g
  • Eggs2
  • Nutmeg1 pinch
  • Butter60 g
  • Fresh sage6 leaves
  • Salt and pepperto taste

Allergens

Gluten, Milk, Eggs

Method

  1. Start by cleaning 500g of cavolo nero leaves, removing the tough central ribs either with a knife or by pulling them away by hand. Submerge the leaves in a large pot of lightly salted boiling water and cook over a high heat for about 10 minutes until tender. Once ready, drain the cabbage and let it cool completely before squeezing it extremely hard between your hands to remove every trace of residual water. The result should be a compact ball of greens that is almost dry to the touch, which is essential for the success of the dough. Chef's tip: squeezing the cabbage thoroughly is the secret to avoiding adding too much flour later, keeping the malfatti as light as clouds.
  2. Take the well-squeezed cabbage and chop it finely using a sharp knife on a chopping board, or use a food processor on pulse mode for a few seconds. You must not blend it into a puree; instead, aim for small, even pieces that will provide the right texture and structure to the dish. You will notice the chopped greens have a deep dark green colour and a characteristic herbaceous aroma. Chef's tip: if you choose to use a processor, ensure the blades are cold and do not run them for too long to avoid oxidising the vegetables and compromising their bright colour.
  3. In a large bowl, combine the chopped cabbage with 250g of well-drained cow's milk ricotta, 2 whole eggs, and 100g of grated Parmigiano Reggiano. Add a generous pinch of freshly grated nutmeg, salt, and pepper to taste, then mix everything with a spatula using steady, circular motions. The dough should appear chromatically homogeneous, with the white of the ricotta evenly speckled with the green of the cabbage. Chef's tip: if the ricotta seems too wet, let it drain in a fine-mesh sieve in the fridge for at least a couple of hours before starting the preparation.
  4. Sift 80g of plain flour directly over the ricotta and vegetable mixture and incorporate it gradually by stirring gently from the bottom upwards. Work the dough at room temperature only for as long as strictly necessary for the white flour streaks to disappear, avoiding overheating it with your hands. You will obtain a dense, slightly sticky mass that holds its shape well when scooped with a spoon. Chef's tip: do not be tempted to add more flour than the indicated dose, otherwise your malfatti will become rubbery instead of melting in your mouth.
  5. Prepare a lightly floured tray and start forming the malfatti by taking small portions of dough about the size of a walnut. You can use the 'quenelle' technique with two wet spoons or roll the dough between lightly floured palms to create irregular balls. Try to maintain a uniform size for all pieces to ensure simultaneous and perfect cooking in the pot. Chef's tip: keep your hands lightly floured or damp during this step to prevent the mixture from sticking excessively to your fingers.
  6. In a large non-stick frying pan, melt 60g of butter with 6 washed and dried fresh sage leaves over a medium-low heat. Let it sizzle gently for about 4-5 minutes until the butter stops hissing, starts to foam, and turns a light hazelnut colour with a toasted aroma. Be careful not to burn the sage, which should become crispy while maintaining its forest-green colour. Chef's tip: the so-called 'beurre noisette' (hazelnut butter) adds a nutty note that incredibly enhances the sweetness of the ricotta and the flavour of the cabbage.
  7. Bring a large pot of plenty of salted water to the boil and lower the heat slightly so the boil is steady but not too turbulent, to avoid breaking the dumplings. Gently drop the malfatti in a few at a time, avoiding crowding the pot so as not to drop the water temperature sharply. When they rise to the surface, usually after 2-3 minutes, let them cook for exactly another 30 seconds to ensure the heat reaches the centre of the malfatto evenly. Chef's tip: use a slotted spoon to move them as little as possible, as their structure is extremely delicate until they are fully cooked.
  8. Lift the cooked malfatti out with the slotted spoon and transfer them directly into the pan with the sage butter, keeping the heat at a minimum. Move the pan with a gentle circular motion to coat every single dumpling with the golden dressing, adding a tablespoon of cooking water if necessary to create a light emulsion. Serve the hot plates immediately, garnishing with the crispy sage leaves and a final dusting of Parmigiano Reggiano. Chef's tip: never use a spoon to stir the malfatti in the pan, but 'toss' them with a flick of the wrist to preserve their shape and softness.

Tips

  • Drain the ricotta in a sieve for at least an hour to prevent the dough from being too wet.
  • Handle the dough as little as possible to keep the dumplings light and tender after cooking.
  • The malfatti are ready as soon as they float to the surface, just like traditional potato gnocchi.

Variations

  • For a crunchy element, top the finished dish with some toasted crushed walnuts before serving.
  • Enhance the dressing by using grated salted ricotta instead of Parmigiano for a sharper finish.
