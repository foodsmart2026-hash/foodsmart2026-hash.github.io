Penne Rigate with Potatoes, Turmeric and Pancetta

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

An energetic first course playing on yellow tones. The potatoes partially melt to create a thick sauce, while the pancetta adds a bold smoky note.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🧒 Kid-friendly🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🇮🇹 Italian

A colorful and savory dish where the sweetness of potatoes meets the bold saltiness of smoked pancetta. Turmeric dyes the pasta a brilliant yellow, making this a visually striking and spicy course.

Ingredients

  • Penne Rigate (ridged penne pasta)320 g
  • Yellow-fleshed potatoes300 g
  • Diced smoked pancetta (Italian cured pork belly)60 g
  • Red onion1 pcs
  • Turmeric powder5 g
  • Fresh parsley1 ciuffo

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Start by peeling 300g of yellow potatoes and cutting them into very small cubes, about 1 cm each, aiming for maximum consistency. Finely chop one red onion and keep the ingredients separate on your workspace. This small size is crucial as it allows the potatoes to cook quickly and begin to break down, releasing the starch needed for the final creaminess. The result should be a regular dice that ensures even cooking in just a few minutes. Chef's Tip: Keep the potatoes submerged in cold water until needed to prevent oxidation from turning them brown.
  2. Place a large non-stick frying pan over medium heat and add 60g of smoked bacon cubes without adding any vegetable or animal fats. Sauté the bacon for about 4-5 minutes, stirring often with a wooden spoon until it becomes well-browned and crispy. Once you achieve an amber colour and a translucent texture, remove the cubes with a slotted spoon and set them aside in a small bowl. This step extracts all the bacon's flavour and keeps it crunchy for finishing the dish. Chef's Tip: Always start with a cold pan to allow the bacon fat to render gradually without burning the meat proteins.
  3. In the same pan, using the flavourful fat released by the bacon, add the chopped red onion and the well-drained potato cubes. Sauté over medium-low heat for about 3 minutes, stirring constantly to coat the potatoes evenly. You want the onion to become translucent and the potatoes slightly glossy, without letting them take on too much colour. This aromatic base will be the heart of your sauce's flavour. Chef's Tip: If the onion starts to brown too quickly, add a tablespoon of hot water to lower the pan's temperature.
  4. Pour a glass of water (about 200 ml) into the pan and add 5g of turmeric powder, distributing it evenly with a spoon. Cover with a lid and continue cooking over low heat for about 10-12 minutes, or until the potatoes are tender. By the end, the potatoes should be soft enough to start breaking apart as soon as you touch them with a fork, creating a sort of yellow emulsion. The aroma will be intense and the colour a vibrant ochre yellow. Chef's Tip: Avoid over-salting the potatoes at this stage, as the bacon and the pasta cooking water will already provide the necessary saltiness.
  5. While the sauce is cooking, bring a large pot with about 3 litres of water to the boil and add coarse salt (approx. 30g). Add 320g of Penne Rigate and cook them according to the package instructions, but be sure to drain them at least 2 minutes before the indicated time. The pasta must be distinctly 'al dente' to withstand the subsequent tossing phase in the pan. Always reserve a couple of ladles of cooking water before draining the pasta. Chef's Tip: Choose a high-quality bronze-die pasta, as its rough surface will better hold the potato and turmeric cream.
  6. Drain the penne using a slotted spoon or a colander and transfer them directly into the pan with the potato and turmeric sauce. Turn the heat up to a brisk medium-high and begin stirring vigorously to incorporate the pasta into the creamy base. During this phase, the pasta will absorb the flavours and the golden colour of the sauce. You will see the pasta starch begin to bond with the potato starch, creating a velvety consistency. Chef's Tip: Do not shake the pan too forcefully to avoid breaking too many of the potato cubes that remained whole.
  7. Add a ladle of the reserved pasta cooking water and continue to toss everything over high heat for another minute. The hot water and residual starch will create a true bright yellow cream that coats every single penne, eliminating any dryness. The final consistency should be fluid and coating—not watery, but not too thick or sticky either. This is the secret to a restaurant-quality first course. Chef's Tip: Add the water a little at a time; you might not need the whole ladle to reach perfect creaminess.
  8. Turn off the heat and add the reserved crispy bacon and a handful of finely chopped fresh parsley to the pasta. Toss one last time to distribute the ingredients harmoniously and serve immediately in individual bowls. The contrast between the creaminess of the potatoes, the salty crunch of the bacon, and the freshness of the parsley makes the dish balanced and inviting. The final result is a colourful, fragrant, and technically perfect pasta dish. Chef's Tip: Do not add the parsley while the heat is still on to prevent the heat from dulling its bright colour and delicate aroma.

Tips

  • Dice the potatoes into very small cubes to ensure they cook through perfectly within the timeframe.
  • Sear the pancetta over medium heat without oil to let the fat render and flavor the potatoes.
  • Drain the pasta slightly al dente to finish tossing it in the pan with the sauce and cooking water.

Variations

  • Replace the red onion with shallots for a more delicate and refined base flavor.
  • Use unsmoked pancetta instead of the smoked variety if you prefer a gentler taste.
