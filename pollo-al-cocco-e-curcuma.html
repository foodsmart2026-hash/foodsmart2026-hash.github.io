Coconut and Turmeric Chicken

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 20 min · Medium

A main course inspired by Southeast Asian flavours, creamy and delicate. Using canned coconut milk ensures a velvety consistency and an enveloping taste.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⚖️ Good value for money🌾 Gluten-free🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🌍 International

A delicate and exotic main course combining tender chicken pieces with the velvety sweetness of coconut milk. Turmeric and fresh ginger provide a spicy note and a vibrant color that invites you to eat.

Ingredients

  • Chicken breast600 g
  • Canned coconut milk400 ml
  • Courgettes2 pcs
  • Turmeric powder15 g
  • Fresh ginger20 g
  • Vegetable oil20 ml
  • Saltq.b.

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Start by cutting the 600g of chicken breast into uniform 3cm chunks, ensuring they are the same size for even cooking. If you prefer a thicker sauce, you can lightly coat them in flour, shaking off any excess to avoid clumps. Arrange the pieces on a clean cutting board without overlapping, making sure the meat is at room temperature. The final result should be meat ready to be seared quickly without releasing too much moisture. My chef's tip is to pat the chicken dry with paper towels before cutting to encourage a perfect Maillard reaction.
  2. Take the 2 courgettes, wash them thoroughly, and trim the ends, then slice them lengthways and cut into very thin half-moons about 2-3 millimetres thick. Peel the 20g of fresh ginger using the back of a teaspoon to remove only the skin and grate it finely into a small bowl. Keep these ingredients ready near the hob to speed up the next stages of preparation. The courgettes must be uniform to cook in moments without turning mushy. The chef suggests not grating the ginger too far in advance to preserve its volatile essential oils and citrusy aroma.
  3. In a large non-stick frying pan or wok, pour in the 20ml of vegetable oil and heat over a high flame until you see a slight shimmer on the surface. Carefully place the chicken chunks in and let them sear for about 5 minutes without moving them too much initially to allow a proper seal. Use kitchen tongs to turn them as soon as an inviting golden crust has formed on each side of the meat. The chicken should be well-sealed on the outside, locking in the juices, even if the centre is still raw. Do not overcrowd the pan: if necessary, cook in two batches to prevent the temperature from dropping sharply.
  4. Slightly lower the heat to a medium intensity and add the courgette half-moons and the grated fresh ginger to the pan with the chicken. Sauté everything energetically for 3 minutes, using a wooden spoon or spatula to evenly distribute the ginger among the chicken pieces. The courgettes should just soften while maintaining a bright green colour and a pleasantly crunchy texture to the bite. You will smell a pungent and refreshing aroma released by the ginger as it hits the heat of the pan. The secret is to maintain a constant heat to flash-fry the vegetables instead of stewing them in their own juices.
  5. Uniformly sprinkle the 15g of turmeric powder over the chicken and courgette mixture while the pan is still hot. Stir well with circular motions for about a minute, letting the heat lightly toast the spice and release all its earthy notes. You will notice the chicken and vegetables immediately take on a very intense ochre-yellow colour and an almost velvety appearance. Ensure every piece of meat is perfectly coated in the golden powder for an even visual result. Be careful not to burn the spice: one minute is enough to activate the turmeric pigments without risking a bitter taste.
  6. Pour the 400ml of canned coconut milk over the ingredients, ensuring you have shaken the tin well before opening to combine the fatty solids and the liquid part. Use a spatula to gently scrape the bottom of the pan and recover the caramelised cooking juices (deglazing), incorporating them into the sauce. Stir thoroughly until the liquid transforms into a vibrant, homogeneous sunny-yellow cream. The aroma of the dish will change instantly, becoming exotic, sweet, and comforting. I recommend using full-fat coconut milk to achieve that velvety creaminess typical of Oriental curries.
  7. Reduce the heat to medium-low and let everything simmer gently for about 10 minutes without covering the pan, allowing the steam to escape. The coconut milk should slowly reduce from its initial volume, thickening until it creates a rich sauce that completely coats the ingredients. Check the chicken is cooked by piercing a chunk with a fork: it should be tender and easy to cut, yet still juicy. The correct sauce consistency is reached when it begins to 'coat' the back of a spoon without running off too quickly. Remember to stir occasionally to prevent the sauce from sticking to the bottom or the fats from separating.
  8. Turn off the heat and season with salt according to your personal taste, stirring one last time to distribute the seasoning evenly. Let the dish rest in the pan for a couple of minutes before serving, allowing the flavours to balance and the temperature to become ideal for the palate. The finished dish features golden, tender chunks immersed in a thick, fragrant cream, contrasted by the green of the courgettes. Serve in wide bowls, perhaps accompanied by steamed basmati rice to soak up every drop of sauce. A touch of freshly cracked black pepper will not only enhance the flavour but also help activate the nutritional benefits of the turmeric.

Tips

  • Sear the chicken well over high heat before adding liquids to seal in the juices.
  • Use canned coconut milk rather than a breakfast beverage to achieve the correct sauce density.
  • Grate the fresh ginger just before use to release its full aromatic potential.

Variations

  • You can replace zucchinis with fresh green beans for a crunchier texture.
  • If you prefer a more intense flavor, add a pinch of dried chili flakes along with the turmeric.
