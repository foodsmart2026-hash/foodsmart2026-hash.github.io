Buckwheat Soup with Mushrooms and Spinach

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

A restorative and naturally gluten-free soup. The intensity of button mushrooms meets the earthiness of buckwheat for a comforting evening meal.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🌱 Vegan🧒 Kid-friendly🌾 Gluten-free🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free

A rustic and comforting soup, perfect for cold days. Whole buckwheat groats provide a pleasantly firm texture that pairs beautifully with soft mushrooms and fresh spinach.

Ingredients

  • Buckwheat groats200 g
  • Button mushrooms400 g
  • Fresh or frozen spinach200 g
  • Leek1 pcs
  • Vegetable stock1.5 l
  • Dried thyme1 cucchiaino
  • Extra virgin olive oil20 ml

Allergens

Celery

Method

  1. Begin by thoroughly rinsing 200g of buckwheat groats in a fine-mesh sieve under cold running water until the water runs completely clear. This process is essential to remove impurities and excess surface starch which could make the soup overly cloudy or gluey. Once rinsed, let it drain for a moment. The goal is to have clean, separate grains ready for toasting. Chef's tip: do not skip this step, or the final taste might be unpleasantly earthy.
  2. Prepare the leek by removing the dark green outer leaves and the roots, then slice it into very thin rounds using a sharp knife. In a large, heavy-based pot, heat 20ml of extra virgin olive oil over medium-low heat and add the sliced leek. Let it sweat gently for about 5-7 minutes, stirring frequently with a wooden spoon so it becomes translucent and soft without browning. You will achieve a sweet, aromatic base that forms the foundation of the dish. Chef's tip: if the leek starts to colour too quickly, add a tablespoon of water to help it steam evenly.
  3. While the leek is softening, clean 400g of button mushrooms by wiping away any dirt with a damp cloth and slice them into uniform 5mm pieces. Increase the heat to medium-high and add the mushrooms to the leek base, sautéing them uncovered for about 5 minutes. The mushrooms will first release their moisture and then begin to golden slightly. The aroma will intensify and the texture will become firmer. Chef's tip: avoid washing mushrooms under the tap, as they act like sponges and will lose their texture and flavour during cooking.
  4. Add the 200g of pre-rinsed buckwheat to the pot with the mushrooms and leeks. Toast the grains over medium heat for about 2 minutes, stirring constantly to ensure every grain is coated in the oil and heat. This technique seals the surface of the buckwheat, ensuring it remains intact and 'al dente' even after boiling. You will notice a pleasant, nutty toasted aroma filling the kitchen. Chef's tip: toasting is the secret to preventing the soup from becoming a mushy porridge; watch the colour change of the grains closely.
  5. Now pour in 1.5 litres of vegetable broth, ensuring it is already boiling so as not to interrupt the cooking process of the buckwheat. Add 1 teaspoon of dried thyme, which provides an earthy, aromatic note typical of mountain cuisine. Lower the heat, partially cover the pot, and let it simmer gently for approximately 15 minutes. The liquid should barely bubble, allowing the grain to absorb flavours without breaking apart. Chef's tip: use a high-quality or homemade vegetable stock to guarantee an authentic depth of flavour to the soup.
  6. With about 5 minutes of cooking time remaining, it is time to add 200g of spinach. If using fresh spinach, ensure it is well washed; if using frozen, you can add it directly. Stir gently: the residual heat of the soup will wilt the leaves in moments, maintaining a bright green colour. The soup will start to look rich and vibrant. Chef's tip: do not overcook the spinach to preserve both its vivid colour and heat-sensitive nutrients.
  7. Taste the soup and adjust the seasoning with salt and freshly ground black pepper according to your preference. Check the consistency of the buckwheat: it should be tender to the bite but still retain a slight firm core, similar to a well-cooked risotto. If the soup appears too thick, you can add a ladle of hot broth to thin it out. Turn off the heat as soon as you reach the desired balance between whole grains and savoury broth. Chef's tip: remember that buckwheat will continue to absorb liquid even after the heat is off, so leave it slightly more liquid than your desired serving consistency.
  8. Turn off the hob, cover the pot completely with a lid, and let the soup rest for at least 2 minutes before serving. This brief resting period allows the flavours of the thyme, mushrooms, and buckwheat to harmonise perfectly and the temperature to stabilise. Serve the soup in pre-warmed bowls, finishing each portion with a light drizzle of raw extra virgin olive oil and, if you enjoy a kick, a pinch of dried chilli flakes. The result is a comforting, balanced, and nutritious meal. Chef's tip: serving the soup while it is scalding hot prevents the palate from perceiving the delicate nuances of the leek and thyme.

Tips

  • Rinse the buckwheat thoroughly under running water to remove any surface debris.
  • Slice the mushrooms into uniform pieces to ensure they cook evenly throughout the dish.
  • If using frozen spinach, squeeze out excess water before adding them to maintain the broth's consistency.

Variations

  • Replace the button mushrooms with a wild mushroom mix for a deeper, more earthy flavor.
  • Add a sprinkle of freshly cracked black pepper to enhance the aromatic notes of the thyme.
