Smooth Cannellini Bean Cream with Sage and Good Olive Oil

4 servings · Prep 5 min · Cooking 20 min · Easy

A budget-friendly and quick alternative to meat, rich in plant-based proteins and iron. The smooth and velvety texture makes it suitable even for the fussiest children.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⬇️ Big saving🌱 Vegan🧒 Kid-friendly🌾 Gluten-free🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🇮🇹 Italian

A velvety and enveloping cream that enhances the clean flavor of cannellini beans. Sage infuses a persistent herbaceous aroma, making this dish a classic of Italian home cooking.

Ingredients

  • Cooked cannellini beans (well rinsed)300 g
  • Potatoes100 g
  • Sage1 leaf
  • Water or unsalted vegetable stock150 ml
  • Extra virgin olive oil20 ml

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Start by preparing the potato (100 g): peel it carefully and cut it into uniform cubes of about one centimetre to ensure even cooking. Place the cubes in a small saucepan and cover them with 150 ml of water or salt-free vegetable stock, bringing it to a boil over medium heat. Allow it to cook for about 12 minutes until the potato is extremely tender when pierced with a fork. The result should be a soft starchy base that will help give body to the soup. Chef's Tip: do not cut the pieces too large, otherwise the outside will fall apart before the core is cooked, compromising the final silkiness.
  2. While the potato is cooking, take the 300 g of pre-cooked cannellini beans and pour them into a fine-mesh sieve. Rinse them under fresh running water for at least two or three minutes, moving them gently with your hands to remove any residue from the canning liquid and excess industrial salt. This step is essential to achieve a clean and natural flavour from the legumes. You will see the typical foam from the brine disappear, leaving the beans shiny and ready. Chef's Tip: use cold water to avoid breaking the skins of the beans while washing.
  3. When the potato is ready and the liquid has slightly reduced, add the well-rinsed beans and the whole sage leaf to the pot. Keep the heat low to prevent the bottom from sticking, as the potato starches tend to settle. Stir gently with a wooden spoon so that the flavours begin to meld harmoniously. The aim is to heat the beans evenly while the sage releases its fragrant essential oils. Chef's Tip: insert the sage leaf whole rather than chopped so that you can easily locate and remove it later.
  4. Continue cooking over a low heat for about 5-7 minutes, maintaining a gentle simmer. Partially cover with a lid to prevent too much stock from evaporating, while allowing the sage to infuse its signature aroma. During this time, the beans will absorb the moisture and become even more tender. You will notice the scent of the sage becoming persistent and enveloping, a sign that the infusion is proceeding correctly. Chef's Tip: if you see the liquid drying out too quickly, add a tablespoon of hot water to maintain the necessary moisture.
  5. Before moving to the next stage, turn off the heat and locate the sage leaf inside the pot. Remove it with tongs or a spoon, ensuring no fragments are left in the mixture. This step is crucial for achieving a cream with a uniform colour and balanced taste, without bitter notes or fibrous textures. The preparation will now look like a thick, fragrant soup. Chef's Tip: lightly squeeze the leaf against the side of the pot before removing it to recover all the extracted aroma.
  6. Take an immersion blender and submerge it completely into the mixture in the pot, tilting it slightly if necessary. Blend at maximum speed with vertical movements to incorporate air and make the cream frothy and velvety. Continue for at least a couple of minutes until the bean skins and potato pieces have completely disappeared. The result must be a very fine, smooth puree with a homogeneous creamy white colour. Chef's Tip: for a restaurant-quality velouté, pass the mixture through a fine-mesh sieve (chinoise) after blending.
  7. Assess the density of your cream: if it appears too thick when lifted with a spoon, add a splash of hot water or stock. Stir vigorously with the heat off to incorporate the added liquid and restore the silkiness. The ideal consistency should coat the spoon without running off like a broth, but without being as thick as a mash. You should have a fluid cream that emits a delicate scent of legumes and sage. Chef's Tip: do not add cold water at this stage to avoid breaking the structure of the cream and altering the serving temperature.
  8. Divide the cream into four pre-warmed shallow bowls to retain the heat longer. Complete each portion by drizzling over the total 20 ml of raw extra virgin olive oil (about one generous teaspoon per plate). The oil should not just be a seasoning, but an integral part of the dish: stir it slightly with a circular motion to create a final shiny and fragrant emulsion. Serve immediately to fully enjoy the fragrance of the high-quality oil and sage. Chef's Tip: a pinch of freshly cracked black pepper will further enhance the sweetness of the cannellini beans.

Tips

  • Thoroughly rinse canned beans to remove the canning liquid and reduce saltiness.
  • For an extra smooth cream, pass the mixture through a fine-mesh sieve after blending.
  • Serve with a drizzle of high-quality raw olive oil to enhance the herbal notes of the sage.

Variations

  • Add a touch of tomato paste during cooking to provide color and depth.
  • Replace the potato with cooked pumpkin for a sweeter taste and dense texture.
