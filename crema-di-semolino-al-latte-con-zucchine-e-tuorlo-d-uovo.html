Milky Semolina Cream with Courgettes and Egg Yolk

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 10 min · Easy

A childhood classic, nutritious and balanced. Semolina cooked in milk is velvety, and courgettes add freshness.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⬇️ Big saving🥕 Vegetarian

A delicate and nutritious preparation characterized by the smoothness of semolina cooked in milk and enriched with egg yolk. Minced zucchinis add freshness and color to this velvety cream.

Ingredients

  • Wheat semolina80 g
  • Whole milk250 ml
  • Water250 ml
  • Medium light-coloured courgettes2 pc
  • Fresh egg yolks2 pc
  • Extra virgin olive oil10 ml

Allergens

Gluten, Milk, Eggs

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the vegetables: thoroughly wash the two light zucchini and trim the ends, then grate them using the coarse side of a box grater to obtain uniform shreds. This process takes about 3 minutes and ensures a texture that will melt perfectly into the final cream. The result should be moist zucchini pulp, ready to release its natural sweetness during cooking. Chef's tip: if the zucchini are particularly watery, pat them lightly with kitchen paper to avoid upsetting the liquid balance of the recipe.
  2. Take a heavy-bottomed saucepan and pour in 250ml of whole milk and 250ml of water, then immediately add the grated zucchini. Place the pan over medium heat and bring the mixture almost to the boil, watching closely so the milk doesn't foam over. You should see small bubbles rising to the surface and smell the delicate aroma of warm milk combined with the fresh vegetables. Chef's tip: use a stainless steel or high-quality non-stick saucepan to prevent the milk from scorching on the bottom.
  3. As soon as the liquid reaches a simmer, turn the heat down to the lowest setting and rain in the 80g of semolina very slowly, just as you would with polenta. While pouring with one hand, use the other to whisk vigorously with a hand whisk, creating a constant vortex in the liquid. This technique is vital to prevent lumps, ensuring a smooth and velvety consistency from the start. Chef's tip: pour the semolina through your fingers or use a sieve for precise control over the flow.
  4. Continue cooking over a very low heat for 5 to 7 minutes, keeping the mixture moving with a whisk or wooden spoon. You will notice the cream gradually thickening, incorporating the zucchini shreds which will become extremely tender. The result should be a thick yet fluid cream, with a consistency similar to a soft custard. Chef's tip: if the cream thickens too rapidly, move the pan away from the heat for a few seconds while continuing to stir.
  5. Once the desired density is reached, turn off the heat and move the pan to a cool surface, letting the cream sit for about a minute. This brief rest is essential to slightly lower the internal temperature before adding the eggs. The cream should look glossy and still be very hot, emitting a gentle steam. Chef's tip: do not let it rest for too long, otherwise it will set too firmly, making it difficult to incorporate the remaining ingredients.
  6. Prepare the two fresh egg yolks and add them to the warm cream one at a time, immediately whisking with great energy. The residual heat of the semolina (approx. 65-70°C) is enough to cook the yolks safely, making them creamy without scrambling them. You will notice the cream instantly takes on a beautiful pale yellow colour and a silky, rich texture. Chef's tip: lightly beat the yolks in a small cup before adding them to ensure an immediate, streak-free blend.
  7. Finish the seasoning by drizzling in the 10ml of extra virgin olive oil while continuing to stir gently to emulsify the fat into the semolina base. The oil should not float on the surface but should vanish into the cream, giving it a natural sheen and a fruity aroma. The final result should be a homogeneous porridge, free of lumps and visually appealing thanks to the contrast between the yellow egg and light green zucchini. Chef's tip: use a mild extra virgin olive oil so as not to overwhelm the delicate flavours of the milk and zucchini.
  8. Divide the cream into four individual bowls and let it rest until it reaches room temperature, which is ideal for fully enjoying the flavours. If the cream becomes too firm for your liking as it cools, you can adjust the density by adding a tablespoon of warm milk and stirring well. The perfect consistency should allow a spoon to sink in without resistance while still holding its shape. Chef's tip: cover the bowls with cling film touching the surface if not serving immediately to prevent a skin from forming.

Tips

  • Rain the semolina into the hot milk while stirring constantly to prevent lumps from forming.
  • Add the egg yolks only when the cream is lukewarm to avoid over-curdling them.
  • Consume the cream immediately after preparation as it tends to firm up as it cools.

Variations

  • Replace zucchinis with steamed pumpkin pulp for a sweeter version.
  • You can flavor the cream with a sprinkle of Parmigiano Reggiano before serving.
