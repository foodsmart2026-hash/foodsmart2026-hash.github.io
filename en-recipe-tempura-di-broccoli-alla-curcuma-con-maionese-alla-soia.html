Turmeric broccoli tempura with soy mayonnaise

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 10 min · Medium

An incredibly light and golden fry thanks to the turmeric, transforming the broccoli into a crispy, Oriental-inspired side dish.

🌿 Seasonal recipe🥕 Vegetarian🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🌍 International

A light and crispy fry with an oriental touch, characterized by the golden hue of turmeric. The accompanying soy mayonnaise creates a velvety contrast that balances the saltiness of the tempura florets.

Ingredients

  • Large broccoli head1
  • Rice flour150g
  • Iced sparkling water200ml
  • Turmeric powder1 tsp
  • Groundnut oil500ml
  • Vegan mayonnaise100g
  • Soy sauce1 tbsp
  • Maldon saltto taste

Allergens

Peanuts, Soy
May contain traces of: Gluten.

Method

  1. Take the large broccoli and use a sharp knife to cut it into medium-sized florets, ensuring you leave a small piece of the stalk attached to each one. Rinse the florets under cold running water and then dry them meticulously by patting them with a clean tea towel or kitchen paper. This step is essential because any residual water would cause dangerous splashes in the hot oil and make the batter soggy. You will have clean, perfectly dry florets ready to be coated. Chef's Tip: If you have time, leave the florets to air-dry for 15 minutes after patting them to remove even the slightest trace of moisture.
  2. In a small bowl, combine 100g of vegan mayonnaise with 1 tablespoon of soy sauce, stirring with a teaspoon until you achieve a smooth and consistent mixture. Work the sauce with gentle circular motions so as not to deflate the creamy texture of the mayonnaise. This process takes less than a minute, and the final sauce should appear a light hazelnut colour with an intense umami aroma. Cover the bowl with cling film and place it in the fridge until serving time to keep it chilled. Chef's Tip: Add the soy sauce gradually to calibrate the saltiness according to your personal taste.
  3. Prepare two bowls, one slightly larger than the other, and fill the larger one with ice cubes and a splash of cold water. Place the smaller bowl on top of the ice to create a freezing work environment that will keep the batter at the optimal temperature. This thermal shock between the freezing batter and the heat of the oil is the secret to achieving an airy and crispy tempura. You should feel the inner bowl become ice-cold to the touch within moments. Chef's Tip: Use a metal bowl for the batter, as it conducts the cold much better than plastic or ceramic.
  4. Inside the chilled bowl, carefully sift 150g of rice flour together with 1 teaspoon of turmeric powder using a fine-mesh sieve. Move the sieve with small side taps to eliminate any lumps and evenly distribute the vibrant colour of the spice. The flour should appear as a light, very fine, pale yellow cloud at the bottom of the bowl. This dry mix forms the light base that will guarantee the typical glassy texture of Japanese frying. Chef's Tip: Rice flour is naturally gluten-free, which makes your tempura much lighter and crispier than one made with wheat flour.
  5. Pour 200ml of ice-cold sparkling water gradually over the flour and turmeric mixture, stirring quickly with a pair of chopsticks or a fork. Do not overwork the batter and absolutely do not try to remove the small lumps of flour, which are fundamental for the final texture. Mix for no more than 15-20 seconds until you get a fluid but not excessively runny batter. The result should be a yellow mixture dotted with small air bubbles and flour lumps. Chef's Tip: If you overmix, the batter will become heavy and rubbery instead of remaining light and crumbly.
  6. Pour 500ml of peanut oil into a high-sided saucepan and heat it over medium heat until it reaches a temperature of 170-180°C. Use a kitchen thermometer to monitor the heat, as oil that is too cold would be absorbed by the vegetables, making them greasy. If you don't have a thermometer, dip a drop of batter into the oil: if it rises immediately while sizzling, the temperature is correct. The oil should appear clear and ready to receive the ingredients without smoking. Chef's Tip: Peanut oil has a high smoke point, making it ideal for safe frying with a neutral flavour.
  7. Dip a few broccoli florets at a time into the cold batter, ensuring they are entirely covered, then shake them gently to remove any excess batter. Carefully drop them into the hot oil, sliding them from the edge of the pan to avoid dangerous splashes towards you. Fry the broccoli for about 2-3 minutes, turning them only once with a slotted spoon or chopsticks for even cooking. The tempura should feel rigid to the touch and maintain a bright yellow colour from the turmeric. Chef's Tip: Do not overcrowd the pan, otherwise the oil temperature will drop sharply, compromising the crispness.
  8. Remove the broccoli from the oil with a slotted spoon and place them immediately on a tray lined with kitchen paper or greaseproof paper. Let them rest for a few seconds so that the excess oil is drained, keeping the crust dry and light. While they are still very hot, sprinkle them with a pinch of Maldon sea salt flakes to enhance the flavours and add a mineral note. You should hear a sharp, crispy sound when you move the broccoli on the paper. Chef's Tip: Never cover freshly fried tempura, as the trapped steam would soften the batter in seconds.
  9. Arrange the very hot broccoli florets on a serving platter, creating a pyramid composition to encourage air circulation. Accompany the tempura with the small bowl of soy mayonnaise that you prepared earlier and kept chilled. The visual contrast between the golden yellow of the broccoli and the creamy colour of the sauce will make the dish inviting and professional. Serve immediately to best enjoy the contrast between the crumbly exterior and the tender heart of the broccoli. Chef's Tip: For an authentic experience, dip only the tip of the broccoli into the sauce so as not to overpower the delicate flavour of the turmeric.

Tips

  • Keep the sparkling water and batter extremely cold to achieve an effective thermal shock during frying.
  • Do not overmix the batter; small lumps will help make the fry airier and crispier.
  • Fry a few pieces at a time to maintain the oil temperature and ensure a dry result.

Variations

  • Add black sesame seeds to the batter for a speckled visual effect and a toasted flavor.
  • Sprinkle with chili powder if you prefer a slightly spicy version.
