Wholemeal Carrot and Almond Muffins

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 22 min · Medium

Moist and nutritious muffins that hide a good dose of vegetables, perfect for children's snacks. Wholemeal flour and almonds guarantee slow-release energy.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🥕 Vegetarian🧒 Kid-friendly🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free

These rustic muffins combine the natural sweetness of carrots with the aromatic touch of almonds in a soft, fragrant texture. Perfect for a home-style breakfast, they offer a balanced flavor and a moist crumb thanks to the whole wheat flour.

Ingredients

  • Peeled carrots250 g
  • Wholemeal flour200 g
  • Light brown sugar90 g
  • Medium eggs2 pcs
  • Sunflower oil70 ml
  • Ground almonds50 g
  • Baking powder12 g

Allergens

Eggs, Gluten, Tree nuts

Method

  1. Begin by preparing 250g of carrots: after peeling them, grate them very finely using the small holes of a grater. Place the grated carrots inside a clean tea towel and squeeze firmly to remove the excess vegetable water, which could make the batter too wet. This process takes about 5 minutes and the final result should be dry and grainy carrot pulp. Chef's tip: do not skip this step, as residual moisture from the carrots is the main reason muffins often remain raw or soggy in the middle.
  2. Take a large bowl and crack 2 medium eggs, immediately adding 90g of brown sugar. Use an electric whisk or a stand mixer and work the mixture at maximum speed for at least 5-7 minutes until it has become pale and fluffy. You will notice the volume double and the sugar completely dissolve, creating an aerated base essential for fluffiness. Chef's tip: ensure the eggs are at room temperature to encourage the formation of a stable and voluminous mass.
  3. Pour 70ml of sunflower oil in a thin stream into the egg mixture, keeping the whisks moving but at a reduced speed. The goal is to create a perfect emulsion without deflating the air previously incorporated, proceeding with a fluid and constant movement. After about a minute, you will have a homogeneous, shiny, and slightly thick liquid. Chef's tip: pour the oil down the side of the bowl rather than directly into the centre to avoid weighing down the fluffy structure of the batter too much.
  4. Add the previously squeezed carrots to the liquid mass, using a silicone spatula (bowl scraper) instead of electric whisks. Distribute the 250g of carrots with folding motions from bottom to top, ensuring that no clumps of vegetable are left in one spot. This step requires gentleness and about 2 minutes of time to achieve a uniform distribution in colour and texture. Chef's tip: if you want a more intense aroma, you can add some grated organic orange zest at this stage, which pairs divinely with carrots.
  5. In a separate bowl, sift 200g of whole wheat flour together with 12g of baking powder and join 50g of ground almonds. Mix the dry ingredients with a spoon to make them homogeneous before inserting them into the liquid batter, thus avoiding the formation of baking powder lumps. You will get a fragrant and well-distributed dry mix. Chef's tip: sifting whole wheat flour is harder due to the bran, so use a coarse sieve or simply break up the largest lumps with a hand whisk.
  6. Incorporate the dry ingredients into the liquid mixture in two or three batches, mixing briefly with a spatula only until the flour disappears from sight. It is crucial not to overwork the batter after adding the whole wheat flour to avoid gluten development, which would make the muffins tough. The result should be a thick, full-bodied batter with the typical graininess of whole wheat flour and almonds. Chef's tip: stop as soon as you no longer see white traces of flour; a slightly lumpy batter is the secret to tall and fluffy muffins.
  7. Prepare a muffin tin with paper cases and transfer the batter using a spoon or an ice cream scoop. Fill each case about 3/4 full, leaving enough space for the leavening agent to work during baking without causing the cake to overflow. This operation requires precision to ensure that all muffins bake evenly. Chef's tip: if the batter is very thick, lightly level the surface with the back of a wet teaspoon to obtain a perfect and regular dome.
  8. Bake the muffins in the preheated oven at 180°C (160°C fan) for about 20-22 minutes, placing the tray on the middle shelf. Do not open the oven during the first 15 minutes so as not to compromise the rising; the surface should appear golden and slightly cracked. Perform the skewer test: inserted into the centre of the muffin, it must come out completely dry. Chef's tip: every oven is different, so if after 20 minutes the muffins are very dark but still moist inside, cover them with foil and continue baking for another 3-4 minutes.
  9. Once taken out of the oven, let the muffins rest inside the tin for about 5 minutes to allow the structure to stabilise with the residual heat. Afterwards, gently remove them and transfer them to a wire rack until completely cool. This step is essential to prevent water vapour from dampening the bottom of the muffin, making it chewy. Chef's tip: the flavour of the whole wheat flour and almonds intensifies after a few hours, so if you can resist, enjoy them lukewarm or cold to appreciate all the aromatic nuances.

Tips

  • Squeeze the grated carrots well to prevent the batter from becoming too watery.
  • Incorporate the dry ingredients gradually to maintain the soft structure of the mixture.
  • Use paper liners inside the muffin tin for easier removal after baking.

Variations

  • Replace the almond flour with chopped hazelnuts for a bolder, toasted flavor.
  • Add a pinch of cinnamon to the batter to further enrich the aromatic profile.
