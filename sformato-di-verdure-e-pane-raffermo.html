Vegetable and Stale Bread Bake

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 35 min · Medium

A traditional peasant-style starter that elevates leftover bread and seasonal vegetables into a soft and nutritious savoury cake. This recipe balances complex carbohydrates, egg proteins, and fibre, offering an economical and sustainable alternative to classic flans.

A rustic dish that transforms stale whole grain bread into a flavorful flan with zucchini and carrots. The addition of pre-cooked chickpeas provides a dense and pleasant texture, perfect for an informal dinner.

Ingredients

  • Stale wholemeal bread200 g
  • Medium courgettes300 g
  • Carrots150 g
  • Pre-cooked chickpeas (well rinsed)120 g
  • Medium eggs2 pcs
  • Semi-skimmed milk150 ml
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Garlic1 clove
  • Fine salt4 g
  • Pinch of nutmeg1 g

Allergens

Gluten, Eggs, Milk

Method

  1. Begin by cutting 200g of stale wholemeal bread into cubes of about 1cm and place them in a large bowl, drizzling them evenly with 150ml of semi-skimmed milk. Stir well with a spoon to ensure all the bread is moistened and let it rest for about 15 minutes at room temperature to allow the fibres to hydrate. At the end, the bread should be soft to the touch but not excessively mushy, having absorbed almost all the liquid. My advice: if the bread is particularly hard, press it down slightly with your hands halfway through to encourage even absorption.
  2. Prepare 300g of courgettes and 150g of carrots by washing them thoroughly under running water, then grate them coarsely using the large holes of a box grater. This technique produces strands that will cook quickly while maintaining a pleasant, non-rubbery texture inside the flan. You should have a mix of uniformly shredded vegetables ready for the pan. Chef's tip: do not peel the carrots and courgettes, as the skin contains most of the essential fibre and nutrients.
  3. In a large non-stick frying pan, heat 15ml of extra virgin olive oil with a lightly crushed, unpeeled garlic clove over medium heat. When the garlic begins to sizzle and release its aroma, add the grated vegetables and sauté over a high heat for about 5-7 minutes. The vegetables should release their excess water and become tender but retain a bright colour. Remember to remove the garlic at the end of cooking to prevent its flavour from overpowering the delicacy of the dish.
  4. Take 120g of pre-cooked chickpeas, rinse them very well under running water to remove the canning liquid and drain perfectly, then crush them coarsely with a fork or a food mill. You are not looking for a smooth purée, but a rustic, grainy pulp that will act as a natural protein binder and give structure to the mixture. You will notice that this pulse base helps firm up the flan without the need for excessive eggs or flour. A little secret: leave a few chickpeas whole to add a different texture to the bite.
  5. Take the milk-softened bread and, if there is any unabsorbed liquid at the bottom of the bowl, squeeze it slightly with your hands before adding it to the sautéed vegetables and chickpea pulp. Mix the ingredients with a spatula or wooden spoon, ensuring the bread cubes integrate harmoniously with the vegetable and pulse mix. The mixture should appear moist, colourful, and evenly distributed. Tip: make sure the vegetables have cooled slightly before adding them, to prevent the excessive heat from ruining the texture of the bread.
  6. In a shallow dish, crack the 2 medium eggs and whisk them lightly with a fork together with 4g of salt and 1g of freshly grated nutmeg. Pour the beaten eggs directly over the bread and vegetable mix, blending everything carefully so that every ingredient is perfectly coated by the protein. The final result should be a soft, fragrant, and cohesive mixture ready for the baking dish. The tip: do not over-beat the eggs; simply breaking the yolks and mixing them with the whites is enough to provide the necessary binding power.
  7. Take a baking dish or four individual ramekins and grease them thoroughly with the remaining 15ml of extra virgin olive oil, using a pastry brush to cover the edges as well. Pour the mixture into the dish, levelling the surface with the back of a spoon and pressing down slightly to eliminate any air pockets. The flan should be about 3-4cm thick to ensure perfect internal cooking and a uniform crust. My advice: tap the dish lightly on the work surface to settle the mixture into the corners.
  8. Place the dish on the middle shelf of a preheated oven at 180°C (static) and bake for about 25-30 minutes. Monitor the flan until the surface appears golden brown and the edges start to pull away slightly from the sides of the dish, indicating the egg has set correctly. The aroma of toasted bread and spices will signal that the dish is nearly ready. Chef's tip: if the surface is still pale after 25 minutes, turn on the grill function for the last 2-3 minutes to achieve a perfect golden finish.
  9. Once removed from the oven, allow the flan to rest for 5-10 minutes before slicing and serving. This resting time is crucial for the internal structure to stabilise, making the slices firm and easy to portion without breaking apart. The texture will be ideal: crispy on the outside and soft and juicy on the inside. The secret: serve it warm to best appreciate the sweetness of the carrots and the aromatic bouquet of the nutmeg.

Tips

  • Thoroughly squeeze the excess milk from the bread to prevent the flan from becoming too moist inside.
  • Cut the vegetables into small pieces to cook them quickly in the pan before mixing with the bread.
  • Let the flan rest for ten minutes outside the oven to make slicing the portions easier.

Variations

  • Replace the zucchini with blanched broccoli for a version with a bolder flavor.
  • Increase the amount of nutmeg to enhance the aroma of the whole grain bread and milk.
