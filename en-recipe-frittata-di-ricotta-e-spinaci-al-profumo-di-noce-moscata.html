Baked Ricotta and Spinach Frittata with Nutmeg

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 30 min · Medium

A tall and fluffy baked frittata that resembles the filling of ravioli but served as a main course. The addition of ricotta makes it moist and perfect for eating cold as well.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🧒 Kid-friendly🌾 Gluten-free🇮🇹 Italian

A thick and fluffy frittata combining the creaminess of ricotta with tender spinach leaves. A touch of nutmeg provides an unmistakable aroma to this dish, which is perfect served either warm or cold.

Ingredients

  • Fresh eggs6 pcs
  • Cow's milk ricotta (a soft, creamy Italian whey cheese)250 g
  • Frozen leaf spinach300 g
  • Parmigiano Reggiano (an aged, hard Italian cow's milk cheese)40 g
  • Nutmeg1 pizzico
  • Extra virgin olive oil10 ml
  • Salt and Pepperq.b.

Allergens

Eggs, Milk

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the vegetable base: cook the 300g of frozen leaf spinach in a pan with a tiny amount of lightly salted water for about 5 minutes over medium heat. Once cooked, drain and let them cool slightly in a colander, then squeeze them extremely thoroughly using your hands or a clean tea towel to remove all residual liquid. You must obtain a very dry pulp to prevent the frittata from becoming watery during baking. Chef's tip: do not skip this step, as the residual moisture from the spinach is the main reason a frittata fails to set properly.
  2. Take the well-squeezed spinach and transfer it to a wooden chopping board, then coarsely chop it with a sharp knife. This step will allow you to distribute the flavour and the green colour evenly throughout the mixture, avoiding large clumps of vegetables. Aim for pieces about one centimetre in size to maintain a pleasant texture when eaten. Chef's tip: use a straight-edged knife instead of a blender to avoid turning the vegetables into a mush that would release too much water.
  3. In a large bowl, pour the 250g of cow's milk ricotta and work it vigorously with a hand whisk or a fork for about 2 minutes. You need to stir with circular motions until you obtain a perfectly smooth, silky cream free of lumps, which will be the structural foundation of your dish. The ricotta should be at room temperature to blend better with the other ingredients. Chef's tip: if the ricotta seems too grainy, pass it through a fine-mesh sieve before working it.
  4. Proceed by adding the 6 fresh eggs to the ricotta cream, incorporating them one at a time with the whisk. Mix with slow, rotating movements, ensuring that each egg is completely absorbed before adding the next one to create a stable emulsion. This process takes about 3-4 minutes and ensures an airy, light texture in the final dish. Chef's tip: crack the eggs into a separate small dish before adding them to avoid dropping shell fragments into the mix or ruining everything if an egg isn't perfectly fresh.
  5. Now it's time to season the mixture: add the 40g of grated Parmigiano Reggiano, a generous pinch of freshly grated nutmeg, and salt and pepper to taste. Finally, add the chopped spinach and stir everything with a spatula or wooden spoon until the batter is chromatically uniform and fragrant. Nutmeg is essential because it enhances the sweetness of the ricotta and the earthy flavour of the spinach. Chef's tip: don't overdo the salt, remember that the Parmigiano is already naturally savoury and its flavour will concentrate during cooking.
  6. Prepare the 22cm round baking tray by greasing it evenly with 10ml of extra virgin olive oil, using a pastry brush or a piece of kitchen paper. Pour the egg and ricotta mixture into the centre of the tray and level it gently with a spatula, moving from the centre towards the edges to maintain a constant thickness. Ensure the oil also covers the sides well to make it easier to remove the frittata once baked. Chef's tip: for a gourmet touch and perfect release, you can lightly dust the greased tray with a thin layer of breadcrumbs before pouring in the mixture.
  7. Preheat your conventional oven to 180°C and, once it reaches temperature, place the tray on the middle shelf for approximately 25 minutes. During baking, you will see the frittata puff up slightly and the edges start to pull away from the sides of the tray, while an enticing aroma of cheese and spices fills the kitchen. The steady heat of a conventional oven will ensure even browning without excessively drying out the core of the dish. Chef's tip: avoid opening the oven during the first 15 minutes of cooking to prevent compromising the structure of the frittata.
  8. Perform the toothpick test after 25 minutes: insert it into the centre of the frittata and, if it comes out dry and clean, it is perfectly cooked. The surface should appear a beautiful old gold colour and should feel springy and firm to the touch, a sign that the egg proteins have coagulated correctly. Turn off the oven and leave the tray inside with the door slightly ajar for a couple of minutes. Chef's tip: if the surface is still pale, turn on the grill function for the last 2 minutes, but watch closely so it doesn't burn.
  9. Remove the frittata from the oven and let it rest in the tray for at least 5 minutes before slicing. This short rest allows the fibres to stabilise and the flavours to meld perfectly, making the consistency ideal for cutting into clean wedges without breaking. Serve the frittata warm or at room temperature, accompanied by a fresh seasonal salad. Chef's tip: flip the frittata onto a flat serving plate to present the most even side and decorate it with a sprig of fresh marjoram if you want an extra touch of colour.

Tips

  • Squeeze the cooked spinach thoroughly to prevent excess moisture from affecting the frittata's texture.
  • Smooth out the ricotta with a fork before adding the eggs to ensure a perfectly lump-free mixture.
  • Choose the correct pan size to achieve the perfect thickness and ensure even baking throughout.

Variations

  • Add smoked provolone cubes for a gooey, melted center and a deeper, smoky flavor.
  • Swap spinach for Swiss chard for a variation with a slightly more earthy and robust taste.
