Baked Potato Gnocchi with Lentil Ragù

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 40 min · Medium

A hearty twist where lentils replace meat in the sauce, providing iron and fibre at a very low cost. A perfect comfort food for the cold months.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🥕 Vegetarian🧒 Kid-friendly🇮🇹 Italian

A rich first course combining soft potato gnocchi with the rustic texture of lentils. Baking them with cheese creates a delicious crust that envelops every single bite.

Ingredients

  • Potato gnocchi (fresh Italian potato dumplings)800 g
  • Dried lentils (small)150 g
  • Tomato passata (smooth sieved tomatoes)400 ml
  • Onion1 pcs
  • Carrot1 pcs
  • Scamorza or Provola cheese (semi-soft Italian cow's milk cheese)100 g

Allergens

Gluten, Milk

Method

  1. Begin by thoroughly rinsing 150g of dried lentils under cold running water to remove any impurities. Transfer them to a pot with plenty of unsalted cold water and bring to the boil, simmering over medium heat for about 20 minutes until tender but still holding their shape. Drain them well and set aside, ensuring you do not salt the water at this stage as it can cause the lentil skins to become tough.
  2. While the lentils are simmering, prepare the aromatic base by finely dicing one onion and one carrot with a sharp chef's knife. Pour a drizzle of extra virgin olive oil into a large saucepan and add the vegetables, letting them soften over low heat for 5-7 minutes until the onion is translucent and fragrant. This slow 'soffritto' is essential for building a deep flavour profile for your vegetable ragu without browning the veg too quickly.
  3. Now, stir 400ml of tomato passata and the pre-cooked lentils into the saucepan with the vegetables. Season with salt and pepper, stir well, and simmer over low heat for approximately 15 minutes with a lid partially on to allow the flavours to meld and the sauce to thicken. You are looking for a rich, chunky consistency similar to a traditional meat ragu that will coat the gnocchi perfectly.
  4. Bring a large pot of water to a rolling boil and, only once it reaches a vigorous boil, add a generous pinch of rock salt. Gently drop the 800g of fresh potato gnocchi into the water; they will initially sink to the bottom but will start rising within about 2 minutes. Have a slotted spoon ready to scoop them out as soon as they reach the surface to prevent them from overcooking and becoming mushy.
  5. Drain the gnocchi carefully as they float and transfer them directly into the saucepan containing the warm lentil ragu. Mix very gently using a wooden spoon or spatula, using folding motions from bottom to top so you don't break the delicate structure of the potato dumplings. Ensure every single gnocco is evenly coated in the red sauce and lentils, creating a harmonious and vibrant mixture.
  6. Select a suitable baking dish and transfer the gnocchi and lentil mixture into it, levelling the surface with the back of your spoon. Slice 100g of scamorza or provola cheese into very thin slices or grate it coarsely, then distribute it evenly over the top of the dish. An even distribution of cheese ensures a perfect golden crust across every serving when placed under the heat.
  7. Switch your oven grill to its highest setting (usually 220-250°C) and place the dish on the top rack for about 5 minutes. Keep a close eye on the oven through the glass: the cheese should melt completely and form a golden, bubbling crust with characteristic toasted spots. The intense heat from the grill creates a wonderful contrast between the soft gnocchi heart and the crispy, savoury topping.
  8. Carefully remove the dish from the oven using oven gloves and let it rest for 2-3 minutes before serving. This short resting period allows the sauce to settle and the gnocchi to firm up slightly, making it easier to serve and ensuring the flavours are balanced. Serve the gnocchi piping hot, making sure each plate gets a generous amount of lentil ragu and that delicious melted cheese crust.

Tips

  • Drain the gnocchi as soon as they float to the surface to prevent them from overcooking in the oven.
  • Sauté the onion and carrot carefully to build a deep aromatic base for the lentils.
  • Cut the scamorza into very small cubes to distribute the melting effect evenly throughout the tray.

Variations

  • Use smoked scamorza cheese to add a smoky note that enhances the flavor of the lentils.
  • Add a pinch of dried chili flakes to the sauce for a spicier and more vibrant version.
