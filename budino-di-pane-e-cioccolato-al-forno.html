Baked Bread and Chocolate Pudding

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 40 min · Medium

An anti-waste rescue recipe that transforms stale bread into a creamy, delicious dessert. Reminiscent of childhood flavours and costing just a few pence per serving.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🥕 Vegetarian🧒 Kid-friendly

This pudding is a classic 'zero waste' recipe, perfect for transforming stale bread into a delicious and comforting dessert. The combination of cocoa and dark chocolate chips creates a creamy and rich texture. It is best served warm on cooler days.

Ingredients

  • Stale bread (crusty loaf type)200 g
  • Whole milk500 ml
  • Medium eggs2 pcs
  • Caster sugar50 g
  • Unsweetened cocoa powder30 g
  • Dark chocolate chips20 g

Allergens

Gluten, Eggs, Milk

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the base of the dessert by cutting 200g of stale bread into cubes of approximately 2 centimetres using a serrated knife. Collect the cubes in a large, deep bowl, aiming for a uniform size to ensure even liquid absorption. Do not remove all the crust, as it will provide a pleasant variation in texture to the final pudding. Chef's tip: use a crusty, firm bread like a loaf that is a couple of days old for the ideal structure.
  2. Heat 500ml of whole milk in a saucepan over medium heat, bringing it almost to a simmer but without letting it reach a full boil. Pour the hot milk directly over the bread cubes and use a fork to gently press them down, ensuring every piece is submerged. Let the mixture rest for about 10-15 minutes so the bread softens and absorbs all the liquid. Chef's tip: if the bread is particularly dry, let it sit for a few extra minutes until it becomes a yielding mass.
  3. While the bread is softening, crack 2 medium eggs into a clean bowl and add 50g of caster sugar. Use a manual or electric whisk to beat the mixture vigorously for about 3-4 minutes until it appears pale, slightly frothy, and the sugar has completely dissolved. You should achieve a fluid cream that leaves a trail when lifted with the whisk. Chef's tip: use room-temperature eggs to encourage a more voluminous and stable foam.
  4. Take 30g of unsweetened cocoa powder and sift it directly over the egg and sugar mixture to prevent the formation of unpleasant lumps. Mix with gentle bottom-to-top movements using a spatula until the cocoa is perfectly incorporated and the colour is dark and uniform. Make sure to scrape the sides of the bowl well so as not to leave any dry powder residue. Chef's tip: sifting the cocoa is essential as it tends to clump into small spheres that are difficult to dissolve later.
  5. Go back to the bowl with the now-softened bread and pour the prepared egg, sugar, and cocoa mixture over it. Carefully combine the two preparations using a wooden spoon or spatula, lightly pressing the bread pieces to blend them into the dark cream. The result should be a thick, moist, and homogeneous mass where no white bread areas are visible. Chef's tip: do not blend the mixture; the beauty of this rustic pudding lies in its irregular texture.
  6. Now add 20g of dark chocolate chips to the batter, distributing them evenly with one final quick stir. Take a medium-sized baking dish and lightly butter the entire inner surface to make it easier to remove the portions. Pour the mixture into the dish and level the surface with the back of a spoon, without compressing the mass too much. Chef's tip: you can set aside a small handful of chocolate chips to sprinkle on the surface for a more inviting visual effect.
  7. Preheat the oven to 180°C (350°F/Gas Mark 4) and, once the temperature is reached, place the dish on the centre rack. Bake the pudding for approximately 40 minutes, monitoring the surface to ensure it doesn't darken too much due to the sugars and cocoa. The dessert should rise slightly and form a thin, crispy crust that contrasts with the tender heart. Chef's tip: if you notice the surface browning too quickly after the first 25 minutes, cover the dish with a piece of foil.
  8. At the end of the cooking time, check the consistency by lightly pressing the centre of the pudding with a finger: it should feel firm to the touch but still springy and soft inside. Remove the dish from the oven and let the dessert cool to room temperature for at least 15 minutes before slicing. This resting period is vital to allow the structure to stabilise and the flavours to concentrate. Chef's tip: serve it lukewarm with a dollop of unsweetened whipped cream to balance the richness of the chocolate.

Tips

  • Cut the bread into uniform cubes to ensure even absorption of the milk throughout the base.
  • Let the bread soak in the milk for at least fifteen minutes so it softens completely to the core.
  • For a perfect top crust, sprinkle the surface with a thin layer of granulated sugar before baking.

Variations

  • Add some grated orange zest to the mixture for an aromatic note that pairs perfectly with chocolate.
  • Replace the dark chocolate chips with coarsely chopped toasted hazelnuts for a crunchy touch.
