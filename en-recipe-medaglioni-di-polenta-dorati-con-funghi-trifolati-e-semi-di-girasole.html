Golden Polenta Medallions with Sautéed Mushrooms and Sunflower Seeds

4 servings · Prep 5 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

A crunchy and savoury starter that uses the convenience of ready-made polenta to create a perfect gluten-free base. The contrast between the softness of the mushrooms and the toasted note of the sunflower seeds makes it ideal for the whole family.

A rustic dish that transforms pre-cooked polenta into a golden, crispy base. Sautéed mushrooms add an earthy flavor, perfectly complemented by the toasted notes of sunflower seeds. A simple preparation ideal for a cozy dinner.

Ingredients

  • Ready-to-eat polenta block (precooked cornmeal block, gluten-free)500 g
  • Fresh button mushrooms300 g
  • Fresh garlic2 spicchi
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 ml
  • Hulled sunflower seeds30 g
  • Fresh parsley10 g
  • Fine salt4 g
  • Ground black pepper2 g

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Start by preparing the base: take the 500g pre-cooked polenta block and slice it with a smooth-bladed knife into 8 even medallions, about 1.5cm thick. Apply steady, firm pressure to prevent the pre-cooked structure from crumbling, keeping the edges clean. This process takes about 2 minutes and ensures all slices cook evenly later. The final result should be a series of regular, compact discs ready for the pan. My chef's tip is to slightly dampen the knife blade between slices to help it glide through more smoothly.
  2. Heat a large non-stick pan with 15ml of extra virgin olive oil over medium-high heat for about a minute. Arrange the polenta medallions spaced apart and let them sizzle for 4 minutes on the first side, then flip them gently with a spatula and continue for another 4 minutes. Try not to move them constantly to encourage the Maillard reaction and achieve a brown, crispy crust on the outside. The polenta will be ready when it is golden outside and still soft at the heart, emitting a toasted corn aroma. A chef's trick: if the pan is too small, cook the polenta in two batches to prevent the bottom temperature from dropping too much.
  3. While the polenta browns, attend to the 300g of fresh mushrooms by cleaning them thoroughly with a damp cloth or brush to remove any soil residue. Slice them vertically with a chef's knife, maintaining a thickness of about 3mm to ensure quick and even cooking in the pan. Never wash them under running water, as their spongy structure would absorb too much moisture, ruining the final texture. You should obtain clean, white slices ready for sautéing. The tip is to discard only the very end of the stem, which is the toughest and dirtiest part.
  4. Prepare the aromatic base by pouring 15ml of extra virgin olive oil into a second large pan along with the 2 slightly crushed garlic cloves. Turn the heat to medium and let it sauté for about 2 minutes, moving the garlic so it releases its essential oils without darkening too much. When the oil begins to smell intense and the garlic is just golden, the base is ready for the mushrooms. Be careful not to burn the garlic, otherwise it will leave a bitter aftertaste. The chef suggests using garlic 'in camicia' (with the skin on) for a more delicate infusion.
  5. Turn the heat to high and add the mushrooms to the pan with the garlic, sautéing them vigorously for about 6 minutes with a wooden spoon. At this stage, it is crucial not to add salt, allowing the mushrooms to brown on the outside instead of boiling in their own juices. You will see the mushrooms reduce in volume and become glossy and slightly amber, while maintaining a firm bite. High heat is the secret to perfect, non-rubbery sautéed mushrooms. If you notice the mushrooms releasing too much water, drain it quickly and continue cooking on an even higher flame.
  6. In a separate small pan, toast the 30g of sunflower seeds over low heat for about 2 minutes, shaking the pan continuously. The seeds should take on a slightly amber colour and release a characteristic toasted nutty fragrance, indicating the internal oils have been activated. This is a quick step requiring maximum attention, as the seeds can burn in seconds and become bitter. Once ready, transfer them immediately to a small cold bowl to stop the residual cooking. The chef's advice is not to use any fats at this stage: dry toasting best enhances the seeds' natural crunchiness.
  7. Once the mushrooms are well sautéed, turn off the heat, remove the garlic cloves, and add the 4g of fine salt, 2g of black pepper, and 10g of finely chopped fresh parsley. Stir well for a few moments so the residual heat of the mushrooms releases the parsley's aroma without cooking it, keeping its bright green colour. The mushrooms will now appear well-seasoned, tasty, and fragrant, ready to garnish the dish. The secret is to chop the parsley only at the last moment to preserve all its volatile oils and freshness.
  8. Move to plating by arranging two hot and crispy polenta medallions in the centre of each individual plate. Use a spoon to distribute the sautéed mushrooms over the polenta, trying to create a small pyramid to give volume and height to the presentation. Ensure the mushrooms are well distributed so that every bite of polenta is accompanied by the topping. The serving temperature is crucial: the contrast between the hot base and the steaming garnish must be immediate. A professional trick is to slightly warm the plates in the oven at 50°C before serving.
  9. Finish the dish by scattering the toasted sunflower seeds over the mushrooms to add a crunchy and decorative note. Complete with a final drizzle of the remaining 10ml of raw extra virgin olive oil, letting it drip gently over both the mushrooms and the polenta to provide shine and a final herbaceous scent. The dish is now a perfect balance of textures: the outer crunch of the polenta, the softness of the mushrooms, and the toasted note of the seeds. Serve immediately to enjoy the full aromatic range. The chef's final touch? A grating of organic lemon zest over the mushrooms for an unexpected hint of freshness.

Tips

  • Dry the mushrooms thoroughly after cleaning to prevent them from releasing too much water while sautéing.
  • Toast the sunflower seeds over low heat to enhance their aroma without burning them.
  • Slice the polenta rounds into even thicknesses to ensure consistent browning in the pan.

Variations

  • Try cutting the polenta into cubes instead of rounds for a more dynamic and rustic visual result.
  • Increase the amount of ground black pepper to emphasize the aromatic and pungent note of the mushrooms.
