Baked Chickpea and Beetroot Burgers

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 30 min · Medium

Homemade veggie burgers, nutritious and eye-catching thanks to the beetroot's colour. Perfect for a quick meal or inside a classic bun.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⬇️ Big saving🥕 Vegetarian🧒 Kid-friendly🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🌍 International

These plant-based burgers stand out for their intense color and earthy flavor. The combination of chickpeas and beetroot creates a soft texture, enriched by the aromatic notes of cumin.

Ingredients

  • cooked chickpeas (canned)400 g
  • pre-cooked beetroot150 g
  • egg1 pcs
  • breadcrumbs80 g
  • ground cumin1 cucchiaino
  • fresh parsley1 mazzetto

Allergens

Gluten, Eggs

Method

  1. Start by thoroughly draining 400g of tinned chickpeas under cold running water, then pat them dry carefully using kitchen paper. It is essential to remove every trace of external moisture to prevent the mixture from becoming too soft and difficult to handle during cooking. Spread the chickpeas out on a clean tea towel and let them air-dry for a few minutes: you will have a perfect base for chopping without releasing liquid. Chef's tip: if the chickpeas remain wet, you'll need to add too much breadcrumb, making the burger heavy and floury.
  2. Take 150g of pre-cooked beetroot and cut it into uniform cubes of about 2 centimetres, then wash and dry a bunch of fresh parsley, removing only the hard stems. Keep these ingredients ready on the chopping board, separate from the chickpeas for now, ensuring an organised preparation. This preliminary phase will allow you to better control the final texture of the burger by avoiding excess juice. Chef's tip: pat the beetroot dry with kitchen paper after cutting it too, as its juice is highly pigmented and watery.
  3. Place the dried chickpeas, beetroot cubes, and fresh parsley into the bowl of a food processor. Operate the appliance using short pulses of 2-3 seconds each, constantly monitoring the texture of the mixture without ever blending at a continuous speed. You should obtain a coarse crumble where the red and yellow colours mix vibrantly without turning into a smooth purée. Chef's tip: stop the mixer as soon as you see there are no whole chickpeas left; the grainy texture is the secret to simulating a real burger's consistency.
  4. Transfer the chopped mixture into a large bowl and add one whole egg and a teaspoon of ground cumin. Start mixing with a wooden spoon to distribute the spices well and bind the vegetable base with the egg protein. You will immediately smell the warm, toasted aroma of the cumin, which perfectly balances the earthy note of the beetroot. Chef's tip: crack the egg into a small separate bowl before adding it to avoid any shell fragments accidentally ending up in the mixture.
  5. Incorporate 80g of breadcrumbs by sprinkling them in a little at a time while you begin to knead directly with clean hands. Work the mixture by squeezing it between your fingers to test the consistency: it should become malleable, compact, and no longer stick to your skin. If the mass still feels too damp, add one final tablespoon of breadcrumbs, but do not overdo it to avoid drying out the burger. Chef's tip: let the mixture rest for 5 minutes before shaping the burgers, allowing the breadcrumbs to absorb any residual moisture.
  6. Divide the mixture into 4 equal parts of approximately 160g each and shape round burgers using a 10cm ring mould or simply your hands. If proceeding manually, first form a firm ball and then gently flatten it until it reaches a uniform thickness of about 2 centimetres. Try to make the edges smooth and compact to prevent them from cracking or drying out excessively during oven baking. Chef's tip: slightly dampen your hands with water or a drop of oil to achieve a perfectly smooth and professional outer surface.
  7. Line a baking tray with greaseproof paper and arrange the burgers well-spaced so that the heat can surround them uniformly on all sides. Brush the surface of each burger with a very thin layer of extra virgin olive oil using a silicone pastry brush. Preheat the oven to 200°C (180°C fan/gas mark 6) and ensure it has reached the temperature before placing the tray on the middle shelf. Chef's tip: a high temperature is essential to immediately trigger the Maillard reaction on the vegetable surface, ensuring crunchiness.
  8. Bake the burgers at 200°C for the first 15 minutes, then remove the tray and turn them over extremely gently using a wide, flat spatula. You will notice that the base has already become dry and an intense burgundy colour, almost brown, a sign that cooking is progressing correctly. Continue cooking for another 15 minutes on the second side, maintaining the same constant temperature to even out the outer crust. Chef's tip: if the burgers seem too soft when it's time to turn them, let them cook for another 2 minutes before touching them.
  9. Remove the burgers from the oven when the surface is visibly crispy and the spicy aroma has become intense and inviting. Let them rest on the tray out of the oven for at least 5 minutes: this step allows the fibres and starches to stabilise, making the structure solid when cut. When served, the burger should be brown on the outside and a vibrant, soft fuchsia at the core. Chef's tip: serve them hot with a yoghurt or tahini sauce to add an acidic note that contrasts the natural sweetness of the beetroot.

Tips

  • Dry the chickpeas perfectly after rinsing to prevent the mixture from becoming too wet.
  • Use a food ring mold to shape the burgers for a consistent and professional look.
  • Lightly brush the burgers with olive oil before baking to achieve a golden crust.

Variations

  • Add grated lemon zest to the mixture for a fresh citrusy note.
  • Replace the cumin with curry powder for a different aromatic profile.
