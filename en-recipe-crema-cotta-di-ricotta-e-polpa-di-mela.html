Baked Ricotta and Apple Pulp Cream

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 30 min · Medium

A very delicate spoon dessert reminiscent of a small cheesecake, but without added sugar and much more economical. The cooked apple acts as a natural sweetener, blending perfectly with the cow's milk ricotta.

A delicate spoon dessert that transforms ricotta and Golden apple puree into a thick, fragrant cream. Slow oven baking and a hint of cinnamon create a dessert with a texture similar to a rustic pudding.

Ingredients

  • Fresh cow's milk ricotta (a creamy Italian whey cheese)250 g
  • Golden Delicious apples200 g
  • Egg1 pcs
  • Ground cinnamon1 g
  • Butter for the moulds5 g

Allergens

Eggs, Milk

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the fruit base: peel 200g of Golden Delicious apples, remove the core, and cut them into very small, uniform cubes. Place them in a small non-stick saucepan with one tablespoon of water and cook over medium-low heat for about 10 minutes, covering with a lid to retain moisture. The final result should be an extremely soft, translucent pulp that breaks down easily. Chef's tip: add a drop of lemon juice to prevent the apples from browning excessively during cooking.
  2. Once the apples are cooked, mash them into a very fine puree using a fork or, for a professional result, an immersion blender. Transfer the puree to a small bowl and let it cool completely at room temperature before proceeding. It is vital that the pulp is not hot to avoid altering the ricotta's structure or curdling the egg prematurely. Chef's tip: spread the puree on a flat plate to speed up the natural cooling process.
  3. Take 250g of fresh cow's milk ricotta and sieve it through a fine-mesh strainer, using the back of a spoon or a spatula. Collect the ricotta in a large bowl: you will notice it has lost its grainy texture, becoming a smooth, silky, and slightly aerated cream. This step is the secret to achieving a baked cream with an impeccable melt-in-the-mouth texture. Chef's tip: never skip sieving, as whisking alone will not eliminate the small protein lumps in the ricotta.
  4. Prepare the necessary equipment by preheating the oven to 160°C (static) and boiling some water for the bain-marie. Use 5g of butter to meticulously grease the inside of four individual ramekins (approx. 125ml capacity). Ensure you cover the bottom corners well to make it easier to unmould the dessert later. Chef's tip: use a pastry brush to distribute the butter in a very thin, even layer, avoiding any build-up at the base.
  5. In the bowl with the sieved ricotta, incorporate the now-cold apple puree and add 1g of ground cinnamon (roughly a generous pinch). Mix gently with a hand whisk or a spatula, using circular motions from the bottom upwards to keep the mixture light. You will smell the spicy aroma of the cinnamon blending with the natural sweetness of the apple. Chef's tip: sprinkle the cinnamon gradually to prevent the formation of small spice clumps.
  6. Add the whole egg to the ricotta and apple mixture, ensuring it is at room temperature to encourage a perfect emulsion. Work the mixture vigorously with a hand whisk for about a minute until the egg is fully absorbed and the batter appears glossy and homogeneous. The mass should be thick yet fluid, a light cream colour speckled with cinnamon. Chef's tip: do not over-whisk the mixture to avoid incorporating too much air, which would cause cracks on the surface during baking.
  7. Distribute the mixture equally among the four buttered ramekins, filling them to about one centimetre from the top rim. Level the surface with the back of a teaspoon or gently tap the ramekins on the worktop to eliminate any trapped air bubbles. This operation ensures even cooking and a neat appearance once baked. Chef's tip: if you want a perfectly smooth surface, run a wet spatula over the top of the mixture before placing it in the oven.
  8. Place the ramekins inside a high-sided baking tray and pour in the hot water prepared earlier until it reaches halfway up the sides of the moulds. Carefully place in the preheated oven at 160°C and bake for 25-30 minutes, monitoring the consistency. The dessert is cooked when the edges appear stable and the centre is firm but with a slight 'wobble' when shaken. Chef's tip: if the bain-marie water starts to boil vigorously, add an ice cube or a little cold water to lower the temperature.
  9. Remove the tray from the oven and take the ramekins out of the water using tongs or an oven glove, letting them cool to room temperature. Once cold, you can serve them directly in their containers or, if you prefer to unmould them, let them rest in the fridge for at least 2 hours to stabilise the structure. When serving, the dessert will be compact, fragrant, and naturally sweet thanks to the apple sugars. Chef's tip: run a thin knife blade around the edge of the ramekin before inverting it for a perfect release.

Tips

  • Sieve the ricotta thoroughly to achieve a perfectly smooth and lump-free final texture.
  • Ensure the egg is at room temperature to promote a homogeneous emulsion with the other ingredients.
  • Carefully grease the molds with butter to make it easier to remove the cream once cooled.

Variations

  • You can use pear pulp instead of apple for an even smoother taste.
  • Replace the cinnamon with grated lemon zest for a fresher, citrusy aroma.
