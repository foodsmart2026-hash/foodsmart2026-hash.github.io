Pasta with Broccoli and Toasted Breadcrumbs

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

A balanced main course that revisits Italian 'cucina povera' (peasant cooking) by adding a protein base of blended legumes for an irresistible creaminess. It is an economical and nutritious recipe, perfect for getting the whole family to eat vegetables thanks to the contrast between the cream and the crunchy crumbs.

Ingredients

  • Wholemeal durum wheat pasta (such as fusilli or orecchiette)320 g
  • Fresh broccoli600 g
  • Pre-cooked cannellini beans (rinsed)200 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 g
  • Coarse breadcrumbs40 g
  • Fresh garlic1 clove
  • Dried chilli flakes1 g
  • Unrefined sea salt5 g
  • Black pepper1 g

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Start by preparing 600g of fresh broccoli: separate the florets from the main stalk and, using a small knife, peel the tough outer layer of the stalk, cutting the tender core into 1cm cubes. Wash them thoroughly under cold running water to remove any soil residues. This process allows you to reduce waste and utilise the sweetest, most nutrient-rich part of the vegetable. The final result should be a bowl of clean, uniformly sized vegetables for even cooking. Chef's tip: don't throw away the tender broccoli leaves; they are edible and add a rustic, authentic flavour note.
  2. Fill a large pot with about 3 litres of water and bring to a boil over high heat, then add 5g of sea salt. Once the water is boiling vigorously, drop in the prepared broccoli and cook for about 5-6 minutes without covering the pot. You want the vegetables to be tender when pierced with a fork but still a vibrant green, indicating the chlorophyll hasn't oxidised. Remove the broccoli with a slotted spoon, keeping the water in the pot as it will be used to cook the pasta. Chef's tip: cooking vegetables in plenty of salted water fixes the green colour and preserves a crisp texture.
  3. In a small non-stick pan, pour 5g of extra virgin olive oil and add 40g of coarse breadcrumbs. Heat over medium flame and toast the bread, stirring constantly with a wooden spoon for about 3 minutes until it turns a deep golden brown and becomes very crunchy. You will smell a toasted grain aroma similar to freshly baked bread; be very careful as breadcrumbs can burn in seconds. Chef's tip: add a pinch of black pepper to the breadcrumbs while toasting to intensify the aroma without adding more salt.
  4. Take a blender or an immersion blender and place half of the previously boiled broccoli along with 200g of rinsed cannellini beans and a ladle of the cooking water. Blend at maximum power for about a minute until you achieve a perfectly smooth, thick, and velvety cream. If the cream appears too grainy, add another tablespoon of hot water and continue blending. This protein and fibre-rich base will be the secret to coating the pasta without using dairy. Chef's tip: pass the cream through a fine-mesh sieve if you want a Michelin-star texture, removing any bean skins.
  5. Bring the water used for the broccoli back to a boil and add 320g of wholemeal pasta (fusilli or orecchiette). Cook the pasta over medium heat following the package instructions, but set a timer to drain it 2 minutes before the end of the suggested time. The broccoli-infused water will transfer a deep flavour to the pasta and help the starches bond better with the sauce. The result should be pasta that still has a firm bite ('very al dente'). Chef's tip: do not add more salt to the water, as the saltiness is already balanced by the reduction that will happen in the pan.
  6. While the pasta cooks, take a wide frying pan and heat the remaining 35g of extra virgin olive oil with a crushed garlic clove (keep the skin on for a milder taste) and 1g of dried chili. Let it infuse over a very low heat for about 2-3 minutes, tilting the pan slightly so the garlic is submerged in the oil and releases its essential oils without browning. The oil should smell intense without producing smoke. Chef's tip: if the garlic starts to turn brown, remove it immediately, as it will release a bitter taste that would ruin the whole dish.
  7. Add the remaining whole broccoli florets to the infused pan and sauté over medium heat for 2 minutes, tossing the pan frequently. This step creates a slight golden crust on the broccoli, making them flavourful and integrating the chili and garlic notes. Remove the garlic clove before proceeding further. The broccoli should look glossy and well-seasoned. Chef's tip: lightly crush a few florets with a fork during this step to create different textures in the final seasoning.
  8. Drain the pasta using a slotted spoon or colander directly into the pan with the broccoli, reserving some cooking water. Immediately pour the cannellini and broccoli cream over the pasta and turn the heat to high. Sauté everything vigorously for 1-2 minutes, adding half a ladle of cooking water if necessary to maintain creaminess. The pasta must finish cooking in the cream, resulting in it being perfectly enveloped in a thick, homogeneous sauce. Chef's tip: the 'mantecatura' is successful when you hear a 'wet' sound while tossing the pan and see a creamy emulsion forming at the bottom.
  9. Turn off the heat and distribute the pasta into individual bowls while it is still piping hot. Sprinkle each portion with 10g of the crunchy toasted breadcrumbs and finish with a generous grinding of fresh black pepper. The contrast between the softness of the cream, the chewiness of the wholemeal pasta, and the 'crunch' of the toasted bread will make every bite dynamic and satisfying. Serve immediately to prevent the breadcrumbs from absorbing the pasta's moisture and losing their crunch. Chef's tip: add a final drizzle of raw oil to enhance the dish's herbaceous scents and improve the absorption of fat-soluble vitamins.
