Sautéed Swiss chard with chickpeas and lemon

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

A humble but nutritionally dense side dish that highlights seasonal vegetables and plant proteins. The citrus note of the lemon not only enhances the flavour but also facilitates the absorption of iron present in the chard.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🌱 Vegan🌾 Gluten-free🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free

Ingredients

  • Fresh Swiss chard (leafy greens with thick stalks)800 g
  • Pre-cooked chickpeas (drained weight)240 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Garlic2 cloves
  • Whole lemon (juice and zest)1 pz
  • Fine salt4 g
  • Dried chilli flakes1 g
  • Water50 ml

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Start by carefully washing the chard under running water to remove any soil residue, paying particular attention to the base of the stalks.
  2. Separate the green leaves from the white part (the stalk). Cut the stalks into approximately 1 cm cubes and the leaves into wide strips, keeping them separate as they have different cooking times.
  3. In a large pan, heat the extra virgin olive oil with the two crushed garlic cloves and the chilli for 2 minutes over medium heat, until the garlic becomes slightly golden and releases its fragrance.
  4. Add only the chard stalks and the 50 ml of water. Cover with a lid and let stew for 5-6 minutes: the stalks should soften but maintain a slight bite.
  5. Drain the chickpeas from their liquid and rinse them well under water to remove excess salt. Pour them into the pan with the stalks and slightly increase the heat for 2 minutes to let them absorb the flavours.
  6. Add the green chard leaves to the pan. They will look voluminous, but will reduce drastically in 3 minutes of cooking; turn them often with tongs until they are tender and bright green.
  7. Remove the garlic cloves. Season with salt and add the grated zest of half a lemon, stirring well to distribute the aromas evenly.
  8. Turn off the heat and pour the freshly squeezed lemon juice over the hot vegetables: this step is essential to preserve the Vitamin C, which is heat-sensitive.
  9. Let the side dish rest for a minute before serving, allowing the flavours to blend perfectly. The dish is ready when the leaves have wilted but are not dark and the stalks are tender.
