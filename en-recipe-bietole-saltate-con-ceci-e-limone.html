Sautéed Swiss chard with chickpeas and lemon

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

A humble but nutritionally dense side dish that highlights seasonal vegetables and plant proteins. The citrus note of the lemon not only enhances the flavour but also facilitates the absorption of iron present in the chard.

A quick and flavorful dish combining the earthy note of chickpeas with the freshness of sautéed Swiss chard. Lemon juice and chili flakes provide a lively character and a perfect balance between acidity and spiciness.

Ingredients

  • Fresh Swiss chard (leafy greens with thick stalks)800 g
  • Pre-cooked chickpeas (drained weight)240 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Garlic2 cloves
  • Whole lemon (juice and zest)1 pz
  • Fine salt4 g
  • Dried chilli flakes1 g
  • Water50 ml

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Begin by thoroughly washing 800g of Swiss chard under cold running water, scrubbing the white ribs to remove any grit or soil. Place the chard on a chopping board and use a knife to separate the fleshy white ribs from the tender green leaves. This step is crucial as the two parts have different cooking times and require separate handling for the best texture. Chef's tip: do not discard the tips of the ribs; simply trim the very base to retain all the valuable fibre of the vegetable.
  2. Cut the white ribs into even 1cm cubes and shred the green leaves into wide strips of about 3-4cm, keeping them in separate bowls. Aim for uniformity when dicing the ribs to ensure they cook at the same rate. This preparation ensures you get a pleasant crunch from the ribs and soft, wilted leaves without them turning into mush. Chef's tip: precise cutting isn't just for looks; it allows for even heat distribution across the entire pan.
  3. Take a very large non-stick frying pan and heat 30ml of extra virgin olive oil along with two lightly crushed garlic cloves and 1g of dried chilli. Maintain a medium heat for about 2 minutes, moving the garlic frequently until it turns lightly golden and releases an intense aroma without burning. The oil needs to be gently infused, forming the aromatic base of the dish. Chef's tip: crush the garlic with the flat of your knife and leave the skin on ('in its jacket') for a more subtle flavour that is easy to remove later.
  4. Add the diced ribs to the hot pan along with 50ml of water, then immediately cover with a lid to trap the steam. Allow to braise over a medium heat for 5-6 minutes; the steam will soften the fibrous ribs while keeping them juicy. By the end of this time, the ribs should look translucent but still offer a slight bite. Chef's tip: if the water evaporates too quickly, add an extra tablespoon to prevent the oil from starting to fry the vegetables prematurely.
  5. While the ribs are cooking, drain 240g of tinned chickpeas and rinse them thoroughly under cold water until no more foam appears. Add them to the pan with the ribs and slightly increase the heat, sautéing for 2 minutes to allow the pulses to absorb the garlic and chilli-infused oil. The chickpeas will heat through evenly, soaking up the cooking juices. Chef's tip: rinsing canned legumes is essential to remove excess sodium and the metallic taste of the preserving liquid.
  6. Add the green chard leaves to the pan, adding them in handfuls if necessary as they will initially appear very bulky. Toss with tongs or a wooden spoon for 3 minutes over medium heat, bringing the bottom leaves to the top until they are completely wilted. The leaves will transform from a dull green to a vibrant, deep emerald, reducing in volume by more than half. Chef's tip: do not overcook at this stage to prevent the leaves from losing their vibrant colour and turning a muddy grey.
  7. Remove the garlic cloves from the pan and season with 4g of fine salt and the freshly grated zest of half a lemon. Stir carefully to distribute the salt and the essential oils from the lemon zest evenly across all ingredients, keeping the heat on for just one more minute. You will immediately notice a fresh, citrusy aroma that balances the earthiness of the chard. Chef's tip: only grate the yellow part of the lemon, as the white pith underneath is bitter and would ruin the dish's balance.
  8. Turn off the heat and, as a vital final step, pour the freshly squeezed lemon juice over the hot vegetables, giving it one last stir. Let the side dish rest covered for 60 seconds before serving to allow the flavours to settle perfectly. The final result will be a harmonious dish where the creaminess of the chickpeas meets the freshness of the lemon and the dual texture of the chard. Chef's tip: adding the juice off the heat protects the Vitamin C from excessive heat, keeping its nutritional properties intact.

Tips

  • Put the chard stalks in the pan a few minutes before the leaves to ensure even cooking times.
  • Rinse the pre-cooked chickpeas thoroughly under running water to remove any residue from the canning liquid.
  • Add the grated lemon zest only after turning off the heat to keep its citrus aroma intact.

Variations

  • Reduce the suggested amount of water to obtain drier chard and a texture more similar to a stir-fry.
  • Increase the amount of garlic and chili flakes if you prefer a more intense and markedly spicy taste.
