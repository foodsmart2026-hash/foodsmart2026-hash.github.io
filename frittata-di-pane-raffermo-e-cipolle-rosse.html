Stale Bread and Red Onion Frittata

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

A traditional European peasant 'recovery' recipe that avoids food waste. The bread absorbs the egg, becoming soft, almost like a savoury cake.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⬇️ Big saving🥕 Vegetarian🧒 Kid-friendly

A zero-waste recipe that transforms leftover bread into a thick, fluffy omelet enriched by the sweetness of stewed red onions. This rustic dish is perfect served warm or cold for a quick lunch.

Ingredients

  • Stale bread200 g
  • Eggs6 pcs
  • Red onions2 pcs
  • Milk100 ml
  • Fresh parsley1 bunch
  • Sunflower oil30 ml
  • Salt and pepperto taste

Allergens

Eggs, Milk, Gluten

Method

  1. Cut 200g of stale bread into 1.5cm cubes and place them in a large bowl, drizzling them evenly with 100ml of room-temperature milk. Let the bread rest for about 15 minutes, stirring occasionally with a spoon so that every piece absorbs the liquid uniformly. The final result should be soft bread that still retains its structure, avoiding it turning into a shapeless mush. Chef’s tip: if the bread is very hard, extend the soaking time by 5 minutes, but squeeze it well before adding to the eggs to avoid watering down the mixture.
  2. Take the 2 red onions, peel them by removing the outer skin, and slice them finely into regular rings using a sharp knife or a mandoline. In a non-stick pan of about 24-26 cm, pour half of the sunflower oil (15ml) and a tablespoon of water, then add the onions and a pinch of salt. Cook over low heat for about 10 minutes, covering with a lid, until the onions are translucent, sweet, and completely softened. Chef’s tip: adding water allows the onions to stew without burning, keeping their ruby colour and digestibility intact.
  3. While the onions are cooking, crack 6 eggs into a clean bowl and add the finely chopped fresh parsley, a pinch of salt, and a grind of black pepper. Beat everything with a fork or a hand whisk for about a minute, trying to incorporate some air but without over-whipping the mixture. You should obtain a bright yellow liquid, fragrant, with the parsley evenly distributed. Chef’s tip: avoid over-salting the eggs if the bread used is already very savoury, as is often the case with traditional regional breads.
  4. Take the now-softened bread, squeeze it gently with your hands if you see excess milk at the bottom of the bowl, and add it to the beaten eggs. Mix gently with a wooden spoon or spatula, ensuring every cube of bread is perfectly coated by the egg mixture. Let this mixture rest for 2-3 minutes so the flavours start to meld before proceeding. Chef’s tip: do not crush the bread too hard, otherwise you will lose the rustic texture that makes this frittata special.
  5. Now fold the stewed red onions into the bowl with the eggs and bread, taking them from the pan (which you will set aside for the final cooking). Mix everything with slow movements from bottom to top to distribute the onions evenly throughout the mass. The mixture will appear thick, rich in different textures, and very aromatic thanks to the sweetness of the cooked onions. Chef’s tip: ensure the onions are warm and not boiling hot when you add them to the eggs to prevent them from starting to curdle prematurely.
  6. Quickly clean the pan used for the onions with paper towels, pour the remaining 15ml of sunflower oil, and heat over medium heat for one minute. When the oil is hot but not smoking, pour the entire mixture into the centre of the pan and level it quickly with a spatula to cover the entire surface. Cover immediately with a lid and turn the heat down to minimum, letting it cook for about 8 minutes. Chef’s tip: occasionally move the pan with small side-to-side jerks to ensure the base is not sticking and is cooking evenly.
  7. Observe the edges of the frittata: when they appear firm and the top surface is almost set but still slightly moist, it is time to flip it. Place a large flat plate over the pan, invert with a firm and quick gesture, then gently slide the frittata back into the pan on the uncooked side. This operation requires firmness to prevent the frittata from breaking or any liquid egg from escaping. Chef’s tip: if you are afraid of burning yourself or dropping everything, use a flat lid without raised edges, which makes sliding easier.
  8. Complete the cooking of the other side for another 4-5 minutes without a lid, maintaining medium heat to encourage the formation of a golden, crispy crust. The frittata will be ready when it feels firm to the touch and has an inviting browned colour on both sides. Turn off the heat and let the frittata rest in the pan for 2 minutes before transferring it to a cutting board or serving plate. Chef’s tip: the final rest is crucial because it allows the residual moisture to redistribute, making the slice perfect when cut and not rubbery.

Tips

  • Let the bread soak in the milk until it is tender but still firm to the touch.
  • Use a high-quality non-stick pan to flip the omelet easily without breaking it.
  • Cook over low heat to allow the center to set properly without burning the bottom.

Variations

  • Add chopped Taggiasca olives to the mixture for a more intense Mediterranean touch.
  • Enrich the dish by replacing some of the onions with sautéed spinach.
