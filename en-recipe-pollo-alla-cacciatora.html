Chicken 'alla Cacciatora'

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 60 min · Hard

A timeless classic of peasant country cooking, made with economical and tasty cuts of chicken slow-cooked in an aromatic stew. This version wisely balances proteins and good fats, using fresh vegetables for the sauté and olives for a deep organoleptic profile.

A timeless classic of Italian rustic cuisine, perfect for Sunday lunches. The chicken is browned and then slowly stewed with fresh herbs and red wine, creating a savory and enveloping sauce.

Ingredients

  • Chicken drumsticks and thighs (about 4-6 pieces)800 g
  • Chopped tomato pulp400 g
  • Brown onion100 g
  • Carrot80 g
  • Celery stick50 g
  • Red table wine100 ml
  • Pitted black olives50 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Garlic1 clove
  • Fresh rosemary and sage10 g
  • Salt and black pepper4 g

Allergens

Celery, Sulphites

Method

  1. Start by preparing the meat: thoroughly dry the 800 g of chicken with paper towels to remove surface moisture and make two small deep cuts on the drumsticks. Patting the skin well allows for even and crispy browning without the meat boiling in its own water. Leave the chicken at room temperature for about 15 minutes before starting, lightly salting the surface. The result will be meat ready to react correctly with the heat of the pan. The chef's tip: do not remove the skin at this stage, as it protects the meat from drying out too much and gives the typical flavour of the cacciatora sauce.
  2. Prepare the aromatic mix (soffritto) by finely chopping the onion, carrot, and celery with a sharp knife or a pulse mixer. Try to obtain pieces of similar size, about 3-4 millimetres, to ensure even cooking that almost dissolves into the final sauce. Dedicate time to this operation because it is the aromatic base that will give body to the whole dish. You should get about a medium bowl of fragrant and colourful chopped vegetables. The chef's tip: add the whole crushed garlic clove, which you can remove at the end of cooking for a milder flavour.
  3. Heat the 30 ml of extra virgin olive oil in a large high-sided casserole or a cast iron pan. Arrange the chicken pieces with the skin side down and brown over high heat for about 5-7 minutes without turning them continuously. The skin must become golden, crispy and release part of its tasty fat into the bottom of the pan. Once a browned crust is obtained, turn the pieces and brown the other side for another 3 minutes. The chef's tip: avoid overcrowding the pan; if necessary, brown the chicken in two batches to keep the oil temperature constant.
  4. Temporarily remove the chicken from the pan and place it on a plate, leaving the cooking juices in the pot. Lower the heat to medium intensity and pour in the chopped carrot, celery, and onion along with the rosemary and sage tied with string. Let the vegetables soften for about 8-10 minutes, stirring often with a wooden spoon until the onion becomes transparent and the mix has reduced in volume. This step extracts the natural sweetness of the vegetables which will balance the acidity of the tomato. The chef's tip: if the bottom seems to dry out too quickly, add a tablespoon of hot water instead of more oil.
  5. Reposition the chicken pieces in the casserole with the vegetables and raise the heat for a minute before deglazing with the 100 ml of red wine. Pour the wine directly onto the meat and let the alcohol evaporate completely over high heat until you no longer smell the pungent odour and the liquid has reduced by half. Gently scrape the bottom of the pot with the spoon to loosen the caramelised sugars of the meat (deglazing), which will enrich the sauce. The result should be a thick and fragrant brown-coloured base. The chef's tip: use a medium-bodied red wine, not too tannic, to prevent the sauce from becoming bitter.
  6. Add the 400 g of tomato pulp and the pitted black olives, distributing them evenly among the chicken pieces. Season with salt and add a generous grind of black pepper, then lower the heat to minimum and cover the casserole with a slightly ajar lid. Let it cook gently for about 35-40 minutes, turning the chicken halfway through to ensure it remains tender everywhere. The tomato must simmer slowly, creating small bubbles on the surface. The chef's tip: if you see that the sauce reduces too much before the chicken is cooked, add half a ladle of vegetable stock or hot water.
  7. Check the chicken's cooking by piercing the thickest part of the drumstick: if the juices that come out are clear and the meat easily pulls away from the bone, the dish is ready. Remove the lid for the last 5 minutes of cooking to allow the sauce to thicken further until it reaches a velvety and enveloping consistency. The final colour should be a deep, intense red, with the olives having released their saline aroma. Remove the bunch of herbs and the garlic clove before serving. The chef's tip: always taste the sauce before turning off to adjust the salt if necessary, as olives can vary the savouriness of the dish significantly.
  8. Turn off the heat and let the chicken cacciatora rest in the closed pot for at least 5-10 minutes before bringing it to the table. This rest allows the meat fibres to relax and the flavours to blend perfectly, making the dish even tastier. Serve each portion with a piece of chicken and plenty of sauce, perhaps accompanying it with a slice of wholemeal bread or soft polenta. The scent will be the rustic one of tradition, with notes of rosemary and wine. The chef's tip: this dish is even better the next day, gently reheated with a drop of water, as the flavours have time to penetrate right to the bone.
  9. For a neat presentation, arrange a drumstick or thigh in the centre of a flat plate and coat generously with the tomato and olive sauce. Decorate with a sprig of fresh rosemary to recall the scents used in cooking and a drizzle of raw oil. Ensure the sauce is not too liquid but coats the spoon, creating a chromatic contrast between the red of the tomato and the green of the herbs. The result will be a familiar dish but presented with the dignity of a restaurant. The chef's tip: if you have guests, you can partially debone the chicken to make consumption easier.

Tips

  • Dry the chicken pieces well before browning to achieve a golden skin.
  • Deglaze with red wine over high heat to allow the alcohol to evaporate completely.
  • If the sauce reduces too much during cooking, add a ladle of hot water.

Variations

  • Replace the red wine with dry white wine for a lighter, clearer version.
  • Add sautéed button mushrooms near the end of cooking to enrich the dish.
