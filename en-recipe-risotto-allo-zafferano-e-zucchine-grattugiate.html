Saffron Risotto with Grated Courgettes

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 18 min · Medium

A creamy and colourful risotto where the grated courgettes melt into the rice during cooking. Economical and impressive thanks to powdered saffron.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🧒 Kid-friendly🌾 Gluten-free🇮🇹 Italian

A creamy twist on the classic saffron risotto, enriched with the freshness of grated zucchinis. This first course combines the delicacy of the vegetable with the enveloping aroma of the golden spice. Its texture is velvety, making it ideal for a friendly dinner.

Ingredients

  • Carnaroli or Arborio rice (typical Italian risotto rice)320 g
  • Medium courgettes2 pcs
  • Saffron1 bustina
  • Vegetable stock
    Make it at home: Vegetable broth ›    1.2 L
  • Shallot1 pcs
  • Butter20 g
  • Parmigiano Reggiano (aged hard cheese)40 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil20 ml

Allergens

Milk

Method

  1. Start by bringing 1.2 litres of vegetable broth to a boil in a small saucepan, then maintain it on the lowest heat throughout the entire process. Take a small cup of this boiling liquid and carefully dissolve one sachet of saffron, stirring well with a teaspoon to prevent lumps. This preliminary step allows the heat of the broth to activate the spice's pigments and aromas, ensuring an even colour. You will obtain a deep golden, highly fragrant liquid. Chef's tip: never use cold water for the saffron, as you would stall the extraction of its aromatic oils.
  2. Thoroughly wash the two medium courgettes, trim the ends, and grate them using the large holes of a box grater, collecting them in a bowl. There is no need to squeeze them, as their natural juices will contribute to the risotto's creaminess during cooking. This technique allows the vegetable to almost melt into the rice grains, providing freshness without altering the dish's structure. The result should be a bright green, slightly moist pulp. Chef's tip: grate the courgettes at the very last moment to prevent them from oxidising and losing their vibrant colour.
  3. Finely mince the shallot with a sharp knife and place it in a high-sided saucepan along with 20 ml of extra virgin olive oil. Turn the heat to medium-low and let it sauté gently for about 3-4 minutes, stirring frequently with a wooden spoon. The shallot must become translucent and soft; it must not brown or burn under any circumstances. You will achieve a sweet and fragrant aromatic base that forms the foundation of the rice's flavour. Chef's tip: if the shallot dries out too quickly, add a tablespoon of broth to steam it without changing its colour.
  4. Pour 320 g of Carnaroli or Arborio rice into the saucepan with the shallot and slightly increase the heat to medium for the toasting phase. Stir the rice continuously for 2-3 minutes, ensuring every grain is well coated in the oil and shallot mixture. Toasting is complete when the grains become translucent at the edges, almost see-through, and feel very hot to the touch. This step is crucial for sealing the grain, allowing it to release starch in a controlled manner. Chef's tip: do not rush this; proper toasting ensures the risotto stays 'al dente' and doesn't turn mushy.
  5. Add the grated courgettes directly onto the toasted rice and stir vigorously for one minute, then begin pouring the first ladle of boiling broth. Maintain a medium heat so the liquid simmers constantly, allowing the rice to absorb the flavours. Continue to stir often: the friction of the grains rubbing against each other is what encourages the starch to release for perfect creaminess. The courgettes will start to cook down, releasing their sweet essence. Chef's tip: only add more broth when the previous ladleful has been almost entirely absorbed.
  6. After about 8-9 minutes (midway through cooking), pour the cup of broth with the dissolved saffron into the saucepan. Stir well to distribute the colour evenly; you will see the risotto instantly turn from white to a brilliant, sunny yellow. Continue adding the remaining broth a ladle at a time, always maintaining a steady simmer so as not to interrupt the cooking of the grains. The scent of saffron will begin to fill the kitchen intensely. Chef's tip: adding saffron halfway through preserves its more delicate aromas which might otherwise fade with over-cooking.
  7. Continue cooking for another 8-10 minutes, tasting frequently to check the cooking point and seasoning. The rice should be cooked but with a core that is still firm to the bite (al dente), and the consistency should be fluid, not overly dry. If necessary, adjust the salt only towards the end, keeping in mind that the Parmesan will add further saltiness. The visual consistency should be like a cream enveloping distinct grains. Chef's tip: always keep an extra ladle of broth handy at the very end in case the rice dries out too quickly.
  8. Turn off the heat, remove the pan from the hob, and add 20 g of cold butter from the fridge and 40 g of grated Parmigiano Reggiano. Cover with a lid and let the risotto rest for about 2 minutes; this allows the cheese proteins and butter fats to stabilise. Remove the lid and 'mantecare' (whip) vigorously with a circular and shaking motion of the pan to create the 'wave' (all'onda) effect. You will achieve a glossy, creamy, and wonderfully aromatic risotto. Chef's tip: the butter must be ice-cold; the thermal shock with the hot rice is the secret to a perfect, velvety finish.

Tips

  • Toast the rice until translucent to ensure it holds its texture perfectly during cooking.
  • Add the broth one ladle at a time, waiting for the previous one to be fully absorbed.
  • Mantecate off the heat with cold butter from the fridge for an impeccable creaminess.

Variations

  • You can replace the shallot with finely sliced leek for a sweeter and more delicate flavor.
  • Add cleaned zucchini flowers in the last few minutes of cooking for a seasonal and colorful touch.
