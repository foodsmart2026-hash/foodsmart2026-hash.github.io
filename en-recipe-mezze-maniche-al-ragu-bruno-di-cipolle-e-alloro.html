Mezze Maniche with Brown Onion and Bay Leaf Ragù

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 30 min · Medium

A vegan first course of humble origins where the slow caramelisation of onions creates a creamy and flavourful sauce. The cost is very low thanks to the use of basic pantry ingredients.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⬇️ Big saving🌱 Vegan🧒 Kid-friendly🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🇮🇹 Italian

A rustic pasta dish inspired by Genovese style, featuring the sweetness of caramelized onions and the balsamic aroma of bay leaves. The texture is creamy and coating, achieved through slow-cooking with broth and brown sugar.

Ingredients

  • Mezze Maniche (short, wide ribbed pasta tubes)320 g
  • Large golden onions500 g
  • Tomato purée (concentrate)30 g
  • Dried bay leaves2 foglie
  • Vegetable stock (from a cube or fresh)250 ml
  • Brown sugar5 g

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Begin by peeling 500g of golden onions, removing the outer skins and ends. Slice them extremely thinly using a sharp chef's knife or a mandoline to achieve a uniform thickness of about 1-2 millimetres. Keep the heat off at this stage and focus on the precision of your cut to ensure all pieces cook evenly. The final result should be a pile of translucent and regular onion veils. The chef's tip: frequently dampen your knife blade with cold water to reduce eye irritation caused by the onion's gases.
  2. Take a wide, heavy-bottomed frying pan and pour in three tablespoons of extra virgin olive oil, then add the sliced onions and 5g of brown sugar. Turn the hob to a very low heat and stir gently with a wooden spoon to distribute the oil and sugar over every slice. Cook for about 15 minutes, stirring the mixture often to prevent the onions from sticking to the bottom or burning at the edges. The onions should soften slowly without browning too quickly, becoming tender and slightly glossy. A little secret: do not rush, low heat is essential for the correct caramelisation of the natural sugars.
  3. Now add the 2 dried bay leaves and a pinch of fine salt to the cooking onions. Stir well while keeping the heat low; the salt will help the onions release their remaining moisture, encouraging a stewing rather than frying process. Continue cooking for another 5 minutes, noticing how the aroma of the bay leaf begins to meld with the sweetness of the onion. The onions should appear wilted, reduced in volume, and almost transparent. Remember not to over-salt at this stage, as the sauce will reduce, concentrating all the flavours.
  4. While the onions are wilting, dissolve 30g of tomato paste into 250ml of warm vegetable stock, stirring with a whisk or spoon until you have a smooth, reddish liquid. Pour this mixture into the pan with the onions when they begin to show the first golden reflections. Slightly increase the heat to a medium-low level to bring the liquid to a simmer. You should obtain a uniform liquid base that entirely covers the bed of onions. The chef's tip: use a vegetable stock that isn't too salty so as not to overpower the delicate nature of the onion.
  5. Cover the pan with a lid and let everything simmer gently on a low heat for another 10 minutes. During this time, the sugars from the onion and the tomato will react to create what is known as a 'brown ragù', a thick sauce with a deep amber colour. Check the consistency occasionally: if the base dries out too quickly, add a splash of hot water. The result must be a rustic, thick, and coating cream where the onions have almost completely broken down. Ensure the aroma is intense and the colour is a warm, inviting brown.
  6. Bring a large pot with about 3 litres of water to the boil and add a handful of rock salt only once it is boiling vigorously. Drop in the 320g of Mezze Maniche and set your timer for two minutes less than the time indicated on the packet. Stir the pasta during the first few minutes to prevent it from sticking to the bottom of the pot. The pasta should be very 'al dente', still slightly firm to the bite, as it will finish cooking directly in the sauce. Do not discard the cooking water: it will be vital for creating the perfect final emulsion.
  7. Take about two ladles of the pasta cooking water and pour them into the pan with the onion ragù, then drain the Mezze Maniche and toss them directly into the sauce. Increase the heat to medium-high to bring it back to a boil quickly. Start tossing the pasta or stirring vigorously with a wooden spoon to encourage the release of starches. This step serves to bind the oil and stock into a thick cream that will cling perfectly to the ridges of the mezze maniche. The pasta will finish cooking by absorbing the flavour of the ragù rather than just salty water.
  8. Remove the 2 bay leaves from the pan to prevent anyone from finding them in their dish and turn off the heat. Let the pasta rest for 30 seconds in the hot pan, giving it one last stir to ensure every single piece is well 'napped' (veiled) by the onion cream. The final consistency should be fluid and glossy, not dry; if necessary, add one last tablespoon of cooking water. Serve immediately in warm bowls, arranging the mezze maniche harmoniously. The chef's touch: a drizzle of raw olive oil and a crack of fresh pepper before serving will further enhance the character of this humble yet noble dish.

Tips

  • Slice the onions uniformly to ensure even cooking and a consistent texture throughout.
  • Add the broth and tomato paste mixture gradually to adjust the sauce thickness to your liking.
  • If the onion sauce becomes too dry during cooking, add a ladle of pasta boiling water.

Variations

  • Add freshly ground black pepper at the end to balance the sweetness of the onions.
  • Swap mezze maniche for rigatoni to better hold the rich and creamy onion sauce.
