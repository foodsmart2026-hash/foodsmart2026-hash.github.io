Creamy Cauliflower and Saffron Soup with Rosemary Croutons

🌿 Seasonal recipe

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 30 min · Easy

A comforting and refined starter that enhances the sweetness of cauliflower with a silky texture and the fragrance of saffron.

Ingredients

  • Cauliflower800g
  • Waxy potatoes200g
  • Saffron strands or powder1 sachet
  • Shallot1
  • Vegetable stock1 litre
  • Rustic bread3 slices
  • Fresh rosemary1 sprig
  • Extra virgin olive oil3 tbsp
  • Salt and black pepperto taste

Allergens

Gluten, Celery

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the vegetables: take 800g of white cauliflower, remove the outer leaves, and cut it into small, uniform florets. Peel 200g of yellow-fleshed potatoes and cut them into cubes of about 2 cm, aiming for similar sizes to ensure even cooking. Wash everything thoroughly under cold running water and set aside. You will have a clean base ready to release all its sweetness during cooking. Chef's tip: do not discard the cauliflower stalk; peel it to remove the fibrous outer layer and dice it, as it is flavourful and nutritious.
  2. Take one shallot, peel it, and slice it finely with a sharp knife. In a large pot, heat two tablespoons of extra virgin olive oil over low heat and add the sliced shallot. Let it sweat gently for about 3-5 minutes, stirring with a wooden spoon until it becomes translucent and tender, without letting it brown. This step will create the sweet and delicate aromatic base typical of great veloutés. Chef's tip: if the shallot starts to brown too quickly, add a tablespoon of hot water to lower the temperature and continue softening it.
  3. Now add the cauliflower and potatoes to the pot with the shallot. Slightly increase the heat to medium and let the vegetables infuse for 4-5 minutes, stirring often to prevent them from sticking. This 'toasting' process allows the sugars in the vegetables to caramelise slightly, making the final flavour much deeper. You will notice the vegetables starting to smell intensely and become shiny from the oil. Chef's tip: make sure the vegetables are well dried before putting them in the pot, so they sauté correctly instead of immediately boiling in their own steam.
  4. Pour 1 litre of hot vegetable broth over the vegetables until they are completely covered. Bring to a boil over high heat, then lower the flame, cover with a lid, and let it simmer for about 20-25 minutes. The vegetables will be ready when they are very tender when pierced with the tines of a fork. The liquid should reduce slightly, concentrating the flavours, but without drying out too much. Chef's tip: use a homemade vegetable stock with celery, carrot, and onion for a true restaurant result; if using store-bought, check that it is not too salty.
  5. While the soup is cooking, prepare the croutons: take 3 slices of rustic bread and cut them into regular cubes of about 1.5 cm. In a non-stick frying pan, heat the last tablespoon of oil with a finely chopped sprig of fresh rosemary. Sauté the bread cubes over medium heat for 5-7 minutes, tossing the pan until they are golden and crunchy on all sides. The bread will absorb the rosemary aroma, becoming an irresistible accompaniment. Chef's tip: add the rosemary only when the oil is hot but not smoking, so it releases its essential oils without burning and becoming bitter.
  6. Take a ladle of hot broth from the pot and pour it into a small bowl. Add a sachet of saffron (or the pistils) and stir well until the powder is completely dissolved, obtaining a vibrant orange liquid. Pour the saffron infusion into the pot with the now-cooked vegetables and stir carefully. You will see the soup change colour instantly, taking on a beautiful golden hue and an intoxicating scent. Chef's tip: if using saffron pistils, let them infuse in the hot broth for at least 10 minutes before adding them, so they release maximum colour and aroma.
  7. Turn off the heat and use an immersion blender to puree the vegetables into a cream directly in the pot. Work the mixture with vertical and circular movements until you achieve a perfectly smooth, silky, and 'velvety' consistency. If the cream seems too thick, add a splash of hot broth; if it is too thin, let it thicken over the heat for another two minutes without a lid. Chef's tip: for a textbook texture, pass the velouté through a fine-mesh sieve (chinois) after blending to eliminate any small residual impurities.
  8. Taste the velouté and adjust with salt and freshly ground black pepper according to your preference. Distribute the hot cream into four deep plates or large bowls. Complete each portion with a generous handful of crunchy rosemary croutons and a drizzle of raw extra virgin olive oil. The contrast between the smoothness of the cream and the crunchiness of the bread will make the dish balanced and refined. Chef's tip: serve immediately after adding the croutons, otherwise the heat of the velouté will make them soggy quickly, losing the pleasant contrast of textures.
  9. Your velouté is ready to be enjoyed in all its elegance. It should present a brilliant yellow colour from the saffron and a rich yet light consistency on the palate. The scent of fresh rosemary should harmonise with the earthy notes of the cauliflower. It is a perfect dish as a refined starter or a healthy comfort food. Chef's tip: for an additional gourmet touch, you can decorate the plate with a few whole rosemary needles or a pinch of pink peppercorns to provide a contrasting note of colour.
