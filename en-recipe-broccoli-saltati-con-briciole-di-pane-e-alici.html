Sautéed Broccoli with Breadcrumbs and Anchovies

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

A tasty and crunchy way to enjoy broccoli, using simple ingredients from the Italian larder. The saltiness of the anchovies enhances the sweetness of the cabbage, making it irresistible.

A dish that enhances the bold flavor of broccoli through the savory contrast of anchovies and the texture of crunchy crumbs. The combination of garlic and chili creates an aromatic base typical of authentic Mediterranean culinary tradition.

Ingredients

  • Broccoli (florets)800 g
  • Stale bread (breadcrumbs)60 g
  • Anchovy fillets in oil4 pcs
  • Garlic2 cloves
  • Dried chilli flakes1 pinch
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 ml

Allergens

Gluten, Fish

Method

  1. Begin by preparing 800g of broccoli: trim the woody ends of the stalks and divide the heads into small, uniform florets, roughly 3-4 cm in diameter. Thoroughly wash the florets under cold running water to remove any grit or soil trapped within the tightly packed buds. Ensure clean cuts to prevent the tips from crumbling during the cooking process. You will be left with clean, evenly sized florets ready for blanching. The chef's tip is not to discard the tender stems: peel their outer skin and slice them into rounds, as they are the sweetest part of the vegetable.
  2. Bring a large pot of lightly salted water to a boil and submerge the prepared florets. Cook over medium heat for about 4-5 minutes, timing from when the water returns to a rolling boil. The broccoli should turn a vibrant emerald green and remain firm at the core, offering slight resistance when pierced with a fork. Do not overcook at this stage to preserve both nutrients and texture. The secret to a perfect result is to drain the broccoli while it is still 'al dente', as it will finish cooking in the pan later.
  3. Prepare a large bowl with very cold water, ideally adding a few ice cubes, and immediately plunge the drained broccoli into it. This technique, known as 'shocking' or 'refreshing', instantly stops the residual cooking and sets the chlorophyll, maintaining the vivid green colour. Leave them submerged for about 2 minutes, then drain again and pat dry with a clean kitchen towel to remove excess moisture. You will notice the broccoli stays perfectly firm and professional-looking. The tip is to dry them well to prevent hot oil splatters during the sautéing phase.
  4. Focus on the aromatic base: in a wide non-stick frying pan, pour 20ml of extra virgin olive oil (about half of the total) and add the 2 crushed garlic cloves and a pinch of dried chili. Warm over very low heat for 2 minutes, allowing the oil to become fragrant without burning the garlic, then add the 4 anchovy fillets. Use a wooden spoon to gently press down on the anchovies until they completely melt into a savoury paste. You will obtain a salty, slightly spicy base with an inviting aroma. Be careful not to raise the heat too high, or the anchovies will turn bitter.
  5. In a separate small pan, prepare the breadcrumbs: pour the remaining 20ml of extra virgin olive oil and add 60g of roughly hand-crumbled stale bread. Toast the crumbs over medium heat for about 4-5 minutes, stirring constantly to ensure an even golden-brown colour on all sides. The crumbs should become crispy and release a pleasant toasted bread fragrance. Once ready, transfer them onto kitchen paper to drain any excess oil. The chef's trick is to use rustic or sourdough bread for a crunch that stays crisp for longer.
  6. Return to the main frying pan, remove the now-golden garlic cloves, and increase the heat to medium-high before adding the well-dried broccoli. Sauté the vegetables with firm pan tosses or gentle stirring for 3-4 minutes so that every floret is coated in the anchovy-infused oil. This step is crucial for deep seasoning and removing the last traces of internal moisture. The broccoli will start to release a rich, savoury scent. My advice is not to overcrowd the pan; if necessary, sauté in two batches to keep the temperature high.
  7. Continue sautéing over high heat for another 2 minutes, watching the broccoli tips: they should start to take on a light brown colour, almost caramelising. This technical detail adds a complex toasted note that contrasts with the natural sweetness of the broccoli. Check that the final texture is perfect: tender to the bite but with a defined structure. The pan should appear dry and the vegetables glossy from the emulsion of oil and flavours. The most common mistake is not moving them enough, risking burning only one side of the florets.
  8. Transfer the piping hot broccoli to a flat serving platter, arranging them harmoniously. Only at this very moment, generously sprinkle the surface with the crispy breadcrumbs you set aside. Serving immediately is essential to prevent the residual heat and moisture from the broccoli from softening the bread, which would ruin the desired 'crunch' effect. The final result will be a balanced dish featuring the saltiness of anchovies, a hint of spice, and a dual texture of vegetable and bread. The final tip is to add the crumbs at the very last second to guarantee the best thermal and textural contrast.

Tips

  • Plunge the broccoli into ice water immediately after boiling to preserve their bright green color.
  • Toast the breadcrumbs in a separate small pan until they are well browned before combining them with the broccoli.
  • Use the tender part of the stems by cutting them into small cubes to avoid waste and add different textures.

Variations

  • Add toasted pine nuts at the end for a sweet note that balances the saltiness of the anchovies.
  • Replace the breadcrumbs with herb-flavored panko for a more complex and rich taste profile.
