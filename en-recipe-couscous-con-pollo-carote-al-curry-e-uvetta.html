Chicken, Curry Carrot and Raisin Couscous

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 20 min · Easy

A one-dish meal that plays on the sweet-savoury contrast typical of Southern Mediterranean cuisines. Very quick to prepare using basic supermarket ingredients.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🌍 International

A vibrant one-pot dish with exotic notes, where sweet raisins meet the warm aroma of curry. Fluffy couscous combines with tender chicken and carrots for a delightful contrast of textures.

Ingredients

  • Semolina couscous250 g
  • Chicken breast400 g
  • Carrots400 g
  • Raisins30 g
  • Curry powder1 cucchiaio
  • Boiling water or stock250 ml

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Begin by soaking 30g of raisins in a small bowl of warm water for about 10 minutes. This process rehydrates the fruit, making it soft and juicy within the final dish. Allow the raisins to sit until they almost double in volume and lose their leathery texture. You will be left with sweet little bursts of flavour ready to balance the saltiness of the chicken. The chef's tip is to use a splash of apple juice instead of water for an even more intense aromatic note.
  2. Take 400g of chicken breast and cut it into even 2cm cubes, then slice 400g of carrots into thin 3mm rounds. Use a sharp chef's knife and a stable cutting board to ensure uniform cuts, which is essential for even cooking. Maintaining similar sizes will allow the meat to stay juicy while the vegetables soften simultaneously. The result will be a visually tidy base that is pleasant to the bite. Tip: pat the chicken dry with kitchen paper before cutting to prevent it from slipping.
  3. Heat a drizzle of oil in a large non-stick frying pan over a medium-high heat, then add the chicken pieces. Let the meat sear for 3-4 minutes without moving it too much, allowing a golden, flavourful crust to form via the Maillard reaction. The meat should not cook through completely at this stage, but only seal on the outside to lock in the juices. You will notice a toasted aroma and an inviting amber colour. Tip: do not overcrowd the pan; if necessary, brown the chicken in two batches to avoid dropping the oil temperature.
  4. Add the carrot rounds and 1 tablespoon of curry powder to the chicken in the pan, stirring well to distribute the spices evenly. Toast the curry for about 1 minute over a medium heat, letting the heat release all the essential oils and aromas from the blend. This phase is crucial for removing the 'raw' taste of the spice powder and intensifying the golden colour of the dish. You will smell a cocooning spicy aroma permeating both the meat and the vegetables. Tip: if you enjoy spicy heat, add a pinch of chilli flakes along with the curry.
  5. Pour a small ladle of water or stock into the pan (about 50-80ml), cover with a lid, and continue cooking over a low heat for 12-15 minutes. The trapped steam will soften the carrots and bring the chicken to a perfect finish, keeping it tender and succulent inside. Occasionally check that the bottom isn't drying out too much, adding a tiny bit more water if needed to maintain moisture. By the end, the carrots should be tender but still intact, and the sauce will be slightly creamy. Tip: use vegetable stock instead of water for a deeper, more savoury flavour profile.
  6. In the meantime, place 250g of semolina couscous into a large bowl, add a tablespoon of olive oil and 250ml of lightly salted boiling water. Stir quickly with a spoon to wet all the grains, then cover the bowl with a plate or cling film for exactly 5 minutes. The residual heat will allow the grains to absorb the water through osmosis, swelling perfectly without overcooking. The couscous will initially appear as a compact but soft mass. Tip: do not use excess water; the ratio must be strictly 1:1 by weight or volume for perfect fluffing.
  7. Once the resting time has elapsed, fluff the couscous using the tines of a fork with quick, light movements from the bottom upwards. This operation separates the grains from each other, incorporating air and ensuring a soft, never sticky texture—the secret to a successful dish. This is the step that distinguishes a professional preparation from a rushed one. The grains should be well-separated, shiny, and light. Tip: fluff the couscous with extreme care; if lumps remain, the dish will feel heavy and unappealing on the palate.
  8. Squeeze the excess water from the raisins and add them to the pan with the chicken and carrots, then incorporate the fluffed couscous. Turn the heat up to maximum and sauté everything for about 1-2 minutes, tossing gently to flavour every grain with the curry seasoning. The high heat allows the couscous to absorb the juices from the pan without losing its structure. The final dish will be an explosion of warm colours, featuring the orange of the carrots and the intense gold of the curry. Tip: serve immediately, garnished with chopped fresh parsley for a herbal freshness that balances the sweetness of the raisins.

Tips

  • Fluff the couscous with a fork and a drizzle of oil to keep it light and airy.
  • Cut the chicken into uniform cubes to ensure quick and even cooking in the pan.
  • Squeeze the raisins well after soaking to avoid adding excess moisture to the final seasoning.

Variations

  • Add toasted flaked almonds at the end to introduce a crunchy element to the dish.
  • Replace raisins with chopped dried apricots for a more velvety sweetness.
