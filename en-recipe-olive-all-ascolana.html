Ascolana-style stuffed olives

Marche · 4 servings · Prep 60 min · Cooking 15 min · Hard

Tender olives stuffed with meat, breaded and fried, a specialty of Ascoli Piceno.

A specialty from the Piceno area, these stuffed olives are a masterpiece of craftsmanship and flavor. A crispy breadcrumb shell encases a tender three-meat filling and a succulent green olive.

Ingredients

  • Giant tender green olives in brine400 g
  • Beef100 g
  • Pork100 g
  • Chicken breast80 g
  • Dry white wine60 ml
  • Grated Parmigiano Reggiano40 g
  • Eggs150 g
  • Plain flour60 g
  • Breadcrumbs150 g
  • Groundnut oil for frying1000 ml
  • Nutmeg0.5 g
  • Fine salt3 g

Allergens

Gluten, Milk, Sulphites, Eggs
May contain traces of: Peanuts, Celery.

Method

  1. Start by cutting the 100g of beef, 100g of pork, and 80g of chicken breast into uniform cubes. Heat a non-stick pan and brown the meats over medium heat for about 10 minutes until they are well-colored and release an intense aroma; this stage is essential to seal in the juices and flavor the filling.
  2. Deglaze the meat mix with 60ml of dry white wine, letting it evaporate completely over high heat. You will know it is ready when you no longer smell the pungent scent of alcohol and the cooking liquid has reduced; this step serves to degrease and delicately flavor the meats.
  3. Finely grind the cooked meats using a meat grinder or a mixer, then transfer them to a bowl. Add 40g of Parmigiano Reggiano, one egg (about 50g of the 150g total), 0.5g of nutmeg, and 3g of salt, kneading until you obtain a firm, homogeneous, and easily moldable mixture.
  4. Take the 400g of olives and cut the flesh starting from the stem, rotating the olive and keeping the small knife close to the pit to obtain a continuous spiral. This is a job of patience: try not to break the peel so you can reconstruct the olive perfectly later.
  5. Take a small amount of the meat filling and form balls about the size of a cherry pit. Wrap the spiral of olive flesh around each meat ball, pressing lightly with your hands to restore the olive to its original and compact shape.
  6. Prepare three bowls for the breading: one with 60g of flour, one with the remaining 100g of beaten eggs, and the last with 150g of breadcrumbs. Coat each olive first in the flour, shaking off the excess, and then in the egg; this creates the adhesive base necessary for the outer crust.
  7. Complete the breading by rolling the olives in the 150g of breadcrumbs, pressing gently to make it adhere well over the entire surface. You will get uniform and dry spheres; a good breading prevents the filling from leaking out during cooking.
  8. Heat 1000ml of peanut oil in a high-sided pot until it reaches 170°C (340°F). Immerse a few olives at a time and fry them for 3-4 minutes until they become uniformly golden and crunchy; the constant temperature is the secret to a dry and non-greasy fry.
  9. Drain them with a slotted spoon and place them on absorbent paper for about a minute to remove excess oil. Serve them while still very hot to appreciate the contrast between the saltiness of the olive, the softness of the meat, and the crunchiness of the breading.

Tips

  • Pit the olives in a spiral shape keeping the pulp intact for a professional look.
  • Fry a few olives at a time to maintain a constant oil temperature.
  • You can prepare them in advance and freeze before frying.

Variations

  • For a different aroma, add a pinch of grated lemon zest to the meat filling.
  • Use a double breading technique for an even more pronounced crunch.
