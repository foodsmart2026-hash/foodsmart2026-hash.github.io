Pasta with Lentils and Tomato

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 45 min · Medium

A reimagining of peasant tradition that combines the creaminess of legumes with the freshness of Mediterranean tomatoes. This one-pot dish ensures a complete protein profile thanks to the pairing of whole grains and lentils.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🌱 Vegan🧒 Kid-friendly🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free

A comforting traditional dish prepared with dried lentils and high-quality peeled tomatoes. Slow cooking with classic aromatics creates a thick sauce that perfectly coats the whole grain ditalini.

Ingredients

  • Wholemeal pasta (ditalini or mixed shapes)320 g
  • Dried lentils (tiny or green variety)200 g
  • High-quality peeled plum tomatoes400 g
  • Yellow onion80 g
  • Carrot80 g
  • Celery (stalk)50 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Bay leaf1 leaf
  • Unrefined sea salt5 g
  • Hot water or vegetable stock1000 ml

Allergens

Gluten, Celery

Method

  1. Begin by thoroughly rinsing 200g of dried lentils under cold running water using a fine-mesh sieve to remove any dust or debris. Move them with your fingers until the water runs clear, then let them drain for a few minutes. This step is crucial to ensure the purity of the pulses and remove surface impurities. The lentils should appear shiny and ready for toasting. Chef's tip: always visually check for tiny stones among the lentils, a rare but possible occurrence in natural products.
  2. Prepare the aromatic base by finely chopping 80g of onion, 80g of carrot, and 50g of celery with a sharp knife on a cutting board. Aim for small, uniform cubes of about 2-3 millimetres so they almost dissolve during cooking. This requires patience but is the secret to a deep, layered flavour. The mirepoix should look colourful and consistent. Chef's tip: avoid using an electric food processor, as it tends to crush the fibres and release water, preventing proper sautéing.
  3. In a heavy-bottomed pot, heat 20ml of extra virgin olive oil over medium-low heat and add the chopped vegetables. Sauté gently for about 6-7 minutes, stirring frequently with a wooden spoon to prevent the vegetables from burning. The vegetables should soften and become translucent, releasing a sweet and inviting aroma. If you see the onion browning too quickly, add a tablespoon of water. Chef's tip: slow cooking of the base is the foundation of every great Italian soup; patience here transforms simple ingredients into a complex base.
  4. Add the drained lentils and the bay leaf to the pot, slightly increasing the heat for about 2 minutes. Stir constantly so that every lentil is coated in the fragrant oil and lightly toasts. You will notice a delicate nutty aroma arising from the heat. This process helps the lentils maintain their shape during the long cooking process. Chef's tip: toasting the pulses intensifies their flavour and creates a thermal barrier that preserves their internal texture.
  5. Pour in 400g of tinned peeled tomatoes, crushing them roughly with a fork directly in the pot, and cover with 800ml of hot water or vegetable stock. Bring to a boil, then lower the heat and simmer with the lid slightly ajar for 25-30 minutes. The lentils should become tender to the bite but not mushy, submerged in a fragrant red sauce. Ensure the liquid does not evaporate too quickly. Chef's tip: always use pre-heated liquids to avoid stalling the cooking process, otherwise the skins may toughen due to thermal shock.
  6. Add 320g of wholewheat ditalini directly into the lentil sauce and add the remaining 200ml of hot water if necessary. The pasta should be covered by about two fingers of liquid; stir frequently and vigorously to stimulate the release of starches. Cook for the time indicated on the packaging (usually 10-12 minutes) over medium heat. You will achieve a thick and coating consistency. Chef's tip: if you prefer a soupier version, add an extra ladle of water towards the end.
  7. Season with salt (approximately 5g) only in the last two minutes of the pasta's cooking, tasting to balance the flavours. Turn off the heat when the pasta is still slightly 'al dente', as residual heat will continue to cook it during the resting phase. The colour should be an intense brick red and the consistency creamy. The bay leaf has now imparted all its balsamic aroma. Chef's tip: never salt pulses at the beginning, as salt inhibits skin hydration, making them tough.
  8. Let the pasta and lentils rest for at least 2-3 minutes off the heat, covering the pot. Before serving, add the remaining 10ml of raw extra virgin olive oil and give a final vigorous stir to emulsify the fats with the pulse cream. The dish should look glossy, velvety, and incredibly fragrant. Serve in bowls, garnishing with a pinch of black pepper if desired. Chef's tip: this final rest is when the pasta absorbs the soul of the soup; never skip it.

Tips

  • Rinse the lentils carefully under running water to remove any impurities before cooking.
  • Add the sea salt only at the end of cooking to prevent the lentil skins from toughening.
  • Cook the pasta directly in the sauce, adding hot water a little at a time for a creamy result.

Variations

  • Replace the bay leaf with a sprig of fresh rosemary for a woodier and more intense aroma.
  • Use cherry tomatoes instead of peeled ones for a version with a fresher and lighter taste.
