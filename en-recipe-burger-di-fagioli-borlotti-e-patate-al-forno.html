Baked Borlotti Bean and Potato Burgers

4 servings · Prep 30 min · Cooking 20 min · Medium

A great vegetarian and budget-friendly alternative to classic meat burgers, rich in fibre and very filling. The texture is crispy on the outside and soft inside, perfect for Saturday night burger buns.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🇮🇹 Italian

Ingredients

  • Pre-cooked borlotti beans480 g
  • Yellow-fleshed potatoes300 g
  • Breadcrumbs60 g
  • Grated Pecorino (a sharp, aged Italian sheep's milk cheese)30 g
  • Medium egg1 pcs
  • Rosemary1 rametto
  • Salt and Pepperq.b.

Allergens

Gluten, Eggs, Milk

Method

  1. Begin by boiling 300g of whole yellow-fleshed potatoes with their skins on in a large pot filled with plenty of salted cold water. Bring to a boil and cook for about 25 minutes over medium-high heat until the core is tender when pierced with a fork. Once cooked, drain the potatoes and peel them while still hot to make the task easier, being careful not to burn your fingers. You will have soft, starchy potatoes, the ideal base for providing structure to the burger without making it heavy. Chef's tip: Starting with cold water ensures even cooking and prevents the potato from falling apart on the outside while remaining raw in the centre.
  2. Take 480g of pre-cooked borlotti beans, drain them from their canning liquid, and rinse thoroughly under cold running water to remove excess salt. Place them in a large bowl and begin to coarsely mash them with a fork or a manual potato masher. Do not aim for a smooth consistency; leave about a third of the beans in larger pieces to ensure an interesting texture when bitten. The result should be a rustic, partially grainy mass. Chef's tip: Dry the beans well after rinsing to prevent the mixture from becoming too wet and difficult to shape.
  3. Use a potato masher to reduce the hot potatoes into a fluffy purée directly over the beans in the bowl. Mix the two ingredients with a wooden spoon until you obtain a base mixture that is homogeneous but with a coarse grain. Allow the mix to cool slightly for a couple of minutes before proceeding, so that the heat doesn't risk prematurely cooking the egg you will add shortly. The mass should appear compact and a lovely golden-beige colour. Chef's tip: Mashing the potatoes while hot is essential so that the starch acts as a natural binder between the legume fibres.
  4. Add 30g of grated pecorino cheese, one finely chopped rosemary sprig, and a pinch of salt and pepper to the bean and potato base. Crack one medium egg into the centre of the bowl and begin working the mixture with your hands to distribute the seasonings evenly. Spend a couple of minutes on this task so that every bite is perfectly balanced between the saltiness of the cheese and the freshness of the herb. The mixture will be very fragrant and slightly sticky. Chef's tip: Do not over-salt, as the pecorino and canned beans already contain a significant amount of sodium.
  5. Gradually incorporate 60g of breadcrumbs by sprinkling them over the mixture while continuing to knead energetically with your hands. Assess the consistency step by step: the mixture is perfect when it no longer sticks to the sides of the bowl and is as mouldable as soft shortcrust pastry. If you feel the mass is still too soft, add the last tablespoon of breadcrumbs; otherwise, stop to avoid making the burgers too dry after cooking. The result will be a firm, homogeneous ball of dough. Chef's tip: Let the mixture rest for 5 minutes; this gives the breadcrumbs time to absorb residual moisture, making shaping much easier.
  6. Divide the mixture into 8 equal portions of about 100-110g each and form smooth balls by rolling them between your palms. Gently press them down to create discs about 2 cm thick, ensuring the edges are compact and free of cracks that could open during cooking. Place the burgers on a baking tray lined with greaseproof paper, previously brushed with a thin film of extra virgin olive oil. You will have 8 uniform and inviting burgers ready for the oven. Chef's tip: If the mixture sticks to your palms, slightly dampen your hands with water or a drop of oil for a smoother finish.
  7. Preheat the oven to 200°C (fan setting) and place the tray on the middle shelf for even heat distribution. Bake for the first 10 minutes, then remove the tray and, using a flat spatula, flip the burgers with a firm yet gentle movement to avoid breaking them. Continue cooking for another 10 minutes until the surface appears well-browned and crispy. You will smell the delicious aroma of toasted rosemary filling the kitchen. Chef's tip: Using the fan function is the secret to achieving a tasty outer crust while keeping the heart of the burger moist.
  8. Once cooking is complete, remove the tray from the oven and let the burgers rest for about 2-3 minutes before handling them. This brief stabilisation time is crucial: the residual heat finishes compacting the fibres, ensuring the burger doesn't fall apart when you lift it with the spatula. Serve the burgers hot, perhaps accompanied by a light mayonnaise or a side of fresh vegetables. The final result will be a rustic vegetarian main course, firm on the outside and pleasantly soft on the inside. Chef's tip: Don't be in a hurry to serve; a few minutes of resting transforms the texture from fragile to perfectly compact.
