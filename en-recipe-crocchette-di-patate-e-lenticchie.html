Potato and Lentil Croquettes

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 30 min · Medium

A nutritious snack that transforms pulses into a finger food loved by the whole family thanks to its delicious texture. This budget-friendly recipe uses basic Italian pantry staples to create a balanced, high-fibre starter.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🥕 Vegetarian🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free

Ingredients

  • Yellow-fleshed potatoes500 g
  • Cooked lentils (boiled or canned, well drained)240 g
  • Egg1 pz
  • Wholemeal breadcrumbs100 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Chopped fresh parsley10 g
  • Organic lemon zest5 g
  • Unrefined sea salt3 g

Allergens

Gluten, Eggs

Method

  1. Start by thoroughly washing 500g of yellow potatoes and place them in a large pot covered with cold, slightly salted water. Bring to a boil and cook them whole with their skins for about 25-30 minutes, checking doneness with a fork which should slide in without resistance. You will achieve a perfect, non-watery starchy base, essential for the croquettes' structural integrity. Chef's advice: boil potatoes with the skin on to prevent excess water absorption, keeping the dough firm.
  2. Drain the potatoes and, while still hot, peel them carefully and immediately pass them through a potato ricer into a large bowl. Let the mash cool for about 10 minutes to prevent the residual heat from scrambling the egg you'll add later. The consistency should be smooth, fluffy, and lump-free. Chef's advice: never use an immersion blender for potatoes, as it breaks down the starches too much, making the mixture gluey.
  3. Take 240g of pre-cooked lentils, rinse under running water to remove excess salt or canning liquid, and dry them meticulously with paper towels. Blend half of the lentils roughly with a hand blender, leaving the other half whole to create a textural contrast. The result will be a mix of a binding cream and small whole pulses that provide bite. Chef's advice: the drier the lentils, the less breadcrumbs you'll need, keeping the croquettes lighter.
  4. In the bowl with the potatoes, add the lentils (both the paste and whole), 1 whole egg, 10g of chopped parsley, 5g of lemon zest, 3g of salt, and 40g of wholemeal breadcrumbs. Knead the mixture vigorously by hand for about 2-3 minutes until all aromatics are well distributed and the mass feels compact. You should get a malleable dough that smells of fresh lemon and parsley. Chef's advice: add the lemon zest last to preserve the volatile essential oils and maintain a vibrant aroma.
  5. Prepare a small bowl of water to slightly dampen your hands and begin shaping the croquettes by taking about 40-50g of dough at a time. Form them into cylinders or flattened discs, roughly the size of a walnut, ensuring they are all the same size for even cooking. The croquettes should look smooth and free of surface cracks. Chef's advice: don't make them too large, or they will be difficult to flip in the oven without breaking.
  6. Pour the remaining 60g of wholemeal breadcrumbs onto a flat plate and roll each croquette in them, pressing gently with your palm to ensure the coating sticks well. Ensure every side, including the edges, is perfectly covered in breadcrumbs to guarantee external crunchiness. The result is a uniformly breaded croquette ready for browning. Chef's advice: gently shake each piece after breading to remove excess crumbs that might burn in the oven.
  7. Line a baking tray with parchment paper and arrange the croquettes with plenty of space between them to allow hot air to circulate freely. Drizzle them uniformly with 30ml of extra virgin olive oil, perhaps using a pastry brush for more even distribution. The croquettes will appear glossy and ready for the Maillard reaction to develop colour and flavour. Chef's advice: heat needs to hit every side, so do not overcrowd the tray or the croquettes will turn out soggy.
  8. Place in a preheated fan oven at 200°C and bake for about 20 minutes in total. Halfway through (after about 10 minutes), turn the croquettes extremely gently using a spatula to ensure even browning on both sides. By the end, the croquettes should be crunchy to the touch and a beautiful dark amber colour. Chef's advice: switch on the grill for the last 2 minutes for extra crispiness, but watch closely to prevent the wholemeal breadcrumbs from over-darkening.
