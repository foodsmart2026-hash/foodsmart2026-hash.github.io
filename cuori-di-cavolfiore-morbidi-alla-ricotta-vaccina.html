Soft Cauliflower Hearts with Cows' Milk Ricotta

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

White cauliflower becomes creamy and delicate when paired with fresh ricotta. An excellent way to introduce cruciferous vegetables with a safe texture.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⬇️ Big saving🥕 Vegetarian🧒 Kid-friendly🌾 Gluten-free🇮🇹 Italian

This preparation transforms cauliflower into a soft and enveloping cream thanks to the addition of fresh ricotta. The aroma of nutmeg completes the dish with an elegant spicy note.

Ingredients

  • White cauliflower400 g
  • Fresh cows' milk ricotta (soft Italian whey cheese)80 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil10 g
  • Nutmeg1 pinch

Allergens

Milk

Method

  1. Begin by taking the 400g of white cauliflower and cleaning it by removing the outer leaves and the tough central core. Using a sharp knife, divide the head into small, evenly-sized florets to ensure consistent cooking throughout the dish. Thoroughly wash the florets under a cold running tap in a colander to remove any debris. You should end up with clean, white 'hearts' ready for the next stage. Chef's tip: do not discard the tender stalks; peel them and cook them with the florets to minimise food waste.
  2. Fill a large pot with approximately 1.5 litres of water and bring it to a boil over a high heat, without adding salt to preserve the vegetable's natural sweetness. Once the water has reached a rolling boil, gently submerge the 400g of prepared cauliflower florets. Ensure the water completely covers the vegetables so the temperature remains constant for every piece. The water should continue to simmer gently throughout the process. Chef's tip: adding a tablespoon of milk to the boiling water will help the cauliflower maintain its bright white colour.
  3. Boil the cauliflower for about 15 minutes over a medium heat. For this recipe, we are looking for a very soft texture, well beyond the classic 'al dente' stage, to allow the fibres to break down easily. Check the progress by piercing a floret with a fork: the prongs should glide through without any resistance, almost as if the cauliflower has become buttery. The final result should be very tender and slightly opaque vegetables. Chef's tip: if the florets are very small, check them after 12 minutes to prevent them from breaking up too much in the water.
  4. Drain the cauliflower using a fine-mesh colander, being careful not to break the florets excessively at this stage and avoiding the hot steam. Immediately transfer the steaming vegetables into a large bowl or a deep plate with high sides. It is vital that the cauliflower is well-drained to prevent any residual water from making the final dish too runny. The vegetables should appear dry but glossy and very hot. Chef's tip: gently shake the colander for about 30 seconds to encourage any excess moisture to evaporate.
  5. Use a fork to vigorously mash the hot florets directly inside the bowl. Work the cauliflower with steady pressing motions until you achieve a fine, soft pulp, while still maintaining a slightly grainy, artisanal texture. Avoid using an immersion blender because the consistency should remain rustic and not become a liquid purée. You will get a thick, fragrant vegetable base that is easy to shape. Chef's tip: mash the cauliflower while it is piping hot, as hot fibres are much easier to pulp than cooled ones.
  6. Take 80g of fresh cow's milk ricotta, which you should have previously drained in a sieve to remove excess whey, and add it to the cauliflower pulp. Immediately add a pinch of freshly grated nutmeg, which will provide a warm and comforting aromatic note to the dish. Start incorporating the two ingredients using a wooden spoon or a spatula, using the heat of the cauliflower to gently melt the ricotta. The aroma will instantly become more complex and milky. Chef's tip: ensure the ricotta is at room temperature so it doesn't drastically lower the temperature of the vegetables.
  7. Carefully mix the preparation until the ricotta and cauliflower are perfectly and homogeneously combined. Continue working the mixture until you no longer see separate white traces of cheese from the creamy-white cauliflower, achieving a thick and fluffy cream. The consistency should be velvety on the palate, with the ricotta acting as a natural binder between the small pieces of vegetable. The visual result will be a creamy, inviting mixture with a cream-white colour. Chef's tip: use a folding motion from bottom to top to incorporate air and make the dish fluffier.
  8. Drizzle 10g of extra virgin olive oil over the still-warm preparation. Mix one last time with quick movements to emulsify the oil with the pulp, giving a silky sheen to the entire dish. This final step not only adds a fruity flavour note but also makes the texture even smoother and more pleasant. You will have a glossy dish, smelling of good oil and spices, ready for the final check. Chef's tip: use a delicate extra virgin olive oil (such as a Ligurian variety) to avoid overpowering the gentle taste of the ricotta.
  9. Before serving, check that the temperature is warm and that the consistency is suitable for the needs of those who will consume it. If the mixture appears slightly too dry, you can add a teaspoon of hot cooking water to soften it further. Divide the preparation into 4 equal portions, serving them in small bowls or arranging them harmoniously on plates. The final result is a healthy, soft comfort food with an extremely delicate flavour. Chef's tip: serve the dish immediately, as ricotta tends to dry out quickly once it cools down.

Tips

  • Drain the ricotta very well before adding it to the cauliflower to avoid a watery consistency.
  • Mash the cauliflower while it is still hot to achieve a smooth and velvety texture.
  • You can store the cream in the refrigerator for one day in an airtight container.

Variations

  • Replace nutmeg with freshly ground black pepper for a sharper taste.
  • For a citrus note, add some grated zest from an organic lemon before serving.
