Fluffy Carrot and Orange Cake

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 45 min · Hard

A vitamin-rich, colourful cake prepared with freshly grated carrots and orange juice. The lack of butter makes it ideal for a healthy breakfast or a light snack.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🥕 Vegetarian🧒 Kid-friendly🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free

A soft and fragrant cake characterized by the vibrant meeting of fresh carrots and intense orange aroma. Its light texture makes it perfect for a children's snack or a wholesome breakfast. The bright orange color is immediately inviting.

Ingredients

  • Peeled carrots300 g
  • Plain flour (extra-fine Italian '00' flour)200 g
  • Caster sugar100 g
  • Medium eggs2 pcs
  • Sunflower oil80 ml
  • Orange (juice and zest)1 pcs
  • Baking powder12 g

Allergens

Gluten, Eggs

Method

  1. Begin by preparing 300g of cleaned carrots: grate them finely using a small-hole grater to achieve a near-velvety pulp. Once grated, carefully pat them dry with kitchen paper to remove any excess vegetable water, which would otherwise hinder the rising process. This step takes about 5 minutes and the result should be a carrot mass that feels damp but not wet to the touch. Chef's tip: never skip drying the carrots, as excess moisture is the main reason cakes end up 'cloggy' or rubbery in the middle.
  2. In a large mixing bowl, crack 2 medium room-temperature eggs and add 100g of caster sugar. Use an electric whisk at maximum speed for at least 5-7 minutes, beating the mixture until it becomes pale, thick, and has tripled in its original volume. The secret is to incorporate as much air as possible; when you lift the whisk, the mixture should leave a visible 'ribbon' on the surface for a few seconds. Chef's tip: use eggs that aren't fridge-cold to achieve a much more stable and voluminous foam.
  3. Reduce the whisk speed to the lowest setting and slowly drizzle in 80ml of sunflower oil, followed by the strained juice of one orange. It is vital to add the liquids very slowly so as not to deflate the airy structure previously created with the eggs. Continue mixing for about a minute until you have a smooth, lightly scented emulsion. Chef's tip: pour the oil down the side of the bowl rather than directly into the centre to keep the lightness of the mixture intact.
  4. At this stage, put away the electric whisk and use a silicone spatula. Add the well-dried grated carrots and the grated orange zest, folding them into the batter with very gentle circular motions. Ensure the carrots do not clump at the bottom but are evenly suspended throughout the liquid. Chef's tip: only grate the orange part of the zest, avoiding the white pith which would impart an unpleasant bitter aftertaste to the cake.
  5. Carefully sift 200g of plain flour together with 12g of baking powder directly over the bowl containing the wet mixture. Sifting is crucial to remove lumps and to further aerate the dry ingredients before they meet the liquids. You should achieve a very fine powder that settles like snow on the surface of the batter. Chef's tip: sift the flour and baking powder together at least twice if you want a cake with an exceptionally fine and even crumb.
  6. Incorporate the dry ingredients using slow movements from the bottom upwards, while simultaneously rotating the bowl with your other hand. Add the flour in two or three batches, waiting until the previous one is fully absorbed before adding the next. The final result should be a bright orange batter, thick yet still pourable, and free from any visible streaks of flour. Chef's tip: never over-mix at this stage, otherwise you will develop the gluten, making the cake tough rather than fluffy.
  7. Prepare a 22cm bundt or cake tin by brushing it with a thin layer of sunflower oil and dusting it with a pinch of flour, tapping out the excess. Pour the batter starting from the centre and letting it spread naturally towards the edges, without forcing it flat with the spatula. The tin should be filled to about two-thirds of its height to allow for optimal rising. Chef's tip: gently tap the tin on the worktop to remove any large air bubbles trapped at the bottom.
  8. Bake in a preheated conventional oven at 175°C on the middle shelf for approximately 45 minutes. It is essential not to open the oven door for at least the first 30-35 minutes, as the temperature drop would cause the cake to sink instantly. The cake is ready when the surface appears golden and a wooden skewer inserted into the centre comes out dry and clean. Chef's tip: if you notice the top browning too quickly after 30 minutes, cover it loosely with a piece of foil without touching the batter.
  9. Once removed from the oven, let the cake rest in the tin for about 10 minutes, then carefully turn it out onto a wire cooling rack. Cooling on a rack is fundamental to allow the residual moisture from the carrots to evaporate properly from the bottom, preventing the base from becoming soggy. Wait until it is completely cold before slicing to allow the orange aromas to settle. Chef's tip: serve the cake with a dusting of icing sugar or, for a gourmet touch, accompany it with a dollop of plain Greek yoghurt.

Tips

  • Grate the carrots very finely so they almost completely melt into the batter during baking.
  • Beat the eggs and sugar for a long time to incorporate air and achieve a fluffier cake.
  • For optimal storage, wrap the cooled cake in plastic wrap to keep it moist for the following days.

Variations

  • Enrich the batter with a pinch of ground ginger for a spicy and slightly pungent note.
  • You can replace the sunflower oil with rice oil to achieve an even more neutral and delicate flavor.
