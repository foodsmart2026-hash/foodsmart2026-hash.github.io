Cardamom Arroz con Leche

4 servings · Prep 5 min · Cooking 50 min · Medium

A creamy rice pudding loved across Latin America. Fragrant with warm spices, it’s an affordable treat for the whole family.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🥕 Vegetarian🌾 Gluten-free🌍 International

Ingredients

  • Originario rice (a common Italian short-grain variety)150 g
  • Whole milk1 l
  • Sugar100 g
  • Lemon zest1 pcs
  • Cardamom pods (or cinnamon)3 pcs
  • Salt1 pinch

Allergens

Milk

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the aromatic base by pouring 1 litre of whole milk into a heavy-based saucepan, which is essential to prevent the milk from scorching. Add the zest of one whole lemon (taking care to use only the yellow part) and the 3 cardamom pods, which should be lightly crushed with the back of a knife to release their essential oils. Heat over a medium flame until you see small bubbles appearing on the surface, indicating the milk has almost reached a boil. The scent of the citrus and spices will begin to infuse, creating a fragrant base for the rice. A chef's tip: crush the cardamom pods just enough to crack them without pulverising them, so they are easy to remove later.
  2. When the milk is hot, pour in 150g of Originario rice and add a pinch of salt to balance the flavours. Immediately turn the heat down to the lowest setting: the milk should just simmer gently to allow the rice to cook slowly without boiling over. Stir thoroughly with a wooden spoon to distribute the grains evenly. At this stage, the mixture will be very liquid, and the rice will just be starting to move within it. My advice is not to cover the pot completely with a lid, but to leave it slightly ajar to allow excess steam to escape.
  3. Continue cooking for approximately 35-40 minutes, paying close attention to how often you stir the mixture. You must stir roughly every 5 minutes, running the spoon along the bottom of the pan to prevent the starch from settling and sticking. This technique encourages the gradual release of starch from the Originario rice, transforming the milk into a velvety cream. You will see the mixture slowly reduce and the grains swell, becoming increasingly tender. Be careful to maintain a steady flame: if the milk boils too vigorously, it will evaporate before the rice is fully cooked.
  4. Monitor the consistency of the dessert around the 30-minute mark; the rice should feel very tender when bitten, and the milk should have achieved the density of a fluid cream. Do not wait until it is too dry on the stove, as the rice will continue to absorb moisture even after the heat is turned off. The ideal consistency is like a very runny risotto or a thick soup. If you notice the milk has reduced too quickly, you can add a splash of extra warm milk. The chef's tip is to always taste a grain: if the centre of the rice is still hard, continue for another 5 minutes.
  5. Once the desired creaminess is reached, stir in the 100g of sugar by sprinkling it over the rice. Stir vigorously for about 5-10 minutes while keeping the flame very low. As the sugar dissolves, it will create a syrupy effect that makes the dessert glossy and even more indulgent on the palate. You will notice the mixture becomes slightly thinner for a moment before settling into a silky cream. Remember that adding sugar too early would prevent the rice from cooking through properly, so always respect this order of ingredients.
  6. Just before turning off the heat, use kitchen tongs or a spoon to find and remove the lemon zest and the 3 cardamom pods. This step ensures that no one encounters tough or overly bitter pieces while eating. The cream should now have an ivory-white colour and an extremely inviting, fragrant appearance. Ensure you leave no spice residues behind, especially if serving to children. The chef's final touch is to give it one last vigorous stir to ensure the density is uniform throughout before serving.
  7. Turn off the heat and immediately pour the Arroz con Leche into four individual ceramic or glass bowls. Distribute the portions evenly, levelling the surface with the back of a spoon if necessary. Allow to cool at room temperature for at least 20 minutes; you will see a light protective film form, which keeps the heart of the dessert soft. The cardamom aroma will become more persistent as the temperature drops. If you prefer a firmer version, you can place the bowls in the fridge once they are cold.
  8. When ready to serve, decorate each portion with a dusting of ground cinnamon or, if you prefer, a whole cardamom pod just for aesthetics. The dessert can be enjoyed warm to enhance the spicy notes or cold from the fridge as a refreshing end to a meal. The final consistency should be that of a soft pudding, not too firm, that melts in the mouth. My final tip: if serving cold the next day, add a tablespoon of cold milk to the top before eating to restore its original creaminess.
