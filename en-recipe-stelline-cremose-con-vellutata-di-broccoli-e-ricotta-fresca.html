Creamy Star Pasta with Broccoli Velouté and Fresh Ricotta

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 20 min · Easy

A delicate first course that transforms broccoli into a velvety cream, perfect for helping little ones enjoy green vegetables. The ricotta adds a soft protein note and an enveloping texture without being heavy.

A soft and colorful dish, perfect for everyday meals that require simplicity and flavor. The cow's milk ricotta adds a milky note that balances the broccoli's flavor, creating a silky texture.

Ingredients

  • Star-shaped pasta (Stelline)160 g
  • Broccoli (florets)300 g
  • Fresh cow's milk ricotta (a soft, mild Italian whey cheese)100 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil20 ml
  • Water or unsalted vegetable stock500 ml

Allergens

Gluten, Milk

Method

  1. Begin by carefully preparing 300g of fresh broccoli, using a small paring knife to separate the tender florets from the main stalk. Remove any tough or fibrous parts, then wash the florets under cold running water to clear away any soil residue. This task takes about 5 minutes and ensures your velouté base is exceptionally tender and easy to digest. The chef's secret is to cut larger florets into uniform bite-sized pieces to guarantee even cooking throughout.
  2. Prepare the liquid base by pouring 500ml of water or unsalted vegetable stock into a medium-sized saucepan. Bring the liquid to a boil over a high heat, covering with a lid to speed up the process and save energy. Once a vigorous boil is reached, you will see large bubbles breaking the surface. My professional advice is to avoid adding salt at this stage, particularly if the meal is for young children, to preserve the natural, sweet flavour of the vegetables.
  3. Carefully lower the broccoli into the boiling liquid and reduce the heat slightly to a medium flame. Let them cook for approximately 15 minutes without a lid, ensuring the vegetables remain fully submerged at all times. By the end of this time, the florets should be very soft and collapse easily when pressed with the tines of a fork. Be careful not to overcook them to the point of turning brown; you want to maintain a vibrant green colour and the broccoli's nutritional profile.
  4. Drain the broccoli using a slotted spoon and transfer them to a high-sided jug or bowl, making sure to keep all the cooking liquid in the saucepan. Use an immersion blender to purée the florets, adding just one tablespoon of the reserved broth to help the blades emulsify the mixture. Blend at high speed until you achieve a perfectly smooth, thick, and lump-free cream with an intense vegetable aroma. For a truly professional, silky texture, you can pass the purée through a fine-mesh sieve.
  5. Bring the remaining cooking liquid back to a boil and stir in 160g of stelline pasta, mixing immediately with a wooden spoon to prevent the stars from sticking to the bottom. Cook over a medium heat for the time indicated on the packet, usually between 5 and 7 minutes, maintaining a constant but gentle simmer. The pasta should absorb some of the broth, becoming tender while keeping its star shape. Do not drain too early; the final result should be a thick, spoonable consistency rather than dry pasta.
  6. While the pasta finishes cooking, take a moment to prepare the creamy component by combining 100g of fresh cow's ricotta and 20ml of extra virgin olive oil in a small bowl. Work the mixture vigorously with a fork or a small hand whisk for about 2 minutes until it forms a smooth paste. You will obtain a glossy, fragrant white cream that acts as the perfect binder for the dish. A top tip is to add a teaspoon of the warm pasta water to make the ricotta even more fluid and easy to mix.
  7. Once the stelline are cooked, turn off the heat and stir the previously prepared broccoli velouté directly into the saucepan with the pasta. Mix gently using circular motions to distribute the green colour evenly across all the pasta stars. The consistency at this stage should be that of a rich cream, where the pasta is completely enveloped by the sauce. If it appears too dry, simply add a final splash of the reserved cooking liquid to loosen the mixture.
  8. Complete the dish by folding in the ricotta and oil mixture with the heat strictly turned off to preserve the dairy's fresh texture. Stir thoroughly until the white of the ricotta has merged with the green of the broccoli, creating an inviting pastel shade and a velvety mouthfeel. Allow the dish to rest for a minute so the flavours can settle and the temperature drops to lukewarm, which is ideal for children. Serve each portion with a final drizzle of raw oil to highlight the fruity notes of the olives.
  9. Before serving, check the temperature of the dish by placing a small amount on your wrist; the velouté should be pleasantly warm, never hot. For the adults in the family, you can customise the meal directly in individual bowls with a pinch of sea salt and a crack of fresh black pepper. The final result is a balanced starter with a sweet, delicate flavour that will delight both adults and children with its extreme smoothness. Remember: serving in a colourful bowl makes the meal even more appealing to little ones.

Tips

  • Cook the stars directly in the broccoli cooking liquid to preserve all the flavors.
  • Whisk the ricotta at room temperature with a fork before adding it to the cream.
  • Eat the dish as soon as it is ready to enjoy the maximum creaminess of the ricotta.

Variations

  • Replace the broccoli with cauliflower for a lighter-colored version with a sweeter taste.
  • Add a drizzle of raw olive oil and some grated Parmesan cheese to enrich the dish.
