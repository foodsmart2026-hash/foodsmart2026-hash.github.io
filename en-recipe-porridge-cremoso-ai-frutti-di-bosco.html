Creamy Mixed Berry Porridge

4 servings · Prep 5 min · Cooking 10 min · Easy

A warm and comforting breakfast made with budget-friendly oats and frozen mixed berries. It is a quick, fiber-rich solution for the whole family.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🥕 Vegetarian🧒 Kid-friendly🌍 International

A classic Anglo-Saxon breakfast staple revisited with a colorful twist of forest berries. The consistency is thick and creamy, featuring a perfect balance between the sweetness of honey and the tartness of red berries.

Ingredients

  • Porridge oats160 g
  • Semi-skimmed milk600 ml
  • Water200 ml
  • Frozen forest fruits150 g
  • Honey40 g
  • Chia seeds (optional)10 g

Allergens

Gluten, Milk

Method

  1. In a thick-bottomed saucepan, combine 160g of oat flakes, 600ml of semi-skimmed milk, and 200ml of water. Stir gently while cold with a wooden spoon to evenly distribute the oats and prevent them from clumping at the bottom. Turn the heat to medium to begin warming the mixture, monitoring the temperature closely. The visual result should be a fluid, whitish mixture with clearly separated flakes. Chef's Tip: Use a heavy-based saucepan to ensure even heat distribution and prevent localised scorching.
  2. Bring the mixture almost to a boil, keeping the heat on medium and paying attention to the edges of the pan where bubbles will start to form. Continue stirring regularly to prevent the milk proteins from forming a skin on the surface. This phase takes about 3-4 minutes and prepares the oats for the release of their starches. You will know this step is successful when steam begins to rise steadily. Chef's Tip: Never leave the stove unattended, as milk can rise and boil over very quickly once it hits boiling point.
  3. As soon as the liquid begins to simmer, immediately reduce the heat to low and continue cooking gently for about 5-8 minutes. During this stage, it is vital to stir continuously in a 'figure of eight' motion to encourage the starches to thicken the mixture. The oats should absorb the liquid slowly, swelling up and making the mixture increasingly cohesive. You will achieve a thick, velvety consistency where the oats hold their shape while being very soft. Chef's Tip: If you prefer a silkier texture, lightly press the flakes against the sides of the pan during cooking.
  4. While the porridge is cooking, prepare the topping by placing 150g of frozen berries in a small saucepan with a tablespoon of water. Heat over medium-low for about 3 minutes, lightly crushing some of the berries with the back of a spoon to release their natural juices. The fruit will soften, creating a rustic, fragrant coulis with a deep red-purple colour. The sauce should be glossy and slightly syrupy, but with the fruit still partially intact. Chef's Tip: Avoid adding sugar to the berries; their natural acidity will perfectly balance the sweetness of the porridge.
  5. Check the final consistency of the porridge: it should drop slowly from the spoon without appearing too dry or, conversely, watery. If you are happy with the texture, turn off the heat and move the pan away from the hot ring. The porridge should look creamy and have a delicate scent of milk and cereal. Remember that the mass will continue to thicken slightly over the next few minutes due to residual heat. Chef's Tip: If the mixture seems too thick, add a tablespoon of warm milk at the last moment to adjust the fluidity.
  6. Now add 40g of honey to the hot porridge with the heat off, incorporating it with vigorous circular movements. This method allows the honey to melt instantly without altering its nutritional properties through excessive heat. The sweetness will be evenly distributed throughout the cream, also giving the mixture a glossy finish. The result will be a porridge with a balanced flavour and a brilliant finish. Chef's Tip: Adding the honey at the end prevents the sugars from caramelising or sticking to the bottom during the long cooking process.
  7. Stir 10g of chia seeds into the porridge and mix well so they are evenly distributed without forming lumps. Let everything rest for one minute; the seeds will begin to hydrate, providing more structure and a light crunchy contrast. You will see small black dots appearing throughout the oat cream, enriching it visually. This technical pause also allows the porridge temperature to become ideal for eating. Chef's Tip: Chia seeds are an excellent natural thickener; always use them to add body to mixtures that are too fluid.
  8. Divide the steaming porridge into 4 individual bowls, smoothing the surface with the back of a wet spoon. Pour the previously prepared hot berry sauce over each portion, creating a chromatic contrast between the ivory oats and the purple fruit. If desired, finish with a pinch of cinnamon to add a warm, spicy aromatic note. Serve immediately to enjoy the maximum creaminess. Chef's Tip: Briefly warm the bowls with hot water before filling them to keep your breakfast hot until the very last spoonful.

Tips

  • Cook over very low heat to prevent the oats from sticking to the bottom of the pan.
  • If you prefer a thinner consistency, add a splash of extra milk at the end.
  • Stir in the honey only after turning off the heat to preserve its aromatic profile.

Variations

  • Add a tablespoon of chia seeds halfway through cooking for an even thicker consistency.
  • Use fresh forest berries instead of frozen ones for a firmer topping.
