Ditalini in Potato Broth with Silky Eggs

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

A thick and comforting soup, typical of winter evenings, where eggs are added at the end of cooking to create a rich texture. It is very affordable and a favourite with children.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🥕 Vegetarian🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🇮🇹 Italian

Ingredients

  • Ditalini pasta (small, short tube-shaped pasta)250 g
  • Potatoes400 g
  • Eggs2 pcs
  • White onion1 pc
  • Fresh parsley1 bunch

Allergens

Gluten, Eggs

Method

  1. Start by preparing the aromatic base: peel the white onion and finely mince it with a sharp knife on a chopping board. In a large, high-sided saucepan, pour a drizzle of extra virgin olive oil and add the onion, letting it sweat over very low heat for about 5-7 minutes. You want the onion to become translucent and soft without browning or burning, as this would release a bitter aftertaste. My chef's tip is to add a tablespoon of water if you notice the onion frying too quickly, allowing it to stew gently instead.
  2. While the onion is sweating, handle the starchy component: peel the 400g of potatoes and cut them into very small cubes, about 1cm each. This small size is essential as it ensures rapid cooking and encourages the release of starches, which will make the broth naturally velvety. Rinse the cubes under cold running water if you prefer a clearer broth, or add them directly for a thicker consistency. Once ready, the cubes should look uniform to guarantee even cooking throughout.
  3. Add the potatoes to the now-translucent onion and sauté for a couple of minutes, stirring with a wooden spoon to coat them well. Cover everything with about 1.5 litres of pre-heated water so as not to drop the cooking temperature, and bring to a boil over medium heat. Simmer for about 15 minutes, or until the potatoes are tender when pierced with the tines of a fork. The aroma of the broth will start to intensify, and the potatoes will begin to slightly break down at the edges.
  4. To achieve an even creamier and thicker base, use the mechanical pressure technique: with the back of a fork or a spoon, roughly mash about a third of the potato cubes against the sides of the pot. Stir the broth well to incorporate the resulting potato pulp, which will act as a natural thickener, transforming the water into an opaque, rich soup. Taste the broth and season with salt at this stage, keeping in mind that the pasta will absorb some of the saltiness. You will obtain a base with a rustic and comforting consistency.
  5. Bring the broth back to a lively boil and pour the 250g of ditalini pasta directly into the pot. Cook the pasta for the time indicated on the packet, usually between 8 and 10 minutes, stirring occasionally to prevent the ditalini from sticking to the bottom. The pasta should cook 'risotto-style' in the potato broth, absorbing all the flavours and releasing its own starch. The final result should be a soup that is neither too dry nor too watery, with the pasta being perfectly al dente.
  6. While the pasta cooks, prepare the egg finish: crack the 2 eggs into a small bowl and whisk them lightly with a hand whisk or a fork, along with a pinch of salt and pepper. Finely chop the bunch of fresh parsley and fold it into the egg mixture, stirring until you have a homogeneous liquid. Do not over-beat the eggs; the goal is simply to combine the yolks and whites. This preparation will be the 'stringy' and protein-rich heart of your dish, ready to be poured in at the very last moment.
  7. At the end of the pasta's cooking time, turn off the heat immediately: this step is crucial to prevent the egg from overcooking and becoming rubbery. Pour the egg and parsley mixture in a thin stream over the boiling soup, immediately starting to stir with a quick, circular motion using a whisk or fork. The residual heat of the broth will cook the egg instantly, creating small, soft, and light flakes distributed evenly. You will see the soup transform into a rich and fragrant 'stracciatella' style dish.
  8. Cover the pot with a lid and let the soup rest for exactly one minute before serving in deep bowls. This short rest allows the flavours to settle and the texture to become perfectly velvety and 'filante' (stringy). Serve the ditalini very hot, completing the dish if you like with a final drizzle of raw olive oil and a grating of black pepper. The chef's secret is to serve immediately: if left to sit too long, the pasta will continue to absorb the liquid and the soup will become too thick.
