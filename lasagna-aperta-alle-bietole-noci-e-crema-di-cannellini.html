Open lasagna with Swiss chard, walnuts and cannellini bean cream

Toscana · 4 servings · Prep 30 min · Cooking 40 min · Medium

A comforting autumn first course where Swiss chard is the star, used both as a cream and sautéed, paired with the sweetness of pulses and the crunchiness of walnuts.

🌿 Seasonal recipe🇮🇹 Italian

A modern take on lasagna, served open to highlight the beauty of each layer. The cannellini bean cream replaces traditional béchamel, providing a velvety contrast to the crunchy toasted walnuts.

Ingredients

  • Fresh Swiss chard800g
  • Egg lasagna sheets250g
  • Pre-cooked cannellini beans300g
  • Walnut halves60g
  • Grated Pecorino Toscano PDO100g
  • Whole milk200ml
  • Shallot1
  • Extra virgin olive oilas required
  • Saltas required
  • Black pepperas required
  • Nutmeg1 pinch

Allergens

Tree nuts, Gluten, Milk, Eggs
May contain traces of: Celery.

Method

  1. Begin by thoroughly washing 800g of Swiss chard, using a sharp knife to separate the tough white stalks from the tender green leaves. Cut the stalks into roughly 2cm pieces and boil them in plenty of salted water over medium heat for about 10 minutes. You want the stalks to be very tender, easily pierced with a fork. Drain them well and let them cool slightly before the next step. Chef's Tip: Do not discard the cooking water; you can use it to boil the pasta sheets to infuse them with extra flavour.
  2. Take an immersion blender and place the boiled stalks in the beaker along with 300g of well-rinsed pre-cooked cannellini beans, a drizzle of extra virgin olive oil, salt, and a pinch of nutmeg. Blend at maximum speed until you achieve a smooth, velvety cream without any lumps, adjusting the thickness with a tablespoon of cooking water if needed. The final consistency should be similar to a thick, enveloping béchamel sauce. Chef's Tip: For a truly professional finish, pass the cream through a fine-mesh sieve to remove any fibrous residue from the stalks.
  3. Finely chop the shallot and let it soften in a large frying pan with two tablespoons of extra virgin olive oil over low heat. Add the roughly chopped green chard leaves and sauté over high heat for about 6-8 minutes until wilted but still a vibrant green. Season with salt and black pepper only at the end of cooking to prevent the vegetables from releasing too much water. Chef's Tip: Do not cover the pan with a lid; this allows moisture to evaporate quickly, keeping the leaves bright rather than turning them dark.
  4. Pour 200ml of whole milk into a small saucepan and heat it over a very low flame without ever letting it reach a boil. Once the milk is hot, add 100g of grated Pecorino Toscano DOP and whisk vigorously by hand. Continue to work the mixture until the cheese is completely melted, creating a fluid, glossy, and lump-free fondue. Chef's Tip: If the fondue starts to separate, remove it from the heat and add a splash of cold milk, whisking quickly to restore the emulsion.
  5. Place 60g of walnut halves in a hot non-stick frying pan, without adding any fats, for about 2 minutes over medium heat. Toss the pan frequently to ensure even toasting until they release an intense woody aroma and their essential oils are activated. Once ready, transfer them to a chopping board and chop them coarsely with a knife to create a crunchy, irregular crumble. Chef's Tip: Keep a very close eye on the walnuts during this stage as they can burn in seconds, becoming bitter and ruining the dish's delicate balance.
  6. Bring the salted water to a boil and submerge 250g of egg pasta sheets, a few at a time so the temperature doesn't drop. Cook according to the package instructions, usually 2-3 minutes, until they are al dente but flexible. Remove them with a slotted spoon and lay them out on a clean cotton tea towel, making sure they do not overlap to prevent them from sticking together. Chef's Tip: Add a teaspoon of oil to the boiling water to create a protective film that helps keep the pasta sheets separated.
  7. Take four flat dinner plates and begin assembling your 'open' lasagne by spreading a thin layer of the chard and cannellini cream in the centre to secure the pasta. Place a first sheet of pasta on top, then continue with more cream, a generous spoonful of sautéed chard leaves, a handful of walnut crumble, and a drizzle of pecorino fondue. Repeat for three layers, aiming to give the dish some height while letting the ingredients peek out from between the pasta layers for a modern aesthetic. Chef's Tip: Slightly warm the plates in the oven before assembly to stop the lasagne from cooling down too fast while you build it.
  8. Transfer the plates or a baking tray into a preheated oven at 200°C for about 5 minutes, just long enough to harmonise the flavours and warm through the core of the dish. Carefully remove from the oven and finish each portion with a final drizzle of raw extra virgin olive oil, a dusting of black pepper, and the remaining crunchy walnuts. The final result will be an elegant, fragrant lasagne with a perfect balance between the creaminess of the beans and the saltiness of the pecorino. Chef's Tip: Do not exceed 5 minutes in the oven to avoid drying out the egg pasta, which should remain soft and velvety on the palate.

Tips

  • Dry-toast the walnuts in a pan for a few minutes to release their full natural aroma.
  • Blend the cannellini with a little pasta water if the cream appears too thick or dry.
  • Boil the lasagna sheets a few at a time to prevent them from sticking together.

Variations

  • Use chopped hazelnuts instead of walnuts for a more delicate and sweeter flavor.
  • Add shavings of Pecorino Toscano between the layers for a more sophisticated gourmet touch.
