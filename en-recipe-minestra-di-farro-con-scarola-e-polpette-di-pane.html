Pearl Spelt Soup with Escarole and Bread Dumplings

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 30 min · Medium

An ancient and simple comfort food, where escarole adds freshness and bread dumplings make the dish hearty and loved by children.

💰 Save Euro🇮🇹 Italian

Ingredients

  • Pearl spelt (a nutty, chewy ancient grain)250 g
  • Fresh escarole (a slightly bitter leafy green)500 g
  • Stale bread100 g
  • Egg1 pcs
  • Grated Parmesan (an aged Italian hard cheese)30 g
  • Garlic1 spicchio
  • Vegetable stock1.5 L
  • Milk50 ml

Allergens

Gluten, Eggs, Milk

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the base for the dumplings: tear 100g of stale bread into a bowl and cover it with 50ml of milk, letting it rest for about 10 minutes until completely softened. Once the bread has absorbed the liquid, squeeze it firmly between your hands to remove any excess moisture and crumble it finely into a clean bowl. You should achieve a moist but not watery mass, perfect for binding with the other ingredients. The chef's tip is to prefer bread with a dense crumb, like a sourdough or rustic loaf, for a heartier texture.
  2. Add one whole egg, 30g of grated Parmesan, and a pinch of salt to the bowl with the bread, then knead by hand until you obtain a homogeneous and mouldable mixture. Take small portions of the dough and form dumplings the size of an olive, rolling them between your palms with steady, light pressure. Arrange them on a plate without overlapping; they must be firm and smooth on the surface so they do not fall apart during cooking. If the mixture feels too sticky, lightly dampen your hands with cold water.
  3. Now turn your attention to the vegetables: take 500g of fresh escarole, remove the base and separate the leaves, washing them thoroughly under running water to remove any grit. Dry them quickly and cut them into coarse strips of about 3 centimetres on a stable chopping board. Fresh escarole will provide the soup with a pleasant contrast between the fleshy stems and the tender leaves. Remember that the greens will reduce drastically in volume once in contact with heat, so don't worry if the quantity seems excessive.
  4. In a large, high-sided pot, heat a drizzle of extra virgin olive oil with a crushed garlic clove, keeping the heat very low. Let the garlic release its essential oils for about 2 minutes until it becomes lightly golden, then remove it to ensure an elegant and subtle final flavour. The oil should be fragrant but must absolutely not smoke, so as not to alter the nutritional properties of the condiment. This step creates the essential aromatic base for the soup's depth of flavour.
  5. Pour the sliced escarole into the pot and increase the heat to medium, stirring frequently with a wooden spoon for about 5 minutes. The leaves must wilt evenly, losing their rigidity and beginning to release their vegetable juices into the cooking base. When the escarole is visibly reduced in volume and a deeper green, it is ready for the liquid. Do not cover with a lid at this stage to allow some of the excess moisture to evaporate quickly.
  6. Add 1.5 litres of boiling vegetable stock and 250g of pearled farro to the pot, making sure to rinse the grain under cold water first to remove any surface impurities. Bring to a gentle boil, then lower the heat and continue cooking for about 20 minutes, partially covering the pot. The farro should swell slowly, absorbing the mineral flavour of the stock and the slightly bitter note of the escarole. Check the seasoning of the stock halfway through cooking, but be cautious as the Parmesan in the dumplings will add more salt later.
  7. When there are about 5 minutes left of the farro's cooking time, gently submerge the bread dumplings into the liquid simmering over medium-low heat. It is vital that the soup is not boiling too violently, as the mechanical agitation could break the delicate structure of the bread and egg. Let the dumplings cook without touching them until they start to float to the surface, a sign that the egg has set and the structure has become stable. You will notice they become lighter in colour and slightly puffed, indicating perfect cooking.
  8. Turn off the heat and let the soup rest for two minutes before serving, allowing the temperatures to stabilise and the flavours to meld perfectly. Ladle the soup into preheated bowls, ensuring an even distribution of farro, escarole, and a generous portion of dumplings in each dish. The final result should be a balanced soup with a savoury broth and ingredients that maintain their textural identity. Complete each portion with a drizzle of raw olive oil and, if you like, a grind of fresh black pepper.
