Penne with White Cannellini and Fennel Sauce

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

An unusual and refined pasta dish that plays on the sweetness of stewed fennel and the creaminess of cannellini beans. An original way to enjoy pulses even in dry pasta dishes.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🌱 Vegan🧒 Kid-friendly🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🇮🇹 Italian

An original white pasta dish that uses the natural creaminess of legumes and the freshness of stewed fennel. The contrast between the blended and whole parts creates a pleasant play of textures. It is an excellent alternative to classic sauces, ideal for a light and fragrant lunch.

Ingredients

  • Penne pasta320 g
  • Canned cannellini beans240 g
  • Large fennel bulb1 pcs
  • Garlic1 spicchio
  • Black peppercornsq.b.

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Start by preparing the large fennel: remove the green fronds and set them aside for decoration, then cut the bulb in half, remove the tough core, and slice it very thinly or into 5mm cubes. Use a sharp knife or a mandoline to obtain uniform cuts that will cook at the same rate during the process. Keep the cutting regular to ensure a pleasant texture on the palate and an elegant presentation. The result should be a homogeneous dice of fennel ready for the aromatic base. Chef's tip: do not throw away the tougher stalks; you can use them to enrich a light vegetable stock in the future.
  2. Take a wide non-stick pan, pour in a drizzle of extra virgin olive oil, and add the slightly crushed garlic clove to release its essential oils. Heat over medium flame for about 2 minutes until the garlic becomes golden and fragrant, being very careful not to burn it to avoid bitter notes. This infusion technique will allow the oil to absorb the delicate aroma of the garlic, making the base more complex. You should smell an inviting aroma as soon as the oil begins to sizzle slightly. Chef's tip: remove the inner germ of the garlic clove to make it milder and more digestible.
  3. Add the cut fennel to the pan, season with salt, and let it stew over low heat for about 10 minutes, adding two tablespoons of hot water if necessary. Cover with a lid to trap the vegetable's natural moisture and allow the fennel to soften gently without browning excessively. Stir occasionally with a wooden spoon to ensure even cooking of all the pieces. At the end of the ten minutes, the fennel should be tender, translucent, and slightly wilted. Chef's tip: if you see the bottom drying out too much, add a splash of hot water instead of more oil to keep the dish light.
  4. Drain the 240g of cannellini beans from their canning liquid and rinse them thoroughly under running water, then add them to the now-tender fennel in the pan. Continue cooking for another 5 minutes over medium heat, blending the flavours well and allowing the legumes to absorb the fennel's aroma. Use a circular motion with a wooden spoon to stir gently without breaking the whole beans too much. The result will be a well-combined mixture where the flavours of the cannellini and fennel have fused harmoniously. Chef's tip: smash a few beans with a fork at this stage to start creating a natural creamy base.
  5. Meanwhile, bring a large pot of water to a boil, salt moderately, and drop in the 320g of penne to begin cooking. Cook the pasta according to the package instructions, but drain it strictly 'al dente', about 2 minutes before the end of the suggested time. Use a colander but remember to reserve at least two ladles of the starch-rich cooking water, essential for creating the final binding. The pasta must still be firm to the bite to perfectly withstand the final sautéing phase in the pan. Chef's tip: always taste the pasta one minute before the expected time to evaluate its correct elasticity.
  6. Take about a third of the fennel and cannellini seasoning from the pan and transfer it to a tall container along with a small ladle of the pasta cooking water. Blend everything with an immersion blender at maximum speed until you get a smooth, velvety cream free of obvious lumps. This emulsion will act as a natural binder for the white sauce, perfectly replacing cream or butter for a nutritionally superior result. You should obtain an ivory-coloured sauce, thick and very fragrant. Chef's tip: pass the cream through a fine-mesh sieve if you want an absolutely gourmet and silky texture.
  7. Pour the drained penne into the pan with the remaining seasoning, add the freshly blended cannellini and fennel cream, and turn the heat to high. Sauté the pasta vigorously for barely a minute, adding another small ladle of cooking water if necessary to encourage the formation of a perfect emulsion between starch and seasoning. Stir with firm, circular movements so that every single penne is uniformly coated by the creamy sauce. The result should be a glossy, creamy dish that is not at all dry on the palate. Chef's tip: the final 'mantecatura' off the heat with a drizzle of raw oil enhances the dish's shine and aromas.
  8. Turn off the heat and complete the dish with a very generous grind of fresh black pepper and the finely chopped fennel fronds you set aside. Distribute the pasta into shallow bowls, trying to balance the presence of penne, whole beans, and cream in every single portion. The fresh black pepper will provide a pungent note that perfectly balances the natural sweetness of the cannellini and fennel. Serve immediately as the anise-like scent releases intensely with the heat of the freshly sautéed pasta. Chef's tip: serve in pre-heated bowls to maintain the sauce's creaminess for longer at the table.

Tips

  • Slice the fennel very thinly to ensure it becomes tender and blends perfectly with the beans.
  • Don't throw away the green fennel fronds; use them finely chopped as a final aromatic decoration.
  • Rinse the canned cannellini beans well under running water to remove the canning liquid before use.

Variations

  • Add a sprinkle of toasted breadcrumbs for a touch of rustic crunch.
  • You can replace black pepper with crushed fennel seeds to emphasize the aroma of the main ingredient.
