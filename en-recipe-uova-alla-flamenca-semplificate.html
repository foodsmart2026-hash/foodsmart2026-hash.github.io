Simplified Flamenco Eggs

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 20 min · Easy

A simple and humble traditional European dish, revisited to be prepared in a single pan. Eggs, tomatoes, and peas create a nutritious and colourful mix that invites you to mop up the sauce with bread.

Ingredients

  • Medium eggs8 pcs
  • Frozen peas200 g
  • Tomato pulp400 g
  • Yellow onion1 pcs
  • Sweet paprika1 cucchiaino
  • Diced prosciutto crudo (Italian dry-cured ham)50 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil20 ml

Allergens

Eggs

Method

  1. Begin by peeling the yellow onion and slicing it very finely with a sharp chef's knife to ensure uniform cooking. Place the slices in a large non-stick frying pan (at least 28-30 cm) along with 20 ml of extra virgin olive oil and a tablespoon of water. Maintain a medium-low heat for about 5 minutes, stirring frequently with a wooden spoon. You want to achieve a translucent, soft, and fragrant onion, strictly avoiding any browning or burning. Chef's tip: adding water at the start allows the onion to stew gently instead of frying, making it much easier to digest.
  2. Add the 50g of diced prosciutto crudo to the softened onions to begin the browning phase. Slightly increase the heat to medium and let the flavours meld for about a minute, moving the ingredients around the pan. The heat will allow the fat from the prosciutto to melt and the lean parts to become slightly crispy. You will see the ham change colour and smell an intense, savoury aroma that will form the heart of the dish. Chef's tip: do not over-brown the prosciutto, as it can become overly salty or tough.
  3. Pour the 400g of tomato pulp and 200g of still-frozen peas into the pan, stirring well to combine with the base. Add a teaspoon of sweet paprika and a small pinch of salt, being careful not to overdo it given the saltiness already provided by the ham. Maintain a medium heat until the mixture begins to simmer steadily. The result should be a vibrant and colourful base where the red of the tomato meets the bright green of the peas. Chef's tip: if the tomato pulp tastes too acidic, you can balance it with a tiny pinch of sugar alongside the paprika.
  4. Cover the pan with a lid and continue cooking over medium heat for approximately 10-12 minutes, stirring occasionally. During this time, the peas will become tender and the sauce will start to reduce, concentrating the flavours of the paprika and ham. You should achieve a thick and hearty consistency, not too liquid, perfect for holding the eggs later. Towards the end, check the seasoning and the tenderness of the peas with a fork. Chef's tip: if the sauce dries out too quickly, add a splash of warm water to keep the base moist.
  5. Once the sauce is ready, use the back of a spoon to create 8 small wells in the surface of the tomato, spaced well apart from each other. This technique provides a specific spot for each egg, preventing the whites from running into each other too much during cooking. Ensure the bottom of each well is still lightly covered with a layer of sauce to protect the base of the egg. The pan will now look like a palette ready to be completed with the main ingredient. Chef's tip: make the wells deep enough so the yolk stays centred and protected.
  6. Take the 8 medium eggs and crack them one at a time, preferably into a small bowl first to avoid dropping any shell fragments into the sauce. Gently slide one egg into each of the previously created wells, trying to keep the yolk intact and centred. Repeat for all eggs, working quickly so there isn't too much of a cooking time difference between the first and last. You will see the whites slowly begin to turn opaque upon contact with the heat of the bubbling sauce. Chef's tip: if a yolk happens to break, do not stir it; let it cook as is to maintain the dish's appearance.
  7. Immediately cover the pan with the lid and lower the heat to the minimum for the final stage of preparation. Cook for about 4-5 minutes without lifting the lid, using the trapped steam to evenly cook the top of the eggs. The goal is to have a perfectly white and set (coagulated) egg white, while the yolk remains visibly creamy and glossy. By the end, the eggs will appear 'poached' in a bed of tomatoes and peas. Chef's tip: watch through the lid (if glass) and turn off the heat as soon as the white over the yolk becomes cloudy to avoid overcooking the centre.
  8. Turn off the heat and let the dish rest for a minute with the lid on before bringing it directly to the table in the pan. Serve the 'Uova alla Flamenca' very hot, accompanied by slices of toasted bread which are essential for scooping up the sauce and the runny yolk. The scent of the sweet paprika and the sweetness of the peas will make for a complete and comforting tasting experience. The final result is a rustic yet elegant dish, rich in colour and texture contrasts. Chef's tip: add a final dusting of fresh paprika or freshly ground black pepper over each egg before serving for an extra pop of colour.
