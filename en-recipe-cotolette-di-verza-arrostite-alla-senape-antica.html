Roasted Savoy Cabbage Steaks with Wholegrain Mustard

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 25 min · Easy

Thick slices of Savoy cabbage roasted in the oven until tender and caramelised, served with a savoury mustard glaze.

🌿 Seasonal recipe🥕 Vegetarian🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🌍 International

An original preparation transforming cabbage into flavorful steaks thanks to a mustard and honey glaze. Oven roasting ensures crispy edges and a tender core, making it a side dish with great character.

Ingredients

  • Whole, firm Savoy cabbage1 (approx. 800 g)
  • Wholegrain mustard3 tbsp
  • Acacia honey1 tbsp
  • Extra virgin olive oil4 tbsp
  • Garlic powder1 tsp
  • Fresh thyme2 sprigs
  • Salt and pepperto taste
  • Breadcrumbs (optional)2 tbsp

Allergens

Mustard
May contain traces of: Gluten.

Method

  1. Preheat your oven to 200°C (fan oven) to ensure it's properly hot when you're ready to roast. Meanwhile, take your whole Savoy cabbage (approx. 800g) and remove only the damaged or very tough outer leaves, then wash it thoroughly under running water. Dry it well with a clean tea towel to prevent excess moisture from creating steam instead of a crispy crust. You should end up with a clean, compact cabbage head ready to be transformed into 'steaks'. Chef's Tip: Do not remove too much of the core at the base, as this is what keeps the leaves attached during cooking.
  2. Place the cabbage on a stable chopping board and, using a sharp chef's knife, cut it vertically into slices about 2.5 cm thick. Proceed steadily, trying to keep the cut straight and ensuring each slice includes a section of the central core to act as an 'anchor'. You will get about 4 or 5 large, firm slices depending on the shape of the cabbage. The visual result should resemble a vegetable steak, with the leaf layers clearly visible and neat. Chef's Tip: If any outer leaves fall off during cutting, don't discard them; simply place them next to the main slices on the baking tray.
  3. Prepare a large baking tray by lining it with parchment paper to prevent the honey and mustard from sticking to the bottom and burning. Arrange the cabbage slices on the tray, making sure they do not overlap and leaving a couple of centimetres between them to allow hot air to circulate. This spacing is essential for achieving crispy edges and even cooking across the entire surface. You will see the slices occupying most of the tray, ready for seasoning. Chef's Tip: If your tray is too small, use two rather than crowding the slices, otherwise the cabbage will turn soggy due to trapped moisture.
  4. In a small bowl, prepare the aromatic glaze by combining 3 tablespoons of wholegrain mustard, 1 tablespoon of acacia honey, 4 tablespoons of extra virgin olive oil, 1 teaspoon of garlic powder, and the leaves from 2 sprigs of fresh thyme. Mix vigorously with a fork or a small whisk until you achieve a smooth, thick emulsion. The aroma should be perfectly balanced between the pungency of the mustard and the sweetness of the honey. This mixture will act as both a seasoning and a protective layer to keep the vegetable moist. Chef's Tip: If the honey is too thick or crystallised, warm it for 5 seconds in the microwave to make it fluid and easier to emulsify.
  5. Using a pastry brush, generously spread about half of the glaze over the top surface of each cabbage slice. Make sure the mixture penetrates slightly between the leaf folds to flavour the inside of the 'steak' as well. Finish this step by seasoning each slice with salt and pepper to your liking. The surface should look glossy, rich with herbs, and well-covered with the mustard grains. Chef's Tip: Don't over-salt at this stage, as wholegrain mustard is naturally savoury and already contains a fair amount of salt.
  6. Place the tray on the middle rack of the preheated oven and set your timer for 15 minutes. During this time, the heat will wilt the inner leaves making them tender, while the edges will begin to brown slightly due to the caramelisation of the honey sugars. You should smell the wonderful aroma of thyme and mustard wafting through your kitchen. The cabbage will begin to shrink slightly in volume, taking on a golden appearance on top. Chef's Tip: Keep an eye on the colour through the oven door; if you see the leaf tips darkening too quickly, slightly lower the temperature to 180°C.
  7. After the first 15 minutes, carefully remove the tray from the oven and, using a wide spatula, gently flip each cabbage slice over. Brush the newly turned side with the remaining glaze from the bowl, being careful not to break the structure of the slice. If you have decided to use them, now is the time to sprinkle the surface with the 2 tablespoons of breadcrumbs. This will result in an even coating that promises an irresistible crunch after the second bake. Chef's Tip: Flip the slices with a firm, smooth motion starting from the core, which is the sturdiest part, to prevent them from falling apart.
  8. Return the tray to the oven for the final 10 minutes of cooking, maintaining the temperature at 200°C. For the last 2 minutes, you can switch on the grill function to achieve an even deeper golden colour and make the breadcrumbs truly crunchy. The cabbage is ready when the edges are brown and crisp, and a knife tip enters the core without resistance. Remove from the oven and let the slices rest for a couple of minutes before serving to allow the flavours to settle. Chef's Tip: Serve the steaks piping hot to fully enjoy the contrast between the tender heart and the crispy, flavourful edges.

Tips

  • Slice the cabbage keeping part of the core in each piece to prevent them from falling apart.
  • Brush the glaze evenly on both sides for uniform caramelization during roasting.
  • If you prefer a bolder crust, add a light dusting of breadcrumbs before putting it in the oven.

Variations

  • Use chestnut honey instead of acacia for a more bitter and intense flavor profile.
  • Add a pinch of cumin seeds to the glaze to evoke typical Central European flavors.
