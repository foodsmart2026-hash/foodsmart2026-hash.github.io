Risotto with Squash and Rosemary

💰 Save Euro

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

A creamy and comforting main course that highlights the sweetness of Mantuan squash with the pungent aroma of fresh rosemary. This version uses exclusively extra virgin olive oil for a healthy and light 'mantecatura' (the traditional Italian process of beating in fat at the end to create creaminess), typical of the Mediterranean diet.

Ingredients

  • Carnaroli or Arborio rice (premium Italian risotto rice varieties)320 g
  • Squash (Delica or Mantuan variety, cleaned)600 g
  • Yellow onion1 pc
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 ml
  • 24-month aged Parmigiano Reggiano (a DOP aged hard cheese)30 g
  • Fresh rosemary2 sprigs
  • Vegetable stock (celery, carrot, onion)1500 ml
  • Unrefined sea saltto taste
  • Black pepperto taste

Allergens

Milk, Celery

Method

  1. Prepare the vegetable stock by placing a carrot, a celery stalk, and an onion in 1.5 litres of water; bring to the boil and keep it simmering throughout the preparation so as not to interrupt the rice cooking.
  2. Finely chop the yellow onion and sweat it in a large saucepan with half of the extra virgin olive oil (20 ml) and two tablespoons of stock for about 5-7 minutes over a very low heat until it becomes translucent.
  3. Cut the squash into very small cubes (about 1 cm) and add them to the onion along with the finely chopped needles from one sprig of rosemary; sauté for 5 minutes until the squash begins to soften.
  4. Add the rice to the saucepan and toast it over a medium heat for 2-3 minutes, stirring constantly: the rice is ready when the grains become translucent at the edges and feel hot when touched with the back of your hand.
  5. Begin adding the boiling stock one ladle at a time, just covering the rice; stir frequently to encourage the release of starch, which will ensure the natural creaminess of the dish.
  6. Halfway through cooking (after about 8-9 minutes), crush some of the squash cubes against the side of the pot with the back of a wooden spoon to create a thick cream that will coat the rice.
  7. Continue cooking by adding stock as it is absorbed; the risotto must never be dry but should have an 'all'onda' (wavy) consistency, a sign that the hydration is correct.
  8. Check the cooking of the grain: it should be tender on the outside but with a slightly firm core (al dente); this usually takes 16-18 minutes in total depending on the rice.
  9. Turn off the heat, add the remaining extra virgin olive oil (20 ml) and the grated Parmigiano Reggiano; stir vigorously (mantecatura) for 1 minute to emulsify the fats with the starch and create the cream.
  10. Cover the saucepan with a lid and let the risotto rest for 2 minutes before serving; this step allows the flavours to balance and the texture to stabilise perfectly.
