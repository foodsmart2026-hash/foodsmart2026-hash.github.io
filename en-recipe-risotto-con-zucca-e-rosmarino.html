Risotto with Squash and Rosemary

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

A creamy and comforting main course that highlights the sweetness of Mantuan squash with the pungent aroma of fresh rosemary. This version uses exclusively extra virgin olive oil for a healthy and light 'mantecatura' (the traditional Italian process of beating in fat at the end to create creaminess), typical of the Mediterranean diet.

An autumn classic combining the sweetness of Mantuan pumpkin with the aromatic note of fresh rosemary. The texture is creamy thanks to the partially mashed pumpkin pulp and the final touch of 24-month aged Parmigiano Reggiano.

Ingredients

  • Carnaroli or Arborio rice (premium Italian risotto rice varieties)320 g
  • Squash (Delica or Mantuan variety, cleaned)600 g
  • Yellow onion1 pc
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 ml
  • 24-month aged Parmigiano Reggiano (a DOP aged hard cheese)30 g
  • Fresh rosemary2 sprigs
  • Vegetable stock (celery, carrot, onion)1500 ml
  • Unrefined sea saltto taste
  • Black pepperto taste

Allergens

Milk, Celery

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the vegetable stock: place 1.5 litres of water in a pot with a carrot, a celery stalk, and an onion; bring to the boil and keep it simmering on low heat throughout the process. It is vital that the liquid remains boiling to avoid thermal shocks that would halt the rice's cooking process. You will obtain a clear, aromatic broth that serves as the savoury foundation of the dish. Chef's tip: avoid over-salting the broth initially, as it will reduce and concentrate in flavour during cooking.
  2. Finely chop the yellow onion and place it in a large casserole pan with half of the extra virgin olive oil (20 ml) and two tablespoons of hot broth. Let it sweat over a very low heat for about 5-7 minutes, stirring frequently until it becomes translucent and soft, almost like a cream. This slow process prevents the onion from browning, ensuring a sweet and delicate flavour. Chef's tip: if the onion starts to colour too quickly, add another splash of broth to maintain the moisture.
  3. Cut the cleaned pumpkin into very small cubes, about 1 cm wide, and add them to the onion base along with the finely chopped needles from one sprig of rosemary. Sauté over medium heat for about 5 minutes, allowing the pumpkin to absorb the flavours and begin softening its outer fibres. This stage releases the rosemary's aroma, which pairs perfectly with the pumpkin's sweetness. Chef's tip: ensure the cubes are uniform in size to guarantee even cooking and partial melting into the sauce.
  4. Pour the 320 g of Carnaroli rice into the pan with the pumpkin and proceed to toast it over medium heat for 2-3 minutes, stirring constantly with a wooden spoon. The grains should become translucent at the edges and feel very hot to the touch, indicating that the heat is sealing the exterior. Toasting is the secret to keeping the grain intact and 'al dente' even after long cooking. Chef's tip: don't rush this; well-toasted rice releases starch gradually without breaking apart.
  5. Begin adding the boiling broth one ladle at a time, just covering the level of the rice and maintaining a steady, gentle simmer. Stir frequently to encourage the grains to release their starch, which will transform the liquid into a natural cream. Continue this process every time the broth is almost completely absorbed, never letting the rice dry out. Chef's tip: move the rice gently but constantly, especially towards the edges of the pan where the heat is most intense.
  6. Halfway through the cooking time, after about 8-9 minutes, press some of the pumpkin cubes against the side of the pan using the back of a wooden spoon. This technique turns some of the flesh into a thick purée that immediately coats the rice grains, giving them a vibrant orange colour. You will achieve a mixed texture: partly creamy pumpkin and partly whole pieces. Chef's tip: don't mash all the pumpkin; keeping some cubes whole provides a more interesting texture on the palate.
  7. Finish the cooking by continuing to add broth for a total of about 16-18 minutes, tasting often to check the grain's texture. The rice should be tender on the outside but with a slightly firm 'soul' at the centre—the classic 'al dente' bite. Season with salt and black pepper only towards the end, bearing in mind the saltiness the cheese will add. Chef's tip: turn off the heat a minute before the rice is perfectly cooked, as residual heat continues the process during the 'mantecatura'.
  8. Turn off the heat and proceed to the 'mantecatura', the most important step: add the remaining 20 ml of cold extra virgin olive oil and the 30 g of grated Parmigiano Reggiano. Stir vigorously and enthusiastically, creating a circular motion to incorporate air and emulsify the fats with the rice starch. The result should be a risotto 'all'onda' (wavy)—fluid and creamy, moving like a wave when you shake the pan. Chef's tip: if the risotto seems too thick, add one last tablespoon of broth before serving.
  9. Cover the casserole with a lid and let the risotto rest for exactly 2 minutes before plating. This resting period allows the temperature to equalise and the cream's structure to stabilise, making every bite harmonious. Serve on flat plates, garnishing with the remaining sprig of fresh rosemary for a fragrant touch. Chef's tip: slightly warm the serving plates before plating to prevent the risotto from cooling down too quickly while eating.

Tips

  • Cut the pumpkin into very small cubes to ensure even cooking alongside the rice grains.
  • Toast the rice over medium heat until it is hot to the touch and translucent before adding broth.
  • Add the boiling vegetable broth one ladle at a time to maintain a constant cooking temperature.

Variations

  • You can replace the rosemary with fresh sage for a smoother aroma.
  • For an extra savory touch, add coarsely ground black pepper during the final creaming stage.
