Chickpea and Spinach Stew with Poached Eggs

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 18 min · Easy

A humble but complete dish, typical of European rural cuisine, combining pulses and leafy greens. The egg cooked directly in the sauce creates a creamy and very economical meal.

A rustic and comforting dish inspired by Mediterranean tradition, perfect for a quick and satisfying dinner. The creamy texture of chickpeas and spinach cradles a soft egg yolk, best enjoyed with toasted crusty bread.

Ingredients

  • Pre-cooked chickpeas480 g
  • Fresh (or frozen) spinach400 g
  • Tomato passata (smooth sieved tomatoes)200 ml
  • Garlic1 spicchio
  • Eggs4 pcs
  • Rustic bread4 fette
  • Extra virgin olive oil20 ml

Allergens

Eggs, Gluten

Method

  1. Begin by pouring 20 ml of extra virgin olive oil into a wide saucepan and add the lightly crushed garlic clove. Heat over medium heat for about 2 minutes until the garlic releases its aroma and turns golden, then remove it with tongs to prevent the flavour from becoming bitter. The goal is to obtain a fragrant oil that will serve as the aromatic base for the entire dish. Chef's tip: never let the garlic brown too much, or it will impart an acrid taste to the spinach.
  2. Add the 400 g of spinach (if fresh, wash well; if frozen, use directly) to the saucepan with the hot oil. Cover with a lid for the first 2 minutes over high heat to facilitate wilting thanks to the vegetable's natural steam. Stir gently with a wooden spoon until the volume has reduced drastically and the colour becomes bright green. Chef's tip: do not overcook them at this stage, as they will continue cooking along with the chickpeas.
  3. Pour the 480 g of pre-cooked chickpeas into the pan, after rinsing them thoroughly under running water to remove the canning liquid. Add the 200 ml of tomato passata and about half a glass of warm water (approx. 100 ml) to adjust the initial density. Stir well to distribute the tomato and aromas among the legumes and vegetables. Chef's tip: rinsing the chickpeas well removes excess sodium and improves the digestibility of the dish.
  4. Season with salt and let the mixture simmer over low heat for about 10 minutes, keeping the saucepan partially covered. During this time, the water will evaporate and the flavours of the passata and spinach will blend with the chickpeas, creating a creamy and thick consistency. The result should be a rich stew, not a watery soup, with the sauce perfectly coating every ingredient. Chef's tip: crush a few chickpeas with a fork against the bottom of the pot to make the cooking base even more velvety.
  5. Using the back of a spoon, create four small, well-spaced wells (or 'nests') within the bubbling chickpea and spinach mixture. These spaces will hold the eggs, allowing them to cook evenly without dispersing into the sauce. Ensure the bottom of the pan is well covered with sauce to prevent the egg from sticking. Chef's tip: make sure the sauce is simmering gently before proceeding to the next step.
  6. Gently crack one egg at a time, pouring one into each well previously created in the saucepan. Try to keep the yolk intact and centred in the white for an impeccable aesthetic result. This cooking technique allows the egg to absorb the juices of the sauce while the base sets. Chef's tip: crack the egg into a small cup first to ensure it is fresh and to prevent shell fragments from falling into the food.
  7. Immediately cover the saucepan with a tight-fitting lid and continue cooking over very low heat for 3 to 4 minutes maximum. The heat trapped by the lid will cook the top of the egg via steam, while the bottom cooks through contact with the sauce. You should achieve a perfectly white and set egg white, but a yolk that is still fluid and creamy to the touch. Chef's tip: do not lift the lid repeatedly, or the steam loss will increase cooking times and toughen the base of the egg.
  8. While the eggs finish cooking, toast the 4 slices of rustic bread on a hot griddle or in a toaster until they are crispy and golden on both sides. The crunchiness of the bread is essential to contrast the softness of the stew and the egg. Place a slice of bread at the bottom of each individual plate or near the edge. Chef's tip: lightly rub the toasted bread with a bit of fresh garlic if you desire a bolder aromatic note.
  9. Using a wide spatula or a flat ladle, gently lift a portion of chickpeas and spinach along with an intact egg and place them over or next to the slice of bread. Complete each plate with a dusting of freshly cracked black pepper and a very light drizzle of raw olive oil to enhance the aromas. Serve immediately while the yolk is still runny. Chef's tip: serve in preheated shallow bowls to maintain the ideal temperature of the dish during the meal.

Tips

  • Rinse the pre-cooked chickpeas thoroughly to remove the preservation liquid and enhance their natural flavor.
  • Cover the pan with a lid while cooking the eggs to help the whites set evenly without overcooking the yolks.
  • Serve the dish immediately to enjoy the contrast between the warm base and the runny egg yolk.

Variations

  • Add a pinch of chili flakes to the tomato sauce for a bold and spicy kick.
  • Replace the fresh spinach with Swiss chard or tender beet greens using the same cooking method.
