Mezze Maniche with Broccoli and Almond Pesto

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 12 min · Easy

A clever way to get even the most skeptical to eat broccoli, creating a silky sauce without any cream. The almonds add a toasted and crunchy note.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🧒 Kid-friendly🇮🇹 Italian

A quick and colorful pasta dish, perfect for a flavorful everyday lunch. The creaminess of the broccoli blends with the crunchiness of toasted almonds and the fresh note of lemon zest.

Ingredients

  • mezze maniche pasta (short tube-shaped Italian pasta)320 g
  • broccoli (florets and tender stalks)500 g
  • blanched almonds40 g
  • grated Pecorino Romano (salty Italian sheep's milk cheese)40 g
  • extra virgin olive oil50 ml
  • lemon zestq.b.

Allergens

Gluten, Milk, Tree nuts

Method

  1. Begin the preparation by thoroughly washing 500g of broccoli under cold running water. Separate the florets from the central stalk and peel the latter with a vegetable peeler to remove the tough outer skin, then dice it into small half-centimetre cubes. This preliminary step takes about 5 minutes and ensures even cooking for all parts of the vegetable. You will have uniform pieces ready for boiling, keeping the nutritious properties of the stalk intact. The chef's tip: never discard the stalks, as they are the sweetest and most fiber-rich part of the broccoli.
  2. Bring a large pot with about 4 litres of water to a boil and add 10g of rock salt per litre. Drop the diced stalks in first, followed by the florets after one minute, and cook over high heat for about 6-7 minutes. The broccoli should be very tender when pierced with a fork, yet maintain a vibrant green colour. Remove them with a slotted spoon and transfer to a bowl, keeping the boiling water in the pot. The chef's tip: cooking the broccoli in the same water as the pasta preserves the volatile aromas that will flavour your dish.
  3. Bring the cooking water back to a rolling boil and drop in the 320g of mezze maniche. Stir immediately with a wooden spoon to prevent the pasta from sticking to the bottom. Cook for the time indicated on the packaging, usually around 10-12 minutes, maintaining a constant boil. The result should be elastic and 'al dente' pasta, ready to absorb the sauce. The chef's tip: always taste the pasta two minutes before the timer ends to check its internal texture.
  4. While the pasta is cooking, spend a couple of minutes toasting 40g of peeled almonds in a non-stick frying pan over medium heat. Toss the pan constantly to prevent the almonds from burning until they release an intense aroma and turn slightly golden. Set aside a small portion (about 10g) and roughly chop them with a knife for the final garnish. The chef's tip: toasting 'awakens' the essential oils in the nuts, making the pesto infinitely more aromatic.
  5. Prepare the pesto by placing the boiled broccoli, whole toasted almonds, 40g of grated Pecorino Romano, and 50ml of extra virgin olive oil into a blender. Add a ladle of pasta cooking water (about 60ml) and pulse to avoid overheating the cream. You should achieve a smooth, thick, and velvety consistency with an emerald green colour. If necessary, add another tablespoon of water to adjust the fluidity. The chef's tip: pulsing the blender prevents the vegetables from oxidising and keeps the colour vivid.
  6. Transfer the broccoli cream to a wide frying pan or a skillet, keeping the heat at the lowest setting just to warm the base. Do not fry or boil the pesto, otherwise, the Pecorino will tend to clump or separate from the oil. If the cream seems too compact, dilute it with another half ladle of starchy cooking water. The result should be a fluid sauce that perfectly coats the back of a spoon. The chef's tip: the residual heat from the pasta will be almost enough to warm the pesto; avoid prolonged cooking in the pan.
  7. Drain the mezze maniche directly into the pan with the pesto, always keeping some cooking water aside just in case. Sauté the pasta over medium heat for about one minute, moving the pan in a circular motion to create a perfect emulsion between the pasta starch and the fats in the pesto. You will see a glossy 'cremina' forming, coating every single piece of pasta. The chef's tip: this 'mantecatura' stage is the secret to transforming a loose dressing into a restaurant-quality dish.
  8. Turn off the heat and complete the dish by grating the zest of an unwaxed lemon over it, being careful not to include the bitter white pith. Add the reserved chopped almonds and a final drizzle of raw extra virgin olive oil to enhance the fragrances. Serve immediately while piping hot, scattering the crunchy almonds on top for a contrast in texture. The chef's tip: adding the lemon zest off the heat acts as a balancer, cleansing the palate from the saltiness of the Pecorino.

Tips

  • Toast the almonds for a few minutes in a pan to enhance their aroma and fragrance.
  • Reserve some pasta cooking water to better emulsify the broccoli pesto.
  • Use fresh, firm broccoli to achieve a bright green color and intense flavor.

Variations

  • Replace the Pecorino Romano with more almonds for a sweeter profile.
  • Use lime zest instead of lemon for a more exotic citrus scent.
