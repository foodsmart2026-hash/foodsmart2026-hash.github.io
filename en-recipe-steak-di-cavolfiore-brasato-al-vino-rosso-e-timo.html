Braised Cauliflower Steaks with Red Wine and Thyme

🌿 Seasonal recipe

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 35 min · Medium

A thick and flavourful vegetable 'steak', slowly braised in a red wine base that gives it an intense colour and a bold taste.

Ingredients

  • Large, firm cauliflower1 piece
  • Still red wine (such as Merlot or Sangiovese)300ml
  • Vegetable stock200ml
  • Tomato purée1 tablespoon
  • Garlic2 cloves
  • Fresh thyme4 sprigs
  • Rice flour2 tablespoons
  • Extra virgin olive oil4 tablespoons
  • Salt and black pepperto taste

Allergens

Celery, Sulphites

Method

  1. Start by trimming the outer green leaves from the large cauliflower, taking care not to damage the central core, which is essential for holding the dish together. Place the vegetable on a chopping board and make firm vertical cuts to produce 4 steaks about 2.5-3 cm thick, ensuring a piece of the core remains in every slice. Use a sharp chef’s knife to achieve clean, symmetrical cuts that mimic the look of a traditional steak. The ideal result is compact 'steaks' that don't crumble when handled. My advice: don't throw away the leftover smaller florets; use them for a soup or a quick sautéed side dish.
  2. Take your 4 cauliflower steaks and season them evenly with a pinch of salt and freshly ground black pepper on both sides. Gently dredge them in 2 tablespoons of rice flour, pressing down lightly with your palm to help it stick, then carefully shake each slice to remove any excess flour. This step must be done just before cooking to prevent the cauliflower's natural moisture from making the flour sticky. You should achieve a dry, lightly veiled surface, perfect for creating a crust during searing. My advice: rice flour is the secret to an extra-crispy golden crust and makes the dish naturally gluten-free.
  3. In a large non-stick frying pan that can comfortably fit all the slices, pour 4 tablespoons of extra virgin olive oil and add 2 crushed garlic cloves (leaving the skin on, 'en chemise') and 4 sprigs of fresh thyme. Heat over a medium flame for about 2 minutes, swirling the pan to infuse the oil without burning the garlic. You should notice an intense fragrance from the herbs and the oil should start to sizzle gently. This step creates the aromatic base that provides character and depth to the entire dish. My advice: keep the heat moderate so the thyme leaves don't burn and turn bitter.
  4. Carefully lay the cauliflower steaks into the hot pan, ensuring they do not overlap. Increase the heat slightly to medium-high and sear for about 4 minutes per side without moving them constantly, allowing the Maillard reaction to work its magic. Use a wide spatula to flip them only when the edges start to turn visibly dark and the base easily releases from the pan. The final result of this stage should be a deep golden-brown, inviting crust on both sides. My advice: if your pan is too small, cook the steaks in two batches to prevent the oil temperature from dropping, which would stop them from browning.
  5. Once the slices are perfectly browned, pour 300ml of dry red wine (such as Merlot or Sangiovese) directly into the pan, being careful of any steam splashes. Keep the heat high for about 2 minutes to allow the alcohol to evaporate completely, leaving behind only the aromatic essence and vibrant colour of the wine. You will smell an intense vinous aroma and see the liquid bubbling vigorously around the base of the steaks. This deglazing process is essential to balance the cauliflower's sweetness with the wine's noble acidity. My advice: always use a good quality wine you would enjoy drinking, as the flavour concentrates significantly during reduction.
  6. In a small bowl or jug, dissolve 1 tablespoon of tomato paste into 200ml of hot vegetable broth, stirring thoroughly with a teaspoon until all lumps are gone. Gently pour this mixture into the pan, distributing it in the spaces between the steaks rather than directly over the crust to avoid softening it too much. The liquid should reach about halfway up the sides of the steaks, creating the moist environment needed for braising. You will have a deep brick-red and fragrant cooking liquid. My advice: make sure the broth is hot before adding it so you don't suddenly stop the cooking process or the flavour development.
  7. Lower the heat to minimum, cover the pan with a tight-fitting lid, and leave the cauliflower to braise for about 20 minutes in total. Halfway through, lift the lid and flip the steaks with extreme care using two spatulas to maintain their shape. At the end of the time, check for tenderness by piercing the central core with the tip of a small knife: it should slide in without resistance, like butter. The cauliflower will appear tender inside but still well-structured on the outside. My advice: do not rush this stage; slow braising is the only way to let the wine and thyme flavours penetrate the heart of the 'steak'.
  8. Remove the lid, increase the heat slightly to medium, and cook for another 3-5 minutes to reduce the remaining cooking liquid. Gently swirl the pan (a technique known as 'nappage') to glaze the steaks with the liquid as it thickens, aided by the rice flour used earlier. The sauce should become glossy, dark, and velvety, thick enough to coat the back of a spoon. Turn off the heat as soon as the consistency resembles a shiny, fragrant syrup. My advice: if the sauce reduces too much and becomes sticky, add a tablespoon of hot water or broth to return it to the ideal fluidity.
  9. Transfer each cauliflower steak to the centre of pre-warmed individual plates and drizzle generously with the red wine sauce remaining in the pan. Garnish each portion with a few fresh thyme leaves set aside earlier and a final grind of black pepper for a touch of pungent freshness. The dish will present as a sophisticated plant-based alternative to meat, with a deep ruby colour and an earthy, enveloping aroma. Serve immediately while the sauce is still hot and fluid for the best tasting experience. My advice: serve this dish with a very silky potato purée or soft polenta to mop up all that delicious wine sauce.
