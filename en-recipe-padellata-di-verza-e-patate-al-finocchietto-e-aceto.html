Pan-fried Savoy Cabbage and Potatoes with Fennel Seeds and Vinegar

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

A rustic and hearty side dish that enhances the sweetness of the potatoes and the texture of the cabbage. The addition of fennel seeds and a final acidic touch creates a perfect aromatic balance to accompany simple protein dishes.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🌱 Vegan🌾 Gluten-free🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🇮🇹 Italian

Ingredients

  • Savoy cabbage600 g
  • Yellow-fleshed potatoes400 g
  • Brown onion1 pc
  • Fennel seeds1 tsp
  • White wine vinegar20 ml

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Begin by prepping 600g of Savoy cabbage: remove any tough outer leaves and the fibrous central core, then slice the leaves into strips about 1 cm wide. Wash the greens thoroughly under running water and dry them well using a salad spinner to prevent excess water from steaming the vegetables instead of sautéing them. You should end up with soft, bright green ribbons ready for the pan. Chef's tip: don't discard the core; if you slice it very thinly with a mandoline, it will become tender and crunchy when cooked.
  2. Take 400g of yellow-fleshed potatoes, peel them carefully, and cut them into uniform 1.5 cm cubes. It is essential that the size is consistent to ensure every piece cooks at the same rate without falling apart. Rinse the cubes under cold water to remove surface starch and pat them dry with a clean kitchen towel. You will have clean cubes that won't stick together during the initial searing phase. Chef's tip: use older potatoes if possible, as they contain less water and hold their shape better.
  3. Finely slice one golden onion and place it in a large pan or wok with two tablespoons of extra virgin olive oil and a teaspoon of fennel seeds. Turn the heat to medium and let it soften gently for about 4-5 minutes, stirring with a wooden spoon so it doesn't burn. The onion should become translucent and release, along with the fennel, a sweet and aromatic fragrance that forms the base of the dish. Chef's tip: if the onion starts to brown too quickly, add a tablespoon of water to stew it gently.
  4. Slightly increase the heat and add the potato cubes to the aromatic base, spreading them out well over the bottom of the pan. Let them sear without touching them for the first 2 minutes, then toss for another 4-5 minutes until a thin golden crust forms on the surface. This technique seals the starch inside and provides a pleasantly crispy exterior with a soft interior. Chef's tip: avoid covering the pan at this stage to prevent steam from forming, which would stop the potatoes from browning properly.
  5. Now add the 600g of prepared cabbage, layering it over the potatoes, and immediately season with a pinch of salt and black pepper. The salt will help the cabbage release its moisture through osmosis, facilitating the natural wilting process. Gently toss with kitchen tongs to integrate the leaves with the onion and potato base. You will notice the volume of the cabbage starts to decrease significantly within a couple of minutes. Chef's tip: lightly press the cabbage with the back of a spoon to ensure it makes contact with the heat at the bottom.
  6. Pour half a glass of hot water into the pan, lower the heat to medium-low, and cover immediately with a tight-fitting lid. Let it braise for about 15 minutes, checking halfway through to ensure the liquid hasn't evaporated completely to avoid burning. The trapped steam will soften the heart of the potatoes and make the cabbage leaves tender and juicy. The final result will be a moist and fragrant preparation with perfectly cooked vegetables. Chef's tip: use vegetable stock instead of water for an extra, deeper boost of flavour.
  7. Remove the lid and turn the heat to high to evaporate any residual moisture at the bottom of the pan. Pour in 20ml of white wine vinegar and let it bubble away for about a minute until the pungent smell of alcohol has completely vanished. The vinegar doesn't just add acidity; it acts as a palate cleanser, balancing the sweetness of the potatoes and cabbage. You will smell an inviting sweet-and-sour aroma and see the vegetables becoming glossy. Chef's tip: do not use balsamic vinegar if you want to maintain the vibrant colour of the vegetables.
  8. Finish the preparation by tossing everything over high heat for the final minute, allowing the flavours to meld perfectly. Turn off the heat and, if desired, add a final drizzle of raw olive oil and a grating of fresh pepper before plating. The side dish should appear well-combined, with intact potatoes and wilted but still structured cabbage. Serve the sauté while hot to best appreciate the aromatic note of the fennel seeds. Chef's tip: let the dish rest for two minutes before serving; the flavours will settle, making it even tastier.
