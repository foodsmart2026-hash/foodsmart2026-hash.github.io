Pan-fried Savoy Cabbage and Potatoes with Fennel Seeds and Vinegar

💰 Save Euro

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

A rustic and hearty side dish that enhances the sweetness of the potatoes and the texture of the cabbage. The addition of fennel seeds and a final acidic touch creates a perfect aromatic balance to accompany simple protein dishes.

Ingredients

  • Savoy cabbage600 g
  • Yellow-fleshed potatoes400 g
  • Brown onion1 pc
  • Fennel seeds1 tsp
  • White wine vinegar20 ml

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Vegetable preparation: Clean the Savoy cabbage by removing the tougher outer leaves and the central core, then cut it into thin strips of about 1 cm; peel the potatoes and cut them into uniform 1.5 cm cubes to ensure they cook at the same time.
  2. Initial sauté: In a large non-stick frying pan or a wok, heat two tablespoons of extra virgin olive oil and soften the finely sliced onion together with the fennel seeds until translucent and fragrant.
  3. Browning the potatoes: Add the potato cubes to the pan and cook over a high heat for about 5-7 minutes, stirring often until they start to brown on the outside, sealing the starches.
  4. Adding the cabbage: Place the cabbage in the pan over the potatoes, season with salt and pepper immediately to help the leaves soften through osmosis.
  5. Stewing: Add half a glass of hot water, cover with a tight-fitting lid, and cook over a medium-low heat for 15 minutes, allowing the steam to soften the centre of the potatoes.
  6. Checking the cooking: Remove the lid and check with a fork that the potatoes are tender; the cabbage should be soft but not mushy, maintaining a vibrant green or slightly brown colour.
  7. Acidic glaze: Turn up the heat to dry any remaining liquid at the bottom, pour in the white wine vinegar, and let it evaporate completely until the pungent smell vanishes, leaving only the aroma.
  8. Finishing: Sauté everything over a high heat for one last minute to distribute the flavours evenly and serve the side dish piping hot, with a final drizzle of raw oil if desired.
