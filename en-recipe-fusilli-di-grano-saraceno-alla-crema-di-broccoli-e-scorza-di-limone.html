Buckwheat Fusilli with Broccoli Cream and Lemon Zest

4 servings · Prep 5 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

A rustic and flavourful first course that uses the natural savouriness of nutritional yeast and the freshness of lemon to enhance the buckwheat. This recipe is naturally gluten-free and free from main allergens, ensuring great palatability in under 20 minutes.

Ingredients

  • Buckwheat pasta (fusilli shape)320 g
  • Fresh broccoli (florets)600 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 ml
  • Nutritional yeast flakes20 g
  • Fresh garlic2 cloves
  • Untreated lemon (zest and juice)1 pcs
  • Unrefined sea salt10 g
  • Ground black pepper2 g

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Start by bringing 4 litres of water to a boil in a large pot and add 10g of wholegrain sea salt only once it has reached a rolling boil. This requires high heat to ensure the gluten-free buckwheat pasta doesn't release too much starch and become sticky. The result should be a vigorous boil that keeps the fusilli well separated during cooking. My professional tip is to keep the lid on the pot to speed up the process and save energy.
  2. While the water is heating, thoroughly wash 600g of fresh broccoli florets and cut them with a sharp knife into small, uniform pieces of about 1-2 cm. Cutting the vegetables into similar sizes ensures quick cooking and a balanced texture between the creamy and whole parts. The pieces should look small and compact, ready to be blanched. A chef's trick: don't discard the tender stems; peel them and cut into cubes as they are packed with flavour and nutrients.
  3. Plunge the broccoli into the boiling water and cook over medium heat for about 4-5 minutes, checking the texture with a fork. The vegetable is ready when tender but still holding a bright green colour and a slight bite; avoid overcooking to preserve vitamins. If the broccoli turns a dull green, it means it has lost most of its aromatic and nutritional profile.
  4. Using a slotted spoon, remove about 300g of the broccoli (half of the total) and transfer to a tall jug for an immersion blender, leaving the rest in the pot. Add 20g of nutritional yeast flakes, the juice of half a lemon, and a ladle of the cooking water to the jug. Blend at maximum power until you achieve a smooth, velvety cream with an intense aroma reminiscent of aged cheese. If the cream seems too thick, add another tablespoon of water to make it silkier.
  5. In the same water where the remaining broccoli is cooking, add the 320g of buckwheat fusilli and maintain a medium-high flame. Follow the cooking times on the package, usually 8-10 minutes, to achieve a perfect 'al dente' texture. It is essential to stir gently during the first few minutes to prevent the pasta from sticking to the bottom. Remember that buckwheat pasta is more fragile than wheat pasta, so handle it with care to avoid breaking the fusilli.
  6. In a large frying pan, heat 40ml of extra virgin olive oil with 2 crushed garlic cloves for about 2 minutes over very low heat. The goal is to brown the garlic slowly so it releases its essential oils into the fat without burning or becoming bitter. The oil should smell intensely of garlic and show small bubbles around the cloves. If the garlic darkens too quickly, move the pan away from the heat source to lower the temperature.
  7. Drain the pasta and the remaining broccoli, reserving a bit of the cooking water, and toss them directly into the pan with the flavoured oil (removing the garlic if preferred). Stir in the blended broccoli cream and sauté over high heat for about 60 seconds to allow the sauce to cling to the rough surface of the buckwheat. You should get a creamy and glossy mixture that coats every fusillo evenly. If the dish feels too dry, add a final splash of cooking water to emulsify everything perfectly.
  8. Turn off the heat and finish the dish by finely grating the zest of the untreated lemon and adding 2g of freshly ground black pepper. The essential oils from the zest will provide a vibrant aromatic note that perfectly balances the earthy, rustic taste of the buckwheat. The final scent should be fresh, citrusy, and inviting. My final touch: use only the yellow part of the zest, as the white pith is bitter and would ruin the balance of the dish.
