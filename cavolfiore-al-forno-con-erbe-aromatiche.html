Roasted cauliflower with aromatic herbs

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

A light and fragrant side dish that transforms cauliflower into a crispy treat thanks to its aromatic coating. Perfect to accompany fish or legume dishes, showcasing an affordable and versatile seasonal vegetable.

A crunchy and aromatic vegetable side dish, ideal for accompanying Sunday main courses. The whole wheat breading, enriched with lemon zest and thyme, provides a grainy texture that contrasts with the tender cauliflower.

Ingredients

  • Cauliflower (trimmed weight)800 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 ml
  • Wholemeal breadcrumbs40 g
  • Fine salt5 g
  • Black pepper1 g
  • Fresh rosemary2 sprigs
  • Fresh thyme3 sprigs
  • Garlic1 clove
  • Organic lemon zest5 g

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Begin by preheating your oven to 200°C using the fan-assisted setting. This high temperature is crucial for triggering the Maillard reaction, which gives the cauliflower its golden colour and delicious toasted flavour. While the oven reaches temperature, prepare a large baking tray by lining it with baking parchment. The result will be a cooking environment ready to receive the vegetables without losing initial heat. Chef's Tip: Use an oven thermometer if possible, as temperature precision is key to achieving perfect crispness.
  2. Take 800g of cauliflower and proceed with cleaning by removing the outer leaves and the tough central core. Using a sharp kitchen knife, cut it into uniform florets, about 3-4 cm in diameter; uniformity is essential for even cooking. Wash the florets under cold running water and, crucially, dry them meticulously with a clean tea towel or kitchen paper. The result should be completely dry cauliflower pieces. Chef's Tip: If the cauliflower is damp, it will steam in the oven rather than roast, becoming soggy instead of crunchy.
  3. Prepare the aromatic herb mix by finely chopping 2 sprigs of rosemary, 3 sprigs of thyme, and the garlic clove (with the central germ removed for a milder taste). Work the herbs on a chopping board until you have a very fragrant, coarse powder. You will immediately smell the essential oils being released. The result is a vibrant green mix with a pungent aroma of garlic and Mediterranean herbs. Chef's Tip: Do not use an electric mixer to chop the herbs, as the heat from the blades oxidises the colour and alters their delicate flavour.
  4. In a very large mixing bowl, pour 40g of wholemeal breadcrumbs and combine them with the herb and garlic mix prepared earlier. Add 5g of finely grated lemon zest (being careful not to include the bitter white pith), 5g of salt, and 1g of black pepper. Mix thoroughly with a whisk or spoon to distribute the flavours precisely. The result will be a uniform, fragrant breading with a rustic brown hue from the wholemeal crumbs. Chef's Tip: Lemon zest adds a note of freshness that perfectly balances the natural sweetness of roasted cauliflower.
  5. Add the cauliflower florets to the bowl with the breading and pour in 40ml of extra virgin olive oil. Use your hands to toss everything together, almost massaging the florets so the oil acts as a glue, ensuring every single piece is perfectly veiled in seasoning and crumbs. This process takes about a minute of manual work. The visual result will be glossy cauliflower with the breading adhering firmly to every crevice. Chef's Tip: Don't be afraid to get your hands dirty; it is the best method to ensure there are no unseasoned areas.
  6. Spread the cauliflower onto the previously prepared baking tray, taking care not to overlap the pieces. Leave space between each floret to allow hot air to circulate freely and evaporate any remaining moisture. If the pieces are too close, steam will build up and ruin the texture. The result will be an orderly tray where every piece has its own 'breathing space'. Chef's Tip: If the tray looks too crowded, split the cauliflower across two separate trays rather than piling it up.
  7. Place the tray on the middle oven rack and bake at 200°C for about 12-15 minutes for this first stage. Do not open the oven door for the first 10 minutes to maintain constant radiant heat. You will notice the herb aroma starting to fill the kitchen and the tips of the cauliflower beginning to change colour. The partial result will be cauliflower that is starting to soften but is still pale. Chef's Tip: Watch the browning through the glass; every oven has its hot spots and you might need to rotate the tray later.
  8. Briefly remove the tray and, using a spatula or kitchen tongs, gently turn the florets to expose the less browned parts to direct heat. Return to the oven and continue cooking for another 10 minutes, or until the edges are well browned and the breading is crispy. The ultimate test is with a fork: it should slide easily into the stem of the floret while meeting slight resistance. The final result is golden, almost caramelised cauliflower on the outside and tender in the centre. Chef's Tip: If you love an extra-crunchy effect, turn on the grill function for the final 120 seconds of cooking.
  9. Remove the cauliflower from the oven and let it rest directly on the tray for 2 minutes before serving; this allows the fibres to stabilise and the breading to become even crunchier upon contact with air. Transfer to a warm serving dish to maintain temperature during the meal. The result is a visually striking, fragrant, and incredibly tasty side dish. Chef's Tip: Serve this as an accompaniment to a lean protein or as part of a rustic antipasto, adding a final squeeze of fresh lemon if you enjoy a sharper acidity.

Tips

  • Spread the florets in a single layer on the baking sheet to achieve even and crispy browning.
  • Use freshly chopped rosemary to best release its scents during the roasting process.
  • Make sure the cauliflower is completely dry before breading it to prevent it from becoming soggy.

Variations

  • Omit the garlic from the mix if you prefer a milder and less lingering aroma.
  • Cut the cauliflower into two-centimeter thick slices for a presentation similar to vegetable steaks.
