Fluffy Carrot and Ricotta Mini-Frittata

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 8 min · Easy

A version of the omelette that is much softer than the original thanks to the ricotta, which prevents the egg from becoming rubbery. The grated carrots add a natural sweetness highly appreciated by little ones.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⬇️ Big saving🧒 Kid-friendly🌾 Gluten-free🇮🇹 Italian

A versatile and fluffy dish characterized by its sweet flavor and inviting color. Fresh ricotta gives an incredible softness to the mixture, while carrots add a slightly rustic and fragrant texture.

Ingredients

  • Medium eggs3 pcs
  • Fresh cow's milk ricotta (a soft, mild Italian whey cheese)100 g
  • Medium carrots2 pcs
  • 24-month aged Parmigiano Reggiano (a hard, aged Italian cheese)15 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil5 g

Allergens

Eggs, Milk

Method

  1. Start by meticulously preparing the 2 medium carrots: wash them under cold running water and peel them with a vegetable peeler. Use a fine-hole grater to reduce the carrots into a very fine pulp, almost a purée, which will blend perfectly with the eggs to ensure quick and even cooking. The result should be a moist, vibrantly orange mass, free of coarse chunks that might disrupt the soft texture. My professional tip is not to squeeze out the carrot juice, as its natural moisture helps keep the omelette hydrated during the cooking process.
  2. Take the 100g of fresh cow's milk ricotta and place it in a large, high-sided bowl to make mixing easier. Use a fork or a small hand whisk to mash and work it vigorously for about 2 minutes until it transforms into a smooth, velvety cream without any lumps. This stage is crucial to avoid finding firm cheese bits in the final dish, ensuring a homogenous structure instead. I suggest using room-temperature ricotta, as cold cheese from the fridge is much harder to emulsify properly.
  3. Crack the 3 medium eggs one by one and add them to the previously prepared ricotta cream. Begin whisking with an electric or hand whisk using circular, upward motions to incorporate as much air as possible. Continue for at least 3 minutes until the mixture appears pale, frothy, and has slightly increased in volume. The chef's secret for a "cloud-like" omelette lies in this aeration phase, which allows the egg proteins to support the fluffy structure despite the carrots' moisture.
  4. Now, fold the 15g of 24-month aged Parmigiano Reggiano and the grated carrot pulp into the mixture. Use a silicone spatula and mix with slow, rotating movements, ensuring the vegetables are evenly distributed without deflating the foam you've created. You should achieve a thick batter, dotted with orange flecks and carrying a delicately sweet, milky aroma. Be careful not to over-mix: once the carrots are distributed, stop immediately to preserve the lightness of the batter.
  5. Take a non-stick frying pan approximately 20-22 cm in diameter and pour in the 5g of extra virgin olive oil, spreading it evenly over the bottom and sides with a pastry brush. Heat the pan over medium-low heat for about a minute, checking that the oil is fluid but not smoking. A controlled temperature is essential to prevent burning the base before the core is cooked through. My advice is to wipe the pan with a piece of kitchen paper after brushing to remove any excess, leaving only a thin protective film.
  6. Pour the entire mixture into the hot pan, levelling it quickly with the back of a spoon, and immediately cover with a lid of the exact size. Cook over a gentle heat for about 4-5 minutes, allowing the trapped steam to promote the natural rising of the egg and cook the top surface. You will know it is time to flip it when the edges pull away easily and the top appears opaque and set, no longer liquid. Avoid lifting the lid during the first 3 minutes to prevent the loss of heat necessary for the rise.
  7. To flip the omelette without breaking it, gently slide it onto the flat lid or a lightly oiled flat plate. Place the pan over the plate and, with a swift and confident motion, flip everything over to return the omelette to the pan on its uncooked side. This operation requires speed to prevent the fluffy structure from collapsing. If you notice the omelette is still too soft in the centre, wait another minute before attempting to flip it.
  8. Continue cooking the second side for no more than 2 minutes, keeping the flame very low to preserve the golden colour. At the end, the omelette should feel springy to the touch and slightly puffed up, with a core that remains moist and melts in the mouth. Turn off the heat and let the dish rest in the pan for a minute before transferring it to a cutting board to slice into strips or cubes. My final tip is to serve it lukewarm, as extreme temperatures would mask the delicacy of its flavours.

Tips

  • Drain the ricotta well from its whey before use to avoid a runny batter.
  • Use a high-quality non-stick pan to flip the omelet easily without breaking it.
  • Let the omelet cool slightly before slicing to get precise and firm wedges.

Variations

  • Add a handful of finely chopped spinach for an even more colorful version.
  • Replace Parmigiano with grated pecorino if you prefer a bolder and savorier taste.
