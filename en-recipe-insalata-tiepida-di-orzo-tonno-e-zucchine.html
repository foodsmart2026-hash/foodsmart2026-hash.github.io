Warm Pearl Barley, Tuna and Courgette Salad

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 30 min · Medium

Pearl barley is an affordable grain with a low glycaemic index. Combined with canned tuna and courgettes, it creates a balanced one-dish meal, perfect for taking to work.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free

A fresh and balanced dish, ideal for a light lunch or a picnic. Pearly barley pairs perfectly with savory tuna and sweet sautéed zucchini, all finished with fragrant mint.

Ingredients

  • Pearl barley280 g
  • Tuna in olive oil (drained weight)160 g
  • Medium courgettes3 pcs
  • Sun-dried tomatoes in oil30 g
  • Lemon (juice)0.5 pcs
  • Fresh or dried mintq.b.

Allergens

Gluten, Fish

Method

  1. Begin by rinsing 280g of pearl barley under cold running water to remove impurities and excess starch that could make it sticky. Place it in a large pot with approximately 2 litres of lightly salted water and bring to the boil over a high heat. Simmer over a medium heat for 25-30 minutes, tasting frequently towards the end to ensure the grains are tender yet retain a firm bite. The ideal result is a well-plumped grain that doesn't collapse when bitten. Chef's tip: do not over-salt the cooking water, as the tuna and sun-dried tomatoes will provide significant saltiness to the final dish.
  2. While the barley is cooking, wash and dry 3 medium courgettes thoroughly. Using a sharp chef's knife, cut the courgettes into small cubes of about half a centimetre, employing a regular mirepoix technique. Aim for uniform dimensions to ensure every piece cooks in the same amount of time. This will give you a precise dice that provides a professional appearance and consistent texture in every mouthful. Chef's tip: if the courgettes have a very seedy core, use only the firmer outer part for a crunchier result.
  3. Take a large non-stick frying pan and heat a tablespoon of oil over a high heat to prepare it for searing the vegetables. Add the diced courgettes and sauté vigorously for about 5-6 minutes, tossing the pan frequently for even browning. The courgettes should take on a lovely golden colour externally due to the Maillard reaction, whilst remaining vibrant and crisp at the centre. Avoid covering the pan, as the steam would over-soften the flesh. Chef's tip: add a pinch of salt only in the last thirty seconds of cooking to prevent the courgettes from releasing water and boiling rather than browning.
  4. Now focus on the ingredients that will provide character and aromatic depth to the salad. Take 160g of well-drained tinned tuna and flake it roughly with a fork, trying to keep some chunks visible. Take 30g of sun-dried tomatoes in oil, pat them dry to remove excess oil, and cut them into thin strips or small pieces. These ingredients will add a savoury umami note and a depth of flavour essential to balance the sweetness of the barley. Chef's tip: use high-quality tuna, preferably fillets in a jar, for a meatier and less floury texture.
  5. As soon as the barley has reached the desired level of doneness, drain it using a fine-mesh sieve so you don't lose any grains. Rinse it quickly under a stream of lukewarm water; this step stops the residual cooking and removes surface starch without completely cooling the dish. Shake the sieve well to remove every trace of excess water, which could otherwise water down the dressing. Transfer the barley to a large bowl, preferably ceramic or glass, to allow for easy mixing. Chef's tip: do not use ice-cold water, otherwise the thermal shock will close the grain's pores, preventing it from absorbing the subsequent flavours.
  6. Prepare the final aromatic component by squeezing the juice of half a fresh lemon and selecting the mint leaves. Take the fresh mint, wash and pat it dry gently, then tear the leaves by hand instead of using a knife to prevent oxidation. The lemon juice will act as an acidity balancer, cutting through the richness of the tuna and oil. You will immediately notice a fresh, citrusy aroma that lifts the overall scent of the preparation. Chef's tip: grate a little organic lemon zest before squeezing it to further intensify the dish's aromatic bouquet.
  7. It is now time to assemble all the elements in the bowl with the still-warm barley. Add the sautéed courgettes, the flaked tuna, the sun-dried tomatoes, and the fresh mint. Pour in the lemon juice and finish with a drizzle of raw extra virgin olive oil, stirring from the bottom upwards with wide, gentle movements. Ensure every grain of barley is coated in the dressing and that the ingredients are harmoniously distributed. Chef's tip: use a wooden spoon or a silicone spatula to avoid breaking the courgette cubes and tuna chunks during mixing.
  8. Allow the warm salad to rest for at least 10 minutes before serving or portioning. This waiting time is crucial so that the barley, as it cools slightly, absorbs the lemon juices and the aromas of the mint and sun-dried tomatoes. You can serve the dish at room temperature, decorating with a few whole mint leaves for a final aesthetic touch. The result will be a balanced, colourful dish rich in tactile nuances, perfect for a light yet nutritious lunch. Chef's tip: if preparing the dish well in advance, add the fresh mint only at the last moment to preserve its bright green colour and maximum fragrance.

Tips

  • Rinse the barley thoroughly under cold running water before boiling to remove any impurities.
  • Sauté the zucchini over high heat for just a few minutes to keep them crunchy and bright green.
  • Let the salad rest for ten minutes before serving to allow the flavors to blend beautifully.

Variations

  • Add desalted capers to give the dish a Mediterranean savory punch.
  • Replace mint with fresh thyme if you prefer a more herbal and less pungent aroma.
