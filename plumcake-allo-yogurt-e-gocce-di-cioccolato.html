Yogurt and Chocolate Chip Loaf Cake

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 45 min · Medium

The classic 'yogurt pot' recipe that requires no scales, perfect for involving children. The yogurt ensures a softness that lasts for days without the use of butter.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🥕 Vegetarian🧒 Kid-friendly

Soft and fragrant, this yogurt loaf cake is the perfect treat for a traditional breakfast or a tasty snack. The chocolate chips add an intense touch that contrasts beautifully with the moist texture of the batter.

Ingredients

  • Natural plain yogurt125 g
  • Plain flour (Type 00)250 g
  • Caster sugar150 g
  • Vegetable oil100 ml
  • Medium eggs3 pcs
  • Chocolate chips50 g
  • Baking powder (Italian 'lievito per dolci')16 g

Allergens

Gluten, Eggs, Milk

Method

  1. Begin by preheating your oven to 180°C (static mode) and prepare a 25 cm loaf tin by greasing it thoroughly with butter and dusting with flour, or lining it with baking parchment. Take the 3 medium eggs at room temperature and the 150g of caster sugar, placing them into a large mixing bowl or a stand mixer bowl. Use an electric whisk or a stand mixer on medium-high speed to beat the mixture for at least 5-7 minutes. You need to achieve a very pale, thick, and aerated consistency that leaves a visible trail when the whisk is lifted. Chef's tip: Using room temperature eggs allows the proteins to trap more air, ensuring a superior fluffiness without extra effort.
  2. Slightly reduce the whisking speed and incorporate the 125g of natural plain yogurt, ensuring it isn't fridge-cold to prevent thermal shock to the batter. Next, slowly drizzle in the 100ml of seed oil in a steady stream while continuing to mix gently to avoid deflating the previously created air volume. Process for about one minute until the fats are perfectly emulsified with the aqueous components. The result should be a fluid, glossy cream without any visible separation. Chef's tip: Prefer sunflower or groundnut oil for a neutral flavour that allows the yogurt's aroma to shine through.
  3. In a separate bowl, meticulously sift the 250g of plain flour (type 00) together with the 16g of baking powder to eliminate any lumps. Gradually add these dry ingredients to the egg and liquid mixture, preferably in three batches, using a rubber spatula. Use slow, circular movements from bottom to top to fold in the flour without overworking the gluten, which would make the cake rubbery. The final batter should appear smooth, velvety, and have a thick but dropping consistency. Chef's tip: Sifting doesn't just remove lumps; it oxygenates the flour, facilitating an even rise in the oven.
  4. Take the 50g of chocolate chips and quickly toss them in a teaspoon of flour (taken from the total or extra), shaking off the excess with a sieve. This step is vital because the flour creates surface friction that prevents the chocolate from sinking to the bottom of the tin during baking. Fold the chips into the batter and stir briefly with the spatula to distribute them evenly. You will notice the chips remain suspended in the thick mass of the batter. Chef's tip: Keep the chocolate chips in the freezer for 15 minutes before use to prevent them from melting as soon as they touch the room-temperature batter.
  5. Gently pour the batter into the previously prepared loaf tin, using the spatula to scrape every bit from the sides of the bowl. Level the surface with the back of a spoon or by slightly tilting the tin, making sure not to bang it on the worktop to avoid losing air bubbles. For a professional aesthetic touch, scatter an extra handful of chocolate chips over the top before baking. The mixture should fill the tin to about two-thirds of its height. Chef's tip: To achieve the classic professional central crack, draw a thin line down the centre of the batter using the tip of a knife dipped in a little melted butter.
  6. Place the loaf tin on the middle rack of the preheated oven at 180°C and set a timer for 40-45 minutes. It is crucial not to open the oven door for the first 30 minutes of baking to prevent the temperature drop from causing the delicate leavening structure to collapse. Observe through the glass: the cake should rise gradually and form a golden-brown crust on top with the characteristic longitudinal split. Towards the end of the time, the scent of vanilla and chocolate will fill your kitchen. Chef's tip: Every oven varies, so if the top browns too quickly, cover it with a sheet of foil for the final 10 minutes.
  7. After 45 minutes, perform the skewer test by inserting a long wooden skewer into the centre of the cake's crack. If the skewer comes out completely dry with no raw batter attached, the cake is cooked; otherwise, continue baking for another 5 minutes. Once removed from the oven, let the plumcake rest in the tin for about 10 minutes on a flat surface. This rest allows the cake walls to stabilise and slightly pull away from the sides of the tin. Chef's tip: Do not let it cool completely in the tin, as the residual heat will create steam, making the base of the cake excessively soggy.
  8. Carefully turn the plumcake out of the tin and transfer it to a wire rack until it has cooled completely. This step is essential to allow water vapour to evaporate evenly from all sides, preserving the crispness of the outer crust. Wait at least one hour before slicing, as the internal structure of yogurt cakes is very fragile when hot. When sliced, the piece should be moist and soft, with chocolate chips well-distributed throughout. Chef's tip: Store the plumcake under a glass dome or wrapped in cling film to keep it as soft as freshly baked for 3-4 days.

Tips

  • Lightly coat the chocolate chips in flour before mixing them in to prevent them from sinking to the bottom.
  • Ensure both yogurt and eggs are at room temperature to achieve a smoother and more aerated batter.
  • Always perform the toothpick test in the center of the cake before removing it from the oven to check for doneness.

Variations

  • Use vanilla-flavored yogurt instead of plain to enhance the aromatic profile of the cake.
  • Swap dark chocolate chips for white chocolate chips for a milder and creamier flavor.
