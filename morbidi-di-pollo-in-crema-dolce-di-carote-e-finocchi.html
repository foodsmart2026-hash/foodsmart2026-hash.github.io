Tender Chicken in Sweet Carrot and Fennel Cream

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

A nutritious and velvety main course where the chicken is finely chopped and folded into a naturally sweet vegetable cream. The texture is ideal for young children, ensuring hydration and ease of chewing without the use of salt.

A delicate main course combining tender chicken with the natural sweetness of a carrot and fennel cream. The smooth texture coats the meat, keeping it juicy and ideal for a sophisticated daily meal.

Ingredients

  • Chicken breast250 g
  • Carrots150 g
  • Fennel (tender heart)150 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil15 g
  • Water100 ml

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Begin by cleaning the vegetables: take 150g of carrots and 150g of fennel hearts. Wash everything thoroughly under cold running water, then peel the carrots with a vegetable peeler and remove any tough or fibrous outer layers from the fennel. You should end up with clean, bright vegetables, ready for preparation without any hard waste parts. Chef's tip: do not throw away the green fennel fronds, as you can use them at the end to add a fresh aroma to the dish.
  2. Now proceed to the technical cutting: dice the carrots and fennel into small, regular cubes of about 1 centimetre using a sharp knife. This uniform size is essential because it guarantees even and rapid cooking, allowing heat to quickly penetrate the core of the fibres. Work on a stable chopping board to achieve a precise and clean result. Chef's tip: keep the cubes small to facilitate the subsequent blending process without straining your blender.
  3. Transfer the vegetables into a large saucepan and add the 100ml of water specified in the recipe, then place it on the hob. Cover with a lid and cook over medium heat for approximately 18-20 minutes, checking occasionally that there is sufficient moisture. By the end of the time, the vegetables must be extremely tender, enough to collapse easily under the light pressure of a wooden spoon. Chef's tip: if the water evaporates too quickly, add a tablespoon of hot water to keep the environment moist.
  4. Use an immersion blender to transform the cooked vegetables into a smooth, thick, and lump-free purée directly in the pot or a tall beaker. Operate the appliance at medium speed with vertical movements to incorporate as little air as possible and achieve a silky consistency. The cream should have a soft orange colour flecked with white and a very delicate scent. Chef's tip: if the cream appears too grainy, pass it through a fine-mesh sieve for an even more refined texture.
  5. While the cream rests, prepare the 250g of chicken breast, removing any cartilage or visible fatty parts. Hand-cut the meat into tiny pieces, similar to shreds or cubes no larger than 5 millimetres. This task requires patience but is essential to ensure easy and safe chewing for a child. Chef's tip: put the chicken in the freezer for 10 minutes before cutting; the firmer meat will allow you to achieve much more precise cubes.
  6. Pour the vegetable cream into a wide non-stick frying pan and add the 15g of extra virgin olive oil. Turn the heat to the lowest setting and stir with a spatula to emulsify the oil into the cream until you see the first very slight bubbles appearing on the surface. The heat must be minimal to avoid altering the nutritional properties of the olive oil. Chef's tip: use a silicone spatula to avoid scratching the pan and to gather every drop of the precious cream.
  7. Gently add the chicken pieces to the warm cream, distributing them evenly with a spoon. Immediately cover with a lid and continue cooking over a very low flame for exactly 10 minutes. The meat must cook gently through indirect heat, absorbing the moisture from the vegetables and thus remaining bright white and juicy. Chef's tip: strictly avoid browning the chicken; the cooking process should be a sort of 'poaching' in the cream to keep the meat tender.
  8. Before serving, perform the final check by mashing a piece of chicken with the tines of a fork directly in the pan. If the fibre separates instantly without resistance, it means the collagen has softened perfectly and the dish is ready. Allow the preparation to cool slightly before portioning it into four small bowls. Chef's tip: serve the dish lukewarm, as high temperatures would mask the naturally sweet flavour of the carrot and fennel.

Tips

  • Cut the chicken into uniform bite-sized pieces to ensure even cooking in the pan.
  • Blend the vegetables with a little water at a time to achieve a thick, velvety consistency.
  • Store the dish in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to one day.

Variations

  • Replace the chicken breast with turkey fillets for a slightly bolder flavor profile.
  • Add a sprinkle of freshly ground black pepper at the end for a more pronounced aromatic note.
