Pea and Lemon Zest Risotto

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

A fresh and affordable variation of the classic Venetian 'Risi e Bisi'. The hint of lemon elevates the simple ingredients, making the dish refined.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⚖️ Good value for money🧒 Kid-friendly🌾 Gluten-free🇮🇹 Italian

A classic spring dish combining the sweetness of peas with the brightness of lemon zest. Stirring in butter and parmesan creates a velvety, creamy texture. This refined first course is perfect for a light lunch.

Ingredients

  • Risotto rice (Arborio or Carnaroli)320 g
  • Fine frozen peas300 g
  • Vegetable stock1.2 l
  • Fresh spring onions2 pcs
  • Butter30 g
  • Organic lemon (zest)1 pcs
  • Grated Parmesan cheese40 g

Allergens

Milk

Method

  1. Begin by preparing 1.2 litres of light vegetable broth and bring it to a boil in a separate saucepan. Once it reaches boiling point, lower the heat to minimum: it is essential that the broth remains piping hot throughout the recipe to avoid shocking the rice when added. Use a ladle to add it gradually, ensuring the liquid is clear and flavourful. The final result should be a constant temperature that allows the grains to cook evenly. The chef's tip is not to over-salt the broth at the start, as the Parmesan will add saltiness at the end.
  2. Take the 2 fresh spring onions, remove the tough green tops, and finely chop the white and light green parts with a sharp knife. In a wide casserole or heavy-based pan, melt 15g of butter (half the total amount) over a low heat and add the chopped spring onions. Let them soften gently for about 3 minutes, stirring often with a wooden spoon so they become translucent without browning. You will achieve a soft, fragrant aromatic base essential for the dish's sweetness. The chef's tip is to add a tablespoon of hot water if the onions sizzle too quickly, to prevent them from burning.
  3. Pour 320g of risotto rice (Arborio or Carnaroli) into the pan with the spring onions and slightly increase the heat to medium-high. Toast the rice for about 2 minutes, stirring constantly: the grains should heat up evenly and become translucent at the edges while keeping a white core. This technique seals the surface of the grain and allows for a controlled release of starch, ensuring a creamy but not mushy risotto. You will smell a slight toasted nut aroma and see the rice 'shine' thanks to the butter coating. The chef's tip is to touch the rice with the back of your hand; when it is too hot to touch, the toasting is perfect.
  4. Add the 300g of frozen fine peas directly into the pan with the toasted rice and stir for one minute. Immediately pour in two ladles of boiling broth, just enough to barely cover the contents, and begin the actual cooking. Maintain a medium heat so the liquid simmers constantly, allowing the peas to defrost and cook along with the rice. The peas will turn a vibrant green as they start releasing their sweetness into the risotto. The chef's tip is not to defrost the peas beforehand, to prevent them from losing their crisp texture during the rice's long cooking process.
  5. Continue cooking the rice by adding one ladle of broth at a time, only when the previous one has been almost completely absorbed. Stir the risotto frequently but gently, using a circular motion: this mechanical action encourages the starch to leave the grains, creating the dish's natural creaminess. The heat should be adjusted so the boil is lively but steady (the so-called 'all'onda' cooking). After about 10-12 minutes, you will notice the liquid transforming into a sort of velvety cream coating the grains. The chef's tip is never to 'drown' the rice in broth, but to add it bit by bit to perfectly control the cooking point.
  6. After about 16-18 minutes of total cooking time from the first ladle of broth, taste the rice to check its consistency. The grain should be 'al dente', meaning tender on the outside but with a central core that is still slightly firm and perceptible to the bite. Season with salt if necessary, keeping in mind that the final 'mantecatura' will add more saltiness. The consistency of the risotto at this stage should still be quite fluid, not excessively dry. The chef's tip is to turn off the heat one minute before the time indicated on the package, as residual heat will continue to cook the rice during resting.
  7. Move the pan away from the heat for the crucial 'mantecatura' phase. Add the remaining 15g of cold butter from the fridge, 40g of grated Parmesan, and the grated zest of one organic lemon, taking care not to include the bitter white pith. Stir vigorously for one minute to incorporate air; the cold butter, creating an emulsion with the starch and cheese, will transform the cooking juices into a silky, glossy cream. The lemon scent will release an irresistible fresh, citrusy note. The chef's tip is to use very cold butter: the thermal shock between the hot rice and cold fat is the secret to a perfect creaminess.
  8. Cover the pan with a lid and let the risotto rest for exactly 2 minutes before serving. This rest allows the temperatures to even out and the flavours to stabilise, making the dish's texture even more harmonious. Once the time is up, give it a final quick stir and plate the risotto on flat plates, lightly tapping the bottom with your palm to spread it out evenly. Garnish each portion with an extra grating of fresh lemon zest to enhance the top notes. The chef's tip is to serve the risotto on pre-warmed plates to prevent the temperature from dropping too quickly and spoiling the creaminess.

Tips

  • Toast the rice until translucent to ensure it maintains a perfect texture during cooking.
  • Use only the yellow part of the lemon zest to prevent any bitter notes from entering the dish.
  • Add the broth one ladle at a time, allowing it to be absorbed before adding more.

Variations

  • Swap the parmesan for grated pecorino romano for a bolder and saltier flavor.
  • Add a grind of fresh black pepper at the end for a spicy and sharp touch.
