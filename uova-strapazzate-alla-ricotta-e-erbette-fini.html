Scrambled Eggs with Ricotta and Fine Herbs

4 servings · Prep 5 min · Cooking 5 min · Easy

A creamy version of classic eggs, where ricotta replaces salt and provides a velvety texture. Fresh herbs add micronutrients and fragrance.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⬇️ Big saving🥕 Vegetarian🧒 Kid-friendly🌾 Gluten-free

A creamy twist on classic scrambled eggs, enriched by fresh ricotta and aromatic herbs. Ideal for a quick brunch or a soft, tasty protein-rich dinner.

Ingredients

  • Fresh eggs2 pc
  • Cow's milk ricotta (a soft, mild Italian whey cheese)100 g
  • Chives or parsley1 tsp
  • Extra virgin olive oil10 ml

Allergens

Eggs, Milk

Method

  1. Begin by thoroughly washing a teaspoon of fresh chives or parsley, drying it well with kitchen paper to remove any excess moisture. Use a sharp knife to finely mince the herbs on a chopping board, taking care to cut cleanly through the fibres rather than bruising them. Maintain a consistent cut to release the aromatic essential oils that will provide freshness to the dish. The result should be a very fine, bright green mince, ready to be folded in. Chef's Tip: Avoid using a blender, as this will oxidise the herbs and make them taste bitter.
  2. Take a medium-sized bowl and gently crack the 2 fresh eggs, ensuring no shell fragments fall inside. Using a fork, beat them lightly with a steady circular motion, aiming only to break the yolks and combine them minimally with the whites. Do not over-beat or incorporate too much air; you want a liquid base that is not perfectly homogenous. You will notice the yolks mixing with the whites to create a slightly streaky texture. Chef's Tip: Crack each egg into a small separate dish first to check for freshness before adding it to the bowl.
  3. Add the 100g of cow's milk ricotta to the lightly beaten eggs, using a spoon to scoop it from the container. Mix the combination coarsely with the fork, attempting to integrate the ricotta without completely dissolving it into the egg liquid. It is essential that small curds of cheese remain visible, as they provide a velvety texture and pleasant contrasts on the palate. You will obtain a thick, lumpy cream where the white of the ricotta stands out against the yellow of the eggs. Chef's Tip: If the ricotta is very watery, let it drain in a fine-mesh sieve for 10 minutes before use.
  4. Add the previously prepared minced chives or parsley to the egg and ricotta mix, distributing it evenly over the surface. Use a silicone spatula to fold in the herbs with upward motions, being careful not to break down the structure of the mixture. You will immediately smell the fresh scent of the herbs blending perfectly with the milky sweetness of the ricotta. The result will be a green-speckled mixture, ready for the pan. Chef's Tip: Add a pinch of white pepper if you desire a more complex aromatic note without altering the pale colour of the dish.
  5. Pour 10ml of extra virgin olive oil into a small non-stick frying pan, ideal for small quantities while maintaining control. Turn on the heat to a very low setting, allowing the oil to warm slowly for about a minute without ever reaching the smoking point. Swirl the pan with a circular wrist motion to distribute the oil evenly across the base. The oil should appear fluid and perfectly coat the surface without frying or sizzling. Chef's Tip: Use a heavy-bottomed pan that distributes heat gently to avoid thermal shock to the eggs.
  6. Gently pour the egg, ricotta, and herb mixture into the centre of the warm pan, scraping the bowl clean with the spatula. Let the mixture sit for exactly 30 seconds without touching it at all, allowing the base to begin coagulating very slowly. The low temperature is key to avoiding a golden crust, which would ruin the delicacy of the dish. You will see the edges just beginning to turn an opaque white. Chef's Tip: Patience during this stage is fundamental to achieving the creaminess typical of a top-tier scramble.
  7. Now begin to move the mixture using a wooden spoon or spatula, bringing the eggs from the outer edges towards the centre with slow, circular motions. Continue this for about 2 to 3 minutes, keeping the flame at the minimum and gently lifting the set parts from the bottom. Do not stir too quickly; you want to create soft, creamy folds rather than a shapeless mush. The eggs should look moist, glossy, and almost like a savoury custard. Chef's Tip: If the cooking seems to progress too quickly, lift the pan off the heat for a few moments to lower the temperature.
  8. As soon as the eggs are nearly cooked but still visibly glossy and wobbly, immediately remove the pan from the heat to stop the residual cooking. Transfer the scramble to a serving plate or divide it into the 4 planned portions, letting it rest for a moment to stabilise. Serve the dish at room temperature to fully appreciate the sweetness of the ricotta and the freshness of the fine herbs. The final result will be a cloud of soft, light, and incredibly velvety eggs. Chef's Tip: Serve with a slice of toasted wholemeal bread to add a crunchy note that balances the softness of the dish.

Tips

  • Cook over very low heat to keep the eggs moist and prevent the ricotta from separating.
  • Stir constantly with a silicone spatula to achieve a smooth and consistent texture.
  • Consume the dish immediately after preparation to enjoy its maximum fluffiness.

Variations

  • Use sheep's ricotta if you prefer a stronger and savorier taste.
  • Add a pinch of freshly ground black pepper to enhance the aroma of the herbs.
