Soft Polenta with Red Lentils and Steamed Baby Chard

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

A complete and creamy single-course dish, where split lentils blend with polenta for a safe and nutritious texture. The chard adds iron and minerals without weighing down digestion.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⬇️ Big saving🧒 Kid-friendly🌾 Gluten-free🇮🇹 Italian

A complete dish combining creamy instant polenta with the texture of red lentils and steamed chard. Perfect for a daily meal, it offers a balance of delicate flavors and a comforting structure.

Ingredients

  • Instant polenta cornmeal (Italian boiled cornmeal)120 g
  • Red split lentils80 g
  • Fresh Swiss chard (leaves only)200 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil20 ml
  • Grated 30-month aged Parmigiano Reggiano20 g
  • Water800 ml

Allergens

Milk

Method

  1. Begin by preparing 80g of split red lentils by rinsing them thoroughly under cold running water using a fine-mesh sieve. This step is essential to remove any dust residues or impurities that could affect the delicate flavour of the final dish. Continue washing until the water runs completely clear and no foam appears on the surface. The result will be clean pulses ready for uniform cooking. A chef's tip: do not soak them beforehand, as split lentils tend to break down too quickly if hydrated in advance.
  2. Now focus on the 200g of fresh chard, washing each leaf carefully under running water to remove any traces of soil. Remove the tough ends of the stems and stack the leaves to cut them into thin strips, then further reduce them into small 5mm pieces. Use a sharp chef's knife on a stable chopping board to achieve a clean and precise cut. This small size is ideal for an even distribution within the polenta and to make swallowing easier, especially for young children. Ensure the pieces are uniform to guarantee they cook perfectly at the same time.
  3. In a large, heavy-based saucepan, pour 800ml of water and bring it to a boil over a high heat. As soon as the first bubbles appear, add the rinsed red lentils and reduce the heat to medium-low. Partially cover with a lid and let them simmer for about 15 minutes, stirring occasionally with a wooden spoon. The lentils should start to break apart and soften, creating a velvety base in the cooking water. A useful tip: do not add salt at this stage, as any residual skin on the pulses might toughen, extending the cooking time unnecessarily.
  4. Once the lentils are tender and starting to dissolve, add the finely chopped chard to the same pot and continue cooking for a further 5 minutes over a gentle heat. The leaves must wilt completely and become very tender, blending perfectly with the pulses into a thick vegetable broth. Keep the heat controlled to prevent excessive water evaporation before adding the cornmeal. You will notice the water turning a light green and the pulses becoming almost creamy. The secret is to maintain a moist, steam-rich environment inside the pot to preserve the vegetables' tenderness.
  5. It is now time to rain in the 120g of instant polenta cornmeal, proceeding very slowly and precisely. While pouring with one hand, use the other to whisk vigorously with a hand whisk, creating a constant circular motion to prevent lumps from forming. Keep the heat medium-low to allow the flour to hydrate correctly without burning on the bottom of the pan. The polenta will begin to thicken almost instantly, incorporating the lentils and chard into a homogenous mixture. Do not stop stirring until all the flour is added and the mixture appears smooth.
  6. Continue cooking the polenta for the time indicated on the packet, usually between 5 and 8 minutes, keeping the heat very low for gentle cooking. Switch to a wooden spoon or a silicone spatula now to move the mass from the bottom upwards, ensuring it doesn't stick to the sides. The consistency you are looking for is a thick, rich, and velvety cream that falls heavily from the spoon when lifted. If you notice the polenta becoming too firm or difficult to stir, add a tablespoon of hot water to restore the correct softness. Remember that instant polenta requires constant attention to remain fluffy.
  7. Once the cooking is complete, turn off the heat and proceed with the 'mantecatura' by adding 20ml of extra virgin olive oil and 20g of grated 30-month aged Parmesan cheese. Stir vigorously to incorporate the fats, making the polenta glossy, fragrant, and naturally tasty thanks to the sapidity of the aged cheese. This step, done off the heat, allows the nutritional properties of the oil and the aromas of the Parmesan to remain intact. The result will be an incredibly creamy and inviting dish with an authentic aroma. No extra salt is needed, as the long-aged Parmesan provides sufficient seasoning.
  8. Let the polenta rest for a couple of minutes directly in the pot before serving to allow the flavours to settle. Pour it into shallow bowls or small dishes, smoothing the surface with the back of a slightly dampened spoon. For children, ensure the temperature is lukewarm and safe, keeping in mind that polenta tends to firm up slightly as it cools while remaining easy to chew. For adults, take their portion and add a pinch of salt and black pepper directly onto the plate. The chef's tip: serve immediately to enjoy maximum creaminess, perhaps with an extra drizzle of raw olive oil.

Tips

  • Rinse red lentils thoroughly to remove any residue before starting the cooking process.
  • Pour the cornmeal slowly in a steady stream to prevent lumps from forming.
  • Serve the polenta hot immediately to enjoy its optimal velvety texture.

Variations

  • You can replace Parmigiano Reggiano with aged pecorino for a bolder flavor.
  • Try using fresh spinach instead of chard for a slightly sweeter note.
