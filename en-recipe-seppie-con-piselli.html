Cuttlefish with Peas

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 45 min · Medium

A timeless classic of Mediterranean cuisine that combines the delicacy of molluscs with the sweetness of peas in a flavourful stew. This economical version uses ingredients easily found in any supermarket, guaranteeing a nutritious and balanced one-pot meal for the whole family.

This dish is a seafood classic, loved for the contrast between the tender cuttlefish and the sweetness of the peas. A simple preparation that releases all the Mediterranean flavor.

Ingredients

  • Cleaned cuttlefish (fresh or frozen)800 g
  • Petit pois (frozen or fresh)400 g
  • Tomato passata (smooth sieved tomatoes)300 g
  • Golden onion80 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Dry white wine50 ml
  • Garlic1 spicchio
  • Fresh parsley10 g
  • Fine salt4 g
  • Black pepper1 g
  • Water or light vegetable stock200 ml

Allergens

Molluscs

Method

  1. Start by preparing the cuttlefish (800 g) by rinsing them under running water and drying them well with kitchen paper. Use a clean chopping board and a sharp knife to cut the bodies into strips of about 2 cm and the tentacles into uniform pieces. Ensure the pieces are of similar size to guarantee even cooking throughout the pan. The final result should be a clean protein base ready for browning. Chef's tip: if using frozen cuttlefish, let them thaw completely in the fridge for 12 hours to prevent them from releasing too much rubbery water during cooking.
  2. Prepare the aromatic base by finely chopping the onion (80 g) and lightly crushing the garlic clove. Pour the extra virgin olive oil (30 ml) into a large saucepan or a deep pan and heat over a low flame. Let the onion and garlic soften for about 5-6 minutes until they become translucent and release an inviting aroma without burning. You will get a soft and fragrant base that will give depth to the dish. Chef's tip: add a tablespoon of water to the soffritto (sautéed base) to prevent the onion from browning too quickly and to keep it more digestible.
  3. Increase the heat to medium-high and add the previously cut cuttlefish to the base. Brown the molluscs for about 3-4 minutes, stirring frequently with a wooden spoon so they don't stick. You will notice that the cuttlefish will change colour from translucent to opaque white and will start to curl slightly. This phase is fundamental to seal in the flavours and give texture to the fish. Chef's tip: do not overcrowd the pan; if necessary, sear the cuttlefish in two batches to avoid a boiled effect.
  4. Deglaze with the white wine (50 ml): pour the liquid directly over the hot cuttlefish while keeping the flame high. Let the alcohol evaporate completely for about 2 minutes, until you no longer smell the pungent scent of wine but only its fruity aroma. Scrape the bottom of the pot well to recover the caramelised juices from the cuttlefish. The result will be a glossy and slightly reduced cooking base. Chef's tip: always use a good quality wine that you would enjoy drinking, as its acidity will balance the sweetness of the peas.
  5. Introduce the tomato passata (300 g) and part of the water or stock (about 100 ml) into the saucepan. Lower the heat to minimum, cover with a lid and let it cook gently for about 15-20 minutes. The tomato should start to simmer slowly (the classic 'pippiatina' - slow bubbling) and blend with the fish juices. You will get a bright red sauce that begins to coat the cuttlefish. Chef's tip: if the passata is too acidic, add a pinch of sugar to balance the flavour of the sauce.
  6. Add the peas (400 g) to the preparation; if they are frozen, you can add them directly without thawing them first. Mix well to distribute them evenly in the sauce and add the remaining water (100 ml) if you see that the liquid has reduced too much. Continue cooking over low heat with the lid on for another 15-20 minutes, until the peas are tender but still intact. The result will be a harmonious stew where the green and red colours blend. Chef's tip: do not overcook the peas beyond the indicated time to prevent them from losing their bright colour and becoming floury.
  7. Adjust the salt (4 g) and pepper (1 g) only towards the end of cooking, after tasting the sauce. Remember that cuttlefish are naturally savoury, so it is better to add salt sparingly. Finely chop the fresh parsley (10 g) and add it once the heat is turned off to preserve its aroma and intense green colour. Stir one last time to distribute the aromatics throughout the pan. Chef's tip: optimal savouriness is reached after a few minutes of resting, so do not over-salt at this stage.
  8. The final step consists of letting the dish rest with the heat off and the lid on for at least 5 minutes before serving. This short time allows the cuttlefish fibres to relax further and the sauce to thicken, reaching a creamy and coating consistency. Serve the cuttlefish and peas in shallow bowls, perhaps accompanied by slices of toasted crusty bread to soak up the delicious sauce. You will have a balanced, warm, and fragrant dish that represents the essence of home cooking. Chef's tip: if the sauce is too liquid, remove the lid for the last 5 minutes of cooking and slightly increase the heat.

Tips

  • Cut the cuttlefish into uniform pieces to ensure even cooking.
  • If using frozen peas, add them directly to the pan without thawing them first.
  • Do not overcook the cuttlefish to prevent them from becoming rubbery.

Variations

  • Omit the tomato puree for a white version scented with lemon zest.
  • Add a pinch of chili pepper to the sauté if you enjoy a spicy note.
