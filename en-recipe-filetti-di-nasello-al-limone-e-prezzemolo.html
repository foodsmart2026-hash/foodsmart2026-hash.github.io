Hake Fillets with Lemon and Parsley

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 10 min · Easy

A light and fragrant main course, perfect for making the whole family enjoy fish with a modest budget. The delicate lemon cream coats the hake fillets, making them juicy and appetizing in just a few minutes of cooking.

A delicate and quick recipe that enhances the tenderness of hake through a velvety lemon and parsley sauce. Lightly flouring the fillets creates a thin crust that locks in juices, making every bite tender and fragrant.

Ingredients

  • Hake fillets (fresh or defrosted)600 g
  • Organic lemons (juice and zest)2 pz
  • Fresh parsley30 g
  • Plain flour (Type 0 flour)40 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Garlic cloves2 spicchi
  • Water or vegetable stock50 ml
  • Fine salt4 g
  • Black pepper1 g

Allergens

Fish, Gluten

Method

  1. Begin by drying the 600 g of hake fillets with extreme care using absorbent kitchen paper to remove any residual moisture. This step is essential to ensure the flour adheres perfectly and that the fish doesn't release water during browning, avoiding a boiled effect. If using frozen fish, ensure it is completely defrosted and pat it dry several times until the surface feels dry to the touch. You will thus have fillets ready for uniform and inviting browning. Chef's tip: never skip this step, as excess moisture is the main enemy of fish crispiness in the pan.
  2. Prepare the aromatics by finely chopping the 30 g of fresh parsley with a sharp knife and squeezing the juice of the 2 lemons after straining them to remove the seeds. Also grate the zest of one of the lemons, taking care to take only the superficial yellow part, rich in essential oils, avoiding the white part underneath which would be bitter. Arrange everything in small bowls on the work surface to have the ingredients ready at the moment of use, as the fish will cook very quickly. The scent of fresh lemon will perfectly balance the natural sweetness of the hake. Chef's tip: use room temperature lemons to extract more juice and aroma compared to cold ones from the fridge.
  3. In a flat plate, mix the 40 g of flour with a pinch of salt and freshly ground black pepper to season the coating evenly. Pass each hake fillet through the flour, pressing gently to make it adhere well on both sides, then shake the fish between your hands to remove the excess. The fillets should appear barely veiled in white, without flour clumps that could become gummy during cooking. This light coating will protect the delicate flesh of the fish and help create a delicious cream in the final step. Chef's tip: flour the fish only a moment before putting it in the pan to prevent the flour from getting damp and sticky.
  4. Heat the 30 ml of extra virgin olive oil in a large non-stick pan along with the 2 garlic cloves, which you will have previously crushed while keeping the skin on (garlic 'in camicia'). Maintain a medium-low flame for about 2-3 minutes, moving the cloves with a fork to distribute the aroma without letting them burn. When the oil starts to sizzle slightly and the garlic appears golden and fragrant, the aromatic base is ready to receive the fish. Remove the garlic if you prefer a more delicate flavour or leave it for a more rustic and bold taste. Chef's tip: tilt the pan slightly to concentrate the oil in one spot and fry the garlic more deeply and evenly without burning the bottom.
  5. Carefully place the hake fillets in the hot pan, taking care not to overlap them to allow each one to cook evenly. Slightly increase the heat to medium-high and let brown for about 3 minutes without moving the fish until a thin golden crust forms on the base. Use a flat, wide spatula to turn the fillets very gently, as hake tends to flake easily once the fibres begin to cook. The fish will change colour, moving from translucent to an inviting opaque milky white. Chef's tip: if the fillets are of different sizes, put the thicker ones in first and the thinner ones after a minute to even out the cooking.
  6. Evenly pour the lemon juice and the 50 ml of water (or vegetable stock) over the golden fillets, maintaining medium heat to start the emulsion. The liquid will begin to simmer and react with the flour on the surface of the fish, instantly creating a fluid and fragrant sauce. Gently move the pan with a circular, waving motion so that the liquid distributes everywhere and thickens slightly. Cook for another 2 minutes, occasionally spooning the liquid over the top of the fish to keep it juicy. Chef's tip: if you notice the sauce reducing too quickly and becoming too thick, add another tablespoon of hot water to maintain the desired creaminess.
  7. Add the grated lemon zest and chopped parsley in the last 45-60 seconds of cooking to keep the bright colours and fresh aromatic notes intact. Adjust salt and pepper only after tasting the sauce, as lemon tends to enhance the natural saltiness of the fish. Turn off the heat while the sauce is still slightly liquid, as the residual heat of the pan will continue to thicken it while you prepare to serve. The visual result should be snow-white fish enveloped in a glossy veil studded with bright green. Chef's tip: the parsley should not cook for long, otherwise it loses its characteristic herbaceous aroma and turns dark.
  8. Let the dish rest for just one minute directly in the pan with the heat off, allowing the flavours to stabilise and the fish fibres to fully relax. Plate the fillets gently, distributing the lemon sauce left on the bottom of the pan over each one with the help of a spoon. Serve immediately, accompanying the hake with a side of boiled potatoes or a crisp green salad for a nutritionally balanced meal. You will get an economical but extremely refined main course, capable of transforming a simple fish into a restaurant-quality dish. Chef's tip: slightly warm the serving plates before serving to prevent the starch-rich sauce from setting instantly on contact with the cold.

Tips

  • Flour the fish only a moment before placing it in the pan to prevent the flour from getting damp.
  • Keep the heat medium-low while cooking the sauce to prevent the lemon juice from tasting bitter.
  • Use a light vegetable broth instead of water if you prefer a rounder, less acidic taste.

Variations

  • Add toasted pine nuts at the end for a crunchy contrast and a richer flavor.
  • Replace parsley with chives for a more delicate and modern aromatic note.
