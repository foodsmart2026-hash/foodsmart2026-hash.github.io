Baked Chickpea and Spinach Soft Bites

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

Small vegetable bakes rich in iron and plant-based protein. The combination of chickpeas and spinach is a classic of 'cucina povera' (traditional Italian peasant cooking), here reimagined in a safe and attractive form for early childhood.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⬇️ Big saving🥕 Vegetarian🧒 Kid-friendly🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🇮🇹 Italian

These bites are a tasty alternative for a quick lunch or an appetizer with friends. They feature a soft interior and a light outer crust, enriched by the delicate flavor of chickpeas and spinach.

Ingredients

  • Pre-cooked chickpeas (rinsed)240 g
  • Fresh or frozen spinach100 g
  • Egg1 pcs
  • Breadcrumbs15 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil5 g

Allergens

Eggs, Gluten

Method

  1. Begin by rinsing 240g of pre-cooked chickpeas under cold running water to remove the canning liquid. Gently rub the legumes between your palms to remove the outer skins; this takes about 5 minutes but ensures superior digestibility for young children. Continue until you see the transparent films naturally detaching from the chickpea body. You will be left with 'naked' legumes that are tender and ready to be creamed. My chef's tip is not to skip this step, as it significantly reduces the risk of abdominal discomfort caused by tough skin fibres.
  2. Place 100g of spinach (fresh or frozen) in a non-stick pan with just one tablespoon of water. Cover with a lid and cook over medium heat for about 3-4 minutes until the leaves have completely wilted. Once ready, transfer them to a fine-mesh sieve and press firmly with the back of a spoon or squeeze them between your hands to remove every trace of water. You should end up with a very dry and compact ball of greens. A common mistake is leaving the spinach damp, which would make the final mixture too soft and difficult to shape.
  3. Transfer the squeezed spinach to a chopping board and finely mince it with a knife until it almost becomes a pulp. This step is crucial for breaking down the long fibres of the leaves which could be difficult for infants to manage. Spend a couple of minutes on this, running the blade over the greens several times in different directions. The result must be a homogenous mince free of any visible strings. Ensure your knife is very sharp so it cuts cleanly through the fibres rather than crushing them, preserving the vibrant green colour.
  4. Place the cleaned 240g of chickpeas into a food processor and blend at maximum speed until you achieve a thick, lump-free paste. If you notice the mixture struggling to move, add just one tablespoon of warm water to help the blades without making it watery. Process for about 2 minutes, stopping occasionally to scrape down the sides with a spatula. The ideal consistency is that of a very firm and velvety hummus. Avoid adding too much liquid, or you will lose the structure required to form the bites.
  5. In a large mixing bowl, combine the chickpea paste, the minced spinach, one whole egg, and 15g of breadcrumbs. Use a fork or spatula to mix the ingredients with circular motions, ensuring the egg is fully absorbed. Work the dough for about 2-3 minutes until it becomes uniform in colour and texture. You should obtain a mouldable mixture that is slightly damp but doesn't stick excessively to your fingers. If the dough feels too wet, let it rest for 5 minutes to allow the breadcrumbs to absorb any residual moisture.
  6. Preheat your oven to 180°C (fan-assisted or static) and prepare a baking tray lined with parchment paper, lightly brushing it with 5g of extra virgin olive oil. Take one tablespoon of dough at a time and form small balls, flattening them slightly between your palms to create a disc or 'nugget' shape. Alternatively, you can distribute the mixture into greased muffin tins for a more regular and playful shape. You should get about 12 small pieces spaced well apart. The chef's secret is to slightly dampen your hands with water to achieve a smooth, crack-free surface.
  7. Place the tray on the middle rack of the preheated oven and bake for approximately 12-15 minutes at 180°C. Do not exceed the stated time; the goal is not to brown the surface or create a hard crust, but simply to set the egg to provide structure. Check for doneness by touching the centre of a bite: it should feel firm to the touch but still springy. The bites should maintain a vivid green colour without darkening. Remember that overcooking will make the dish dry and difficult for a small child to swallow.
  8. Once removed from the oven, allow the chickpea and spinach bites to rest for at least 5-10 minutes before handling. This cooling period at room temperature allows the egg proteins and starches to stabilise, making them less fragile. Serve the portions warm, always checking with your fingers that the inside is soft and easy to break into small pieces. The final appearance will be that of tender little croquettes, perfect for baby-led weaning. If you have leftovers, store them in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 24 hours.

Tips

  • Squeeze the cooked spinach thoroughly to prevent the mixture from becoming too moist.
  • If the mixture is too soft, add an extra tablespoon of breadcrumbs to firm it up.
  • Store them in the refrigerator in an airtight container for up to two days.

Variations

  • You can replace chickpeas with cannellini beans for an even creamier taste.
  • Try coating the bites in sesame seeds before cooking for a crunchy touch.
