Cod and potato patties

4 servings · Prep 25 min · Cooking 45 min · Hard

A homemade take on classic croquettes, where the delicate flavour of frozen cod meets the creaminess of boiled potatoes. This budget-friendly recipe is perfect for getting the whole family to enjoy fish, providing a nutritious and crispy dish without excessive frying.

These meatballs are a versatile main course, perfect for a tasty dinner thanks to their tender texture and delicate flavor. The combination of cod and potatoes ensures a creamy interior protected by a thin golden crust.

Ingredients

  • Frozen cod (hearts or fillets)500 g
  • Yellow-fleshed potatoes400 g
  • Egg1 pz
  • Fine breadcrumbs100 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 ml
  • Fresh parsley15 g
  • Garlic1 clove
  • Organic lemon (zest)0.5 pz
  • Fine salt5 g
  • Black pepper1 g

Allergens

Gluten, Eggs, Fish

Method

  1. Boil 400 g of unpeeled potatoes in plenty of salted water for about 25-30 minutes, until tender when pierced with a fork. Once cooked, drain and peel them while still hot to make it easier, then immediately pass them through a potato ricer into a large bowl. You will get a smooth and fluffy puree, essential for the consistency of the final mixture. The chef's tip is not to use an electric mixer to mash the potatoes, as it would make them gluey due to the excessive release of starch.
  2. Cook 500 g of previously defrosted cod, either steamed or in a non-stick pan with a tablespoon of water for about 6-8 minutes. When the fish appears opaque and flakes easily, drain it carefully and press it lightly with a fork in a sieve to remove all residual water. Pat the fillets with kitchen paper: well-dried fish is the secret to patties that don't fall apart during cooking. Once dry, flake it coarsely with your hands or a fork directly into the bowl with the potatoes.
  3. Finely chop 15 g of fresh parsley and a clove of garlic, removing the central germ to make it more digestible. Add the fragrant mixture to the bowl with the cod and potatoes, stirring with a wooden spoon to distribute the aromas evenly. The scent of garlic and parsley should be present but not overpowering, creating a balanced aromatic base. The chef suggests using only the parsley leaves to avoid fibrous textures inside the patty.
  4. Add 1 whole egg, the grated zest of half a lemon, salt, and a grind of black pepper to the mixture. Work the dough with your hands or a spoon until the egg is completely absorbed, binding the main ingredients together. You should obtain a homogeneous and slightly moist mass, with a pleasant citrus note from the lemon. For an extra touch of flavour, you can add a tablespoon of grated Parmesan, although the traditional Mediterranean recipe prefers the purity of the fish.
  5. Incorporate about 50 g of breadcrumbs into the mixture, adding them a little at a time to adjust to the desired consistency. The final mass should be firm, not sticky, and easily shaped between the palms of your hands. If the mixture still seems too soft, add another tablespoon of bread, but without overdoing it so as not to harden the patties too much. Let the mixture rest in the fridge for at least 15 minutes: this step allows the starches to stabilise, making shaping much easier.
  6. Take about 35-40 g of the mixture at a time and form spheres the size of a small walnut, rolling them between your hands. Arrange the patties on a clean tray, trying to keep them all the same size to ensure simultaneous and even cooking. You will get about 18-20 patties with a smooth and compact surface. A small trick to prevent the mixture from sticking to your fingers is to moisten them slightly with cold water every 4-5 patties created.
  7. Pour the remaining 50 g of breadcrumbs onto a flat plate and coat each patty in the bread, rolling it gently to cover every side. Press the surface lightly so that the breading adheres perfectly, forming a thin and protective layer. Shake the patties between your hands to remove excess bread before placing them back on the tray. The chef advises against using egg for the outer breading: the natural moisture of the mixture is sufficient to hold the bread for a lighter and crispier crust.
  8. Heat 40 ml of extra virgin olive oil in a large non-stick pan over medium-high heat. When the oil is hot, place the patties in the pan, spacing them a couple of centimetres apart to allow heat to circulate. Cook them for about 4-5 minutes per side, turning them very gently using tongs or two spoons. The result should be an intense and even golden brown across the entire surface, a sign of perfect crispness. Avoid turning them too often: wait for the crust to form before the first movement.
  9. Drain the patties on kitchen paper to remove surface grease and let them rest for a minute before serving. Bring them to the table while still hot, accompanied by fresh lemon wedges and a rich green salad or seasonal tomatoes. The patties should be crispy on the outside and very soft on the inside, releasing a delicate aroma of sea and lemon. Final tip: if you prefer an even lighter version, you can bake them in a fan oven at 200°C for 15-18 minutes, drizzling them with a little oil halfway through cooking.

Tips

  • Mash the potatoes while they are still hot to get a smoother, lump-free mixture.
  • Let the mixture rest in the refrigerator for thirty minutes before shaping the balls to make handling easier.
  • If you prefer a lighter cooking method, bake them in the oven at 200°C until golden.

Variations

  • Add a handful of chopped desalted capers to give a Mediterranean savory boost to the mixture.
  • Replace the parsley with fresh chives for a sharper and more refined aroma.
