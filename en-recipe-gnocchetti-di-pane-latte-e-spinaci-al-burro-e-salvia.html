Bread, Milk, and Spinach Gnocchetti with Butter and Sage

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 10 min · Easy

A traditional rural 'recovery' recipe, incredibly soft and easy to chew. These little gnocchi literally melt in your mouth.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⬇️ Big saving🥕 Vegetarian🧒 Kid-friendly🇮🇹 Italian

A traditional rural recovery dish where stale bread is transformed into soft, small bites. The delicate flavor of spinach pairs perfectly with the classic aroma of butter and sage.

Ingredients

  • Stale bread (crumb only)150 g
  • Whole milk150 ml
  • Cooked and squeezed spinach80 g
  • Type 0 flour (refined white wheat flour)40 g
  • Butter15 g
  • Sage2 leaves

Allergens

Gluten, Milk

Method

  1. Begin by finely crumbling 150g of stale breadcrumbs with your hands or a knife, placing them into a large bowl where you will pour 150ml of slightly warmed whole milk. Let the mixture soak for about 15 minutes, mashing occasionally with a fork so the bread absorbs the liquid evenly. The warmth of the milk helps break down the cell walls of the dry bread quickly. You will obtain a soft, moist mass with a milky aroma, which will serve as the structural base for your gnocchetti. Chef's tip: do not use milk straight from the fridge, as the bread would take twice as long to soften correctly.
  2. Take 80g of previously cooked spinach and squeeze them extremely thoroughly between your hands or inside a clean tea towel to remove all traces of water. Once dry, chop them finely with a knife on a board until they are reduced almost to a smooth pulp without long fibres. This step is essential to ensure a silky texture for the gnocchetti and prevent them from falling apart during cooking. The result should be a bright green and very compact purée. Chef's tip: the drier the spinach, the less flour you will need to add later, ensuring incredibly light gnocchetti.
  3. Return to the bowl with the bread and squeeze it slightly between your fingers if you notice excess milk at the bottom, then add the chopped spinach. Mix the two ingredients vigorously with a wooden spoon or your hands, perfectly integrating the vegetable into the bread mass. The dough should take on a uniform green colour and a thick consistency. Ensure no large isolated pieces of white bread remain. Chef's tip: work the dough with the palm of your hand against the sides of the bowl to make the bread grain as fine as possible.
  4. Sift 40g of type 0 flour directly over the mixture and knead briefly with your hands for no more than one minute. The flour serves exclusively as a binder; overworking it would activate the gluten, making the gnocchi rubbery instead of fluffy. You should obtain a very soft dough, slightly sticky to the touch but one that holds its shape when lifted with a spoon. If the dough feels excessively liquid, add only one extra teaspoon of flour at a time. Chef's tip: respect the flour quantities even if the dough seems too soft, as the structure will stabilise during cooking in water.
  5. Fill a wide pot with plenty of water, add a pinch of salt, and bring it to a boil over high heat, then lower the flame slightly to maintain a gentle but constant simmer. A boil that is too violent would risk breaking the gnocchetti as soon as they are dropped in, while water that is too still would not allow for the correct coagulation of the starches. Prepare a clean tray or a slotted spoon ready for use nearby. The water should be clear and ready to welcome the small quenelles. Chef's tip: add a teaspoon of oil to the water to prevent the gnocchi from sticking together if you decide to cook many at once.
  6. Use two teaspoons to form the gnocchetti: take a small amount of dough with the first one and shape a small ball (quenelle) by sliding it against the second spoon directly into the boiling water. Repeat the operation quickly for each portion, trying to maintain uniform sizes for even cooking. You will see the gnocchi sink to the bottom and then slowly rise to the surface. The shape will be rustic and irregular, typical of manual preparation. Chef's tip: dip the spoons into the boiling water before forming each dumpling to let the dough slide off without any resistance.
  7. Monitor the cooking, which will take about 2-3 minutes from the moment the gnocchetti rise to the surface: this time allows the flour to cook and the bread to compact further. As soon as you see them floating stably, remove them with a slotted spoon, draining the excess water well, and keep them momentarily warm. Their consistency should appear firm but yielding to pressure. The colour will remain a soft green dotted with the white of the bread. Chef's tip: do not drain the gnocchi all at once in a colander, as their delicacy would cause them to squash under their own weight.
  8. In a small non-stick pan, melt 15g of butter with 2 fresh sage leaves (washed and dried), keeping the heat very low. Let the butter foam slightly and the essential oils of the sage release, scenting the fat without letting it darken or burn (it must not become nut-brown). The butter should appear clear and golden, with an intense and inviting herbal aroma. This simple sauce will enhance the delicate flavour of the spinach and bread. Chef's tip: if you want a more intense aroma, tear the sage leaves by hand instead of leaving them whole.
  9. Transfer the cooked gnocchetti directly into the pan with the butter and sage, swirling them gently for a few moments so that each piece is glazed by the seasoning. Immediately serve 4-5 gnocchetti per plate, ensuring they are very hot and glossy. For younger diners, you can cut the balls in half to make chewing and cooling easier. The final dish will appear elegant in its simplicity, with an enveloping scent of melted butter and cooked bread. Chef's tip: add a tablespoon of cooking water to the butter if you prefer to create a creamier emulsion instead of a purely fatty seasoning.
  10. Complete the dish with a very light dusting of Parmesan if desired (not included in the basic ingredients) or simply serve them as they are to appreciate the purity of the flavours. The internal texture should be almost creamy, melting in the mouth at the first bite. Serve the indicated portion, remembering that these gnocchetti are very nutritious thanks to the combination of carbohydrates and milk proteins. Chef's tip: serving on pre-heated plates will help maintain the ideal temperature of the butter, which tends to solidify quickly.

Tips

  • Squeeze the cooked spinach thoroughly to prevent the dough from becoming too watery.
  • Use two wet teaspoons to shape uniform dumplings without the mixture sticking.
  • Serve immediately to enjoy the best texture and warmth.

Variations

  • You can enhance the seasoning by adding grated cheese before serving.
  • Replace spinach with tender Swiss chard for a slightly different flavor profile.
