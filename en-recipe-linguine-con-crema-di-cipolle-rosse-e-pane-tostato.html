Linguine with Red Onion Cream and Toasted Breadcrumbs

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

Red onions, slow-cooked for a long time, become sweet and creamy, creating a perfect base for the pasta. Toasted bread adds the necessary texture for a balanced dish.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🇮🇹 Italian

Ingredients

  • Linguine320 g
  • Tropea red onions (sweet red onions from Calabria)400 g
  • Grated Pecorino Romano (sharp, salty sheep's milk cheese)40 g
  • Stale bread (breadcrumbs)50 g
  • Apple cider vinegar1 cucchiaio
  • Sugar1 cucchiaino
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Salt and pepperq.b.

Allergens

Gluten, Milk

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the 400g of Tropea red onions: peel them carefully and slice them very thinly and uniformly using a sharp knife or a mandoline to ensure even cooking. Place them in a large frying pan with 30ml of extra virgin olive oil and a pinch of salt, which helps draw out their natural juices. Keep the heat medium-low for about 5 minutes, stirring frequently until they become soft and translucent. The chef's tip is not to rush this stage: the onions must soften gently without browning or burning to preserve their natural sweetness.
  2. Add the teaspoon of sugar and the tablespoon of apple cider vinegar to the onions, stirring well to allow the acidic part of the vinegar to evaporate. Cover the pan with a lid and let them stew on very low heat for about 20 minutes, adding a tablespoon of water if the base becomes too dry. When finished, the onions should have an almost jam-like consistency with a balanced sweet-and-sour aroma. The secret to perfect digestibility is this prolonged cooking which transforms the onion's complex sugars.
  3. While the onion base continues to cook, prepare the crunchy element: take 50g of stale breadcrumbs and crumble them finely by hand or with a small blender. Heat a non-stick pan with a drizzle of oil and toast the crumbs over medium heat for about 4 minutes, moving them constantly with a wooden spoon. You should achieve a crunchy texture and an intense golden-brown colour, similar to earth. The chef suggests adding a pinch of black pepper to the pan to fragrance the crumbs as they heat up.
  4. Once the onions are ready, remove about half of them and transfer to a blender beaker along with a ladle of hot water (from the pasta pot). Blend at maximum power until you obtain a thick, smooth, and brilliant purple cream, ensuring there are no residual strands. This cream will be the velvety heart of your dish, coating the pasta evenly. If the cream appears too lumpy, pass it through a fine-mesh sieve for a professional restaurant-quality result.
  5. Bring a pot of plenty of salted water to the boil, keeping in mind to use less salt than usual as the final seasoning is naturally salty. Add the 320g of linguine and cook according to the package instructions, but set a timer to drain them exactly 2 minutes before the end of the cooking time. The pasta must be very 'al dente' because it will finish cooking with the sauce, absorbing all the flavours. Remember to reserve at least two cups of the cooking water before draining: it is the secret ingredient for creaminess.
  6. Transfer the drained linguine directly into the pan with the remaining stewed onions and immediately add the blended onion cream. Turn the heat back to medium and start tossing the pasta energetically, adding a ladle of cooking water to create a fluid emulsion. You will see the linguine begin to turn purple and release its starch, binding perfectly with the onions. Continue stirring until the liquid has reduced, leaving a glossy sauce at the bottom of the pan.
  7. Turn off the heat completely and proceed with the 'mantecatura': add the 40g of grated Pecorino Romano and a final splash of cooking water if necessary. Stir with rapid circular motions or toss the pasta with a flick of the wrist to incorporate air and form a silky cream as the cheese melts. It is vital to do this off the heat to prevent the Pecorino from clumping or becoming stringy due to thermal shock. The final result should be 'all'onda' pasta, where the sauce is creamy rather than dry.
  8. Plate the linguine by creating a nest in the centre of each dish using a carving fork and a ladle. Generously sprinkle each portion with the toasted breadcrumbs you prepared and finish with a final grind of fresh black pepper. Serve the dish immediately while the cream is still fluid and the bread retains its maximum crunch. The contrast between the sweetness of the onion, the saltiness of the pecorino, and the toasted note of the bread creates a complete sensory experience.
