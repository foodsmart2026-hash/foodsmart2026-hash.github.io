Roasted Miso and Black Sesame Cauliflower Florets

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 25 min · Easy

A crunchy umami-rich side dish where the saltiness of miso paste perfectly complements the charred sweetness of roasted cauliflower.

🌿 Seasonal recipe🌱 Vegan🌾 Gluten-free🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🌍 International

Ingredients

  • Cauliflower (white or coloured)800g
  • White miso paste2 tablespoons
  • Soy sauce1 tablespoon
  • Maple syrup1 tablespoon
  • Sunflower oil2 tablespoons
  • Freshly grated ginger1 teaspoon
  • Black sesame seeds1 tablespoon
  • Spring onion (green part only)1

Allergens

Sesame, Soy

Method

  1. Begin by preheating your oven to 200°C (fan oven), ensuring the rack is positioned in the centre. Take 800g of cauliflower and, using a sharp chef's knife, divide it into small florets that are as uniform as possible. It is essential they are the same size to guarantee even cooking throughout the tray. You should aim for bite-sized pieces about the size of a walnut, removing any overly tough stalks. Chef's tip: don't discard the central stalk; peel away the fibrous outer layer and dice the core, it will be delicious once roasted.
  2. Prepare the aromatic glaze in a very large bowl by combining 2 tablespoons of light miso paste, 1 tablespoon of soy sauce, 1 tablespoon of maple syrup, 2 tablespoons of sunflower oil, and 1 teaspoon of freshly grated ginger. Use a small whisk or a fork to mix vigorously until you achieve a thick, smooth cream without any miso lumps. The glaze should be perfectly emulsified, with an intense aroma balancing the saltiness of the miso and the sweetness of the syrup. Chef's tip: grate the ginger using a fine-hole grater to avoid finding stringy bits in the final dish.
  3. Add the cauliflower florets into the bowl with the glaze and mix thoroughly using your clean hands. This is the best way to ensure every single crevice of the cauliflower is well-coated by the marinade, creating a glossy and even film. Gently massage the vegetables until the white colour of the cauliflower is completely veiled by the golden-brown sauce. Chef's tip: use latex gloves if you prefer not to get messy, but avoid using spoons which might break the delicate tips of the florets.
  4. Line a large baking tray with baking parchment and spread the cauliflower out in a single layer, taking care not to overlap the pieces. If the florets are too close together, they will release steam and become soggy rather than roasting correctly. You must leave at least a centimetre of space between each piece to allow the hot air to circulate freely. Chef's tip: if one tray isn't enough, use two rather than crowding the first; space is the secret to impeccable crispness.
  5. Place the tray in the preheated oven and roast for about 12 minutes for the first cooking stage. During this time, the sugars in the miso and maple syrup will begin to caramelise on the cauliflower's surface, releasing an irresistible toasted aroma. You will notice the edges of the florets starting to darken slightly. Chef's tip: keep an eye on the cooking through the oven door, as miso tends to burn quickly if the heat is not perfectly distributed.
  6. Once the initial time is up, remove the tray and, using a spatula or kitchen tongs, gently turn each floret over to expose the other side to direct heat. Return the tray to the oven and continue roasting for another 8-10 minutes at 200°C. This step is vital for achieving an all-over golden colour and an even texture. Chef's tip: act quickly so the oven temperature doesn't drop too much while the tray is outside.
  7. Check for final doneness: the cauliflower should be tender when pierced with the tip of a knife, but with well-browned, almost charred edges. This contrast between the soft heart and the crispy, caramelised tips is the hallmark of a perfect roast. The scent will be a complex blend of umami and toasted notes. Chef's tip: don't be afraid of a few dark spots; those 'burnt' areas are packed with the most flavour thanks to the Maillard reaction.
  8. While the cauliflower finishes cooking, wash the spring onion and finely slice only the green part into rounds. Once you take the cauliflower out of the oven, transfer it immediately to a serving platter and sprinkle with 1 tablespoon of black sesame seeds. The residual heat will help the seeds stick to the glaze and release their nutty aroma. Chef's tip: lightly toast the sesame seeds in a dry pan for a minute beforehand to maximise their fragrance.
  9. Complete the dish by scattering the fresh spring onion rounds over the hot florets for a touch of colour and freshness. Serve the dish warm, as the complexity of the miso sauce expresses itself better at this temperature than when piping hot. The final result should be an elegant, glossy side dish with a balanced sweet and savoury flavour. Chef's tip: this dish is also extraordinary served at room temperature as part of a basmati rice or quinoa bowl.
