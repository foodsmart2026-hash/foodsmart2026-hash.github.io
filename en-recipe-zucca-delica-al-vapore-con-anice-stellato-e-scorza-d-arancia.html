Steamed Delica pumpkin with star anise and orange zest

🌿 Seasonal recipe

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 12 min · Easy

An aromatic side dish that uses steaming to keep the nutritional properties and texture of the pumpkin intact.

Ingredients

  • Prepared Delica pumpkin600g
  • Star anise3 pods
  • Unwaxed orange (zest)1
  • Cinnamon stick1
  • Extra virgin olive oil3 tbsp
  • Black Cyprus saltto taste
  • Curly parsley for garnishto taste

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Start by taking the 600g of cleaned Delica pumpkin and cut it into regular wedges or cubes of about 3 cm using a sharp chef's knife. Try to keep the sizes as uniform as possible to ensure that every piece cooks within the same timeframe. The result should be a series of compact pieces ready to be placed in the steamer. My professional advice is not to remove the skin: Delica has a thin, edible skin that adds a pleasant texture and a nutty flavour to the dish.
  2. Prepare the aromatic base by filling a large pot with about two litres of water and add the 3 star anise pods and the cinnamon stick, previously broken in half. Bring the water to a boil over a high heat until you see the steam rising, which will begin to smell intensely of spices. This stage takes about 5-7 minutes and serves to create an aromatic atmosphere that will penetrate the pumpkin flesh. Ensure the water level is sufficient for boiling but never touches the bottom of the steamer basket you will place on top.
  3. Place the pumpkin pieces inside the steamer basket, distributing them carefully in a single layer or trying not to overlap them too much to allow the steam to circulate. Place the basket over the pot of aromatic boiling water and immediately cover with a lid that seals the edges well. Maintain the hob at a medium heat to generate constant but not overly violent steam. The pumpkin should look well-arranged and ready to absorb the scents of anise and cinnamon without being submerged in liquid.
  4. Proceed with steaming for about 10-12 minutes, carefully monitoring the texture of the pumpkin in the final moments of cooking. To check if it is ready, pierce a cube with a wooden skewer or a fork: it should enter without resistance, but the pumpkin must remain firm and not fall apart. The flesh will turn a vivid orange, slightly translucent and very fragrant. Be careful not to overcook it, otherwise you will end up with a texture that is too floury and not elegant enough for serving.
  5. While the pumpkin is cooking, thoroughly wash the untreated orange and remove thin strips of zest using a zester or a vegetable peeler, taking care not to pick up the bitter white pith called albedo. Finely grate the remaining part of the peel with a Microplane-style grater to obtain a very fine citrus powder. You should get bright decorative filaments and an intensely aromatic powder. A little secret: use a cold orange from the fridge, as the firmer skin is easier to grate precisely.
  6. In a clean small bowl, pour the 3 tablespoons of extra virgin olive oil and add the finely grated orange zest you have just prepared. Stir vigorously with a teaspoon for a few moments, allowing the citrus essential oils to infuse the olive oil with their fresh fragrance. Let the emulsion rest at room temperature for the duration of the pumpkin's cooking time. You will get a shiny and highly fragrant dressing that will act as a perfect link between the sweetness of the pumpkin and the spicy notes. Do not salt the oil at this stage to avoid drawing moisture from the zest.
  7. Gently remove the basket from the pot, being careful of the hot steam, and arrange the still-steaming pumpkin on a flat serving plate, trying not to break the cubes. Immediately drizzle the surface of the pumpkin with the orange-flavoured oil, so that the residual heat helps to further release the citrus aromas. Now sprinkle with the Black Cyprus salt as needed, which will create a spectacular chromatic contrast and a pleasant crunchy saltiness. The pumpkin will look glossy and inviting, with the salt crystals standing out against the orange background.
  8. Complete the presentation by harmoniously distributing the reserved orange zest filaments and a few leaves of fresh curly parsley for a touch of bright green colour. Serve the pumpkin immediately while it is still hot to fully enjoy its softness and the contrast between the sweetness of the Delica and the freshness of the orange. This dish is a tribute to refined simplicity and will amaze your guests with its aromatic balance. My final touch: if you want an even deeper aroma, drop one or two of the star anise pods from the pot onto the plate as a purely visual decoration.
