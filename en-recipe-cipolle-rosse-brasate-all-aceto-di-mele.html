Red Onions Braised in Apple Cider Vinegar

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

An affordable side dish with an irresistible sweet and sour flavour. Perfect to accompany legumes, veggie burgers or white meats.

A vibrant sweet and sour side dish, ideal for enhancing cheese boards or roasted meats. The red onions become tender and glossy thanks to slow braising in apple cider vinegar and sugar.

Ingredients

  • Large red onions600 g
  • Apple cider vinegar50 ml
  • Brown or white sugar1 tsp

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Start by carefully cleaning 600g of red onions, removing the dry outer skins and the root ends. Place the onion on a chopping board and slice it evenly into discs about half a centimetre thick, using a sharp chef's knife. Aim for a consistent thickness to ensure every slice cooks at the same rate and to prevent thinner pieces from burning. You will end up with beautiful, compact rings ready for braising. Chef's tip: lightly dampen the knife blade before starting; the water helps limit the release of vapours that cause your eyes to water.
  2. Take a wide non-stick frying pan, about 28-30 cm, and heat 2 tablespoons of extra virgin olive oil over medium heat. Arrange the sliced onions so they are well distributed across the bottom, trying not to overlap them too much so that every piece touches the hot surface. You should hear a gentle sizzle as soon as they hit the oil, a sign that the temperature is right to begin. The onions will start to release their sweet, characteristic aroma as the oil coats them. Chef's tip: do not crowd too many onions into a small pan, as they will release too much water and boil rather than sauté properly.
  3. Turn the heat down to a gentle level and let the onions sweat for about 5-7 minutes, stirring occasionally with a wooden spoon. During this time, the onions should lose their stiffness and gradually become translucent and soft to the touch. They shouldn't take on colour or burn, but simply 'sweat' to concentrate their natural sugars. You will notice the volume starting to reduce slightly as they become more tender. Chef's tip: if you notice the onions are browning too quickly, add a tablespoon of warm water to lower the temperature of the pan.
  4. Now sprinkle one teaspoon of sugar (brown or white) over the onions and increase the heat slightly for one minute. Stir carefully so that every slice is coated in the sugar, which will begin to melt and start the caramelisation process. This step serves to balance the acidity we will add later and gives the onions a deeper, more brilliant colour. The onions will start to look slightly glossier and darker. Chef's tip: use brown sugar if you want a slightly more honeyed aftertaste and a more intense colour tone.
  5. Pour 50ml of apple cider vinegar into the pan, turning the heat up to high to allow the sharpest part of the acidity to evaporate quickly. Stir well to pick up all the flavours from the bottom of the pan, a technique called 'deglazing' which creates the base of the sauce. You will smell an intense aroma that shifts from sharp to fruity in moments thanks to the apple base of the vinegar. Let it bubble for about a minute until you no longer smell the strong scent of alcohol or pure acid. Chef's tip: keep your face away from the pan during this stage to avoid the intense vinegar steam which can be irritating.
  6. Add a pinch of salt to balance the sweetness, turn the heat down to minimum, and cover the pan with a tight-fitting lid. Continue braising for about 10-15 minutes, checking occasionally to ensure the bottom doesn't dry out. The moist environment created by the lid will allow the onions to become incredibly soft, almost buttery, without falling apart. You should aim for tender onions that still hold their disc shape. Chef's tip: if you see that the liquid has evaporated entirely before the time is up, add another tablespoon of water to maintain the ideal humidity.
  7. Remove the lid for the final two minutes of cooking and increase the heat slightly to reduce the cooking juices. Stir with slow movements until the remaining liquid transforms into a shiny, thick, and syrupy glaze that completely coats every onion slice. The final colour should be an intense and brilliant ruby red, looking extremely inviting. At this point, the onions will be so tender they can be cut with light pressure from a fork. Chef's tip: do not over-reduce the sauce; the glaze should be fluid and not become too sticky or like hard candy.
  8. Turn off the heat and transfer the braised onions to a ceramic or glass bowl to cool down. You can serve them warm as a side dish for meats or cheeses, or let them cool completely to enjoy in a salad or on crusty bread. The flavour will stabilise and become more rounded and harmonious if left to rest for at least an hour before eating. You will notice that cooling makes the glaze even more velvety and pleasant on the palate. Chef's tip: prepare these onions a day in advance; resting overnight in the fridge allows the apple cider vinegar aromas to meld perfectly with the onion's sweetness.

Tips

  • Use a large pan to allow the vinegar to evaporate properly and caramelize the sugars.
  • Slice the onions uniformly to ensure even cooking throughout the entire batch.
  • Leftovers can be stored perfectly in the refrigerator for 2-3 days in a sealed glass container.

Variations

  • Replace the brown sugar with honey for a different and more aromatic flavor profile.
  • Add a few black peppercorns during the cooking process for a subtle spicy touch.
