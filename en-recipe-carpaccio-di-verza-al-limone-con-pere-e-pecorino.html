Savoy Cabbage Carpaccio with Lemon, Pears and Pecorino

🌿 Seasonal recipe

4 servings · Prep 45 min · Cooking 0 min · Easy

A fresh and crunchy starter where raw Savoy cabbage is softened by a citrus marinade and paired with the sweetness of pears.

Ingredients

  • Savoy cabbage (heart only)400 g
  • Abate pears2
  • Pecorino Romano PDO shavings80 g
  • Walnut halves40 g
  • Lemon (juice)1
  • Extra virgin olive oil4 tbsp
  • Fine saltto taste
  • Ground black pepperto taste

Allergens

Tree nuts, Milk

Method

  1. Start by taking 400g of cabbage heart, carefully removing the tougher, darker, and more leathery outer leaves. Wash it thoroughly under a stream of cold running water and dry it perfectly using a salad spinner or a clean cotton cloth. It is essential that the cabbage is bone dry to allow the dressing to adhere properly without sliding off. You should have a compact heart, pale green in colour and crunchy in texture, ready for the next stage. Chef's tip: do not throw away the discarded outer leaves; they are excellent boiled and then sautéed in a pan or added to a vegetable soup.
  2. Take a very sharp mandoline and slice the cabbage heart into extremely fine, almost transparent strips, working calmly and precisely. Rest the cabbage on the blade with a firm hand but without applying excessive pressure, moving back and forth for about 5 minutes until the required weight is finished. The result should be a sort of 'cloud' of cabbage, extremely thin, which will be much more pleasant on the palate than a coarse cut. Chef's tip: always use the mandoline's finger guard to protect your hands; safety is the most fundamental ingredient in any professional kitchen.
  3. In a small bowl, prepare the emulsion for the marinade by combining the strained juice of one lemon, 4 tablespoons of extra virgin olive oil, a pinch of fine salt, and a dusting of black pepper. Use a small hand whisk or a fork and beat vigorously for about a minute until the liquids are perfectly emulsified. You should obtain an opaque citronette, slightly thick and with a fresh, citrusy aroma. Chef's tip: if the lemon is not very juicy, roll it on the work surface while pressing with the palm of your hand for a few seconds before cutting it, so it releases more juice.
  4. Transfer the sliced cabbage into a very large bowl and drizzle the freshly prepared lemon and oil emulsion evenly over it. Now proceed with the 'massage': use your hands to manipulate the vegetables gently but firmly for about 2 minutes, lightly squeezing the strips between your fingers. This mechanical step serves to break down the cellular fibres of the raw cabbage, making it tender and easier to digest. You will notice the cabbage slightly decrease in volume and take on a shinier, less rigid appearance. Chef's tip: do not be afraid to get your hands dirty; this is the only way to guarantee a perfect texture for the carpaccio.
  5. Cover the bowl with cling film and place it in the refrigerator at a temperature of about 4°C, letting the cabbage marinate for at least 30 minutes. During this time, the acidity of the lemon will act on the fibres making them even softer, while the flavours will meld deeply. At the end of the resting time, the cabbage should be well-seasoned but must still retain a pleasant and light crunch when bitten. Chef's tip: if you prefer a more intense flavour and a softer texture, you can extend the marination up to an hour, making sure to stir it halfway through.
  6. Just before assembling the dish, take the 2 Abate pears, peel them with a vegetable peeler, and cut them in half lengthways to remove the central core. Slice them very thinly and immediately brush them with a few drops of lemon juice to prevent oxidation from turning them brown. The slices should appear elegant, uniform, and glossy, ready to be laid over the vegetable base. Chef's tip: use pears that are ripe but still very firm to the touch; if they are too soft, they will fall apart during slicing and ruin the aesthetics of the dish.
  7. Now prepare the final garnish elements: take the 40g of walnuts and chop them roughly with a knife on a cutting board, keeping visible pieces for the crunchy part. Next, reduce the 80g of Pecorino Romano PDO into thin flakes using a vegetable peeler or a smooth-bladed cheese knife. You will now have all the necessary contrasts at hand: the saltiness of the cheese and the nuttiness of the dried fruit which will balance the sweetness of the pear. Chef's tip: lightly toast the walnut halves in a hot non-stick pan for a minute without fat to incredibly enhance their essential oils and aroma.
  8. For the final plating, arrange the marinated cabbage at the base of four individual plates, creating a uniform and soft bed. Lay the pear slices on top in a fan shape, then carefully distribute the pecorino flakes and finally the walnut pieces, finishing with a final grind of black pepper. The dish should look fresh, colourful, and with the various ingredients well-distributed to ensure every flavour in every bite. Chef's tip: serve the carpaccio at room temperature or just slightly chilled, accompanying it with some toasted dark bread for a complete tasting experience.
  9. Your Cabbage Carpaccio is ready to be served as a refined starter or a gourmet side dish. Check that the pecorino does not excessively overpower the delicate taste of the pear, maintaining the balance between saltiness and sweetness. The cabbage should be silky on the palate but still vibrant and fragrant with lemon. Chef's tip: if you want an extra touch of colour, you can add a few pomegranate seeds which will also add a pleasant acidic and crunchy note.
