Valle d'Aosta style cheesy polenta

Valle d'Aosta · 4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 45 min · Easy

Soft polenta enriched with Fontina cheese and melted butter.

🇮🇹 Italian

A symbol of Aosta Valley tradition, polenta concia is a rich and hearty dish perfect for cold days. The sweetness of the cornmeal blends harmoniously with the distinct character of Fontina DOP and the creaminess of melted butter.

Ingredients

  • Polenta flour300 g
  • Water1200 ml
  • Fontina DOP cheese200 g
  • Butter60 g
  • Coarse sea salt10 g

Allergens

Milk
May contain traces of: Gluten.

Method

  1. Start by pouring 1200 ml of water into a copper cauldron or a heavy-bottomed pot and add 10 g of coarse salt. Bring to a boil over high heat, ensuring the salt dissolves completely to season the base of your polenta evenly from the very beginning.
  2. When the water reaches a boil, lower the heat slightly and pour the 300 g of cornmeal in a steady stream, very slowly. Use a hand whisk and stir vigorously to prevent the formation of annoying lumps, a fundamental step to achieve a final consistency as smooth as silk.
  3. Continue cooking for about 40 minutes over low heat, replacing the whisk with a wooden spoon to better manage the thickening mass. Stir regularly from the bottom upwards; you will know the polenta is ready when it starts to pull away slightly from the sides of the pot, emitting an intense aroma of toasted corn.
  4. While the polenta is cooking, take the 200 g of Fontina DOP and cut it into small, uniform cubes. Reducing the cheese into small pieces is an essential trick to facilitate a quick and homogeneous melting once you add it to the boiling polenta, preventing any solid chunks from remaining.
  5. In a separate small saucepan, melt the 60 g of butter over medium-low heat until it begins to sizzle and takes on a light hazelnut color. This step, known as 'beurre noisette', will release an irresistible toasted aroma that characterizes the true tradition of the Aosta Valley.
  6. After the 40 minutes of cooking time, turn off the heat and add the Fontina cubes to the piping hot polenta. Stir vigorously with the wooden spoon until the cheese is completely melted, creating a stringy and delicious cream that defines this 'concia' version.
  7. Let the polenta rest in the pot for one minute with the heat off to allow the flavors of the corn and Fontina to blend perfectly. The ideal consistency should be soft and velvety, capable of settling gently on the plate without being too firm.
  8. Divide the polenta concia into four deep plates or large bowls, trying to level it slightly with the back of the spoon. It is important that the dish is hot to keep the Fontina stringy throughout the entire meal.
  9. Complete each portion by pouring the still hot, golden melted butter over the top, distributing it evenly over the surface of each plate. Serve immediately while the polenta is steaming to enjoy the contrast between the softness of the corn and the richness of the melted butter.

Tips

  • Stir constantly with a wooden spoon to prevent lumps from forming and ensure a velvety texture.
  • Cut the Fontina into very small cubes so it melts quickly and evenly once added to the hot polenta.
  • Use a copper pot or a heavy-bottomed saucepan for optimal heat distribution during the long cooking process.

Variations

  • Place the polenta under the oven grill for a few minutes to create a delicious golden crust on top.
  • Add a generous sprinkle of freshly ground black pepper to balance the richness of the cheese and butter.
