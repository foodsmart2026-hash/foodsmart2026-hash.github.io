Eggs en Cocotte with Swiss Chard and Parmesan

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 20 min · Medium

A refined main dish in presentation but extremely economical. The eggs cook gently on a bed of flavourful vegetables.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🌾 Gluten-free

Eggs en cocotte are an elegant and simple dish to prepare, great for brunch. The soft egg yolk melts into a savory base of Swiss chard and a Parmesan cheese crust.

Ingredients

  • fresh eggs4 pcs
  • Swiss chard500 g
  • Parmigiano Reggiano (hard Italian cheese)50 g
  • milk40 ml
  • butter15 g
  • dried chilli flakes1 pizzico

Allergens

Eggs, Milk

Method

  1. Begin by thoroughly washing 500g of Swiss chard under cold running water to remove any traces of soil. Separate the white stalks from the green leaves and dice the stalks into very small cubes, about half a centimetre, while shredding the leaves into thin strips. This distinction is vital because the stalks are tougher and require more consistent cooking than the tender leaves. Chef's tip: if the stalks are particularly large, use a vegetable peeler to remove the tough outer strings before dicing them.
  2. In a large non-stick frying pan, melt approximately 10g of butter over medium heat and add a pinch of dried chili to infuse the fat. Add the diced stalks first, sautéing them for 3-4 minutes, then add the shredded leaves and a pinch of salt. Continue cooking for another 6-7 minutes until all the vegetable water has completely evaporated and the chard is tender and well-seasoned. Chef's tip: ensure the pan is hot before adding the leaves to prevent them from boiling in their own juice instead of browning.
  3. While the chard cools slightly, preheat your oven to 180°C (static mode) and grease 4 individual ramekins with the remaining 5g of butter. Distribute the sautéed chard equally across the bottom of each dish, pressing down lightly with the back of a spoon to create an even base. Create a small hollow in the centre of the greens, like a nest, which will hold the egg yolk. Chef's tip: use a pastry brush to spread the butter evenly up the sides of the ramekins to prevent the egg from sticking during baking.
  4. Pour one tablespoon of milk (about 10ml) into each ramekin over the layer of chard, spreading it as evenly as possible. This liquid is the secret to keeping the vegetables extremely juicy and will create a delicious emulsion with the fats from the cheese and the egg. The result should be a moist but not flooded base that protects the chard from the direct oven heat. Chef's tip: use whole milk if you desire an even more velvety and rich mouthfeel.
  5. Crack one fresh egg at a time into a small separate bowl and then gently slide it onto the bed of chard in each ramekin. Take great care not to break the yolk membrane; it must remain perfectly intact and centred in the hollow you prepared earlier. The egg white will naturally spread towards the edges of the dish, covering the chard. Chef's tip: using a separate bowl for cracking eggs allows you to check for freshness and remove any shell fragments before they affect the final presentation.
  6. Take 50g of grated Parmigiano Reggiano and sprinkle it generously over the surface of each ramekin, focusing mainly on the egg white area. Try to leave the yolk partially visible in the centre to achieve a professional visual effect where the bright yellow contrasts with the white of the melted cheese. The Parmigiano will form a savoury crust that protects the egg from overcooking. Chef's tip: do not add extra salt over the egg, as the Parmigiano is naturally salty and will perfectly balance the dish.
  7. Place the four ramekins inside a high-sided baking tray and pour very hot water into the tray until it reaches about halfway up the sides of the dishes. This bain-marie technique is essential for transmitting heat gently and steadily, preventing the bottom of the vegetables from scorching or the egg from becoming rubbery. The water should be hot but not at a rolling boil to avoid splashing into the ramekins. Chef's tip: place a piece of kitchen paper at the bottom of the tray to prevent the ramekins from sliding or vibrating during cooking.
  8. Bake the tray at 180°C for approximately 10-12 minutes, constantly monitoring the coagulation through the oven door. The goal is to achieve an egg white that is opaque and set, while the yolk must remain glossy and liquid/creamy on the inside. Once removed from the oven, let the ramekins rest for a minute before serving to stabilise the temperature. Chef's tip: cooking time may vary slightly depending on the thickness of your ramekins, so always rely on the visual appearance of the egg white.

Tips

  • Ensure the Swiss chard is well-drained to avoid diluting the dish's consistency.
  • Cook the eggs until the white is set but the yolk remains creamy to the touch.
  • Serve the cocottes very hot with slices of bread toasted in butter.

Variations

  • Add an extra pinch of dried chili for a significantly spicier version.
  • Replace the milk with a teaspoon of cream for an even richer base.
