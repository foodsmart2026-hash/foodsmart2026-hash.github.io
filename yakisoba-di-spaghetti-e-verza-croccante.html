Spaghetti and Crunchy Savoy Cabbage Yakisoba

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

An affordable Asian-style reinterpretation using Italian spaghetti instead of noodles. An excellent way to get children to enjoy savoy cabbage thanks to the savoury glaze.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🌱 Vegan🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🌍 International

A homemade take on famous Japanese stir-fried noodles, using spaghetti for a familiar texture with an Oriental flair. The cabbage and carrots remain crunchy, perfectly balancing the saltiness of soy sauce and ginger. It is a quick main course bursting with aromatic flavors.

Ingredients

  • Spaghetti n.5 (a standard thin Italian dried pasta)320 g
  • Savoy cabbage300 g
  • Carrots2 pcs
  • Spring onions2 pcs
  • Soy sauce40 ml
  • Brown sugar10 g
  • Sunflower oil30 ml
  • Fresh ginger10 g

Allergens

Gluten, Soy

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the vegetables: thoroughly wash 300g of Savoy cabbage and 2 carrots, then dry them well. Slice the cabbage into very thin strips and julienne the carrots into matchstick-sized pieces using a sharp knife, aiming to mimic the shape of the spaghetti. Separate the white parts of the 2 spring onions from the green ones, finely chopping the former and setting aside the latter, sliced into rings, for the final garnish. You will achieve a colourful and uniform vegetable base; the chef's tip is to keep the cut very thin to ensure quick and crunchy cooking.
  2. Prepare the aromatic base by finely grating 10g of peeled fresh ginger, collecting any juice that escapes. In a small bowl, pour 40ml of soy sauce and add 10g of brown sugar, stirring with a teaspoon until the crystals have completely dissolved. This emulsion will create the characteristic dark, glossy glaze of yakisoba; make sure the sugar is well dissolved to prevent it from burning in the pan later.
  3. Bring a large pot with about 3 litres of lightly salted water to the boil (use less salt than usual as the soy sauce is already very salty). Drop in the 320g of spaghetti no.5 and set a timer for 2 minutes less than the cooking time indicated on the pack. Stir gently at first to prevent sticking; the result should be very 'al dente' pasta, ready to finish cooking in the pan without becoming mushy.
  4. While the pasta is cooking, heat a wok or a very large non-stick frying pan over medium-high heat and pour in 30ml of sunflower oil. When the oil is fluid and begins to shimmer, add the white part of the spring onions and the grated ginger, letting them sauté for about 2 minutes. You should smell a pungent and aromatic fragrance without the ingredients browning; the chef's tip is not to exceed the heat at this stage to avoid making the ginger bitter.
  5. Turn the heat to maximum and add the previously prepared cabbage and carrots into the searing hot wok. Sauté the vegetables vigorously for 5 minutes using tongs or a flick of the wrist, so the heat hits them evenly. The vegetables should soften slightly on the outside but maintain a crunchy core and a vivid colour; never cover with a lid to prevent moisture from making them soft and grey.
  6. Just before draining the pasta, reserve half a small cup of cooking water (about 30ml) and add it to the wok with the vegetables to create a moist environment. Drain the spaghetti very al dente and transfer them directly into the pan with the vegetable seasoning. Use a high flame and start mixing with quick movements to distribute the vegetables among the pasta strands; this step allows the spaghetti to absorb the flavours of the vegetables.
  7. Pour the soy sauce and sugar mixture over the spaghetti in the wok, keeping the heat very high. Toss everything for another 3 minutes, continuing to move the pasta so the sauce reduces into a thick glaze coating every single ingredient. The spaghetti should take on a deep, uniform brown colour and appear glossy; the chef's secret is to let the pasta 'sizzle' well on the bottom of the pan to get that light toasted scent typical of Asian street food.
  8. Turn off the heat and add half of the green part of the spring onions cut into rings, stirring one last time with the residual heat. Distribute the yakisoba into four deep plates or large bowls, trying to create volume upwards using tongs and a ladle. Complete by garnishing each portion with the remaining fresh green spring onion, which will provide an herbaceous note and a pleasant chromatic boost to the dish.
  9. Serve immediately while the dish is steaming hot to fully enjoy the contrast between the softness of the pasta and the residual crunch of the cabbage. The final result will be a balanced dish with a lingering sweet-salty aroma and an enveloping texture; the chef's tip for an extra touch is to add, if desired, a pinch of toasted sesame seeds just before serving.

Tips

  • Drain the spaghetti very al dente as they finish cooking in the wok while absorbing the seasoning.
  • Slice the vegetables into thin, uniform strips to ensure quick cooking and a signature crunchy texture.
  • Use the reserved cooking water to adjust the dish's creaminess without adding any unnecessary fats.

Variations

  • Add toasted sesame seeds at the end for extra crunch and a lovely nutty aroma.
  • Use tamari sauce instead of regular soy sauce for a version suitable for those avoiding gluten.
