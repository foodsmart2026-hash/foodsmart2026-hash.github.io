Fusilli with White Turkey Ragù and Black Olives

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 20 min · Easy

A light and budget-friendly alternative to traditional meat ragù. Minced turkey is affordable and pairs perfectly with the aroma of the olives.

💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🧒 Kid-friendly🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🇮🇹 Italian

A flavorful and quick first course, ideal for a daily lunch. Ground turkey creates a light yet tasty seasoning thanks to the aroma of rosemary and the saltiness of black olives.

Ingredients

  • Fusilli (corkscrew-shaped pasta)320 g
  • Minced turkey300 g
  • Pitted black olives50 g
  • Dry white wine50 ml
  • Onion1/2 pcs
  • Rosemary1 rametto
  • Extra virgin olive oil20 ml
  • Salt and pepperq.b.

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Begin by finely chopping half an onion and the needles from a sprig of fresh rosemary on a cutting board using a sharp chef's knife. Ensure the rosemary is very finely minced to prevent any woody texture, while the onion should be uniform for even cooking. This step takes about 5 minutes and creates the essential aromatic base for your white ragù. The result should be a fragrant mix ready for sautéing. Chef's tip: remove the woody central stem from the rosemary before chopping to ensure maximum delicacy in the final bite.
  2. Pour 20 ml of extra virgin olive oil into a large frying pan or sauté pan and heat over medium heat. Add the previously prepared chopped onion and rosemary and let them soften gently for about 5 minutes, stirring frequently with a wooden spoon. The onion should not burn but should become translucent and golden, releasing an intense aroma combined with the woody notes of the rosemary. If you see the onion browning too quickly, lower the heat and add a tablespoon of hot water. The chef's secret is to keep the heat gentle to avoid extracting bitterness from the rosemary.
  3. Add the 300 g of turkey mince to the pan and increase the heat to a lively level to begin browning. Use a fork or wooden spoon to constantly break up the meat, ensuring no large lumps form so it cooks evenly. Cook for about 6-7 minutes until the meat has lost its pink colour and is slightly browned on the surface. This process, known as the Maillard reaction, is vital for adding depth of flavour to a white ragù. The chef advises against adding salt at this stage to prevent the meat from releasing too much liquid, which would hinder proper browning.
  4. Once the meat is well-browned, pour the 50 ml of dry white wine over the surface of the pan. Let the alcohol evaporate over high heat for about 2 minutes, lightly scraping the bottom of the pan with your spoon to lift any caramelised meat juices. The aroma will become more delicate, losing the sharp scent of alcohol and leaving only the wine's bouquet. It is crucial that the wine evaporates almost completely, leaving the base moist but not liquid. The chef suggests using a good quality white wine, the same one you intend to serve at the table.
  5. Take the 50 g of pitted black olives and slice them into thin rings before adding them to the turkey ragù. Stir well to incorporate the olives and season with salt and freshly ground black pepper, being careful with the salt as olives are naturally briny. Continue cooking over medium-low heat for about 10 minutes, covering the pan with a lid. If the sauce becomes too dry, add a small ladle of hot water to keep the meat tender. The final result should be a well-combined sauce where the flavours of the meat and olives are perfectly balanced.
  6. While the ragù finishes cooking, bring a large pot of salted water to the boil. Drop in the 320 g of fusilli and cook them according to the package instructions, but aim to drain them about 2 minutes before the suggested time. The spiral shape of fusilli is ideal for this type of ragù as it perfectly captures the small pieces of meat within its coils. Always remember to reserve a cup of the starchy pasta water before draining. Chef's recommendation: do not add oil to the pasta water, as it is unnecessary and prevents the sauce from adhering to the pasta.
  7. Drain the fusilli and transfer them directly into the pan with the turkey ragù, turning the heat back up to medium. Add a ladle of the reserved pasta cooking water and toss the pasta vigorously for a couple of minutes. This movement, combined with the starch from the water, will create a delicious light sauce that binds the dressing to the pasta homogeneously. You will see the ragù no longer sits at the bottom of the pan but coats every single spiral. The chef advises never to skip this step, as it elevates a simple pasta dish to restaurant quality.
  8. Turn off the heat, remove any remaining large pieces of rosemary, and finish the dish with a final drizzle of raw extra virgin olive oil. Toss one last time to give the pasta a beautiful sheen and serve immediately on pre-heated plates. The texture should be creamy, the aroma fresh, and the taste balanced between the mild turkey and the savoury olives. You can garnish each portion with a small sprig of fresh rosemary for a professional look. The chef's final touch: a tiny bit of freshly grated lemon zest will add an incredible layer of freshness to the dish.

Tips

  • Finely chop the fresh rosemary to release its full aroma without leaving whole needles in the dish.
  • Deglaze the white wine well over high heat to remove the alcohol and leave only the fragrance.
  • Reserve some pasta water to toss the fusilli and make the white ragù creamier.

Variations

  • You can replace fusilli with penne rigate to better hold the meat sauce.
  • Add some grated lemon zest at the end of cooking for a touch of citrus freshness.
