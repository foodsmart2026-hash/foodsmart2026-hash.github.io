Valtellina Pizzoccheri

Lombardia · 4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

Buckwheat tagliatelle with savoy cabbage, potatoes, Valtellina Casera cheese and garlic butter.

Ingredients

  • Dried pizzoccheri320 g
  • Potatoes250 g
  • Savoy cabbage250 g
  • Valtellina Casera cheese180 g
  • Grated Grana Padano40 g
  • Butter70 g
  • Garlic6 g
  • Sage3 g
  • Fine salt8 g

Allergens

Gluten, Milk
May contain traces of: Soy, Eggs.

Method

  1. Start by preparing the vegetables: wash the cabbage and cut it into 2 cm strips, then peel the potatoes and cut them into regular 1.5 cm cubes. Precise cutting ensures they cook evenly alongside the pasta.
  2. Fill a large pot with about 4 liters of water and bring it to a rolling boil over high heat. Once boiling, add the 8 g of fine salt, stirring briefly until it is completely dissolved.
  3. Submerge the cubed potatoes and sliced cabbage into the boiling water and let them cook for about 5 minutes over medium-high heat. The vegetables will begin to soften, releasing their characteristic aroma into the cooking water.
  4. Add the 320 g of dried pizzoccheri into the same pot with the vegetables and continue cooking for another 10-12 minutes. Stir occasionally with a wooden spoon to prevent the pasta from sticking to the bottom of the pot.
  5. While the pasta is cooking, prepare the seasoning: melt 70 g of butter in a small saucepan over low heat along with 6 g of garlic and 3 g of sage leaves. Let it sizzle until the butter turns a hazelnut color and gives off an intense scent, then remove the garlic if you prefer a milder flavor.
  6. Prepare the cheeses: cut the 180 g of Valtellina Casera into thin flakes or small cubes to help it melt. Keep the 40 g of grated Grana Padano nearby; it will add saltiness and creaminess to the final dish.
  7. Once the cooking time is up, check that the pizzoccheri are al dente and the potatoes are soft but still intact. Drain everything using a slotted spoon or a large colander, keeping a tiny bit of moisture so the pasta doesn't dry out.
  8. In a previously warmed serving dish, start layering by alternating a portion of pizzoccheri and vegetables with a handful of Valtellina Casera and Grana Padano. This layering method allows the residual heat from the pasta to start melting the cheese evenly.
  9. Pour the boiling, sizzling melted butter over the final layer of cheese and pasta. You will see the Casera melt instantly upon contact with the heat of the butter, creating the typical Valtellina stringy texture.
  10. Mix everything very gently so as not to break the potatoes and serve your hot pizzoccheri immediately. The dish is perfect when the enveloping creaminess of the cheeses harmoniously binds the buckwheat pasta to the vegetables.
