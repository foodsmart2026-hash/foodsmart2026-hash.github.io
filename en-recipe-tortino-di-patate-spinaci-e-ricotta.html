Potato, spinach and ricotta bake

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 45 min · Hard

A complete and balanced main course that combines the creaminess of cow's milk ricotta with the fibre of fresh spinach. An affordable solution, perfect for the family, using simple yet nutrient-rich essential ingredients.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🧒 Kid-friendly

This savory pie is a versatile comfort food, featuring a soft interior and a gratin top. The combination of potatoes, spinach, and ricotta creates a delicate balance, enhanced by the aromatic touch of nutmeg. Great served warm as a main course or a rich side dish.

Ingredients

  • Yellow-fleshed potatoes800 g
  • Fresh spinach (or frozen)500 g
  • Fresh cow's milk ricotta (a soft Italian whey cheese)250 g
  • Medium egg1 pz
  • Grated Parmigiano Reggiano (a hard, aged Italian cheese)40 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Wholemeal breadcrumbs30 g
  • Unrefined sea salt5 g
  • Nutmeg1 g

Allergens

Milk, Eggs, Gluten

Method

  1. Start by thoroughly washing 800g of yellow-fleshed potatoes under cold running water to remove any soil residue. Place them whole and unpeeled into a large pot filled with cold, lightly salted water and bring to a boil over high heat. Boil for approximately 25-30 minutes, checking readiness with a fork; the tines should slide into the centre with no resistance. You will achieve tender potatoes that retain all their starch inside the skin. Chef's tip: choose potatoes of a similar size to ensure even cooking and never peel them beforehand, as they would absorb too much water.
  2. While the potatoes are boiling, prepare the 500g of fresh spinach by washing it well and placing it in a very large frying pan. Add just one tablespoon of water and a pinch of salt, cover with a lid, and let it wilt over medium heat for about 5-7 minutes. You will see the volume decrease drastically as the leaves become bright green and tender. The result should be cooked through but still vibrant, not mushy. Chef's tip: if using frozen spinach, let it defrost naturally in a sieve before pan-searing to remove any excess ice water.
  3. This is the most critical step for a successful bake: drain the spinach and squeeze it with extreme force using a potato ricer or by pressing it firmly between your hands. You must remove every trace of vegetable liquid to prevent the mixture from becoming soggy in the oven. You will end up with a compact, almost dry 'block' of spinach, which you should then roughly chop with a knife. Chef's tip: do not discard the spinach liquid; it is rich in minerals and can be added to a vegetable stock or soup.
  4. Once the potatoes are ready, drain and peel them while still hot, using a small knife to help you. Pass them immediately through a potato ricer, letting the mash fall directly into a large, clean mixing bowl. Allow the mash to cool for about 5-10 minutes at room temperature; this prevents the residual heat from scrambling the egg as soon as it is added. The mash should look fluffy, dry, and completely lump-free. Chef's tip: peeling potatoes while hot is essential as the skin slips off easily and the texture remains much silkier.
  5. In the bowl with the potatoes, add 250g of cow's milk ricotta (ensure it has been well-drained in a sieve), the whole egg, 30g of Parmigiano Reggiano, and a generous grating of nutmeg. Mix everything with a wooden spoon, using slow, circular motions until you have a smooth, velvety, and fragrant base. Ensure the ricotta is perfectly incorporated and the egg is evenly distributed. The mixture should be thick yet easy to spread. Chef's tip: whisk the ricotta with a fork in a small bowl before adding it to make it creamier and prevent small white lumps from appearing.
  6. Take the chopped spinach and add it to the potato and ricotta base, folding it in gently with a spatula using an upward motion. The goal is an uneven distribution to maintain a pleasant marbled effect between the green spinach and the creamy white base. Taste and adjust with the 5g of sea salt, keeping in mind the saltiness already provided by the cheese. You will have a rich, colourful, and aromatic mixture. Chef's tip: do not overmix at this stage, otherwise the entire dish will turn a dull, uniform green, losing the visual appeal of the contrast.
  7. Prepare a baking dish by brushing the bottom and sides with roughly 15ml of extra virgin olive oil and dusting with half of the wholemeal breadcrumbs (15g). Rotate the dish so the crumbs adhere to all oiled surfaces, tapping out any excess. This creates a protective coating that ensures a crispy base and prevents sticking. The dish should be lightly veiled in oil and evenly dotted with breadcrumbs. Chef's tip: for a gourmet touch, rub a halved garlic clove onto the bottom of the dish before oiling it.
  8. Transfer the entire mixture into the baking dish and level the surface with the back of a spoon or a silicone spatula to achieve a uniform thickness of about 3-4 cm. Sprinkle the top with the remaining 15g of breadcrumbs, the final 10g of Parmigiano, and finish with the remaining 15ml of EVO oil drizzled in a zigzag pattern. The surface must be well-covered to allow the dry ingredients to react with the oil and heat. Chef's tip: score light lines with fork tines across the surface; the oil and cheese will settle there, creating even tastier, extra-crunchy ridges.
  9. Place the dish in a preheated static oven at 200°C and bake on the middle shelf for about 25-30 minutes. During the last 5 minutes, turn on the grill function to intensify the Maillard reaction and achieve a perfect golden-brown finish. You should notice an inviting aroma of toasted bread and cheese and see a dark, crispy crust forming. Check that the edges are starting to pull away slightly from the dish. Chef's tip: if the top browns too quickly, cover with foil and remove it only for the final minute of grilling.
  10. Once removed from the oven, it is essential to let the bake rest for at least 10-15 minutes before slicing. This resting period allows the potato starch and the proteins from the egg and cheese to set, ensuring the slices remain firm and clean. When serving, the bake should be warm but not piping hot, releasing its full range of flavours. Chef's tip: this dish is also excellent at room temperature or the next day, making it perfect for a healthy packed lunch or a buffet.

Tips

  • Squeeze the cooked spinach very carefully to prevent them from releasing water and compromising the pie's firmness.
  • Mash the potatoes while they are still hot to get a smoother puree that is easier to mix with the ricotta.
  • Sprinkle the surface with a mix of parmesan and breadcrumbs to achieve a golden and crispy crust in the oven.

Variations

  • You can replace the cow's milk ricotta with sheep's milk ricotta for a more intense and bolder flavor.
  • Add some cubes of melting cheese into the mixture to get an even softer and heartier center.
