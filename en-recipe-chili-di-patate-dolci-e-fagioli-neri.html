Sweet Potato and Black Bean Chilli

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 35 min · Medium

A revisited Mexican one-pot dish, naturally meat-free and very budget-friendly. The sweetness of the potato pairs perfectly with the savouriness of the black beans.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🌱 Vegan🌾 Gluten-free🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🌍 International

Ingredients

  • Sweet potatoes (orange flesh)600 g
  • Pre-cooked black beans480 g
  • Tomato passata (smooth sieved tomatoes)400 g
  • Canned sweetcorn150 g
  • Red onion1 pcs
  • Tomato purée30 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil20 ml
  • Coriander or parsleyq.b.

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Start by preparing the vegetables: peel 600g of sweet potatoes and cut them into regular 2cm cubes, aiming for precision to ensure even cooking. Finely chop the red onion after removing the outer skin. Use a sharp chef's knife and a stable cutting board to work safely. You will have bright orange potato cubes and a minced onion ready for sautéing. Chef's tip: do not make the cubes too small, or they will break down completely during the cooking process.
  2. Take a large, heavy-bottomed pot, pour in 20ml of extra virgin olive oil, and turn the heat to medium. Add the chopped red onion and let it sauté gently for about 5 minutes, stirring occasionally with a wooden spoon. The onion should become translucent and soft without browning too much or burning. This step is essential for creating a sweet and fragrant aromatic base. Chef's tip: if the onion starts to brown too quickly, add a tablespoon of hot water to slow down the Maillard reaction.
  3. Now it is time to add the 30g of tomato purée (concentrate) and the 600g of prepared sweet potato cubes. Stir carefully for about 3 minutes over medium-high heat, ensuring every cube is well coated with the seasoning. You will notice the heat lightly toasts the concentrate, intensifying its flavour and colour. The potatoes will begin to 'seal' on the outside, which helps them hold their shape during the subsequent wet cooking phase. Chef's tip: do not skip this step, as caramelising the tomato is the secret to a deep and savoury chili.
  4. Pour 400g of tomato passata and about 200ml of water (one glass) into the pot to partially cover the potatoes. Lower the heat to medium-low and cover the pot with a lid to keep the moisture constant inside. Let it cook for about 20 minutes, stirring occasionally to prevent the bottom from sticking. The result should be a saucy base where the potatoes are starting to soften significantly but remain firm at the core. Chef's tip: use hot water so as not to stop the simmer and to maintain a constant cooking temperature.
  5. While the potatoes are cooking, drain and rinse 480g of pre-cooked black beans and 150g of tinned sweetcorn thoroughly under running water. Once the 20 minutes for the potatoes have passed, add them to the pot, stirring gently with an upward motion. Continue cooking for another 5-10 minutes, this time without a lid to allow the excess liquid to evaporate. You will see the chili thicken and the flavours begin to merge into a harmonious and colourful whole. Chef's tip: rinsing the legumes removes the canning liquid, which could otherwise spoil the final flavour.
  6. Check the thickness of the preparation: the chili is ready when the sauce has become a thick cream and the spoon meets slight resistance when moving through the pot. The sweet potatoes should be tender when pierced with a fork but still whole, while the beans should be hot and creamy. Adjust the salt only at this stage, remembering that tinned legumes are often already salted. The colour will be a deep, vibrant brownish-red. Chef's tip: mash a couple of potato cubes against the side of the pot to release starch and make the sauce even creamier.
  7. Turn off the heat and let the chili rest for a couple of minutes so the flavours can settle and the temperature drops slightly, enhancing the aromas. Meanwhile, coarsely chop a bunch of fresh coriander or parsley, depending on your personal taste. Sprinkle the fresh herbs over the surface to provide a fresh note that balances the natural sweetness of the potatoes and corn. You will notice how the scent of the fresh herbs immediately elevates the dish's aromatic bouquet. Chef's tip: add the herbs only after the heat is off to preserve their bright green colour and volatile properties.
  8. Serve the chili piping hot in large bowls, ensuring each portion has a balanced distribution of potatoes, beans, and corn. You can accompany the dish with lime wedges or a corn tortilla for a complete and balanced experience. The final result is a nutritious, colourful one-pot meal with a rich texture that will satisfy both the eyes and the palate. Chef's tip: this chili is even better when reheated the next day, as the resting period allows the flavours to integrate perfectly.
