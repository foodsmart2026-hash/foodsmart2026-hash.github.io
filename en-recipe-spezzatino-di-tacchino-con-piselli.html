Turkey stew with peas

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 50 min · Hard

A classic of Italian home cooking, nutritious and low-cost, ideal for warming up family dinners. Turkey meat, lean and economical, becomes very tender thanks to slow cooking and the creaminess of the peas.

A delicate and creamy main course where turkey morsels become incredibly tender through slow cooking. Peas add a natural sweetness that pairs beautifully with the aromatic base of carrots and celery. A rustic and simple dish for any occasion.

Ingredients

  • Diced turkey leg600 g
  • Fine frozen peas400 g
  • Brown onion100 g
  • Carrots80 g
  • Celery50 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 g
  • Plain flour (Type 00)30 g
  • Dry white wine100 ml
  • Vegetable stock (or water)250 ml
  • Fine salt5 g
  • Black pepper2 g
  • Fresh rosemary1 sprig

Allergens

Gluten, Celery

Method

  1. Cut 600g of turkey leg into cubes of about 3 centimetres, trying to keep them uniform to ensure even cooking of all pieces. Use a clean cutting board and a sharp knife, taking care to remove any remaining cartilage or excess fat before proceeding. This operation takes about 5-7 minutes and allows you to obtain bite-sized pieces ideal for eating comfortably. The final result should be a bowl of clean meat of consistent size. Chef's tip: do not cut pieces too small, otherwise the meat will tend to become fibrous and dry during the browning phase.
  2. Prepare the aromatic base by finely chopping 100g of onion, 80g of carrot, and 50g of celery, previously washed and peeled. Use a chef's knife or a mezzaluna (curved chopping blade) on a stable cutting board, working the vegetables until they are reduced to small regular cubes of about 2-3 millimetres. Devote about 5 minutes to this step to ensure the chop is fine, thus favouring the release of aromas. You will get a colourful mix that will be the flavour soul of your stew. Chef's tip: add a pinch of salt to the vegetables while chopping to help them release their natural juices already during the cutting phase.
  3. Pour 30g of flour onto a deep plate and toss the turkey cubes in it, turning them well with your hands to cover them entirely. Vigorously shake each piece of meat in a sieve or between your hands to remove all excess flour, leaving only a very thin and almost invisible veil. This technique is fundamental because the residual flour will serve to create a delicious cream during cooking without forming lumps. The meat will appear opaque and dry to the touch, ready to meet the heat of the pot. Chef's tip: flour the meat only at the last second before cooking, otherwise the moisture from the turkey will make the flour sticky and pasty.
  4. In a large saucepan, heat 30g of extra virgin olive oil over medium heat for about a minute until it becomes fluid and slightly shimmering. Add the chopped vegetables and let them sauté gently for 6-8 minutes, stirring often with a wooden spoon so they don't brown. The vegetables should soften and the onion become translucent, releasing a sweet and inviting scent throughout the kitchen. The result should be a tender and glossy soffritto (aromatic base), not burnt. Chef's tip: if the vegetables seem to dry out too quickly, add a tablespoon of hot water to stew them instead of frying them excessively.
  5. Increase the heat slightly and add the floured turkey cubes to the saucepan along with the soffritto, distributing them well over the bottom. Let the meat brown without touching it for the first 2 minutes, then turn it on each side for another 3-4 minutes until it has taken on a nice uniform golden colour. This surface caramelisation reaction is what gives deep flavour to the final dish. You will see a light brown residue form on the pot, which is pure flavour essence. Chef's tip: avoid overcrowding the pot; if necessary, brown the meat in two separate batches to keep the temperature high and prevent juices from leaking out.
  6. Deglaze everything with 100ml of dry white wine, maintaining a high heat to facilitate the immediate evaporation of the alcohol. With the wooden spoon, vigorously scrape the bottom of the saucepan to detach all the meat juices that have stuck (deglazing). Let boil for about 2 minutes until the pungent smell of wine completely vanishes, leaving only a fragrant note. The liquid will reduce slightly, enveloping the bites in a tasty sauce. Chef's tip: ensure the wine is at room temperature so as not to cause a thermal shock that would harden the meat fibres.
  7. Add a sprig of rosemary, a sprinkling of black pepper and cover the meat with 250ml of hot vegetable stock, pouring it slowly down the sides of the pot. Lower the heat to minimum, put the lid on and let the stew simmer for about 20-25 minutes, checking occasionally that the liquid does not dry out completely. The meat will progressively become tender by absorbing the aromas of the stock and vegetables. The result will be a stew already partially bound by a fluid sauce. Chef's tip: always use hot stock so as not to stop the meat from cooking, keeping the fibres soft and juicy.
  8. Add the 400g of frozen peas directly into the pot and season with salt according to your personal taste. Continue cooking without a lid for another 10-12 minutes over medium heat, stirring gently every now and then with the wooden spoon. The peas must cook while remaining whole and a beautiful bright green, while the sauce will thicken thanks to the flour used initially, becoming velvety. The scent will become intense and the consistency of the meat will yield to the pressure of the spoon. Chef's tip: do not overcook the peas beyond the indicated time, otherwise they will turn greyish and lose their pleasant crunchy texture.
  9. Turn off the heat and let the stew rest covered for 5 minutes before bringing it to the table to allow the flavours to stabilise and the meat to redistribute its juices. Remove the rosemary sprig and serve the dish very hot, distributing meat, peas, and sauce equally into four deep plates. The result will be a balanced dish, with bright colours and a sauce perfect for being accompanied by a slice of wholemeal bread. The meat will be very soft and the peas sweet. Chef's tip: if the sauce is too thin, remove the meat and reduce the sauce over high heat for a minute before reuniting the ingredients.
  10. Arrange the plates on the table and, if desired, add a final drizzle of raw extra virgin olive oil to enhance all the Mediterranean scents. Ensure each portion has a generous amount of peas and cream, which represent the most delicious part of the dish. Accompany the stew with a side of fresh vegetables or a moderate portion of polenta or pilaf rice for a complete meal. Turkey cooked this way will also win over children for its delicacy. Chef's tip: this dish keeps very well in the fridge for 2 days and, if reheated with a drop of water, becomes almost tastier because the aromas have had time to blend.

Tips

  • Coat the turkey cubes thoroughly in flour to create a thick, velvety sauce while simmering.
  • Deglaze with wine over high heat to evaporate the alcohol while retaining its full aroma.
  • If the stew becomes too dry, add a ladle of warm vegetable broth to keep the meat moist.

Variations

  • Swap the rosemary for fresh thyme for a more subtle and elegant herbal fragrance.
  • Add a pinch of turmeric to the flour coating for a vibrant golden color and exotic flair.
