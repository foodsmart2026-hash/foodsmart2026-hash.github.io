Risi e bisi

Veneto · 4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

A thick Venetian soup made with rice and fresh peas, cooked in stock.

🌾 Gluten-free🇮🇹 Italian

This historic Venetian recipe sits between a risotto and a soup, celebrating the seasonal sweetness of fresh peas. Vialone Nano rice absorbs the broth's flavors, pairing perfectly with savory pancetta and Grana Padano cheese.

Ingredients

  • Vialone Nano rice280 g
  • Fresh shelled peas400 g
  • Pancetta50 g
  • Onion50 g
  • Butter30 g
  • Vegetable stock1300 ml
  • Grated Grana Padano40 g
  • Parsley5 g

Allergens

Milk
May contain traces of: Celery, Eggs.

Method

  1. Start by finely chopping 50 g of onion and cutting 50 g of pancetta into small cubes. In a large pot, melt half of the butter (15 g) over low heat and add the mixture; let it soften for about 4 minutes until the onion becomes translucent and the pancetta releases its fragrant fat. This step is essential to create a rich aromatic base that will give character to the entire dish.
  2. Add 400 g of fresh shelled peas to the base and pour in about 200 ml of the total vegetable broth. Cover and cook over medium heat for about 5 minutes: the peas should begin to soften but maintain a bright green color. This way, the legumes will start releasing their natural sweetness into the cooking base.
  3. Pour 280 g of Vialone Nano rice into the pot directly over the peas. Stir well for a minute over high heat so that the grains begin to absorb the flavors of the sautéed base and the pancetta. Vialone Nano is perfect for this recipe because it absorbs liquids very well while maintaining an excellent texture during cooking.
  4. Start adding the rest of the vegetable broth (1100 ml), which must already be boiling to avoid stopping the rice from cooking. Unlike a classic risotto, here you can pour almost all the broth together, as 'risi e bisi' should result in a thick soup. Stir regularly to encourage the release of starch, which will make everything creamy.
  5. Continue cooking over medium heat for about 16-18 minutes, frequently checking the consistency. The rice will be ready when the grain is tender on the outside but still slightly resistant to the bite. Ensure that the liquid level is generous: the dish should not be dry, but have a fluid and velvety consistency.
  6. When a couple of minutes of cooking time remain, check that the mixture resembles a thick soup. Turn off the heat when the rice is still very al dente, as residual heat will continue to cook the grains during the resting period. You will smell an inviting aroma combining the sweetness of the peas with the savoriness of the pancetta.
  7. Move the pot away from the heat and add the remaining 15 g of butter and 40 g of grated Grana Padano. Stir vigorously to incorporate air and make the dish velvety. This off-heat 'mantecatura' technique ensures impeccable creaminess without making the ingredients heavy.
  8. Finally, complete with 5 g of finely chopped fresh parsley and stir one last time to distribute the fresh aroma of the herb. Let everything rest for a minute before serving in shallow bowls. Your 'risi e bisi' should be served hot, with the typical 'all’onda' consistency that moves gently if you shake the plate slightly.

Tips

  • Keep the vegetable broth boiling throughout the process to maintain the correct cooking temperature for the rice.
  • The final consistency should be 'all'onda,' meaning fluid and creamy rather than too dry.
  • Use very fresh peas shelled just before cooking to ensure maximum sweetness and a tender texture.

Variations

  • For a vegetarian version, omit the pancetta and add a bit more onion to the initial sauté.
  • Substitute Vialone Nano with Carnaroli rice if you prefer a grain that maintains a firmer bite.
