Korean-Style Rice Bowl (Simplified Bibimbap)

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 20 min · Medium

A colourful and balanced rice bowl, ideal for using various seasonal vegetables. The finishing touch of a fried egg makes the dish rich and creamy.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🥕 Vegetarian🌾 Gluten-free🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🌍 International

Ingredients

  • Sushi rice or Originario rice (a versatile Italian short-grain rice)300 g
  • Fresh spinach200 g
  • Bean sprouts100 g
  • Carrots2 pcs
  • Eggs4 pcs
  • Soy sauce30 ml
  • Sesame oil (or vegetable oil)20 ml
  • Sesame seedsq.b.

Allergens

Eggs, Soy, Sesame

Method

  1. Start by rinsing 300g of sushi or short-grain rice under cold running water to remove excess starch until the water runs clear. Pour the rice into a large pot with plenty of lightly salted boiling water and cook according to the package instructions, usually about 12-15 minutes over medium heat. Drain the rice perfectly and keep it aside in a covered container to maintain its moisture and heat. The result should be well-separated, soft grains with a slightly firm core. Chef's tip: do not over-stir the rice during boiling to avoid breaking the grains and making them too gluey.
  2. Thoroughly wash 200g of fresh spinach and blanch them in a pot with a little boiling water for just 2 minutes over high heat. Drain immediately and refresh under cold running water to set the bright green colour, then squeeze them extremely hard between your hands to remove any residual liquid. Place the spinach in a small bowl and season with 5ml of sesame oil and a pinch of salt, mixing well with your hands to loosen the fibres. You will get tender, tasty, and non-watery greens. Chef's tip: squeezing is crucial; if the spinach remains wet, it will ruin the texture of the rice at the base of the bowl.
  3. Prepare the carrots by peeling 2 medium roots and cutting them into thin matchsticks (julienne) about 5 centimetres long. Heat a non-stick frying pan with 5ml of sesame oil over medium-high heat and sauté the carrots for about 4 minutes, moving them often with a spatula. The carrots should be tender to the bite but maintain a vivid orange colour and a slight external crunch. The final result must be shiny and fragrant vegetables, ready to enrich the dish. Chef's tip: cut the carrots into uniform sizes to ensure they all cook at the same time without scorching.
  4. In the same pan used for the carrots, pour 100g of fresh soy sprouts and add 15ml of soy sauce. Sauté the sprouts over high heat for no more than 2 minutes, keeping them moving constantly to distribute the seasoning evenly. The sprouts should absorb the saltiness of the soy while remaining extremely crunchy and plump to the bite. As soon as they start to become slightly translucent, remove them immediately from the heat to prevent them from releasing too much water. Chef's tip: use a very high flame because slow cooking would make the sprouts soggy and unappealing.
  5. Take a wide non-stick pan, grease it with a film of sesame oil, and heat it over medium heat. Gently crack the 4 eggs, trying not to break the yolks, and fry them sunnyside up for about 3-4 minutes. The secret is to obtain a perfectly set, opaque white egg while the yolk must remain completely liquid and silky. Lightly salt only the white so as not to stain the glossiness of the yolk. Chef's tip: if the white struggles to cook on top, cover the pan with a lid for 30 seconds; the trapped steam will cook the top without solidifying the yolk.
  6. Prepare 4 large bowls and equally distribute the hot rice at the bottom of each, levelling it slightly without pressing too hard. Visually divide the surface of the rice into sections and neatly place the seasoned spinach, sautéed carrots, and soy sprouts. Try to alternate the colours to create an inviting and harmonious chromatic contrast that recalls the typical radial pattern of traditional Bibimbap. The bowl should appear rich and well-balanced among the various vegetable ingredients. Chef's tip: keep the ingredients separate until serving to allow each guest to appreciate the individual flavours.
  7. Extremely carefully place one egg in the centre of each bowl, on top of the vegetables and rice, taking care not to break the yolk at this stage. Complete the garnish by sprinkling sesame seeds over the entire surface and finish with the remaining 15ml of soy sauce drizzled over. The dish will present as a mosaic of bright colours, with the egg acting as the central focal point. Chef's tip: slightly warm the bowls in the oven before assembling them to prevent the rice from cooling down too quickly while arranging the vegetables.
  8. Bring the bowls to the table immediately while they are still steaming and invite your guests to stir everything vigorously. By breaking the egg yolk, it will flow over the rice and vegetables, creating a creamy sauce that will bind all the flavours and different textures together. The final result is a complete mouthful, balanced between the sweetness of the carrots, the saltiness of the soy, and the creaminess of the egg. Chef's tip: the true secret of Bibimbap is the final mix; do not be afraid to stir thoroughly so that every grain of rice is seasoned by the yolk.
