Hungarian Goulash

Hungary · 4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 120 min · Medium

A slow-cooked beef stew with onions and plenty of sweet paprika.

Ingredients

  • Beef chuck800 g
  • Onions400 g
  • Hungarian sweet paprika20 g
  • Tomato purée30 g
  • Red pepper150 g
  • Caraway seeds2 g
  • Garlic6 g
  • Lard30 g
  • Water700 ml
  • Fine salt8 g

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.
May contain traces of: Celery, Mustard.

Method

  1. Start by carefully preparing the ingredients: cut the 800g of beef shoulder into uniform 3cm cubes and thinly slice the 400g of onions. Consistent meat sizing ensures even cooking, while finely sliced onions will melt away during the long cooking process to create a rich, thick sauce.
  2. In a large pot, preferably cast iron or heavy-bottomed, melt the 30g of lard over very low heat. Add the sliced onions and let them soften slowly for about 15 minutes until they become tender and translucent; they shouldn't brown too much, as their sweetness is the foundation of the dish.
  3. Momentarily remove the pot from the heat and stir in the 20g of sweet paprika using a wooden spoon. This step is crucial: excessive heat would burn the paprika and make it bitter, while the lard's fat at a moderate temperature will enhance its bright red color and essential aromas.
  4. Return the pot to medium heat and add the 800g of beef, 6g of minced garlic, 2g of cumin seeds, and 30g of tomato paste. Sauté everything for about 5 minutes, stirring often so every beef cube absorbs the flavors and the tomato paste begins to release its deep color.
  5. Add the 150g of red pepper, previously cleaned and cut into small pieces. This ingredient will provide a note of freshness and a slightly different texture that perfectly balances the intensity of the Hungarian spices.
  6. Pour the 700ml of water into the pot, ensuring it is already hot, and add the 8g of fine salt. It is important to use hot water to avoid causing thermal shock to the meat, thus keeping the fibers soft and juicy throughout the process.
  7. Cover the pot with a tight-fitting lid and let it simmer over very low heat for about 1 hour and 45 minutes, maintaining a gentle and constant bubble. The slow and prolonged cooking will allow the beef shoulder's collagen to transform, making the cubes incredibly tender to the bite.
  8. Toward the end of the specified time, check the consistency of the goulash: the sauce should be thick, glossy, and deep red, with the onions now almost completely dissolved. If the meat breaks apart easily with a light press of a fork, your masterpiece is ready to be served steaming hot.
