Bavette with Savoy Cabbage Pesto and Breadcrumbs

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

A recipe that transforms a humble vegetable like Savoy cabbage into a refined and creamy dressing, balanced by the crunchiness of pan-toasted bread.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🌱 Vegan🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🇮🇹 Italian

Ingredients

  • Bavette or Linguine (long, flat pasta strands)320 g
  • Savoy cabbage (inner leaves)400 g
  • Stale bread60 g
  • Dried chilli flakes1 pizzico
  • Garlic1 spicchio
  • Lemon juice10 ml

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Begin by cleaning 400g of Savoy cabbage, removing the tough, white central ribs which would be fibrous and unpleasant to eat. Slice the leaves into very thin strips using a sharp chef's knife on a stable cutting board. Wash the cabbage thoroughly under cold running water and let it drain for a moment. You should end up with a pile of tender, uniform strips ready for quick cooking. My professional tip: don't discard the ribs; you can use them later for a flavourful vegetable stock in another recipe.
  2. Bring a large pot with about 4 litres of lightly salted water to a boil. Submerge the 400g of cabbage and blanch for exactly 5 minutes over medium heat until tender but still vibrant green. Use a slotted spoon to retrieve all the cabbage and transfer it immediately to a bowl, being careful to keep all the cooking water in the pot. This step is crucial as the cabbage-infused water will give the pasta a unique depth of flavour. Remember that brief blanching helps preserve the vegetable's heat-sensitive vitamins.
  3. Place the cooked cabbage into a blender or use an immersion blender along with 2 tablespoons of extra virgin olive oil (roughly 20ml) and 10ml of lemon juice. Add a small ladle of the hot cooking water and blend at high speed for about 1 minute until you achieve a smooth, velvety, deep green pesto. If the consistency seems too thick, add a drop more cooking water to loosen it. The pesto should have the density of a light spreadable cream. Always taste it: the lemon balances the natural sweetness of the cabbage and prevents oxidation.
  4. Prepare the crispy crumbs by coarsely crumbling the 60g of stale bread with your hands or a food processor, aiming for irregular pieces. Heat a wide non-stick pan with a drizzle of oil, the crushed garlic clove, and a pinch of dried chili over low heat. Add the bread and toast for about 4-5 minutes, stirring constantly to prevent burning. You will get golden, crunchy, and highly aromatic crumbs that provide the 'crunch' element to the dish. Once ready, remove the garlic and set the crumbs aside in a small bowl to keep them fragrant.
  5. Bring the cabbage cooking water back to a boil and add more salt if necessary. Drop in the 320g of bavette (or linguine) and cook according to the package instructions, usually between 8 and 10 minutes. Stir the pasta during the first few minutes with a pasta fork to prevent sticking, keeping the heat high. The result should be 'al dente' pasta, with the core still slightly firm to the bite. A chef's trick: drain the pasta one minute before the suggested time to finish cooking it directly in the sauce.
  6. While the pasta is cooking, pour the cabbage pesto into the wide pan used for the bread (after quickly wiping away any crumbs). Turn the heat to very low just to warm the cream, avoiding further cooking to preserve the bright colour. Add another ladle of pasta cooking water to create a fluid emulsion. The sauce base should look glossy and homogeneous. Never heat the pesto over high flame, or the oil will separate, ruining the creamy texture.
  7. Drain the bavette using tongs or a colander, transferring them directly into the pan with the cabbage pesto. Toss the pasta over medium heat for about a minute, moving the pan with firm gestures to cream everything together perfectly. If you notice the pesto drying out too quickly, add a little cooking water at a time until every strand of pasta is perfectly coated in the green cream. The aroma released will be a mix of earthy notes and citric freshness. Proper 'mantecatura' is the secret to a restaurant-quality dish.
  8. Distribute the bavette into individual plates, creating a nest in the centre with the help of a ladle and long tongs. Generously sprinkle each portion with the spicy breadcrumbs prepared earlier, ensuring they stay on top. Complete with a final drizzle of raw oil if you desire an extra layer of flavour. Serve immediately: the contrast between the softness of the pasta and the crunch of the bread is the soul of this recipe. My final advice is never to mix the crumbs in the pan, as they would absorb moisture and lose their delightful texture.
