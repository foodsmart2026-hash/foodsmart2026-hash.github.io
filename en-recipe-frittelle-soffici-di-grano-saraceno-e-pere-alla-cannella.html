Fluffy Buckwheat and Cinnamon Pear Pancakes

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 10 min · Easy

An energetic and fragrant breakfast, naturally free from common allergens and rich in fibre thanks to the buckwheat. The natural sweetness of the pear and banana means no added sugars are needed, making the recipe perfect for the whole family.

Ingredients

  • Buckwheat flour240 g
  • Very ripe bananas2 pcs
  • Abate or Conference pears2 pcs
  • Still water250 ml
  • Gluten-free baking powder10 g
  • Sunflower oil30 ml
  • Ground cinnamon2 g
  • Fine salt1 g

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the binding base of the recipe: peel the 2 very ripe bananas and place them in a large bowl, mashing them vigorously with a fork or a potato masher. Work the fruit for about 2 minutes until you achieve a completely smooth purée free of any solid lumps, as this pulp will replace the eggs by providing the necessary moisture. The final result should be a thick, consistent cream that will act as the glue for the dry ingredients. Chef's tip: use bananas with almost black skins, as they are higher in natural sugars and make the batter much fluffier.
  2. Now focus on the dry ingredients: in a separate bowl, carefully sift the 240g of buckwheat flour together with the 10g of baking powder and 2g of ground cinnamon. Use a hand whisk to combine the three ingredients with circular motions, ensuring the leavening agent is evenly distributed throughout the flour. This aeration process will allow the pancakes to rise properly during cooking, preventing the formation of unpleasant lumps. Chef's tip: do not skip sifting, as buckwheat flour tends to clump easily and needs to be lightened.
  3. Proceed by combining the dry ingredients with the previously prepared banana purée, also adding 1g of fine salt to enhance the aromatic bouquet of the cinnamon. Mix initially with a wooden spoon, starting from the centre and gradually incorporating the flour towards the edges of the bowl. You will obtain a rather firm and lumpy mixture, but this is the necessary foundation to build the structure of the pancake. Chef's tip: the salt is not just for taste; it acts chemically to stabilise the buckwheat proteins during mixing.
  4. Now it is time to hydrate the batter: pour the 250ml of water in a thin stream, adding it a little at a time while working energetically with a hand whisk for about 2 or 3 minutes. Maintain a steady rhythm to incorporate air, continuing until the batter is fluid, velvety, and free of raw flour streaks. You should achieve the consistency of a thick custard that slides slowly off the whisk without breaking. Chef's tip: use room temperature water so as not to inhibit the baking powder, which activates best in a lukewarm environment.
  5. Prepare the pears to enrich the mixture: thoroughly wash the 2 Abate or Conference pears, peel them, and remove the core, then cut them into tiny cubes about 5mm wide. It is vital that the pieces are small and uniform so that the heat can quickly penetrate the fruit during the brief pan-frying, making it melt-in-the-mouth. You will notice the cubes release a sweet fragrance that pairs perfectly with the nutty notes of the buckwheat. Chef's tip: if the pears are very juicy, pat them dry with kitchen paper after dicing to avoid thinning the batter.
  6. Complete the batter by gently folding in the pear cubes and the first 15ml of sunflower oil using a silicone spatula. Use over-and-under motions to avoid deflating the incorporated air and to distribute the fruit evenly throughout every portion of the batter. The oil added at this stage ensures the inside of the pancake remains extremely soft and prevents it from becoming rubbery once cooled. Chef's tip: do not overmix at this stage; stop as soon as the fruit is distributed to maintain the lightness of the batter.
  7. Prepare for cooking by heating a flat-bottomed non-stick pan over medium heat, greasing the surface with the remaining 15ml of sunflower oil distributed with a brush or kitchen paper. Wait about 1-2 minutes until the temperature is correct; you can test it by dropping a small bit of batter which should sizzle immediately without turning black. A well-heated pan is the secret to achieving that crispy outer crust that encases the soft heart. Chef's tip: avoid butter in this recipe; sunflower oil allows for a higher smoke point, ideal for buckwheat flour.
  8. Cook the pancakes by portioning about two generous tablespoons of batter for each one, aiming to form discs about 8-10cm in diameter, well-spaced apart. Let them cook undisturbed for about 3 minutes over medium heat: the top surface will begin to fill with small air bubbles and the edges will look dry and slightly lifted. This is the unmistakable visual signal that the base has formed a solid structure and is ready to be handled. Chef's tip: do not press down on the pancakes with the spatula while they cook, otherwise you will lose all the internal fluffiness.
  9. Flip the pancakes with a swift motion using a wide, flat spatula, continuing to cook on the second side for about 2 more minutes. The final colour should be a deep golden brown, typical of buckwheat's natural hue, and to the touch, the centre should feel springy and firm, no longer yielding. Once ready, place them on a serving plate without stacking them too much to prevent residual steam from softening the outer crust. Chef's tip: serve the pancakes while still hot to fully enjoy the contrast between the molten pear and the fragrance of the cinnamon.
