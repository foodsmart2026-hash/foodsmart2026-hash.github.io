Pumpkin in saor with crispy polenta crostini

Veneto · 4 servings · Prep 30 min · Cooking 40 min · Medium

An autumnal twist on the classic Venetian 'saor': fried pumpkin marinated in a sweet and sour sauce with onions, pine nuts, and raisins, served on crispy polenta crostini.

🌿 Seasonal recipe🌱 Vegan🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🇮🇹 Italian

A Venetian classic reimagined with the sweetness of pumpkin and the crunch of fried polenta. This dish balances sweet and sour flavors with varied textures, making it a perfect autumn appetizer or rich side dish.

Ingredients

  • Cleaned Mantua or Delica pumpkin600g
  • White onions400g
  • White wine vinegar100ml
  • Raisins40g
  • Pine nuts30g
  • Plain flour50g
  • Caster sugar10g
  • Cooked, firm polenta400g
  • Groundnut oil500ml
  • Extra virgin olive oil30ml
  • Salt and black pepperto taste

Allergens

Peanuts, Tree nuts, Gluten, Sulphites
May contain traces of: Sesame.

Method

  1. Start by carefully prepping 600g of Mantuan or Delica pumpkin, removing the hard skin and inner seeds, then cut it into slices about half a centimetre thick. Aim for uniform slices using a sharp chef's knife and a stable cutting board to ensure precision. This step takes about 10 minutes and should result in a series of even slices ready for cooking. My professional tip is to maintain a consistent thickness so that all slices cook at the same rate, preventing some from staying raw while others burn.
  2. Take 50g of plain flour and pour it onto a flat plate, then dredge each pumpkin slice in the flour, pressing lightly with your fingers to ensure it adheres well to both sides. Vigorously shake each slice between your hands or against the edge of the plate to remove any excess flour, leaving only an almost invisible veil. This must be done just moments before frying to prevent the pumpkin's natural moisture from making the flour damp and gummy. You will get perfectly dry and slightly opaque slices ready for the oil. A chef's trick: don't flour all the pumpkin at once; proceed in small batches as you fry.
  3. Pour 500ml of peanut oil into a deep pan and heat it over medium heat until it reaches 170-180°C. Submerge the pumpkin slices a few at a time, letting them fry for about 3-4 minutes until they appear golden on the surface and the flesh is tender when pierced with a fork. Adjust the flame to maintain a constant temperature, preventing the oil from smoking or the temperature from dropping too low. Once ready, the pumpkin should be crispy on the outside and meltingly soft inside. Remember not to overcrowd the pan, otherwise the pumpkin will boil in the oil instead of frying, absorbing too much grease.
  4. Drain the pumpkin slices using a slotted spoon and place them on plenty of kitchen paper to remove any residual oil. While they are still piping hot, season with salt and a pinch of freshly ground black pepper so the seasonings are better absorbed by the hot fibres. Let them rest for a moment so the texture stabilises. The final result should be a dry, tasty fry with an inviting amber colour. The chef's advice is to always salt fried foods as soon as they are removed from the oil, never before, to preserve the crispness of the outer crust.
  5. Now focus on the 'saor' base: finely slice 400g of white onions and place them in a large pan with 30ml of extra virgin olive oil. Let them soften over very low heat for about 15 minutes, stirring often with a wooden spoon so they become translucent and tender without browning or catching. If you notice them drying out too quickly, add a tablespoon of warm water to help them stew. In the end, the onions should be almost like a sweet, fragrant cream. Do not rush this step: the slow sweating of the onion is the secret to obtaining that sweet note that balances the acidity of the vinegar.
  6. Add 40g of raisins (previously soaked in warm water for 10 minutes and squeezed dry) and 30g of pine nuts to the onions. Pour in 100ml of white wine vinegar and add 10g of granulated sugar, slightly increasing the heat to encourage the alcohol to evaporate and the flavours to meld. Let it simmer vigorously for about 2-3 minutes until the liquid has slightly reduced, creating a fragrant emulsion. You will get a glossy and very aromatic sweet and sour dressing, typical of the Venetian tradition. My tip is to toast the pine nuts in a separate small pan for a minute before adding them: they will release a much more intense toasted aroma.
  7. Take a ceramic or glass dish and begin to assemble the plate by alternating a layer of fried pumpkin with a generous portion of the onion and aromatics mixture. Continue layering carefully until all ingredients are used, finishing with a layer of onions that covers the pumpkin well. Cover the dish with cling film and place it in a cool place or in the refrigerator for at least 12 hours. This prolonged rest is essential for the pumpkin to marinate perfectly, absorbing the character of the saor. Do not be tempted to eat it immediately: the flavour improves drastically after a night's rest.
  8. At the time of serving, cut 400g of firm pre-cooked polenta into uniform rectangles about 2 centimetres thick. Heat a cast-iron griddle or a non-stick pan until smoking hot and grill the polenta for 4-5 minutes per side over high heat. You want to achieve a dark, crispy outer crust with typical grill marks, while keeping the heart of the slice hot and soft. To prevent the polenta from sticking or breaking, make sure the griddle is very hot before placing it down and do not turn it until the crust has formed. The thermal contrast between hot polenta and room-temperature pumpkin is the winning element.
  9. Arrange the crispy polenta croutons on a serving platter and place a generous portion of pumpkin in saor on top of each, making sure to take it out of the fridge at least an hour before serving. Distribute the remaining onions, raisins, and pine nuts from the bottom of the dish over the top so as not to lose the precious marinating juices. The result will be a rich starter where the sweetness of the pumpkin meets the zing of the vinegar and the crunch of the corn. Serve immediately to enjoy the difference in textures. A master's touch: garnish with a sprig of fresh thyme or rosemary for a final herbaceous note.

Tips

  • Let the pumpkin rest in the saor marinade for at least 24 hours to blend the flavors.
  • Thoroughly dry the fried pumpkin on paper towels before adding it to the onions.
  • Slice the polenta thinly to achieve a uniform crispy crust when browning.

Variations

  • Replace pine nuts with other toasted nuts for a different crunchy note.
  • Use apple cider vinegar instead of white wine vinegar for a milder sweet and sour taste.
