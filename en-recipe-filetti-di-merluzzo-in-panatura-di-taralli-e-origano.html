Cod Fillets in a Taralli and Oregano Crust

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 18 min · Medium

Transform simple frozen fish fillets into a gourmet dish using leftover or affordable taralli. The crunchy and aromatic crust protects the white cod meat, keeping it juicy.

A crispy main course that gives a Puglian twist to cod by using crushed taralli crackers for the crust. The mild fish pairs perfectly with the savory, aromatic coating of oregano and lemon zest. A quick recipe, ideal for a light yet flavorful dinner.

Ingredients

  • Cod fillets (fresh or frozen)600 g
  • Classic oil taralli (crunchy Italian savoury snack rings)100 g
  • Dried oregano1 cucchiaino
  • Lemon zest1 pcs
  • Extra virgin olive oil20 ml
  • Saltq.b.

Allergens

Fish, Gluten

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the 600g of cod fillets, ensuring they are perfectly dry. If you are using frozen fish, allow it to thaw slowly in the refrigerator for several hours, then pat it dry thoroughly with plenty of kitchen paper on both sides. It is vital to remove all surface moisture to prevent the fish from releasing water during cooking, which would compromise the crust's crunchiness. You will have clean fillets ready to hold the breading without it sliding off. My chef's tip is to press down gently with your palm to squeeze out any residual water from the deeper fibres of the fish.
  2. Take the 100g of olive oil taralli and place them in a sturdy food bag, sealing it tightly. Use a rolling pin or a meat mallet to crush the taralli until you achieve a coarse and irregular crumb. Do not aim for a fine flour, as the larger pieces will provide that signature crunch typical of this dish. You should end up with a mix of crumbs of various sizes that will add texture to the plate. The chef recommends not using an electric mixer for this task, as the blades tend to heat the fats in the taralli, making them less fragrant.
  3. Transfer the taralli crumbs into a large bowl and add a teaspoon of dried oregano and the grated zest of one unwaxed lemon. Mix well with a spoon to distribute the aromatics evenly throughout the breading. You will immediately smell an intense Mediterranean aroma as the lemon's essential oils bond with the oregano. The result should be a fragrant breading that is visually varied between the yellow zest and the green herbs. Make sure to grate only the yellow part of the lemon, avoiding the white pith which would be bitter.
  4. Pour the 20ml of extra virgin olive oil directly into the bowl with the flavoured crumbs. Stir thoroughly with a fork until every taralli crumb is lightly coated and glossy. This step is crucial because the oil acts as a heat conductor in the oven, allowing the breading to brown perfectly without burning. The final consistency of the mix should resemble wet sand, ready to adhere to the fish. Do not overdo the oil to avoid making the dish heavy, but ensure there are no excessively dry patches of breading.
  5. Prepare a wide baking tray by lining it with a sheet of baking parchment to prevent the fish from sticking to the bottom. Arrange the cod fillets well-spaced apart and season them with a pinch of salt across the entire surface. Remember that taralli are already savoury, so use salt sparingly to maintain a flavour balance. The tidy arrangement of the fillets will allow heat to circulate evenly during cooking. A professional trick is to lightly oil the parchment under the fish to ensure the base stays succulent and doesn't tear when serving.
  6. Distribute the taralli breading over each fillet, creating a generous and even layer that covers the flesh well. Use your fingertips to gently press the crumbs onto the surface of the cod, making it adhere firmly. You should create a sort of protective 'shield' that will retain the fish's internal moisture while in the oven. Ensure the edges are well covered to prevent the thinner parts of the fillet from drying out. The chef suggests not being stingy with the breading: the crunchy layer must be the star of the contrast with the cod's softness.
  7. Place the tray in a preheated conventional oven at 190°C and bake for approximately 15-18 minutes. The exact time will depend on the thickness of your fillets: if they are very thick, they might need an extra couple of minutes. During this time, the heat will transform the crumbs into a golden crust, while the cod cooks gently in its own juices. Towards the end of the specified time, check that the fish flesh has turned opaque white rather than translucent. For the best result, place the tray on the middle shelf of the oven to ensure even cooking from above and below.
  8. Switch on the oven's grill function for the final 2 minutes of cooking to achieve an extremely crispy and beautifully amber-coloured crust. Stay by the oven during this stage, as the taralli breading can go from golden to burnt in a matter of seconds. The scent of toasted bread and lemon will fill the kitchen, indicating the dish is ready to be taken out. The surface should be visibly crunchy and inviting to the touch of a fork. The chef advises turning off the oven as soon as you see the first bubbles of oil sizzling on the crust's surface.
  9. Remove the tray from the oven and let the cod rest for just one minute before serving. Use a wide spatula to transfer the fillets to individual plates, taking care not to break the taralli crust. Accompany each portion with a fresh lemon wedge, the juice of which can be squeezed over at the last second to balance the saltiness of the taralli and cleanse the palate. The fish should be incredibly tender, flaking at the slightest touch, while the breading must offer a sharp, fragrant contrast. Serve immediately to fully enjoy the difference in textures between the soft heart and the crunchy exterior.

Tips

  • Pat the fish fillets very dry before coating to ensure maximum crunchiness.
  • If using frozen cod, thaw it completely in the fridge to prevent excess moisture release.
  • For a more rustic texture, crumble the taralli by hand instead of grinding them into a fine powder.

Variations

  • Substitute oregano with fresh thyme for a more citrusy and delicate aroma.
  • Try using chili-flavored taralli to add a bold, spicy kick to your crust.
