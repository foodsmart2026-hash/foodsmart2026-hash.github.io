Potato Gnocchetti with White Chicken Ragù and Swiss Chard

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 10 min · Easy

Soft homemade or store-bought gnocchetti (salt-free), seasoned with a very light ragù. The Swiss chard adds iron and colour in a delicate way.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⬇️ Big saving🧒 Kid-friendly🇮🇹 Italian

A complete dish pairing soft potato gnocchetti with a white ragù of finely chopped chicken and fresh chard. The 30-month aged Parmigiano Reggiano completes the preparation with a savory and bold note.

Ingredients

  • Small potato gnocchetti (small Italian potato dumplings)300 g
  • Chicken breast140 g
  • Swiss chard (leaves only)100 g
  • 30-month aged Parmigiano Reggiano (hard Italian cow's milk cheese)15 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil10 ml

Allergens

Gluten, Milk

Method

  1. Take 140g of chicken breast and mince it finely with a sharp knife or pulse it briefly in an electric mixer for a few seconds. The aim is to achieve a very fine texture, similar to a traditional ragù, which is ideal for easy chewing for little ones. Work on a clean chopping board, being careful not to turn the meat into a mush, but rather keeping a distinct grainy consistency. You should end up with a homogeneous, finely minced meat ready for quick and even cooking. Chef's tip: Ensure the chicken is well-chilled from the fridge before mincing to get a cleaner, more precise cut.
  2. Thoroughly wash 100g of Swiss chard leaves under cold running water and use a knife to remove the tough white central ribs, keeping only the tender green parts. Stack the leaves, roll them up slightly, and slice them into very thin strips, then chop them again in the opposite direction. This fine shredding ensures the vegetable integrates perfectly with the meat during the short cooking time, almost disappearing into the sauce. You will get a bright green, soft mince rich in vitamins. Chef's tip: Dry the leaves well with a salad spinner or a cloth before cutting to prevent them from slipping on the board.
  3. In a large non-stick frying pan, combine the minced chicken and the chopped chard, adding just one tablespoon of plain water. Cover immediately with a lid and start cooking over a medium-low heat for about 8-10 minutes, stirring occasionally with a wooden spoon. The steam trapped inside will keep the meat extremely tender and cook the chard gently without the need for added fats. The chicken should look white and opaque, while the chard should be wilted and soft. Chef's tip: Do not brown the meat; the cooking process must remain moist and 'white' to avoid hardening the chicken's protein fibres.
  4. While the sauce is cooking, constantly check that the base does not dry out too much or become too compact. If you notice the mixture starting to stick to the bottom of the pan, add a couple of tablespoons of hot water to maintain a moist environment. The final result should be a soft, juicy ragù that can coat the gnocchetti evenly without sliding off. The chard will release its natural juices, creating a light binding effect with the meat. Chef's tip: Use filtered water or the water about to boil in the pasta pot so as not to alter the delicate flavours of the ragù.
  5. Fill a tall pot with about 2 litres of water and bring it to a rolling boil over a high heat, strictly avoiding the addition of salt. For children, it is essential to limit sodium intake, as the Parmigiano will provide the necessary savouriness at the end of the preparation. When you see bubbles rising to the surface constantly and vigorously, the water is ready for the pasta. Have a slotted spoon ready on the counter for the draining stage. Chef's tip: Using a large pot is essential so the gnocchetti have enough room to not stick together as soon as they are immersed.
  6. Gently drop 300g of small potato gnocchetti into the boiling water and wait a few moments for them to float spontaneously to the surface. This process is very fast and usually takes between 60 and 90 seconds, depending on the freshness of the product. As soon as you see the gnocchetti floating steadily, scoop them out immediately with the slotted spoon, shaking them slightly to remove excess water. The gnocchetti should be soft to the touch but perfectly retain their round shape. Chef's tip: Avoid draining gnocchetti in a traditional colander because the weight and the drop could squash or break them.
  7. Transfer the freshly drained gnocchetti directly into the pan with the chicken and chard ragù, which you should have kept warm. Turn the heat to medium and sauté everything for about a minute, moving the pan in circular motions or stirring extremely gently. This 'tossing' phase allows the gnocchetti to absorb the flavours of the sauce and the chard to distribute evenly over the surface of the pasta. You will see the ragù binding harmoniously to every single piece. Chef's tip: If the mixture appears too thick, add one last tablespoon of cooking water before turning off the heat to achieve maximum creaminess.
  8. Turn off the heat and move the pan away from the direct heat source, then add 10ml of extra virgin olive oil and 15g of very finely grated 30-month Parmigiano Reggiano. Stir vigorously for about 30 seconds so the residual heat of the gnocchetti melts the cheese and emulsifies it with the oil and the moisture from the sauce. The oil added raw will keep all its nutritional properties intact and give the dish an inviting gloss. You will smell the intense aroma of the Parmigiano, which acts as a natural seasoning instead of salt. Chef's tip: Emulsifying off the heat is the professional secret to preventing the cheese from becoming stringy or rubbery.
  9. Plate the gnocchetti, dividing them into four equal portions and ensuring the chicken ragù is evenly distributed in each bowl. Before serving to young children, check the size of the gnocchetti and, if necessary, cut them in half with a fork to make them even safer to eat. Let the dish rest for two or three minutes at room temperature so it reaches an ideal temperature for a child's mouth. The result will be a balanced, colourful meal with a soft texture. Chef's tip: Cutting gnocchi in half is a fundamental safety precaution to prevent choking hazards, especially for children under 3 years old.

Tips

  • Chop the chicken very finely with a knife to create a ragù that perfectly clings to the gnocchetti.
  • Use only the green part of the chard, removing the harder central rib for faster cooking.
  • Drain the gnocchetti as soon as they float to the surface to maintain their ideal consistency.

Variations

  • You can replace the chicken breast with turkey breast for a variation in the ragù flavor.
  • Try using fresh spinach leaves instead of chard for a milder taste.
