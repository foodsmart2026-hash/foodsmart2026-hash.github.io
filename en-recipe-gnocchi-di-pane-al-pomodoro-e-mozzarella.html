Bread Gnocchi with Tomato and Mozzarella

4 servings · Prep 30 min · Cooking 15 min · Medium

A rescue recipe that transforms old bread into soft gnocchi. A complete meal thanks to the presence of carbohydrates, dairy protein, and lycopene from the tomato.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🧒 Kid-friendly🇮🇹 Italian

A traditional dish that turns stale bread into soft, flavorful dumplings. Served with tomato sauce and melty mozzarella, they are perfect for a comforting and tasty family meal.

Ingredients

  • Stale bread (crumb only)300 g
  • Milk200 ml
  • Egg1 pcs
  • Grated Parmesan or Grana Padano (hard Italian aged cheeses)50 g
  • Plain flour100 g
  • Tomato passata500 ml
  • Firm mozzarella [low moisture pizza style]125 g

Allergens

Gluten, Eggs, Milk

Method

  1. Cut 300g of stale bread crumb into small cubes and place them in a large bowl, pouring 200ml of room temperature milk over them. Press down well with a fork so the bread absorbs all the liquid and let it rest for about 15-20 minutes. The result should be a soft, uniform mass with no dry parts remaining. Chef's Tip: Use a crusty, farmhouse-style bread for the best gnocchi texture.
  2. Take the soaked bread and squeeze it firmly between your hands to remove any excess milk, then transfer it to a clean second bowl. Add one whole egg, 50g of grated Parmesan or Grana, and a pinch of salt for seasoning. Work the mixture with your hands until the ingredients are perfectly combined. You will obtain a moist but not overly wet base, fragrant with cheese. Chef's Tip: Squeeze the bread very thoroughly to avoid having to add too much flour later, which would make the gnocchi gummy.
  3. Begin incorporating 100g of plain flour a little at a time, sifting it directly over the bread mixture. Work everything quickly with your fingertips, avoiding over-handling the dough or over-developing the gluten. Stop when you achieve a firm, elastic dough that is no longer sticky to the touch. The final consistency should allow you to handle the dough without it sticking to your fingers. Chef's Tip: Do not add all the flour at once, as the bread's residual moisture can vary.
  4. Dust your work surface with a light layer of flour and divide the dough into small pieces, rolling them with your palms to form long, thin ropes about 1.5cm thick. Using a smooth-bladed knife, cut the ropes into 2cm pieces, trying to keep them all the same size for even cooking. Pass each gnocco over the prongs of a fork, applying gentle pressure to create the classic surface ridges. The result will be a series of well-formed gnocchi ready to catch the sauce. Chef's Tip: Use a light touch so you don't squash the ridges you've just created.
  5. In a large, high-sided pan, pour in 500ml of tomato passata, a drizzle of extra virgin olive oil, and a pinch of salt. Turn the heat to medium and let it simmer gently for about 10 minutes, stirring occasionally with a wooden spoon. The sauce should reduce slightly, becoming thicker, glossier, and more fragrant. You should aim for a velvety tomato base that isn't too watery. Chef's Tip: Add a fresh basil leaf halfway through cooking for an extra aromatic touch.
  6. Bring a large pot of salted water to the boil and, once it is boiling vigorously, gently drop in the bread gnocchi. Cook them over medium heat for about 2-3 minutes, monitoring the surface of the pot closely. As soon as the gnocchi rise to the surface, it means the egg proteins have set and the flour starch is cooked. The visual cue is immediate: the gnocchi will float lightly on top. Chef's Tip: Cook the gnocchi in two batches to prevent the water temperature from dropping too much.
  7. Retrieve the floating gnocchi using a slotted spoon and transfer them directly into the pan with the hot tomato sauce. Immediately add 125g of low-moisture mozzarella cut into small, uniform cubes. Toss everything over very low heat for one minute, moving the pan gently so as not to break the gnocchi. The mozzarella should begin to melt gradually without turning into a single clump. Chef's Tip: Drain the gnocchi well to avoid thinning the sauce too much with cooking water.
  8. Continue stirring gently until the mozzarella starts to create inviting white strings between the gnocchi and the red tomato. Serve immediately in preheated shallow bowls to maintain the dish's temperature and creaminess. The final texture should be soft and coating, with perfectly stringy mozzarella. The aroma will be that of typical Mediterranean tradition. Chef's Tip: A final dusting of freshly cracked black pepper will enhance the flavour of the bread and cheese.

Tips

  • Squeeze the soaked bread crumbs very hard to prevent the dough from becoming too watery.
  • Add the flour gradually to reach the right consistency without making the gnocchi too tough.
  • Use low-moisture mozzarella as it releases less liquid when heating with the sauce.

Variations

  • Flavor the dough with a pinch of nutmeg for a deeper aromatic profile.
  • Replace Parmesan with Pecorino Romano for a sharper and more savory taste.
