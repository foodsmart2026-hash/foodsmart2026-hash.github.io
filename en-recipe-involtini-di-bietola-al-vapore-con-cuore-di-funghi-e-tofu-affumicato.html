Steamed Swiss chard rolls with a mushroom and smoked tofu filling

🌿 Seasonal recipe

4 servings · Prep 30 min · Cooking 15 min · Medium

Large Swiss chard leaves enclosing a savoury, protein-rich filling, gently steamed to preserve colours and nutrients.

Ingredients

  • Very large Swiss chard leaves8
  • Smoked tofu250g
  • Finely chopped button mushrooms200g
  • Shallot1
  • Nutritional yeast flakes2 tbsp
  • Soy sauce (Tamari)1 tbsp
  • Sunflower oil2 tbsp
  • Grated fresh ginger1 tsp

Allergens

Soy
May contain traces of: Gluten.

Method

  1. Begin by carefully washing the 8 large chard leaves under running water, removing any traces of soil. Bring a large pot of salted water to the boil and blanch the leaves for just 30 seconds, then immediately transfer them into a bowl of ice water using tongs. This thermal shock will fix the vibrant green colour and stop the cooking instantly, keeping the fibres elastic. You will obtain leaves that are tender yet strong, ready to be handled without tearing. Chef's tip: do not skip the ice bath, otherwise the leaves will turn brown and mushy, making it impossible to form the rolls.
  2. Take the drained chard leaves and lay them out gently on a clean tea towel or a large chopping board to dry them thoroughly. Using a sharp paring knife, trim the end of the central rib if it is too thick and woody, making a small 'V' cut or simply thinning it down with a horizontal cut. Be very careful not to puncture the leaf's surface, as it must remain intact to perfectly contain the filling. The leaf should be flat and flexible across its entire surface, ready to be rolled. Chef's tip: if the rib is truly massive, remove it entirely and slightly overlap the two remaining leaf halves to close the gap.
  3. Let's move on to the aromatic base: finely chop 1 shallot and place it in a non-stick frying pan with 2 tablespoons of sunflower oil and 1 teaspoon of freshly grated ginger. Turn the heat to medium-low and let the shallot soften until translucent and releasing the citrusy scent of the ginger, for about 3-4 minutes. Ensure the shallot does not brown too much to avoid a bitter taste in the dish. The result should be a sweet, fragrant, and translucent base. Chef's tip: grate the ginger fresh at the last moment to keep all its essential oils and natural heat intact.
  4. Slightly increase the heat to medium and add the 200g of finely chopped mushrooms to the shallot base. Let them cook, stirring often with a wooden spoon until they have released all their moisture and it has completely evaporated. This process will take about 6-8 minutes and will allow the mushrooms to concentrate their umami flavour and brown slightly. You should achieve a dry and compact mushroom mixture, ideal for not making the chard leaf too soggy later. Chef's tip: do not add salt at this stage, otherwise the mushrooms will boil in their own juices instead of browning properly.
  5. Take the 250g of smoked tofu and crumble it coarsely with your hands or using the prongs of a fork directly into the pan with the mushrooms. Mix well to distribute the tofu evenly and continue cooking over medium heat for another 3 minutes, allowing the tofu to warm through and absorb the flavours from the pan. The heat will release the smoky aroma of the tofu which pairs perfectly with the earthiness of the mushrooms. The filling will begin to look like a rustic and very inviting vegetable mince. Chef's tip: if you prefer a finer texture, you can pulse the tofu in a food processor for a few seconds before adding it to the pan.
  6. Complete the filling by pouring 1 tablespoon of Tamari soy sauce and 2 tablespoons of nutritional yeast flakes over the mixture in the pan. Stir vigorously for about a minute with the heat turned off so the yeast acts as a natural binder and the soy distributes its deep savouriness. The filling should be well-combined, compact, and intensely flavoured to balance the sweetness of the chard. Allow the mixture to cool slightly for a few minutes before proceeding to shape the rolls. Chef's tip: always taste the filling at this point; soy sauce is already salty, so you most likely won't need to add extra salt.
  7. Now let's proceed with the assembly: place two generous tablespoons of filling in the centre of each opened chard leaf, positioning it towards the base. Fold the long sides of the leaf inwards over the filling and then roll from the bottom upwards, maintaining slight pressure to make the cylinder compact. Ensure the ends are well-sealed to prevent the filling from escaping during steaming. You will get 8 elegant, firm rolls of similar size. Chef's tip: do not overstuff the leaves, otherwise the leaf might tear while you are trying to close it.
  8. Prepare the steamer by lining the basket with a piece of baking parchment in which you have poked small holes to allow the steam to circulate. Arrange the rolls with the seam facing downwards to ensure they stay closed, leaving a little space between each one. Cover with the lid and cook over a pot of constantly boiling water for about 10 minutes on medium heat. When finished, the chard leaf will be very tender but still intact and the heart of the roll will be perfectly heated. Chef's tip: lining the steamer prevents the chard from sticking to the basket, preserving the dish's perfect aesthetic.
  9. Carefully lift the rolls out of the steamer using a wide spatula and place them on a serving platter or individual plates. Serve them immediately while they are still hot and steaming, finishing the dish with a drizzle of extra virgin olive oil or a few extra drops of soy sauce on the side for those who prefer a stronger taste. The velvety external texture of the chard pairs incredibly well with the savouriness of the smoked tofu and mushrooms. You have created a light, healthy, and visually stunning starter or main course. Chef's tip: garnish with some toasted sesame seeds to add a crunchy note that contrasts with the softness from the steaming.
