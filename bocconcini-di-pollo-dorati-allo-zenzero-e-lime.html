Golden Ginger and Lime Chicken Bites

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 10 min · Easy

A light main course with an exotic touch, perfect for brightening up chicken breast with affordable ingredients. The quick marinade ensures juicy meat and a fresh aroma, ideal for the whole family.

Tender chicken bites infused with the zesty aroma of lime and the sharp kick of fresh ginger. A quick pan-seared dish that offers a delightful contrast between a golden exterior and a juicy heart.

Ingredients

  • Whole chicken breast600 g
  • Limes (or lemons)2 pcs
  • Fresh ginger30 g
  • Plain flour (Farina tipo 0)50 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Fine saltq.b.
  • Fresh parsley1 mazzetto

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the meat: take the 600g of whole chicken breast and, on a clean chopping board, cut it into uniform cubes of approximately 2-3 cm. It is essential that the pieces are of equal size to ensure even cooking and prevent smaller cubes from drying out. Use a very sharp knife to achieve clean cuts without tearing the muscle fibres. The result should be a batch of consistent bite-sized pieces ready for marinating. Chef's tip: carefully remove any cartilage or fatty parts before cutting for a more refined texture.
  2. Prepare the aromatic base by finely grating 30g of peeled fresh ginger and squeezing the juice of 2 limes into a large glass or ceramic bowl. Whisk these two ingredients together thoroughly to create a fragrant emulsion. The acidity of the lime and the spicy kick of the ginger will work together to tenderise the meat. You should obtain a cloudy and intensely citrusy liquid. Chef's tip: avoid using reactive metal bowls which could alter the acidic flavour of the marinade.
  3. Submerge the chicken pieces completely in the bowl with the lime juice and ginger, ensuring every piece is well coated. Cover with cling film and leave to rest in the refrigerator for exactly 15 minutes. During this time, the citric acid will partially break down the surface proteins, allowing the aromas to penetrate the heart of the meat. You will notice the chicken turning a slightly paler colour. Chef's tip: do not exceed 20 minutes of marinating, otherwise the meat will become excessively soft and lose its pleasant bite.
  4. After 15 minutes, carefully drain the chicken from the marinade liquid, making sure to reserve all the juice in a small cup. Toss the chicken cubes in a shallow dish containing 50g of plain flour, turning them well so they are evenly coated. Shake each piece with your hands or use a wide-mesh sieve to remove any excess flour, which would cause unpleasant lumps. The result should be a very thin, almost invisible coating. Chef's tip: flour the chicken only moments before putting it in the pan to prevent the flour from absorbing the meat's moisture and becoming sticky.
  5. Take a large non-stick frying pan, pour in 30ml of extra virgin olive oil and heat it over a high flame. When the oil is hot (but not smoking), arrange the chicken pieces in a single layer, avoiding overcrowding. Let them sear for about 5 minutes without moving them too much initially, then turn them to seal every side. You should achieve an inviting golden crust over the entire surface. Chef's tip: if the pan is too small, cook the chicken in two separate batches to prevent the oil temperature from dropping sharply and creating steam instead of browning.
  6. Once the chicken is well browned, reduce the heat to medium and deglaze the pan by pouring in the reserved ginger and lime marinade. Cook for about 4 minutes, frequently moving the pan with a circular motion. The liquid, combining with the residual flour on the cubes, will begin to thicken into a velvety, glossy sauce. The aroma will become very intense and enveloping. Chef's tip: if you notice the sauce drying out too quickly, add one or two tablespoons of hot water or vegetable stock to maintain the desired creaminess.
  7. Continue cooking until the chicken is opaque and white at the centre (you can check by cutting the largest cube) and the sauce has reached the consistency of a glaze that perfectly coats every piece. Do not overcook: the chicken breast must remain juicy inside. The final texture should be soft on the inside and glazed on the outside. Turn off the heat as soon as the sauce stops being runny. Chef's tip: using a meat thermometer is ideal; aim for an internal temperature of 72-74°C for a restaurant-quality result.
  8. With the heat off, add a pinch of fine salt and the bunch of fresh parsley, previously washed, dried well, and finely chopped. Stir gently one last time to distribute the flavours and the brilliant green colour of the herbs. Serve the chicken pieces immediately while piping hot, perhaps accompanied by basmati rice or steamed vegetables. The dish will appear glossy, fragrant, and perfectly balanced between acidic, spicy, and savoury. Chef's tip: add salt only at the end as the reduction of the marinade naturally concentrates the dish's saltiness.

Tips

  • Pat the chicken dry after marinating to ensure a perfectly crisp and golden crust.
  • Grate the ginger just before use to maximize its fresh and sharp aromatic profile.
  • Serve immediately to enjoy the best texture of the sautéed flour coating while it's hot.

Variations

  • Use lemon instead of lime for a more classic and familiar Mediterranean flavor profile.
  • Sprinkle with toasted sesame seeds at the end for an added layer of crunch and flavor.
