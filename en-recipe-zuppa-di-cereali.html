Grain Soup

2 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 50 min · Easy

🌱 Vegan🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🇮🇹 Italian

Ingredients

  • pearled farro120 g
  • pearled barley100 g
  • buckwheat80 g
  • small dried lentils100 g
  • carrot1
  • celery stalk1
  • small onion1
  • vegetable broth
    Make it at home: Vegetable broth ›    1.5 l
  • extra virgin olive oil25 ml
  • sprig of rosemary1
  • Salt and pepperto taste

Allergens

Gluten, Celery
May contain traces of: Tree nuts, Mustard, Soy.

Method

  1. Check the cooking instructions on the grain and lentil packaging. Thoroughly rinse the farro, barley, buckwheat, and lentils under running water.
  2. Finely chop the onion, carrot, and celery. Pour the oil into a large pot and prepare a light sauté for about 5 minutes.
  3. Add the lentils and stir for a few moments to flavor them with the vegetables.
  4. Pour in about 1.2 liters of hot broth, add the rosemary, and cook the lentils for about 15 minutes, checking their actual cooking time.
  5. Add the farro and barley and continue cooking for about 15–20 minutes, stirring periodically.
  6. Incorporate the buckwheat and continue cooking for another 10–15 minutes, checking the consistency of the different ingredients.
  7. If necessary, add more hot broth to keep the soup soft and creamy. Season with salt and pepper.
  8. Turn off the heat, remove the rosemary, let it rest for 5 minutes, and finish with a drizzle of raw extra virgin olive oil.

Chef's tips

Tips, mistakes and alternatives:

- Cooking times change based on the type of grain and processing: always check the packaging.

- Do not add all the broth at once: the quantity needed depends on absorption.

- For a creamier consistency, blend a small portion of the lentils.

- You can substitute buckwheat with oats, brown rice, or millet, adjusting the cooking times.

- For a richer soup, add potatoes, pumpkin, or mushrooms.

- Warning: farro, barley, and wheat contain gluten. For a gluten-free version, use exclusively certified gluten-free grains, such as rice, buckwheat, and millet, avoiding cross-contamination.
