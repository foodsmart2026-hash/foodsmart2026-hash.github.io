Roasted Potatoes and Red Onions with Rosemary

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 35 min · Medium

A rustic and crispy side dish that enhances the sweetness of caramelised red onions along with the savouriness of potatoes. It is the perfect choice to accompany any main course with minimal expense.

A timeless Mediterranean classic combining the sweetness of red onions with the crunchiness of roasted potatoes. Rosemary infuses an aromatic scent that makes this tray irresistible. A simple and versatile preparation suitable for any Sunday lunch.

Ingredients

  • Yellow-fleshed potatoes800 g
  • Red onions200 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Fresh or dried rosemary2 sprigs

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Begin by thoroughly washing 800g of yellow potatoes under running water, then peel them with a vegetable peeler and cut into uniform 2cm cubes. It is crucial to maintain this consistent size to ensure all pieces cook evenly without the smaller ones burning. Rinse the cubes in cold water to remove excess starch and dry them perfectly with a clean kitchen towel; this step is the secret to achieving a crispy outer crust. Chef's tip: if the potatoes are damp, they will never become truly crisp, so ensure they are bone-dry before proceeding.
  2. Take 200g of red onions, remove the outer papery skin and the ends, then cut them into chunky wedges about 2-3cm wide. Unlike potatoes, onions tend to wilt and burn faster, so a larger thickness will protect them during their long stay in the oven. You should aim for intact, fleshy wedges that will retain their natural sweetness. Chef's tip: leave the root base slightly intact on each wedge to prevent the onion layers from falling apart completely during roasting.
  3. Transfer the potatoes and onions into a very large mixing bowl and add 30ml of extra virgin olive oil, a generous pinch of sea salt, and some freshly ground black pepper. Take the needles from 2 sprigs of fresh rosemary, strip them from the woody stems, and roughly chop them with a knife before adding to the vegetables to release their essential oils. Mix everything vigorously with clean hands, massaging each piece so it is evenly coated with the seasoning. Chef's tip: seasoning in a bowl rather than directly on the tray ensures an impeccable oil distribution, avoiding greasy or dry spots.
  4. Preheat your oven to 200°C (fan assisted) and line a large baking sheet with high-quality baking parchment. Spread the potato and onion mix onto the tray, trying to arrange the pieces in a single layer, strictly avoiding overlapping or crowding. Adequate space between the cubes allows the hot air to circulate freely, promoting the Maillard reaction and the formation of a crust. Chef's tip: if the tray seems too small, use two rather than crowding the vegetables, otherwise you'll get a steamed effect instead of roasted.
  5. Slide the tray onto the middle rack of the preheated oven and set a timer for the first 20 minutes of cooking without opening the door to maintain a constant temperature. During this phase, you will see the potatoes start to puff slightly and the edges of the onions become translucent, while the scent of rosemary begins to fill the kitchen. The goal is to achieve a light initial browning on all exposed surfaces. Chef's tip: don't be in a hurry to turn them immediately; the crust needs time to form at the base so they don't stick to the paper.
  6. Once the 20 minutes are up, partially remove the tray and use a flat metal or wooden spatula to gently turn the vegetables, bringing the side that was in contact with the tray to the top. You will notice that the bottom has already turned a beautiful amber colour and feels firmer to the touch than the top. Try not to break the onion wedges, which will now have become soft and almost caramelised. Chef's tip: perform this task quickly so the internal temperature of the oven doesn't drop too much.
  7. Return the potatoes to the oven and continue cooking for another 15 minutes, increasing the temperature to 210°C for the final 5 minutes if you desire an even deeper golden finish. By the end of the time, the potatoes should look a deep golden yellow with brown, crispy corners, while the inside should be melt-in-the-mouth soft under fork pressure. The onions will be sweet, wilted, and slightly charred at the edges, creating a delicious contrast. Chef's tip: you can tell they are perfectly cooked by the sound; if you hear a 'dry', crispy rustle when moving the tray, they are ready.
  8. Remove the tray from the oven and let the side dish rest for a couple of minutes before serving, allowing the internal steam to redistribute and the fibres to stabilise. Transfer the potatoes and onions to a warmed serving dish, adding a pinch of sea salt flakes and, if desired, a final drizzle of raw oil to glaze the dish. Serve immediately to enjoy the maximum crunch of the potato contrasting with the sugary softness of the red onion. Chef's tip: never cover roasted potatoes with a lid or foil before serving, as the steam will turn them soggy in seconds.

Tips

  • Dry the potatoes well after washing to promote the formation of a crispy outer crust.
  • Distribute the vegetables on the baking sheet without overlapping to allow hot air to circulate evenly.
  • Use a flat spatula to turn the ingredients halfway through cooking without breaking the onion slices.

Variations

  • Use dried crushed rosemary if fresh sprigs are unavailable for a more concentrated herbal flavor.
  • Add a drizzle of raw extra virgin olive oil before serving to enhance the dish's natural aromas.
