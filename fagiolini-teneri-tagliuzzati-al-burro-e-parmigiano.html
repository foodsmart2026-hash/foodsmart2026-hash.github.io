Tender Chopped Green Beans with Butter and Parmesan

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 20 min · Easy

Green beans cooked until very tender and cut into small segments to be handled safely. Butter and Parmesan offer a comforting, rounded flavour.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🧒 Kid-friendly🌾 Gluten-free

A simple and comforting recipe, perfect for enhancing green beans with a classic creamy finish. The butter and Parmigiano dressing creates a rich emulsion that coats every piece.

Ingredients

  • Fresh or frozen green beans400 g
  • High-quality butter10 g
  • Grated Parmigiano Reggiano (hard aged Italian cheese)20 g

Allergens

Milk

Method

  1. Begin the preparation by trimming 400g of fresh green beans: remove the stalk ends by hand or with a small knife and wash them thoroughly under cold running water. If you are using frozen green beans, you can skip this step and drop them directly into the water. Place the beans in a colander to drain any excess water before cooking. You will have clean, bright green beans ready for the pot. My chef's tip is not to soak them for too long to prevent the loss of water-soluble vitamins.
  2. Fill a large pot with approximately 2 litres of water and bring it to a boil over a high heat, without adding salt at this stage. Once the water is boiling, gently submerge the 400g of trimmed beans to avoid splashing. Maintain a medium heat so that the boil is constant but not too turbulent. The beans will begin to soften as their fibres break down. Chef's advice: cook the beans without a lid to preserve their vivid green colour by allowing volatile acids to escape.
  3. Continue cooking for about 15-20 minutes, which is longer than the standard 'al dente' time, to ensure they become extremely tender and easy to chew. Monitor the texture by piercing them with a fork; they should be yielding but not completely falling apart. This prolonged cooking is essential for the success of this recipe, making the legume sweet and velvety on the palate. The beans should appear well-softened. Do not be afraid to exceed the usual 10 minutes: tenderness is the primary goal here.
  4. Drain the beans using a colander and immediately transfer them onto a clean chopping board, letting them cool slightly for about 2 minutes. Do not rinse them under cold water, as they need to retain their residual heat for the following steps. Lightly pat them with kitchen paper if they seem excessively wet. The result will be cooked, steaming produce ready for processing. A little trick: spread them out well on the board to allow excess moisture to evaporate, which could otherwise dilute the seasoning.
  5. Using a sharp chef's knife, cut the green beans into small, uniform segments approximately 1 centimetre long. Proceed with slow, precise movements, grouping the beans parallel to each other to speed up the task and achieve regular cuts. This operation transforms the perception of the side dish, making it similar to a refined vegetable 'battuto'. You will get a dice of tender beans ready to be seasoned. Be careful to keep your fingers tucked in to avoid any mishaps with the blade.
  6. In a non-stick small saucepan or the same cooking pot (now dried), place 10g of high-quality butter and turn the heat to the lowest setting. Let the fat melt slowly without ever letting it reach its smoking point or change colour to a nutty brown. The butter should simply become fluid and coat the bottom of the pan evenly. You will get a fragrant and clear fat base. A professional secret: add a teaspoon of water to the butter to prevent the temperature from rising too quickly.
  7. Add the chopped green beans to the pan with the melted butter and stir gently with a wooden spoon for one minute over a very low heat. Ensure that every small piece of bean is perfectly glossy and coated in the buttery film. This stage serves to evenly warm the vegetables and distribute the milky aromas thoroughly. The result will be a fragrant side dish that shines under the kitchen light. Do not sauté the beans on high heat, as the delicacy of the flavour comes specifically from the gentle residual heat.
  8. Turn off the heat completely and sprinkle the preparation with 20g of finely grated Parmigiano Reggiano. Stir carefully for about 30 seconds until the cheese, melting with the residual moisture and butter, creates a light, savoury cream that coats the beans. The final consistency should be bound and velvety, not grainy or dry. You will obtain a dish with an intense aroma of milk and sweet vegetables. The final tip is to wait a few moments before serving: resting allows the flavours to meld perfectly.
  9. Plate the side dish by distributing it into small moulds or directly onto the serving plate, ensuring the temperature is warm rather than piping hot. The texture must be such that it can be easily picked up even with a teaspoon or fingers for younger diners. The colour will be a bright forest green enriched by the golden reflections of the butter and cheese. The dish should look inviting and creamy in its simplicity. Always check the internal temperature before serving to ensure a pleasant and safe experience.

Tips

  • Cook the green beans for a few extra minutes to make them tender enough for easy chopping.
  • Dry the pot thoroughly before melting the butter to avoid unpleasant splattering.
  • Serve the dish immediately to enjoy the perfectly melted cheese and butter.

Variations

  • You can use clarified butter to achieve a nuttier and more intense flavor.
  • Replace Parmigiano Reggiano with Grana Padano for a milder taste.
