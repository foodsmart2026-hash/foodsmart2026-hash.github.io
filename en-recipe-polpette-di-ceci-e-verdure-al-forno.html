Baked Chickpea and Vegetable Patties

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 20 min · Medium

A nutritious and tasty starter that makes the most of plant-based proteins and seasonal vegetables, perfect for the whole family. These patties, crispy on the outside and soft inside, are an excellent budget-friendly alternative to ready-made supermarket products.

These vegetable meatballs are a tasty alternative for an appetizer or a quick dinner. The crunchy whole wheat exterior meets a soft heart of chickpeas and fresh vegetables, scented with lemon zest.

Ingredients

  • Canned chickpeas (drained and rinsed)480 g
  • Medium courgettes200 g
  • Carrots150 g
  • Wholemeal breadcrumbs70 g
  • Egg1 pz
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Chopped fresh parsley10 g
  • Fine salt3 g
  • Black pepper1 g
  • Untreated lemon zest5 g

Allergens

Gluten, Eggs

Method

  1. Start by draining 480g of pre-cooked chickpeas from their canning liquid and rinse them thoroughly under cold running water to remove excess sodium. Meticulously dry them by patting with a clean tea towel or paper towels until they are completely free of surface moisture. This step is essential to prevent the mixture from becoming too soft and difficult to handle. You will have clean, dry legumes ready to be transformed into a solid base. Chef's tip: do not skip the drying process, as residual water is the primary cause of meatballs falling apart during cooking.
  2. Take 200g of courgettes and 150g of carrots, wash them and grate them using a coarse grater to obtain uniform strands. Gather the grated vegetables in a clean tea towel or a fine-mesh sieve and squeeze them with great force using your hands to expel all their vegetable water. Spend at least 2 or 3 minutes on this until no more liquid comes out. The result should be a compact mass of vegetables that feels almost dry to the touch. Chef's tip: the more water you remove now, the fewer breadcrumbs you'll need later, keeping the vegetable flavour more intense.
  3. Pour the dried chickpeas into a food processor and use the pulse function to blend them for a few seconds at a time. You don't want a smooth hummus-like cream, but a coarse, uneven grain that resembles the texture of minced meat. Check the consistency after each pulse to avoid overheating the blades. You should see small, distinguishable pieces of chickpeas that will provide structure to the bite. Chef's tip: blend the chickpeas in small batches if your processor is small, so you avoid turning the bottom into purée while the top remains whole.
  4. In a large bowl, combine the pulsed chickpeas, the squeezed vegetables, 10g of finely chopped fresh parsley, and 5g of grated lemon zest. Add 3g of fine salt and 1g of freshly ground black pepper to enhance the natural flavours of the ingredients. Mix vigorously with a wooden spoon until the aromas and colours are evenly distributed. The scent of lemon and parsley should be clearly released during mixing. Chef's tip: rub the lemon zest between your hands along with the salt before adding it to the other ingredients to release all the essential oils.
  5. Add 1 whole egg to the mixture and start incorporating 70g of wholemeal breadcrumbs, pouring them in a little at a time while working the dough with your hands. Continue kneading until the mass becomes homogeneous, elastic, and no longer sticks to the sides of the bowl or your fingers. You might not need all the breadcrumbs if you squeezed the vegetables well, so stop as soon as you can form a compact ball. The mixture should be mouldable like shortcrust pastry. Chef's tip: let the dough rest in the fridge for 10 minutes before shaping the meatballs; as the wholemeal bread hydrates, it will make the mixture more stable.
  6. Preheat your oven to 200°C (static mode) and prepare a baking tray by lining it with high-quality baking paper. Take about 15ml (one tablespoon) of extra virgin olive oil and lightly brush the surface of the paper to create a non-stick, crispy base. Ensure the oven reaches the set temperature before baking, which usually takes 10-15 minutes. The prepared tray should look shiny but not excessively greasy. Chef's tip: place the oven rack in the middle position to allow the heat to surround the meatballs symmetrically.
  7. Take about 30g of dough at a time, press the portion between your palms to compact it, and then roll it to form a smooth ball about the size of a large walnut. Ensure the surface of the meatball has no deep cracks, which could open during cooking due to steam. Repeat until all ingredients are used, trying to maintain an identical size for all meatballs. You will get about 20-24 perfectly spherical and firm meatballs. Chef's tip: slightly moisten your hands with water or oil if the dough tends to get too warm and sticky.
  8. Arrange the meatballs on the tray spacing them at least 2cm apart and drizzle them evenly with the remaining 15ml of extra virgin olive oil. Bake at 200°C for a total of 15-18 minutes; halfway through (after about 8-9 minutes), gently turn each meatball with tongs or a spoon. The meatballs should develop a golden, crispy crust all over while the inside remains soft. Chef's tip: if you want extra browning, activate the grill function for the last 2 minutes of cooking, but watch closely to prevent burning.
  9. Remove the tray from the oven when the meatballs are well-browned and feel firm to a light finger press. Let them rest directly on the baking tray for at least 5 minutes before touching or plating them. This resting time is crucial as it allows the vegetable fibres and egg proteins to fully set, preventing the meatballs from breaking. Serve them warm or at room temperature, perhaps accompanied by a yoghurt sauce. Chef's tip: do not cover the hot meatballs with a lid, otherwise the steam will soften the outer crust, making them soggy.

Tips

  • Squeeze the grated zucchini and carrots very well to prevent the mixture from becoming too wet.
  • Let the mixture rest in the refrigerator for 30 minutes before forming the balls to make handling easier.
  • You can store the cooked meatballs in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to two days.

Variations

  • Replace parsley with fresh mint for a more summery and Mediterranean aroma.
  • Add a pinch of turmeric or cumin to the mixture for a spicier version.
