Soft Pumpkin and Sultana Muffins

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 18 min · Medium

Mantuan pumpkin (a sweet, dense Italian variety) gives a vibrant colour and natural sweetness to these small muffins. They are spongy and melt in the mouth, ideal for a snack for the whole family.

These soft muffins are perfect for an autumn breakfast or a genuine snack. The pumpkin puree provides an intense orange color and a moist texture that pairs beautifully with the natural sweetness of the raisins.

Ingredients

  • Steamed pumpkin pulp150 g
  • Type 0 flour (Italian medium-strength wheat flour)100 g
  • Sunflower oil30 ml
  • Sultanas30 g
  • Baking powder4 g
  • Whole milk20 ml

Allergens

Gluten, Milk

Method

  1. Begin by soaking 30g of sultanas in a small bowl of warm water for about 10 minutes. Once rehydrated, drain them thoroughly, squeeze out the excess water, and mince them finely with a sharp knife on a chopping board. This step is vital to ensure the natural sweetness of the fruit is evenly distributed throughout the batter without clumping. You should achieve a fine, consistent mince that will blend seamlessly with the pumpkin. My chef's tip is to pat the chopped raisins dry with kitchen paper to prevent excess moisture from interfering with the rise.
  2. While the raisins are soaking, preheat your oven to 180°C (static) and prepare your muffin tin by inserting paper liners or lightly greasing silicone moulds. Place 150g of steamed pumpkin flesh into a jug and add 30ml of sunflower oil and 20ml of whole milk. Use a stick blender on medium speed until you obtain a smooth, velvety purée with a vibrant orange hue. The consistency should be similar to a thick yoghurt, with no visible lumps. A professional tip: if your pumpkin is very watery, let it drain in a fine sieve for a few minutes before blending.
  3. In a large mixing bowl, carefully sift 100g of plain flour (type 0) along with 4g of baking powder using a fine-mesh sieve. Lift the sieve high above the bowl as you work to incorporate as much air as possible into the flour particles. This simple but essential technique will ensure your muffins are incredibly soft and light once baked. By the end, you should have a very fine, uniform powder ready for the wet ingredients. Remember that sifting the baking powder prevents bitter clumps from forming in the finished treat.
  4. Make a small well in the centre of the dry ingredients and gently pour in the previously prepared pumpkin purée. Using a silicone spatula or a wooden spoon, begin to mix with slow, circular motions, working from the centre outwards. Continue with gentle folding motions from the bottom upwards to avoid deflating the air incorporated during sifting. The mixture will start to form a thick, fragrant batter with a beautiful uniform colour. Avoid overworking the batter: stop as soon as the flour is no longer visible to prevent the gluten from toughening the muffins.
  5. Now add the finely chopped raisins to the batter, sprinkling them evenly over the surface. Stir once more with the spatula, using just two or three full folds to incorporate them homogeneously. The raisins must be well distributed so that every bite offers the same flavour experience. You will notice the batter maintains its structure without becoming too runny. My tip is to taste a tiny bit of the batter: if the pumpkin isn't very sweet, the raisins will perfectly balance the flavour without the need for added sugar.
  6. Use a spoon or a piping bag to transfer the batter into the muffin moulds, filling each liner to about two-thirds of its capacity. Do not fill them to the brim, as the baking powder will cause the muffins to rise and form the classic dome shape during baking. Lightly level the surface if necessary with the back of a damp teaspoon. You should aim to produce 4 medium-sized muffins of equal height. Carefully wipe any drips off the tin to prevent burnt smells in the oven.
  7. Place the muffins on the middle rack of the preheated oven and bake for approximately 15-18 minutes at 180°C. During this time, resist the urge to open the oven door for at least the first 12 minutes to ensure the rise isn't compromised. The muffins are ready when the tops appear slightly golden and feel springy to the touch. Perform the skewer test: insert a toothpick into the centre of a muffin and, if it comes out clean and dry, they are perfectly cooked. If the toothpick is still damp, bake for a further 2 or 3 minutes.
  8. Once out of the oven, let the muffins rest in the tin for just 2 minutes, then carefully remove them and place them on a wire cooling rack. This step is crucial as it allows the residual heat and moisture to evaporate correctly from all sides of the muffin. If left in the tin, the steam will make them soggy and rubbery at the base. Wait until they are completely cool before serving so the internal structure can set properly. For children, slice them in half or into small cubes to make eating safer and more enjoyable.
  9. Store any leftover muffins in an airtight container at room temperature for up to two days, or freeze them individually if you want to have them ready for another time. The pumpkin keeps the batter naturally moist, so they will stay soft for a while. If you want an extra touch of aroma, you can add a pinch of ground cinnamon to the flour in step 3. Always remember to check that the muffin's temperature is suitable before offering it to little ones.

Tips

  • Squeeze the raisins well after soaking to prevent excess moisture in the batter.
  • If the pumpkin is too watery after cooking, drain it in a fine-mesh sieve before mixing it with the flour.
  • Store the muffins in an airtight container to keep them moist for up to three days.

Variations

  • You can replace the raisins with dark chocolate chips for a richer version.
  • Add a pinch of cinnamon or nutmeg to the dry ingredients to further enhance the pumpkin flavor.
