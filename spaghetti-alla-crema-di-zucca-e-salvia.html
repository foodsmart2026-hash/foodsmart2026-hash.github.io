Spaghetti with Pumpkin and Sage Cream

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 20 min · Medium

An enveloping autumnal recipe where the pumpkin becomes a silky sauce that hugs the spaghetti. Sage fried in oil provides a persistent scent and a sophisticated aromatic note.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⬇️ Big saving🌱 Vegan🧒 Kid-friendly🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🇮🇹 Italian

An autumn pasta dish that celebrates the sweetness of Mantua or butternut squash enhanced by the scent of sage. The texture is velvety and enveloping, ideal for a simple yet refined meal. The spaghetti is tossed directly in the cream to capture every flavor.

Ingredients

  • Spaghetti320 g
  • Mantuan or butternut squash (Mantuan squash is a starchy, sweet Italian variety)500 g
  • Fresh sage6 leaves
  • Red onion1/2 pc
  • Vegetable stock200 ml

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the pumpkin (500 g): remove the skin with a sharp knife, scoop out the seeds and fibres, and dice the flesh into small, 1 cm cubes. Use a stable chopping board and a chef's knife to ensure precise, uniform pieces, which are essential for quick and even cooking. Consistency in size prevents some pieces from remaining hard while others overcook. By the end, you should have a bowl of vibrant orange cubes ready to release their natural sweetness. Chef's tip: cutting the pumpkin into very small dice shortens the cooking time and better preserves its brilliant colour.
  2. Finely dice the half red onion until the pieces are almost imperceptible. In a large frying pan, heat a drizzle of extra virgin olive oil and add the onion along with the 6 whole fresh sage leaves. Sauté over a gentle heat for about 4-5 minutes, stirring frequently with a wooden spoon to prevent the onion from browning or turning bitter. You want to achieve a translucent, fragrant, and lightly golden base, with the sage starting to release its aromatic oils. Chef's tip: sautéing over a very low heat allows the onion to soften gently, making it much easier to digest.
  3. Add the pumpkin cubes to the pan, season with salt and freshly cracked black pepper, then pour in the 200 ml of warm vegetable stock. Increase the heat to medium and cook with a lid on for approximately 15-20 minutes, stirring occasionally. The pumpkin needs to absorb the stock and become extremely tender, to the point where it collapses when pressed with a fork. The final result should be a thick, fragrant stew with an intense orange hue. Chef's tip: if the stock evaporates too quickly, add a splash of hot water to keep the environment moist and prevent sticking.
  4. Once the pumpkin is cooked, remove the sage leaves from the pan and transfer the contents into a high-sided container or a blender jug. Use an immersion blender at maximum speed, moving it vertically until you obtain a perfectly smooth, thick cream without any lumps. If the cream appears too dense, you can add a tablespoon of stock to adjust the consistency. The ideal texture should resemble a silky velouté that coats the back of a spoon. Chef's tip: pass the cream through a fine-mesh sieve if you desire an extremely refined, gourmet finish.
  5. Fill a large pot with about 4 litres of water, bring it to a rolling boil over high heat, and add rock salt only once the water has started boiling. Submerge the 320 g of spaghetti, ensuring they are completely covered, and stir immediately to prevent them from sticking together. Cook the pasta according to the package instructions, but be ready to drain it early. The water must maintain a constant boil to ensure the core of the spaghetti cooks evenly. Chef's tip: use a wide pot so the spaghetti have enough room to hydrate without clumping together.
  6. About 2 minutes before the ideal cooking time, drain the spaghetti, reserving at least two ladles of the starchy pasta water. It is crucial to collect the water at the very end, as it will be more concentrated and useful for binding the sauce. The pasta should still be quite firm to the bite—definitely 'al dente'—as it will finish cooking directly in the pan. The spaghetti will still look slightly pale, ready to absorb the flavours of the sauce. Chef's tip: never rinse pasta under cold water, as you would lose the starch necessary for creaminess.
  7. Transfer the pumpkin cream back into the previously used pan, turn the heat to medium-high, and add the drained spaghetti along with a ladle of the cooking water. Toss the pasta vigorously, moving the pan in a circular motion or using kitchen tongs to incorporate air and encourage the emulsion between the cream and the water. Continue for about 2 minutes until the liquid reduces into a glossy sauce that clings perfectly to every strand of spaghetti. You should see the pasta 'sliding' fluidly in the cream without looking dry. Chef's tip: this final 'mantecatura' is the secret to preventing the pumpkin cream from becoming separated or grainy.
  8. Turn off the heat and plate the spaghetti immediately by creating a nest in the centre of each bowl using a ladle and long tongs. Garnish each portion with a few fresh sage leaves or, for an extra touch, lightly toasted sage for added crunch. The dish should be served steaming hot, with a velvety texture and an inviting sweet and herbal aroma. Serve immediately to enjoy the perfect consistency of the cream before it sets. Chef's tip: add a final crack of black pepper to balance the natural sweetness of the pumpkin.

Tips

  • Sauté the red onion over low heat to prevent it from burning and ruining the bright orange color of the squash.
  • Keep a ladle of cooking water aside to adjust the cream's density before serving.
  • Remove the sage leaves before blending the squash to maintain uniform color and balanced flavor.

Variations

  • Add toasted pumpkin seeds at the end to give the dish a crunchy note.
  • You can enrich the initial sauté with a pinch of chili flakes to contrast the sweetness of the squash.
