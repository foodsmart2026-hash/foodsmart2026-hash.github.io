Maccheroncini with Cream of Cannellini Beans and Sweet Fennel

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

Cannellini beans are the most delicate of pulses. Combined with cooked fennel, they create a fragrant and digestible cream.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro🌱 Vegan🧒 Kid-friendly🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🇮🇹 Italian

Ingredients

  • Short pasta like maccheroncini (small macaroni)160 g
  • Cooked cannellini beans (canned or jarred)200 g
  • Large fennel bulb1 pc
  • Extra virgin olive oil20 ml
  • Fennel seeds (optional)1 pinch

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Begin by preparing the large fennel: remove the green stalks and base, discard the tough outer layers, and wash thoroughly under running water. Slice the fennel heart very thinly using a sharp knife or a mandoline to ensure quick and even cooking. This process takes about 5 minutes, resulting in a pile of fragrant, white fennel shavings. My chef's tip is to save the feathery green fronds to garnish the final dish, adding a pop of colour and a fresh herbal note.
  2. Place the fennel slices in a deep saucepan and add just enough cold water to barely cover them. Bring to a boil over medium heat, then lower the flame and simmer gently for about 12 minutes until the fennel appears translucent and very tender when pressed with a fork. Monitor the pot to ensure the water doesn't evaporate completely; add a tablespoon if necessary during cooking. The fennel should be melt-in-the-mouth soft, releasing a sweet and delicate aroma that forms the base of our cream.
  3. While the fennel is simmering, take the 200g of cooked cannellini beans and place them in a fine-mesh sieve. Rinse them thoroughly under cold running water to remove the canning liquid and any excess starch, moving them gently with your fingers to keep them intact. Let them drain well for a moment; this step is crucial for achieving a clean flavour in the final cream, free from any metallic or overly salty aftertaste. The beans should look glossy, firm, and ready to be pureed.
  4. Add the well-drained cannellini beans to the saucepan with the tender fennel, keeping the small amount of remaining cooking liquid. Use a hand blender, processing the mixture with vertical motions at medium-high speed until you reach a thick, smooth, and frothy consistency. This will take about 2-3 minutes, and you will notice the cream lightening in colour as it incorporates air. The chef recommends not adding too much water at the start, as you can always adjust the thickness later with a spoonful of water.
  5. To achieve a truly professional texture free from any tough fibres, pass the puree through a wide-mesh strainer or a food mill. Push the mixture through with the back of a spoon into a clean bowl, discarding any stringy bits of fennel that might interfere with the eating experience. You will be left with a silky, ivory-coloured cream with a balanced vegetal and buttery scent. Cover the bowl to keep the heat in while you proceed to cook the pasta.
  6. Fill a large pot with approximately 2 litres of water and bring to a vigorous boil over high heat. Following your nutritional guidelines, do not add salt to the water; this allows the natural sweetness of the vegetables to shine through. Once boiling, add the 160g of maccheroncini and stir immediately with a wooden spoon to prevent sticking. Cooking time will vary by brand, so follow the package instructions closely to ensure the pasta is cooked perfectly through.
  7. Drain the maccheroncini a minute before the end of the suggested time, ensuring the pasta is soft enough for easy chewing but still holds its shape. Before discarding the water, set aside a small cup of the starchy cooking water to adjust the sauce's fluidity if needed. Transfer the pasta directly into the bowl containing the warm cannellini and fennel cream. Stir thoroughly so that every piece of pasta is completely coated, ensuring an even distribution of flavours in every bite.
  8. Complete the dish by drizzling the 20ml of extra virgin olive oil over the top while still hot. If desired, add a pinch of fennel seeds that have been lightly crushed in a mortar to intensify the aromatic profile and add a gourmet touch. Stir one last time with care; the oil will emulsify the cream, making it glossy and even more velvety on the palate. Serve the maccheroncini immediately in shallow bowls, ensuring the consistency is moist and easy to swallow.
  9. The final result should be a delicate first course where the sweetness of the fennel blends perfectly with the creamy protein of the cannellini beans. The presentation should look clean, with the cream clinging to the maccheroncini without being watery at the bottom of the plate. For a professional finish, garnish with the reserved green fronds. Remember, the simplicity of these ingredients requires meticulous attention to texture to turn a daily recipe into a chef-standard meal.
