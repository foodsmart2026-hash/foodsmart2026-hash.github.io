Roasted Cauliflower with Turmeric and Black Pepper

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

A very simple preparation that transforms cauliflower into a tasty and visually inviting side dish thanks to its golden colour. Turmeric adds an exotic note without impacting the budget.

Roasted cauliflower is a modern preparation that turns this vegetable into a spicy and inviting bite. Turmeric provides an intense golden color, while black pepper defines its character in a balance of warm, spiced flavors.

Ingredients

  • White cauliflower800 g
  • Turmeric powder1 tsp
  • Garlic powder1/2 tsp

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Begin by preheating your oven to 200°C (fan-assisted), the ideal temperature to trigger the Maillard reaction and ensure a crispy finish. Take 800g of white cauliflower, carefully remove the outer leaves and the woody core, then break it into medium-small florets that are as uniform as possible. Use a sharp knife to make clean cuts, avoiding crumbling the delicate buds too much. The goal is to have a series of regular-sized bites that will cook simultaneously without uneven results. My chef's tip: don't discard the tender inner leaves; if thinly sliced, they can be roasted along with the florets for an extra crunchy texture.
  2. Wash the florets under cool running water to remove any dirt residue, then place them on a clean kitchen towel or absorbent paper. This step is crucial: pat them dry with extreme care until they feel perfectly dry to the touch. If the florets remain damp, the oven heat will create steam, causing the cauliflower to boil internally rather than roasting on the outside. This will leave you with a surface ready to hold the oil and spices perfectly. Remember: water is the ultimate enemy of oven-baked crispiness.
  3. In a small separate bowl, prepare the aromatic spice rub by combining 1 teaspoon of turmeric powder, 1/2 teaspoon of garlic powder, and a generous amount of freshly cracked black pepper. Stir the spices with a teaspoon to break up any turmeric clumps and create a uniform ochre powder. The black pepper isn't just for flavour; it is essential to activate the piperine, which aids in the absorption of curcumin. The scent of the mix should be intense and slightly pungent. A little secret: add a pinch of chilli flakes if you want a bolder kick of heat.
  4. Transfer the dry cauliflower into a very large mixing bowl and drizzle with 2 tablespoons of high-quality extra virgin olive oil. Move the bowl in a circular motion to distribute the oil, then sprinkle the previously prepared spice mix over the top like rain. The oil acts as a glue, allowing the turmeric and garlic to adhere perfectly to the porous surface of the florets. You should see the florets starting to turn a vibrant yellow. Do not add salt at this stage to prevent the cauliflower from releasing its moisture too early.
  5. Now move to the 'massage' phase: use your clean hands to toss the cauliflower, ensuring every single crevice of the florets is coated with a thin layer of oil and spices. This manual technique is superior to any spoon because it allows you to work the oil into the plant fibres, ensuring a millimetre-perfect distribution of flavour. Continue for about a minute until the colour is perfectly homogeneous across all pieces. The result will be uniformly golden and highly aromatic cauliflower. Using your hands also allows you to feel if any florets are too large and need further splitting.
  6. Line a large baking tray with baking parchment and spread the florets in a single layer, taking care to leave space between each piece. Never overlap the pieces: total hot air circulation is required to allow moisture to evaporate. If the tray is too small, it's better to use two rather than crowding the product. You will see that well-spaced florets start to sizzle as soon as they hit the heat. An orderly layout is the secret to a professional roast.
  7. Place the tray on the middle oven rack and bake for the first 12-15 minutes without opening the door to maintain a constant 200°C. After this time, briefly remove the tray and, using a steel spatula, gently turn the florets to expose the less browned parts to direct heat. You should notice the tips starting to take on an inviting golden-brown colour. Continue roasting for another 10 minutes, monitoring the colour closely through the glass. The scent of toasted spices will fill the kitchen, indicating that caramelisation is well underway.
  8. The cauliflower is ready when the tips appear slightly charred and the texture, when tested with a fork, is tender but still offers a slight resistance (al dente). Remove the tray from the oven and only now season with fine salt to taste, tossing one last time while the residual heat helps fix the salt crystals. Let it rest for 2-3 minutes before serving in a warm dish. This resting period allows the flavours to settle and the texture to stabilise. Final tip: add a squeeze of fresh lemon juice just before serving for a touch of acidity that perfectly balances the earthiness of the turmeric.

Tips

  • Ensure the cauliflower florets are completely dry before seasoning to achieve optimal roasting and avoid a soggy texture.
  • Distribute the spice mix by massaging the cauliflower well with your hands to evenly coat every single floret.
  • Roast the cauliflower in a single layer with enough space between pieces to promote air circulation and even browning.

Variations

  • Add some smoked paprika to the spice mix for a bolder, slightly woody and smoky aroma.
  • Replace the garlic powder with thin slices of fresh garlic for a more intense flavor and a rustic texture.
