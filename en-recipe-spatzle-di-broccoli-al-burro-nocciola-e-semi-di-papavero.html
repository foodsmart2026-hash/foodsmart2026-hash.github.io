Broccoli spätzle with brown butter and poppy seeds

🌿 Seasonal recipe

Trentino-Alto Adige · 4 servings · Prep 30 min · Cooking 15 min · Medium

Small Tyrolean dumplings made with a broccoli-based batter, served with brown butter and poppy seeds for a rich, comforting flavour.

Ingredients

  • Broccoli (trimmed weight)400g
  • Plain flour250g
  • Medium eggs2
  • Whole milk50ml
  • Butter40g
  • Poppy seeds1 tbsp
  • Nutmeg1 pinch
  • Grated Parmesan cheese30g
  • Saltto taste

Allergens

Gluten, Milk, Eggs

Method

  1. Start by bringing a large pot of salted water to the boil and cook 400g of broccoli florets for about 8-10 minutes over medium heat. The vegetables must be very tender when pierced with a fork to make the subsequent blending process easier. Once cooked, drain the broccoli thoroughly and let them steam for a moment to remove excess moisture. You will get softened, bright green florets ready to be pureed. My chef's tip is not to throw away all the cooking water; keep a ladleful aside, as you might need it to adjust the consistency of the cream.
  2. Transfer the cooked broccoli to a tall-sided container and use an immersion blender to process them until you achieve a perfectly smooth and thick cream. Work the mixture with vertical movements to incorporate as little air as possible, ensuring no whole pieces remain. The final consistency should be velvety and lump-free, similar to a soft mash. Let the cream cool slightly for a few minutes before proceeding with the other ingredients. A little chef's trick: if the pulp seems too watery, quickly sauté it in a pan for a couple of minutes to dry it out further.
  3. In a large bowl, crack 2 medium eggs and combine them with 50ml of whole milk, adding a pinch of salt and a generous grating of nutmeg. Use a hand whisk to beat the mixture vigorously for a couple of minutes until it appears light and slightly frothy. It is essential that the eggs are at room temperature to blend perfectly with the milk and spices. The result should be a homogeneous liquid with a spicy and inviting aroma. For a professional touch, always use freshly grated nutmeg to preserve all its aromatic intensity.
  4. Add the now lukewarm broccoli cream to the egg and milk mixture, stirring gently with a whisk or a spatula. Blend the two mixtures with constant circular movements until the colour becomes a uniform green and the base is fluid but consistent. Ensure there is no separation between the liquid part and the vegetable pulp. You should obtain a bright green, fragrant base ready to receive the flour. Remember not to add the cream while it is too hot to avoid prematurely cooking the eggs and ruining the dough's structure.
  5. Sift 250g of 00 flour directly into the bowl with the broccoli and egg mixture, incorporating it a little at a time with a wooden spoon or a spatula. Work the dough energetically until every trace of powder disappears and the mass becomes viscous and starts to pull away slightly from the sides of the bowl. The correct consistency is vital: it should fall from the spoon with effort, in a heavy manner, without being liquid or too hard. The result will be an elastic, dense dough with an intense green colour. Avoid overworking the dough once the flour is added to prevent the spätzle from becoming rubbery.
  6. Cover the bowl with a damp cloth or cling film and let the dough rest at room temperature for about 15 minutes. This step is essential to allow the gluten to relax and the flour to fully hydrate, ensuring perfect structure during cooking. While waiting, bring a new large pot of water to the boil and salt it adequately. You will notice that after resting, the dough will appear shinier and silkier to the touch. A professional secret: resting also helps to highlight the flavour of the broccoli and nutmeg within the dumplings.
  7. Position the specific spätzle maker (or a large-holed potato ricer) over the pot of boiling water and pour part of the dough into the hopper. Slide the device back and forth firmly, letting the small dumplings drop directly into the boiling water over high heat. As soon as the spätzle rise to the surface, usually after about a minute, scoop them out immediately with a slotted spoon to prevent overcooking. You should get small, irregular dumplings that are firm but soft to the bite. Cook them in batches to avoid lowering the water temperature too much and to prevent them from sticking together.
  8. In a very large frying pan, melt 40g of butter over medium-low heat until it begins to sizzle and change colour. Let it cook until it takes on an amber hue and releases an unmistakable toasted hazelnut aroma, typical of 'beurre noisette'. Be careful not to burn it: the transition from hazelnut colour to burnt happens in seconds. Once ready, add a tablespoon of poppy seeds which will release their aroma into the hot fat. The butter should appear clear and caramel-coloured, with the black seeds creating a beautiful visual contrast.
  9. Transfer the freshly drained spätzle directly into the pan with the brown butter and poppy seeds, slightly increasing the heat. Sauté the pasta for a minute, moving the pan with a firm wrist flick so that every dumpling is perfectly glazed by the fragrant butter. Turn off the heat and add 30g of grated Parmigiano Reggiano, stirring one last time to create a light, binding creaminess. The final result will be a glossy, fragrant dish balanced between the sweetness of the broccoli and the toasted note of the butter. My final tip: serve the dish very hot on preferably preheated plates to maximise the aromas.
