Bruschetta with Cannellini Beans, Rosemary and Cherry Tomatoes

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 10 min · Easy

A traditional Tuscan 'cucina povera' starter revisited to ensure a balanced intake of fibre and healthy fats. This recipe uses affordable ingredients always available in any supermarket to create a perfect contrast between the creaminess of the pulses and the crunch of wholemeal bread.

A rustic appetizer that enhances the simplicity of traditional ingredients. The creaminess of rosemary cannellini beans pairs perfectly with fresh cherry tomatoes and crunchy whole wheat bread.

Ingredients

  • Rustic wholemeal bread200 g
  • Canned cannellini beans240 g
  • Cherry tomatoes200 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Fresh rosemary2 sprigs
  • Garlic1 clove
  • Fine salt2 g
  • Black pepper1 pinch

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Begin by draining 240g of pre-cooked cannellini beans in a fine-mesh sieve, rinsing them thoroughly under cold running water. This step is crucial for removing the sodium-rich canning liquid and any surface impurities from the legumes. Let them drain well for a few minutes so they don't release too much moisture during the subsequent cooking process. You will be left with clean beans, neutral in flavour and ready to be seasoned. Chef's tip: handle the beans gently to avoid breaking the skins prematurely.
  2. Prepare a large non-stick frying pan and heat 10ml of extra virgin olive oil along with one crushed whole garlic clove and a sprig of fresh rosemary. Keep the heat low for about 2 minutes, allowing the gentle warmth to extract the essential oils from the aromatics without burning the oil's fats. Once the garlic is lightly golden and releases an intense aroma, the aromatic base is ready. The result should be a highly fragrant, clear oil. Chef's tip: if the garlic browns too quickly, move the pan away from the heat to drop the temperature instantly.
  3. Add the well-drained cannellini beans to the pan along with a single tablespoon of water to help create a little moisture. Let them infuse over medium heat for about 5 minutes, stirring gently with a wooden spoon to keep them intact. This process serves to soften the legume skins and allows the garlic and rosemary fragrance to penetrate the heart of the beans. You will notice the cannellini becoming glossy and slightly more tender. Chef's tip: do not add salt at this stage to prevent the bean skins from toughening.
  4. Turn off the heat and remove the garlic clove and rosemary sprig from the pan, setting the garlic aside for later. Using a fork, roughly mash about half of the beans directly on the bottom of the pan, then mix them back into the remaining whole beans. You will achieve a pleasant, rustic texture—a blend of velvety cream and the bite of whole beans. The mixture should be thick rather than watery, perfect for spreading. Chef's tip: avoid using a hand blender, as the irregular texture is the secret to a gourmet bruschetta.
  5. Thoroughly wash the 200g of cherry tomatoes, dry them, and cut them into small dice or quarters depending on their size. Transfer them to a bowl and season with a pinch of salt and the needles from the second rosemary sprig, which you should have finely chopped with a sharp knife beforehand. Let them rest for a few minutes so they release some of their tasty juice, which will help bind the ingredients on the bruschetta. The tomatoes will appear glossy and intensely fragrant. Chef's tip: dice the tomatoes very small so they remain stable on top of the bean cream.
  6. Take the 200g of rustic whole grain bread and slice it into 8 slices about 1cm thick, trying to keep them uniform in size. Heat a griddle or cast-iron pan over high heat and toast the bread for about 2-3 minutes per side until you see classic dark brown grill marks. The bread should become stiff and crispy on the outside while maintaining a slightly springy core to support the toppings. The smell of toasted grain will signal that it is perfectly cooked. Chef's tip: do not overlap slices in the pan to ensure even and rapid toasting.
  7. Retrieve the garlic clove that you previously cooked in the pan with the beans and rub it very gently over the rough surface of the still-warm toasted bread. Since the garlic is already cooked, its flavour will be much sweeter and more digestible than raw garlic, yet it will still leave a lingering aromatic note. The heat of the bread will help melt the garlic essence into the fibres of the crumb. You will get an elegantly scented base that isn't overpowering. Chef's tip: use light, circular motions so as not to oversaturate the bread with garlic.
  8. Evenly distribute the warm cannellini mixture over the 8 bread slices, starting from the centre and moving towards the edges. Use the back of a spoon to lightly press the bean cream onto the bread surface, ensuring the whole beans stay firmly in place. This step creates the protein-rich, creamy layer of the bruschetta, acting as a 'glue' for the subsequent topping. Each slice should be generously covered. Chef's tip: if the beans have cooled too much, reheat them for 30 seconds before spreading for an optimal thermal contrast with the bread.
  9. Complete each bruschetta by placing the rosemary-marinated tomatoes over the bean cream and finishing with a pinch of freshly ground black pepper. Finally, drizzle the remaining 20ml of extra virgin olive oil over everything, which will act as a natural flavour enhancer and provide precious antioxidants. The final result is a colourful, balanced dish rich in varying textures. Serve immediately to enjoy the crunch of the bread against the softness of the cannellini. Chef's tip: finishing with raw oil is vital; do not skimp on quality as it defines the final aromatic profile of the dish.

Tips

  • Toast the bread on a very hot griddle to create a firm base capable of supporting the topping.
  • Lightly rub the garlic on the warm bread slices for a more intense and lasting aroma.
  • Serve the bruschettas immediately to prevent the moisture from the tomatoes from softening the bread too much.

Variations

  • Add some chopped Taggiasca olives to the cherry tomatoes for a saltier and bolder flavor.
  • Replace cannellini with borlotti beans for a more full-bodied and rustic texture.
