Potato Gnocchi with Carrot and Ginger Sauce

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 20 min · Medium

A velvety and slightly spicy sauce that transforms classic gnocchi into a modern and vibrant dish. The cost is negligible and the flavour is surprising.

A vibrant first course that combines soft potato gnocchi with a silky carrot and ginger cream. The contrast between the vegetable's sweetness and the root's slightly spicy note creates a modern balance. The sesame garnish adds a pleasant crunchy component to every bite.

Ingredients

  • Fresh potato gnocchi (Italian potato dumplings)800 g
  • Carrots400 g
  • Fresh ginger15 g
  • Whole milk100 ml
  • White onion1/2 pcs
  • Extra virgin olive oil20 ml
  • Sesame seeds1 cucchiaio
  • Saltq.b.

Allergens

Gluten, Milk, Sesame

Method

  1. Begin by carefully peeling 400g of carrots and slicing them into very thin rounds, ensuring uniform thickness for even cooking. Finely chop half a white onion on a stable chopping board using a sharp chef's knife. This preliminary step takes about 5 minutes and should produce vegetables ready for stewing. The result must be a clean, fragrant dice without any overly large chunks. Chef's tip: slice the carrots on a diagonal to increase the surface area in contact with the heat, which speeds up the softening of the fibres.
  2. In a large saucepan, pour 20ml of extra virgin olive oil and heat over medium heat for about a minute. Add the chopped onion and the carrot rounds, letting them sauté gently for 4-5 minutes without browning. Stir constantly with a wooden spoon to distribute the heat evenly and activate the onion's aromas. The carrots should appear glossy and the onion should become slightly translucent and soft. Chef's tip: if you notice the onion starting to brown too quickly, add a tablespoon of hot water to lower the temperature and continue cooking gently.
  3. Pour enough hot water into the saucepan to completely cover the carrots and add a pinch of salt. Cover with a lid, lower the heat and simmer for about 15 minutes or until the carrots are very tender when pierced with a fork. Check the water level occasionally to ensure it doesn't dry out too much, maintaining a light and constant simmer. You will obtain stewed carrots with an intense orange colour, immersed in a flavourful cooking liquid. Chef's tip: do not discard the carrot cooking water, as it is rich in nutrients and essential flavour for adjusting the density of the sauce.
  4. While the carrots are cooking, prepare the fresh ginger by peeling about 15g of root with the back of a teaspoon to remove only the thin outer skin. Finely grate the flesh using a fine grater, making sure to collect any juice that comes out. Set the grated ginger aside in a small bowl ready to be added to the cream. The ginger's scent should be pungent and fresh. Chef's tip: grate the ginger directly over a bowl so as not to lose the essential oils, which are the most aromatic part of the root.
  5. Drain the carrots, keeping their cooking water aside, and transfer them to a blender beaker. Add 100ml of whole milk at room temperature and the grated ginger, then blend at maximum speed until you obtain a perfectly smooth and velvety cream. If the consistency is too thick, gradually add some of the reserved cooking water until you reach the desired fluidity. You should have a bright orange, glossy sauce free of lumps. Chef's tip: for a Michelin-star texture, pass the cream through a fine-mesh sieve (chinois) to remove any fibrous residue from the carrots or ginger.
  6. Transfer the resulting cream into a very wide frying pan, ideal for tossing the gnocchi later, and adjust the salt to your taste. Heat the sauce over very low heat to keep it warm, avoiding a strong boil so as not to alter the delicacy of the milk. Taste the sauce to check the balance between the sweetness of the carrots and the spicy note of the ginger. The sauce should appear creamy, warm, and coating. Chef's tip: if you want a touch of acidity to contrast the sweetness, add a few drops of lemon juice at the end of the sauce preparation.
  7. Bring a large pot with plenty of water to the boil and, only when it is boiling vigorously, add coarse salt. Carefully drop in the 800g of fresh potato gnocchi, avoiding overloading the pot so as not to drastically lower the water temperature. Keep the heat high and watch the gnocchi: they will be ready as soon as they float to the surface spontaneously, which will take about 2-3 minutes. The result will be soft gnocchi with the right consistency, ready for tossing. Chef's tip: remove the gnocchi with a slotted spoon as they surface, rather than draining them all at once in a colander to prevent them from getting squashed.
  8. Transfer the cooked gnocchi directly into the pan with the carrot and ginger sauce using the slotted spoon. Turn the heat to medium-low and gently combine with circular movements or by tossing the pan for about a minute, ensuring each gnocco is uniformly coated with the cream. If the sauce thickens too much, add a tablespoon of the gnocchi cooking water to maintain creaminess. You should see a perfectly bound dish where the sauce adheres well to the surface of the gnocchi. Chef's tip: do not stir too vigorously to avoid breaking the gnocchi, which are extremely delicate due to the moisture in the sauce.
  9. Plate immediately, distributing the gnocchi into four pre-warmed shallow bowls, making sure to coat the surface with the remaining sauce from the pan. Decorate each portion by sprinkling a tablespoon of sesame seeds (divided among the plates) to add a crunchy and visually pleasing note. Serve immediately while the dish releases its citrusy and spicy aroma. The final result is an elegant first course, balanced in flavour and vibrant in colour. Chef's tip: lightly toast the sesame seeds in a small non-stick pan for a few seconds before serving to fully release their toasted aroma.

Tips

  • Use fresh, firm ginger and peel it with the back of a teaspoon to avoid waste.
  • Blend the carrots with a bit of warm milk until the texture is perfectly smooth.
  • Lightly toast the sesame seeds in a pan before adding them to release their full aroma.

Variations

  • Try replacing the whole milk with an unsweetened plant-based drink for a lighter version.
  • You can add grated lime zest when serving to emphasize the ginger's freshness.
