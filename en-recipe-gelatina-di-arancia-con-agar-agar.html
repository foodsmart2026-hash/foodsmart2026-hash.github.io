Orange jelly with agar agar

Kid-friendly

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 5 min · Easy

A natural orange juice jelly thickened with agar agar, containing no artificial colours. Free from major allergens.

Ingredients

  • Freshly squeezed orange juice450 ml
  • Agar agar powder2 g
  • Sugar10 g
  • Water50 ml

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Start by pouring 2 g of agar agar powder into a small bowl containing 50 ml of room temperature water. Stir vigorously with a teaspoon to dissolve any lumps, ensuring the powder is completely hydrated. This preliminary step is essential to prevent the gelling agent from forming annoying lumps once added to the hot juice.
  2. In a large saucepan, combine 450 ml of strained orange juice and 10 g of granulated sugar. Heat the mixture over medium-low heat, stirring with a spoon until the sugar crystals have totally disappeared. The juice should become hot to the touch, but it should not reach a boil in this initial phase.
  3. When the juice is hot, pour the water and agar agar solution into the saucepan, scraping the edges of the bowl well. Use a hand whisk to incorporate the gelling agent into the orange juice with constant, circular motions. Thorough mixing ensures the final jelly has a perfectly smooth and uniform consistency in every portion.
  4. Slightly increase the heat and bring the mixture to a gentle, steady boil. Let it cook for exactly 2 minutes, continuing to stir gently to prevent the bottom from sticking. Agar agar, unlike traditional gelatin sheets, must reach boiling point to activate its thickening properties.
  5. Observe the liquid carefully during cooking: you will notice it becoming slightly glossier and the citrus scent becoming more intense. Do not worry if the mixture still seems very liquid on the stove; the gelatinous consistency will only begin to develop when the temperature drops below 40°C.
  6. Turn off the heat and immediately distribute the liquid into 4 individual molds, dividing it into equal parts. I recommend acting quickly because agar agar sets very fast and starts to solidify as soon as the temperature begins to fall. If you notice bubbles on the surface, lightly tap the mold on the counter to make them rise and pop.
  7. Let the molds rest at room temperature for about 10 minutes, in a cool place away from drafts. During this time, the jelly will begin to stabilize and the surface will become opaque and firmer. Avoid moving or shaking the molds during this critical phase to avoid compromising the formation of the dessert's internal structure.
  8. Transfer the portions to the refrigerator and let them set completely for at least 1 hour. The cold not only helps the jelly reach its final firmness but also makes the dessert extremely refreshing and pleasant on the palate. Proper cold aging will allow you to unmold the jelly without the risk of it breaking.
  9. Once the necessary time has passed, gently run the tip of a small knife along the inner edge of each mold to detach the jelly. Turn the dessert over onto a cutting board, cut it into regular cubes, and serve immediately. You will get a natural, elastic jelly with a vibrant orange flavor, perfect as a light end to a meal.
