'Sandy' Roast Potatoes with Sweet Paprika

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 30 min · Medium

A rustic and tempting version of roast potatoes, made crunchy by a quick coating that creates a savoury 'sand'. The paprika gives an amber colour and an enveloping scent.

These roasted potatoes feature a crunchy outer crust from the breadcrumbs and a soft center. The aroma of sweet paprika and rosemary makes them a flavorful side dish ideal for roasts or vegetarian meals.

Ingredients

  • Yellow-fleshed potatoes800 g
  • Breadcrumbs40 g
  • Sweet paprika10 g
  • Fresh rosemary2 sprigs
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 ml
  • Salt and pepperq.b.

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Begin by carefully peeling the 800g of yellow-fleshed potatoes, rinse them under cold running water, and cut them into even cubes of approximately 2cm. Maintaining a consistent size is essential so that every piece cooks uniformly without some staying raw or others falling apart. Use a sharp chef's knife and a stable chopping board to achieve clean, precise cuts. The final result should be a bowl of homogeneous cubes, ready to be transformed into crunchy bites. Chef's tip: do not cut the potatoes too small, otherwise they will lose their texture and become overly fragile during roasting.
  2. Bring a large pot of salted water to the boil and blanch the potato cubes for exactly 5 minutes from the moment the water returns to the boil. This technique par-cooks the interior and releases surface starches that will later help form a perfect crust. Keep the heat at medium to prevent violent boiling from breaking the corners of the cubes. You will obtain potatoes that are partially tender on the outside but still firm at the core. Chef's tip: add a tablespoon of white vinegar to the water to help the potatoes maintain their shape during this stage.
  3. Drain the potatoes very gently and transfer them to a wide bowl, letting them cool slightly for about 5-10 minutes so that residual moisture evaporates completely. The remaining heat must dry the surface of the cubes, a critical step as any moisture would prevent the breading from sticking correctly and ruin the crunch. Move them occasionally by lightly shaking the bowl to encourage air circulation. You will see the surface turn matte and slightly floury, a sign they are ready. Chef's tip: if you have time, spread them out on a clean tea towel to ensure they are perfectly dry before proceeding.
  4. While the potatoes are drying, prepare the aromatic breading by mixing 40g of breadcrumbs, 10g of sweet paprika, a pinch of pepper, and the two sprigs of rosemary (previously very finely chopped) in a small bowl. Use a fork to distribute the paprika into the breadcrumbs until you achieve a consistent, fragrant orange-coloured powder. This mixture is the soul of the dish, providing the spicy flavour and the characteristic 'sandy' texture. The scent of fresh rosemary will be released immediately upon contact with the other ingredients. Chef's tip: chop the rosemary at the very last moment to preserve all its volatile essential oils.
  5. Drizzle the now-dry potatoes with 40ml of extra-virgin olive oil, mixing with a wooden spoon or directly with your hands very gently. Ensure every single cube is perfectly coated in oil, which will act as a natural glue for our sandy coating. There should be no oil pooling at the bottom of the bowl, only a thin, glossy film on each potato piece. This step guarantees that the coating remains attached throughout the oven-roasting phase. Chef's tip: use high-quality extra-virgin olive oil, preferably light and fruity, so as not to overpower the sweet taste of the paprika.
  6. Pour the breadcrumb and spice mix over the oiled potatoes and shake the bowl with firm upward motions for about a minute. The powder must distribute evenly, creating a thin, grainy layer on every side of the cubes, just as if they had been rolled in sand. The 'tossing' technique in the bowl allows you to coat the par-boiled potatoes without breaking them. The result will be a rustic appearance and a vibrant colour provided by the paprika. Chef's tip: if you notice the breading isn't sticking well, add one extra teaspoon of oil and shake vigorously again.
  7. Prepare a large baking tray by lining it with baking parchment and spread out the sandy potatoes, taking care never to overlap them. It is essential that each cube has its own space so the hot air in the fan oven can circulate freely, ensuring an even golden finish rather than a soggy 'steamed' effect. If necessary, use two separate trays rather than crowding them into one. This orderly arrangement is the secret to a professional restaurant-style crunch. Chef's tip: place the potatoes with their flattest side in contact with the parchment for an even crispier base.
  8. Place in a preheated fan oven at 200°C and bake for about 20-25 minutes, making sure to turn the potatoes halfway through with a spatula. Visually monitor the colour: they should become intensely golden, with slightly darker edges and a texture that is visibly crunchy to the touch. The scent of toasted paprika and rosemary will start to fill the kitchen after the first 10 minutes. Once removed from the oven, add a final pinch of fine salt only if necessary before serving. Chef's tip: do not open the oven door too often to avoid dropping the temperature; only do so when you need to turn them.
  9. Remove the potatoes from the oven and let them rest for just one minute before transferring them to a serving dish, so the outer crust can further stabilise. Serve the potatoes piping hot, perhaps accompanied by a light yoghurt sauce or simply as a side dish for white meats or baked fish. The texture should present a sharp contrast between the creamy heart and the grainy, spicy surface. Every bite will be an explosion of flavour and crunch that will win over all your guests. Chef's tip: avoid covering the hot potatoes with a lid after cooking, as the released steam will make them soft in moments.

Tips

  • Dry the potatoes perfectly after parboiling to help the oil and breading adhere better.
  • Shake the tray halfway through cooking to achieve even browning on all sides of the cubes.
  • Serve the potatoes hot from the oven to preserve the crunchiness of the sandy crust.

Variations

  • Add garlic powder to the breading for a bolder and more rustic taste.
  • Replace fresh rosemary with dried oregano for a more Mediterranean flavor profile.
