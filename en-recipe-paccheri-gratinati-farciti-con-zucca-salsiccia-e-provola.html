Stuffed Paccheri Gratin with Pumpkin, Sausage and Provola

🌿 Seasonal recipe

Campania · 4 servings · Prep 30 min · Cooking 40 min · Medium

A stunning baked pasta dish where the pumpkin becomes a creamy filling that perfectly complements the bold flavour of the sausage.

Ingredients

  • Gragnano paccheri320g
  • Prepared Mantovana pumpkin600g
  • Pork sausage200g
  • Smoked provola cheese150g
  • Grated Parmigiano Reggiano60g
  • Brown onion1/2
  • Fresh rosemary1 sprig
  • Dry white wine50ml
  • Extra virgin olive oilas needed
  • Salt and pepperto taste

Allergens

Gluten, Milk

Method

  1. Start by preparing the base: finely chop half a yellow onion and cut 600g of cleaned Mantuan pumpkin into regular 1cm cubes. Use a very sharp knife to achieve uniform sizes, which ensures all pieces cook at the same rate. You should aim for clean, fragrant dice ready to be sautéed in the pan. Chef's tip: Choose Mantuan (or Kabocha) pumpkin because its firm, starchy flesh is perfect for a creamy filling that won't become watery.
  2. In a large frying pan, heat a drizzle of extra virgin olive oil and sweat the onion over a low heat for 3-4 minutes until translucent. Add the pumpkin and the rosemary sprig, pouring in a ladle of hot water to help the cooking process without burning the onion. Cover with a lid and cook for about 15 minutes over medium heat until the cubes are tender when pressed with a fork. Chef's tip: Do not salt the pumpkin immediately, otherwise it will release its juices too early and you risk boiling it rather than browning it.
  3. While the pumpkin finishes cooking, handle the protein: remove the casing from 200g of pork sausage and crumble it with your hands into small, irregular pieces. In another very hot pan, brown the sausage over a high heat for about 5-7 minutes until golden and crispy, then deglaze with 50ml of dry white wine, letting the alcohol evaporate completely. The result should be flavourful, well-browned meat with an inviting aroma of wine and spices. Chef's tip: Use a fork while browning to break up any lumps of meat and prevent large chunks from forming.
  4. Now create the base for your filling: remove the rosemary sprig and mash the cooked pumpkin with a fork directly in the pan until it becomes a coarse purée. Combine the browned sausage with the pumpkin cream, mixing the two ingredients well with the heat turned off to perfectly blend the flavours. You should obtain a thick, colourful, and fragrant mixture that holds its shape easily. Chef's tip: Absolutely avoid using a hand blender, as the 'rustic' texture provided by the fork makes the filling much more pleasant on the palate.
  5. Transfer the mixture to a bowl and, once cooled slightly, add 150g of smoked provola cheese cut into tiny cubes and about 30g of grated Parmigiano Reggiano. Season with salt and freshly ground black pepper, stirring carefully to evenly distribute the cheeses which will act as a binder. The filling should appear rich and compact, with the bits of provola ready to melt in the oven. Chef's tip: Cut the provola into very small cubes, almost minced, so it doesn't clog the piping bag nozzle during stuffing.
  6. Bring a large pot of salted water to the boil and drop in 320g of paccheri, cooking them for only half the time indicated on the packet (around 6-7 minutes). This brief pre-cooking is essential so the pasta maintains its cylindrical structure and can finish cooking in the oven without becoming mushy. Drain the paccheri when they are still very 'al dente', almost hard in the centre. Chef's tip: Add a drop of oil to the boiling water to prevent the paccheri from sticking to each other and potentially breaking.
  7. As soon as they are drained, run the paccheri under cold running water to instantly stop the cooking and set the starch. Gently transfer them to a large bowl and toss with a drizzle of extra virgin olive oil, using your hands to coat them completely. This step is the secret to handling the pasta easily without the paccheri sticking together while you stuff them. Chef's tip: Don't leave the paccheri in the colander for too long, as their weight will squash the ones at the bottom, causing them to tear.
  8. Place the pumpkin, sausage, and provola filling into a sturdy piping bag without a nozzle or with a very wide plain nozzle. Take one pacchero at a time and fill it generously starting from the bottom and moving upwards, ensuring no air pockets remain inside the tube. You should feel the pacchero become heavy and firm between your fingers as you complete the stuffing. Chef's tip: If you don't have a piping bag, use a long coffee spoon, but be careful not to force the walls of the pasta to avoid tearing them.
  9. Take a rectangular baking dish and grease the bottom and sides well with a drizzle of oil. Arrange the stuffed paccheri vertically, side by side, creating a sort of compact 'honeycomb' that fills the entire space. Sprinkle the surface with the remaining 30g of Parmigiano Reggiano and finish with a final swirl of extra virgin olive oil. Chef's tip: Pack the paccheri tightly together so they support each other, staying perfectly upright during the gratin process in the oven.
  10. Preheat the oven to 200°C (static mode) and bake the tray on the middle rack for about 15-20 minutes. In the last 5 minutes, switch to the grill function to achieve an amber gratin and an irresistible crust on the edges of the pasta. The dish is ready when you see the cheese sizzling and the surface turns a beautiful golden brown. Chef's tip: Let the paccheri rest outside the oven for 3 minutes before serving, so the filling settles and the flavours become more balanced.
