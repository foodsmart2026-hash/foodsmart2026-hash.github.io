Turkey Breast 'alla Pizzaiola'

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 20 min · Easy

An economical and succulent version of the classic pizzaiola, where the turkey breast is enhanced by a tomato sauce scented with oregano. Ideal for families, this recipe ensures very tender meat thanks to the light flouring technique and gentle cooking.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⚖️ Good value for money🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free

Turkey breast pizzaiola is a quick and flavorful main course, perfect for a fast family dinner. The meat stays tender thanks to a short simmer in tomato sauce, enhanced by Mediterranean aromas of olives and capers.

Ingredients

  • Sliced turkey breast600 g
  • Finely chopped tomatoes (polpa)400 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Pitted black olives50 g
  • Type 0 or wholemeal flour40 g
  • Salted capers15 g
  • Garlic2 cloves
  • Dried oregano5 g
  • Fine salt2 g
  • Black pepper1 g
  • Fresh parsley10 g

Allergens

Gluten

Method

  1. Start by preparing the turkey breast (600 g): place the slices on a cutting board and, if too thick, flatten them slightly with a meat mallet between two sheets of baking paper to make them uniform. Dredge each slice in the flour (40 g), pressing well with your hands, and shake firmly to remove the excess that could make the sauce too sticky. This operation should take place just before putting the meat in the pan to prevent the moisture of the turkey from making the flour too damp. You will get dry slices ready for browning. Chef's tip: use rice flour if you want an even more velvety sauce and a naturally gluten-free recipe.
  2. Take a large non-stick pan and pour the extra virgin olive oil (30 ml) together with the two peeled and crushed garlic cloves. Heat over medium heat for about 2 minutes, tilting the pan slightly so that the garlic is immersed in the oil and releases all its aroma without burning. When the garlic is slightly golden and the oil begins to smell intensely, you can decide whether to leave it for a bolder taste or remove it. The goal is to obtain an aromatic fat base perfect for sealing the meat.
  3. Place the floured turkey slices in the pan, taking care not to overlap them to allow even cooking. Slightly increase the heat and brown the meat for about 2-3 minutes per side, turning it with kitchen tongs instead of piercing it with a fork. You should get a golden and slightly crunchy surface, while the inside should remain partially raw so as not to dry out too much. The scent of the browned meat will indicate that the Maillard reaction has occurred correctly.
  4. Pour the tomato pulp (400 g) directly onto the turkey slices and distribute it evenly with a wooden spoon. Lower the heat to medium-low intensity and add a pinch of salt and pepper, taking into account the saltiness of the capers that you will add later. The tomato will start to simmer slowly, mixing with the cooking juices of the meat and the released flour, creating the base of the sauce. In this phase the tomato must completely envelop the meat without drowning it.
  5. Add the pitted black olives (50 g) cut into rings and the capers (15 g), which you have previously rinsed thoroughly under running water to remove excess salt, to the pan. Sprinkle everything with the dried oregano (5 g), rubbing it between the palms of your hands as you pour it to reactivate its essential oils and scent. Mix gently so as not to break the turkey slices. The sauce will start to take on the classic Mediterranean scent of pizzaiola, becoming darker and more inviting.
  6. Cover the pan with a lid and let it cook over low heat for about 10 minutes, turning the meat halfway through. If the sauce should reduce excessively becoming too thick, add 2-3 tablespoons of hot water to maintain the necessary moisture. The meat must finish cooking gently inside the sauce, becoming tender and juicy. At the end, the tomato should be shiny and well-bound, not watery. The secret is to maintain a constant temperature that allows a light simmer.
  7. Turn off the heat and add the finely chopped fresh parsley to give a note of colour and final freshness to the dish. Let the preparation rest in the covered pan for 2-3 minutes before serving; this short rest allows the meat fibres to redistribute the internal juices, ensuring superior softness. The sauce will stabilise further, becoming the perfect consistency to be mopped up with bread. Check the final savouriness and correct if necessary.
  8. Plate the turkey slices distributing them into individual plates and coating them abundantly with the pizzaiola sauce, making sure that each portion has a fair amount of olives and capers. Accompany the main course with a side dish of seasonal vegetables or toasted wholemeal bread for a complete meal. The result will be a home-cooked dish with an intense flavour, where the simplicity of large-scale distribution ingredients is transformed into nutritional excellence. Chef's tip: serve in warm plates to prevent the sauce from thickening too quickly during consumption.

Tips

  • Lightly flour the slices to achieve a thicker and more velvety sauce.
  • Thoroughly rinse the salted capers under running water before adding them to the pan.
  • Do not overcook the meat to prevent it from becoming too dry.

Variations

  • You can use veal slices instead of turkey for a more delicate flavor.
  • Add thinly sliced bell peppers along with the tomato pulp for a richer version.
