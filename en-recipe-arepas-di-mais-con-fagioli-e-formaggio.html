Corn Arepas with Beans and Cheese

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 20 min · Medium

Typical Venezuelan and Colombian corn flatbreads, naturally gluten-free and versatile. Here they are served with a budget-friendly filling of beans and melty cheese.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⬇️ Big saving🥕 Vegetarian🧒 Kid-friendly🌾 Gluten-free🌍 International

Traditional to Venezuelan and Colombian cuisine, arepas are corn cakes crispy on the outside and soft inside. This pairing with beans and melted cheese provides an irresistible contrast of textures, perfect for a casual dinner.

Ingredients

  • Pre-cooked cornmeal (type P.A.N.)300 g
  • Warm water450 ml
  • Pre-cooked black or kidney beans240 g
  • Edam-style or Mozzarella cheese150 g
  • Salt5 g
  • Vegetable oil20 ml

Allergens

Milk

Method

  1. PROCEDURE: In a large bowl, dissolve 5g of salt in 450ml of warm water. Gradually pour in 300g of pre-cooked corn flour, stirring initially with your fingertips to hydrate the flour evenly. Work the mixture with circular motions until every lump is eliminated. You will get a dough that is initially very soft and moist, almost grainy. CHEF'S TIP: Always add the flour to the water and not vice versa; this allows you to better control the corn's hydration and prevents dry flour pockets from forming.
  2. PROCEDURE: Knead vigorously with the palm of your hand for about 2 minutes, pressing the mixture against the sides of the bowl. Continue until you obtain a smooth, elastic, and compact mass that leaves no residue on your fingers. Cover the bowl with a damp cloth and let the dough rest for 5 minutes at room temperature. This step allows the corn fibres to fully absorb the liquids, ensuring flexibility for the discs. CHEF'S TIP: If the dough appears too dry or shows cracks after resting, add a tablespoon of warm water and knead for another minute.
  3. PROCEDURE: Divide the rested dough into 8 equal portions, weighing approximately 90-95g per ball if you prefer precision. Round each portion between your hands to form perfect spheres, then gently press them between your palms to obtain discs about 1.5 cm thick and 10 cm wide. Ensure the edges are smooth and crack-free, lightly moistening your fingers if necessary. The result should be a uniform and well-smoothed disc shape. CHEF'S TIP: Keep the portions you are not working on covered to prevent the air from drying out the surface, which could compromise cooking.
  4. PROCEDURE: Heat a non-stick griddle or a heavy-bottomed pan over medium heat and grease it with 10ml of vegetable oil using a brush or kitchen paper. Place the arepas on the hot surface and cook them undisturbed for about 5-7 minutes. During this phase, do not move them too much to allow a solid base to form. You should achieve a golden and crispy external crust, while the disc will start to detach easily from the bottom. CHEF'S TIP: If you have a cast-iron pan, use it: the even heat distribution ensures perfect browning without burning the corn.
  5. PROCEDURE: Use a spatula to flip the arepas and continue cooking on the second side for another 5-7 minutes over medium-low heat. The temperature should not be excessive to allow the heat to penetrate the centre of the dough, which must be cooked and not raw. At the end, the arepas will be firm to the touch and produce a dull, almost hollow sound when tapped on the surface. Maintain constant heat to avoid browning them too quickly. CHEF'S TIP: Do not rush; slow cooking ensures the heart of the arepa loses excess moisture, becoming soft and fragrant.
  6. PROCEDURE: While the arepas finish cooking, pour 240g of pre-cooked beans into a small saucepan with a tablespoon of water and heat over moderate heat for 4-5 minutes. Use a fork or a potato masher to reduce about half of them into a thick paste, leaving the rest whole for texture. Mix well to create a homogeneous cream that will serve as the base for the filling. The aroma of the legumes will intensify as they heat up. CHEF'S TIP: Mashing part of the beans is the key trick to ensuring the filling stays inside the arepa and doesn't slide out during the first bite.
  7. PROCEDURE: Take the still-hot arepas and, using a serrated knife, cut them horizontally for about two-thirds of their circumference to create a deep pocket. Fill each pocket with a generous tablespoon of the prepared bean cream and add about 18-20g of thinly sliced Edam or Mozzarella cheese. Gently close the top, pressing lightly to stabilise the content. You will see the cheese start to soften instantly upon contact with the internal heat. CHEF'S TIP: Protect your hand with a clean towel while cutting the arepas to avoid burns from escaping steam.
  8. PROCEDURE: Place the stuffed arepas back onto the hot griddle over low heat, brushing the surface with the remaining 10ml of oil. Let them heat for about 2 minutes per side, just enough time for the cheese to melt completely and become stringy. The outer crust will become even more crumbly and golden, releasing an intense aroma of toasted corn. Serve the arepas immediately, piping hot, to enjoy the contrast between the crispy shell and the melting heart. CHEF'S TIP: Do not overload the filling; an excess of beans or cheese would make it difficult to flip them on the griddle for the final step.

Tips

  • Let the dough rest for a few minutes to allow the corn flour to hydrate properly.
  • Cook the arepas over medium-low heat to prevent them from burning while remaining raw inside.
  • Split the arepas while still hot to help the cheese melt instantly inside them.

Variations

  • Replace black beans with red beans for a different color and a slightly sweeter flavor profile.
  • Try using mozzarella instead of edam for an even stretchier cheese filling.
