Baked Aubergine and Chickpea Flour Burgers

4 servings · Prep 45 min · Cooking 25 min · Hard

A plant-based alternative to classic burgers, very economical as it uses seasonal aubergines and legume flour as a natural binder.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro👍 Low price🌱 Vegan🧒 Kid-friendly🌾 Gluten-free🐄 Dairy-free🥛 Lactose-free🇮🇹 Italian

These vegetable burgers offer a Mediterranean flavor thanks to oregano and pan-sautéed eggplants. Chickpea flour acts as a natural binder, creating a texture that is firm on the outside and soft inside.

Ingredients

  • Aubergines800 g
  • Chickpea flour (Gram flour)120 g
  • Garlic1 spicchio
  • Fresh parsley1 mazzetto
  • Dried oregano5 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Salt and Pepperq.b.

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Begin by thoroughly washing 800g of eggplants and removing the stems, then cut them into uniform cubes of approximately 1 centimetre. Use a sharp knife and a stable chopping board to ensure all pieces are the same size, which guarantees even cooking. This step requires about 10 minutes of precise manual work. The final result should be a pile of clean cubes ready for the pan. Chef's tip: do not peel the eggplants, as the skin provides structure to the burger and contains most of the nutrients.
  2. Heat a large non-stick frying pan with 15ml of extra virgin olive oil and one slightly crushed garlic clove to infuse the oil. Add the eggplant cubes and a pinch of salt, cooking over medium heat for about 12-15 minutes while stirring frequently with a wooden spoon. The eggplants must soften completely, losing their initial volume and becoming very tender. You will know they are ready when they turn golden and the flesh feels almost creamy. Chef's tip: if the eggplants become too dry, add a tablespoon of water instead of more oil to keep them light.
  3. Remove the garlic and transfer the cooked eggplants to a chopping board or a large bowl to process them while still hot. Mash the pulp with the tines of a fork or chop it coarsely with a knife until you obtain a homogeneous but not overly smooth mixture. This transformation phase takes about 5 minutes of constant attention. You should achieve a thick pulp that still retains some small visible pieces to improve the texture of the finished burger. Chef's tip: let the pulp cool for 5 minutes before proceeding, so the flour doesn't create gummy lumps upon contact with excessive heat.
  4. Add the finely chopped fresh parsley and 5g of dried oregano to the eggplant pulp, then sift 120g of chickpea flour directly into the bowl. Mix vigorously using a spatula or your hands, gradually incorporating the flour so it absorbs the natural moisture of the vegetable. Work the dough for about 5 minutes until it appears visually uniform and fragrant. The result should be a compact, slightly sticky but mouldable mass. Chef's tip: sifting the chickpea flour is essential to prevent small pockets of raw flour from remaining inside the burgers.
  5. Cover the bowl with cling film and place the mixture in the refrigerator for at least 30 minutes at a temperature of about 4°C. This cold rest is crucial because it allows the eggplant fibres and chickpea flour proteins to bind stably. During this time, the mixture will firm up significantly, becoming much easier to handle. You will notice that after the rest, the mass no longer sticks excessively to your fingers. Chef's tip: never skip this step, otherwise the burgers will be too soft and risk falling apart during oven cooking.
  6. After the resting time, divide the mixture into 4 or 8 equal portions and form balls with slightly damp hands. Gently flatten each ball into a disc about 2 centimetres thick, carefully smoothing the edges to prevent cracks. It will take about 5-7 minutes to shape all the burgers so they are aesthetically pleasing and uniform. You should obtain compact discs ready for a light dusting. Chef's tip: keeping your hands slightly wet with cold water will prevent the dough from sticking to your palms, allowing you to achieve a smooth surface.
  7. Lightly dust each burger with a tiny bit of extra chickpea flour and arrange them on a baking tray previously lined with parchment paper. Take the remaining 15ml of extra virgin olive oil and brush the surface of each burger with gentle strokes. This finishing operation takes only a few minutes but is essential for the Maillard reaction in the oven. The burgers will appear slightly matte and ready for baking. Chef's tip: use a silicone brush to distribute the oil evenly, ensuring perfect browning without using excessive fat.
  8. Place the tray on the middle rack of the preheated oven at 200°C and bake for approximately 25 minutes using the static setting. Halfway through, after about 12 minutes, flip the burgers with a wide, flat spatula, being very careful not to break them. The constant heat will transform the outer surface into a crispy crust, while the inside remains soft yet firm. The result will be a burger with a dark amber colour and an intense aroma of toasted chickpeas. Chef's tip: if your oven has a fan setting, activate it for the last 3 minutes for extra crunchiness.
  9. Remove the burgers from the oven and let them rest on the tray for 2 to 3 minutes before serving or assembling your bun. This brief rest outside the oven allows the internal structure to fully stabilise, making the burger perfect for cutting. Serve them hot accompanied by a fresh tomato sauce or inside wholemeal bread with rocket leaves. You will find the texture is ideal: crispy on the outside and pleasantly melting on the inside. Chef's tip: avoid eating them straight out of the oven, as the flavour of the herbs is better released when the temperature drops slightly.

Tips

  • Follow the refrigeration rest time to allow the mixture to stabilize so the burgers don't fall apart.
  • If the mixture feels too wet, add an extra tablespoon of chickpea flour to reach the right consistency.
  • Lightly brush the burgers with olive oil before baking to achieve a golden, crispy crust.

Variations

  • Add some chopped desalted capers to the mix for an extra Mediterranean savory kick.
  • Replace the parsley with fresh basil for a typically summer-inspired aroma.
