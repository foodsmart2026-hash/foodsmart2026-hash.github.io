Savings

Saving time, energy and money in the kitchen

Optimising household resources through food science and smart organisation.

In an era characterised by a fast-paced lifestyle and a growing focus on sustainability, the kitchen becomes the ideal laboratory for applying efficiency strategies. Saving time, energy and money is not just an economic necessity, but an ethical and scientific choice. As food technologists, we know that kitchen efficiency comes from understanding the physical processes of heat and the rational organisation of workflows, in full compliance with food safety and current regulations, such as EU Regulation 1169/2011 on the transparency of consumer information.

Planning: the secret of Meal Prep

The first step in optimising budget and time is weekly menu planning, often referred to as 'Meal Prep'. Organising preparations into a single session allows you to make the most of appliance energy. For example, turning on the oven to cook multiple dishes simultaneously on different levels drastically reduces electricity consumption compared to repeated single sessions. However, it is essential to follow official health guidelines on temperature management: once cooked, food must be cooled quickly and stored in the refrigerator (below 4°C) in labelled airtight containers to prevent bacterial proliferation.

Low-energy cooking techniques

There are cooking techniques that allow gas or electricity consumption to be cut by exploiting thermal inertia. The most well-known is passive cooking, which is particularly effective for pasta: once boiling point is reached and the pasta is added, wait two minutes, turn off the heat and cover with a tight-fitting lid. The heat accumulated by the water is sufficient to complete the starch gelatinisation within the time indicated on the packet. Besides saving energy, this method better preserves the structure of the gluten.

To maximise daily efficiency, here are some practical tips:

  • Always use a lid: this reduces boiling times and energy consumption by up to 30%.
  • Prefer a pressure cooker: ideal for pulses and whole grains, it reduces cooking times by more than half.
  • Utilise residual heat: turn off the oven or electric hob a few minutes before the end of cooking.
  • Size the flame: the diameter of the flame should never exceed the bottom of the pan to avoid unnecessary heat loss.
  • Cut food into small, uniform pieces to speed up heat exchange.

Pantry management and smart storage

Saving money inevitably involves the fight against food waste. A pantry organised according to the FIFO (First-In, First-Out) method ensures that products with the closest expiry dates are used first. It is important to distinguish between the 'Use-by date' (a strict limit for safety) and the 'Best-before date' (indicated by 'best before'), after which the product can still be consumed if its sensory properties are intact. Storing dry products correctly in cool, dry places, away from light, preserves nutritional properties and prevents the oxidation of fats, avoiding the need to throw away perfectly good food.

The technological efficiency of appliances

Technology also comes to our aid. The microwave oven, for example, is one of the most efficient tools for heating or cooking small portions, as it acts directly on the food's water molecules without dispersing heat into the surrounding environment. Similarly, modern air fryers, being ultra-fast small-scale convection ovens, reach operating temperature in a very short time, cutting consumption compared to traditional built-in ovens. Keeping appliances clean (such as refrigerator coils or the bottom of the oven) ensures optimal performance and extends their service life, protecting the initial financial investment.

Conclusions

Adopting a smart approach in the kitchen means combining gastronomic wisdom with scientific precision. Through small changes in daily habits — from the choice of cooking method to rational shopping management — it is possible to make a significant impact on the household budget and the environment, without ever sacrificing the pleasure and safety of a good home-cooked meal.
