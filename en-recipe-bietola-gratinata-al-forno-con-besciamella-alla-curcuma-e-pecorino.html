Baked Swiss Chard Gratin with Turmeric Béchamel and Pecorino

🌿 Seasonal recipe

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 25 min · Easy

A rich and comforting version of Swiss chard, where the leaves and stalks are coated in a spiced sauce and baked until golden brown.

Ingredients

  • Swiss chard1kg
  • Whole milk500ml
  • Butter40g
  • Plain flour40g
  • Turmeric powder1 tsp
  • Grated Pecorino Romano50g
  • Breadcrumbs2 tbsp
  • Salt and pepperto taste

Allergens

Gluten, Milk

Method

  1. Begin by carefully cleaning 1 kg of Swiss chard, using a knife to separate the green leaves from the tougher white stalks. Cut both the stalks and the leaves into pieces of about 4-5 cm and wash them thoroughly under fresh running water. This step is vital because the stalks require a longer cooking time than the tender leaves. The final result should be clean vegetables, uniformly divided to ensure even cooking. My chef's tip: use a sharp knife to avoid crushing the fibres of the stalks, keeping their structure intact.
  2. Prepare a steamer and, once the water reaches a boil, place the stalks in first for an initial cooking time of 5 minutes over medium heat. After this time, add the green leaves to the same basket and continue steaming for another 3 minutes, covering with the lid. The vegetables should be tender when pierced with a fork but still have a beautiful, vivid colour, not overly mushy. My chef's tip: do not overcook at this stage, as the chard will undergo a second cooking in the oven to finish its texture.
  3. Once cooked, drain the chard and let it cool slightly in a colander, then press them very gently using the back of a spoon to remove excess water. This step is crucial to prevent the vegetables from releasing liquid during the gratin process, which would make your bechamel too watery and thin. You should obtain vegetables that are well-drained and compact to the touch. My chef's tip: if you want a professional result, wrap the chard in a clean tea towel and squeeze lightly to remove every trace of residual moisture.
  4. Focus on the bechamel base by melting 40g of butter in a small saucepan over very low heat to prevent it from browning. When the butter is melted and just sizzling, pour in 40g of plain flour all at once and stir vigorously with a hand whisk for about a minute. You will get a thick, golden paste called a 'roux', which will serve as the structural backbone for your sauce. My chef's tip: don't rush, let the flour cook in the butter until you smell a slight biscuit aroma, a sign that the starch is ready to thicken.
  5. Pour 500ml of room-temperature whole milk in a steady stream over the roux, continuing to whisk constantly to instantly dissolve any lumps. Increase the heat to medium and bring the mixture to a gentle boil, continuing to work it until the sauce starts to coat the back of a spoon with a thick consistency. The result must be a smooth, velvety cream, absolutely free of imperfections. My chef's tip: pour the milk very slowly at first, creating a smooth paste before gradually thinning it for total control over the density.
  6. Turn off the heat and enrich your bechamel by adding 1 teaspoon of turmeric powder, a pinch of salt, ground black pepper, and 25g of grated Pecorino Romano (half the total amount). Mix well until the sauce takes on an intense golden-yellow colour and an inviting spicy aroma reminiscent of the East. Always taste the sauce to adjust the salt, keeping in mind that the Pecorino will already add a significant savoury note. My chef's tip: always add the turmeric with the heat off to best preserve its delicate aroma and nutritional properties.
  7. Take a medium-sized baking dish, lightly grease it with a thin layer of butter, and arrange the drained chard inside, distributing it evenly. Ensure that the stalks and leaves are well mixed across the entire surface of the dish to guarantee a balanced bite for every guest. The dish should look well-filled but tidy, ready to receive the dressing. My chef's tip: sprinkle a pinch of pepper directly onto the vegetables before covering them with the sauce to enhance the flavour of the chard itself.
  8. Pour the turmeric bechamel over the chard, levelling it with a spatula or the back of a spoon to cover every gap between the vegetables. Evenly sprinkle the surface with the remaining 25g of Pecorino Romano and the 2 tablespoons of breadcrumbs, creating a thin, uniform layer. This mix of cheese and breadcrumbs will be the secret to an irresistible crust. My chef's tip: do not overdo the breadcrumbs; a light dusting is enough to keep the dish light and allow the bechamel to stay soft under the crust.
  9. Place the dish in a preheated oven at 200°C using the fan setting and bake for about 15 minutes on the middle shelf. In the final 2 minutes of cooking, activate the grill function at maximum power to achieve an amber browning and a pleasantly crunchy surface. You should see golden bubbles around the edges and smell the intense aroma of toasted cheese. My chef's tip: watch the oven constantly during the grilling phase, as the intense heat can burn the breadcrumbs in just a few seconds.
