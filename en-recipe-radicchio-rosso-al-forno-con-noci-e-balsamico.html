Baked Red Radicchio with Walnuts and Balsamic

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 12 min · Easy

The heat of the oven softens the bitterness of the radicchio, making it almost sweet. The crunchiness of the walnuts and the acidity of the balsamic vinegar create an elegant and affordable pairing.

Baked red radicchio acquires a pleasant toasted note that softens its natural bitterness. Balsamic vinegar and walnuts add sweet-and-sour complexity and a crunchy texture.

Ingredients

  • Red radicchio (Trevigiano or round variety - a bitter Italian leaf vegetable)2 heads
  • Walnut halves40 g
  • Balsamic Vinegar of Modena30 ml
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Salt and pepperto taste

Allergens

Tree nuts

Method

  1. Start by carefully washing 2 heads of red radicchio under cold running water, taking care not to damage the outer leaves. Dry them meticulously with a clean tea towel or kitchen paper, as any residual moisture will create steam and prevent proper roasting. Cut each head lengthways into four equal wedges, ensuring you keep a portion of the base attached so the leaves don't fall apart during cooking. This will give you 8 compact, clean wedges. My advice is not to remove too much of the base: it is the sweetest part that balances the natural bitterness of the leaves.
  2. Preheat your oven to 200°C (fan oven 180°C) and prepare a large baking tray by lining it with parchment paper. Arrange the radicchio wedges on the tray, making sure to space them out well and not overlap them, so the heat can circulate freely around each piece. This tidy arrangement ensures even cooking for both the tough core and the delicate tips. You should see a vibrant spread of deep red ready for seasoning. The chef's tip: if you crowd the radicchio, it will end up boiling in its own juices rather than roasting correctly.
  3. Season the radicchio by drizzling 30ml of extra virgin olive oil evenly over all the wedges. Add a pinch of salt and a good grind of fresh black pepper, then gently massage the leaves with your hands to work the seasoning into the inner folds. This manual step ensures every fibre is protected from the heat and flavoured consistently. The radicchio will appear glossy and fragrant, with salt crystals visible on the surface. A little trick: use a light, fruity oil so as not to overpower the characteristic taste of the Trevigiano variety.
  4. Place the tray on the middle shelf of the preheated oven and set the timer for the first 6 minutes of cooking. During this phase, the leaves will begin to wilt and release their toasted aroma. Keep an eye on the tips to ensure they don't darken too quickly, as they are the most sensitive part to intense heat. You will notice the red colour shifting towards a deeper, brownish hue while the edges start to crisp up. The chef's tip is to keep the oven door closed to maintain the heat required for the Maillard reaction.
  5. Once the first few minutes have passed, partially slide out the tray and turn each radicchio wedge using kitchen tongs or two forks. This operation must be done very gently to avoid breaking the structure of the wedges, which have now become tender and fragile. By turning them, you allow the heat from the bottom of the tray to act evenly on the opposite side, ensuring a consistent texture in every bite. The radicchio should be softened but still hold its original shape. Be careful of the hot steam that will escape the tray as you move it.
  6. Return to the oven for another 5-6 minutes, keeping the temperature steady at 200°C, until the base feels tender when poked. The total cooking time should not exceed 12 minutes in total to prevent the vegetable from losing too much water and becoming overly mushy. The final result will be wilted radicchio with browned edges and a base that is easily pierced with a fork. Don't worry if the tips look slightly dark: that 'charred' touch adds a delicious smoky note that contrasts with the residual sweetness.
  7. Immediately remove the radicchio from the oven and, while it is still piping hot on the tray, drizzle it uniformly with 30ml of Modena balsamic vinegar. The residual heat from the vegetables and the tray will instantly evaporate the watery part of the vinegar, turning it into a thick, glossy emulsion. You will smell an irresistible sweet-and-sour aroma enveloping the radicchio leaves. The result is a fascinating visual contrast between the dark purple and the brown glaze of the vinegar. Chef's tip: use a good quality, thick vinegar for a more persistent natural glaze.
  8. Take 40g of walnut halves and crumble them coarsely with your hands directly over the hot radicchio before plating. Distributing the walnuts at the very last moment preserves their essential oils and natural crunchiness, preventing them from softening against the moisture of the vegetable. The dish now offers a wonderful variety of textures: the softness of the radicchio, the creaminess of the vinegar, and the crunch of the nuts. Serve the dish immediately while hot to fully appreciate the harmony of flavours. The final secret: don't chop the nuts too finely; larger pieces provide a more pleasant contrast on the palate.

Tips

  • Cut the radicchio into even wedges, keeping part of the core to prevent the leaves from falling apart.
  • Distribute the oil evenly between the leaves to ensure perfect and uniform browning in the oven.
  • Add the balsamic vinegar only in the final minutes of cooking to prevent the flavor from becoming too acrid.

Variations

  • Replace the walnut halves with crushed toasted hazelnuts for a rounder flavor.
  • You can use a thicker balsamic vinegar glaze at the end of cooking for a more elegant presentation.
