Short Pasta with Sweet Aubergine Sauce and Mozzarella

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 15 min · Easy

By using only the flesh of the aubergine and peeling it carefully, you obtain a soft sauce free from bitter parts or pieces that are difficult to chew.

🌿 Seasonal recipe💰 Save Euro⬇️ Big saving🥕 Vegetarian🧒 Kid-friendly🇮🇹 Italian

A simple and tasty Mediterranean first course, characterized by the contrast between the sweetness of peeled eggplants and stringy mozzarella. Short pasta perfectly captures the creamy tomato sauce.

Ingredients

  • Mezze penne or small fusilli160 g
  • Medium aubergine1 pc
  • Sweet tomato passata (sieved tomatoes)150 ml
  • Pizza mozzarella (low-moisture variety)60 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil15 ml

Allergens

Gluten, Milk

Method

  1. Begin by carefully peeling the eggplant with a vegetable peeler, removing every trace of the dark skin to make the flesh more digestible. Slice the flesh into rounds, then into strips, and finally into tiny cubes about 5 mm in size. This precise dicing ensures the eggplant melts away during cooking, resulting in a velvety and smooth sauce. The chef's secret lies in the accuracy of your cutting: the smaller the cubes, the more the final texture will be child-friendly as it integrates perfectly with the tomato.
  2. Place a large non-stick frying pan over the hob and add your freshly diced eggplant along with 150 ml of sweet tomato passata. Pour in half a glass of water (approx. 100 ml) to help the vegetables stew gently and prevent the tomato from thickening too quickly. Stir well with a wooden spoon to ensure the tomato is evenly distributed throughout the eggplant cubes. Avoid adding any fats at this stage to keep the eggplant’s flavour pure and the preparation extremely light.
  3. Set the heat to low and let the sauce simmer for about 15 minutes, ensuring the pan is covered with a lid. During this time, keep an eye on the moisture levels; if the liquid evaporates too much, simply add a tiny splash more water to maintain humidity. You are looking for the eggplant to become incredibly soft, almost indistinguishable from the tomato passata. The ideal result is a thick, fragrant sauce with no firm chunks, perfect for coating short pasta.
  4. While the sauce finishes cooking, fill a large pot with plenty of water and bring it to a rolling boil over a high flame. Remember not to add any salt to the cooking water, following the guidelines for a balanced, low-sodium child's diet. Once the water is boiling vigorously, add the 160 g of mezze penne or small fusilli. Stir immediately with a large fork to prevent the pasta from sticking to the bottom and ensure it remains fully submerged.
  5. Cook the pasta according to the packet instructions, usually about 8-10 minutes, maintaining a steady but gentle boil. Test the pasta by tasting a piece: it should be tender to facilitate chewing but still hold its shape. Meanwhile, take 60 g of low-moisture "pizza" mozzarella and dice it into microscopic cubes or grate it using a coarse grater. This step is crucial so that the cheese melts instantly upon contact with the heat of the pasta.
  6. Drain the pasta carefully, reserving a tablespoon of the cooking water, and transfer it directly into the pan with the eggplant sauce. Increase the heat slightly to medium and toss the pasta for about one minute, stirring energetically so the sauce penetrates the grooves of the pasta. This 'mantecatura' technique allows the pasta starches to bond perfectly with the creamy condiment. You will achieve glossy pasta, beautifully orange-red in colour and completely enveloped in the sauce.
  7. Turn off the heat, move the pan away from the burner, and sprinkle the mozzarella cubes evenly over the top of the pasta. Cover immediately with a lid and let it sit undisturbed for about 30 seconds without further stirring. This brief rest uses latent heat to melt the mozzarella so it becomes slightly stringy without forming rubbery lumps. You will notice the cheese beginning to melt gently, creating small, creamy white spots throughout the pasta.
  8. Remove the lid and complete the dish by drizzling 15 ml of extra virgin olive oil over the top, giving it one final stir to emulsify everything. Serve the portions immediately, ensuring the mozzarella pieces are well distributed and ready to be eaten safely. Adding the oil at the very end preserves all its vitamin properties and provides a fresh aroma and inviting shine. My final touch is to serve the dish warm to best highlight the natural sweetness of the eggplant.

Tips

  • Peel the eggplant completely to achieve a velvety sauce texture without any bitter notes.
  • Use low-moisture mozzarella as it releases less water when mixed with the hot pasta.
  • Always save some cooking water to better emulsify the pasta with the sauce.

Variations

  • Add a few fresh basil leaves at the end for a typically summer aroma.
  • You can replace mozzarella with grated salted ricotta for a bolder flavor.
