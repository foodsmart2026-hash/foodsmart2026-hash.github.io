Passatelli in broth

Emilia-Romagna · 4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 5 min · Easy

Small cylinders of breadcrumbs, eggs and Parmesan cooked in broth, typical of the Romagna region.

🇮🇹 Italian

A classic from Romagna, passatelli offer a rough texture and an intense scent of citrus and spices. Perfect for festive gatherings, they melt in your mouth, releasing the bold flavor of Parmigiano Reggiano.

Ingredients

  • Breadcrumbs120 g
  • Grated Parmesan Reggiano120 g
  • Eggs150 g
  • Lemon zest2 g
  • Nutmeg0.5 g
  • Meat broth1500 ml

Allergens

Gluten, Milk, Eggs
May contain traces of: Celery.

Method

  1. Start by preparing the dry base: in a large bowl, combine the 120 g of breadcrumbs with the 120 g of grated Parmigiano Reggiano. Add the 2 g of finely grated lemon zest and the 0.5 g of nutmeg to fragrance the mixture. Mix well with a fork so that the aromas are evenly distributed; this will prevent having overly flavored or unbalanced spots.
  2. Create a small well in the center of the dry ingredients and pour in the 150 g of lightly beaten eggs. Start incorporating the dry ingredients from the center, gradually bringing the breadcrumbs and cheese toward the liquid heart. At this stage, you will notice the mixture starting to become grainy, moist, and very aromatic.
  3. Work the mixture vigorously with your hands until you obtain a smooth, firm, and not too sticky dough ball. If you press it with a finger, the dough should feel elastic yet compact; a good dough is the secret to preventing the passatelli from falling apart during cooking. Do not work it for too long to avoid overheating the fat contained in the Parmigiano.
  4. Wrap the dough in a clean cloth or leave it in the covered bowl and place it in the refrigerator to rest for about 20 minutes. This step is essential so that the breadcrumbs properly absorb the moisture from the eggs, stabilizing the structure of the passatelli. During the rest, the spicy hints of nutmeg and the freshness of the lemon will meld perfectly.
  5. While the dough rests, pour the 1500 ml of meat broth into a large pot and bring it to a boil over medium heat. Ensure the broth is very hot, as the high temperature is crucial for the instant sealing of the pasta. When you see the first vigorous bubbles, maintain a moderate flame to ensure a constant but gentle boil.
  6. Take the dough from the refrigerator and cut it into small pieces, placing them a few at a time into a large-hole potato ricer. Position yourself directly over the pot of boiling broth and squeeze firmly, letting the small cylinders, about 4-5 cm long, fall directly into the liquid. Use a knife to detach the passatelli from the bottom of the tool if they do not fall on their own.
  7. Let the passatelli cook in the boiling broth for about 2 minutes, avoiding excessive stirring to prevent breaking them in the initial phase. You will know they are ready when they gently rise to the surface, just like small gnocchi, reaching a tender consistency with a still-perceptible core. The intense aroma of the cooked cheese will be released immediately.
  8. Turn off the heat as soon as all the passatelli have surfaced and let them rest in the broth for just one minute before plating. Serve the 4 portions in deep plates, distributing the cylinders and the flavorful boiling meat broth equally. This dish should be enjoyed immediately to best appreciate the typical melt-in-your-mouth texture of the Romagnolo dough.

Tips

  • Let the dough rest in the fridge to make it firmer and easier to shape.
  • Use a potato ricer with large holes to achieve the traditional shape effortlessly.
  • Serve them immediately as soon as they float to the surface in the boiling broth to maintain texture.

Variations

  • You can serve them dry with a fish sauce or porcini mushroom sauce.
  • Replace the meat broth with a light vegetable broth for a more delicate version.
