Baked gratin anchovies

4 servings · Prep 20 min · Cooking 12 min · Medium

A humble main dish very rich in Omega-3, typical of the Italian maritime tradition, made crispy with a wholemeal aromatic panure. The combination of oily fish and cherry tomatoes creates a perfect balance between savouriness and freshness, ideal for a healthy and affordable dinner.

A main course that encapsulates Mediterranean flavors, where the saltiness of anchovies meets the sweetness of cherry tomatoes. The whole wheat gratin creates a fragrant crust that protects the tenderness of the oily fish.

Ingredients

  • Fresh anchovies (cleaned and butterflied)600 g
  • Wholemeal breadcrumbs100 g
  • Cherry tomatoes200 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil40 ml
  • Garlic clove1 pz
  • Fresh parsley20 g
  • Dried oregano2 g
  • Fine salt3 g
  • Lemon (grated zest)1 pz

Allergens

Fish, Gluten

Method

  1. Begin by gently rinsing 600g of cleaned, butterflied anchovies under a slow stream of cold running water to remove any residues. Place them on a tray lined with kitchen paper and pat them dry thoroughly to eliminate every trace of surface moisture. This step is crucial: well-dried fish allows the breading to adhere perfectly without becoming soggy during baking. The final result should be a clean, dry fillet ready to absorb the Mediterranean aromas. My professional advice is to handle them with extreme care to avoid breaking the delicate flesh of the oily fish.
  2. Prepare the aromatic base by finely chopping one garlic clove (with the germ removed) and 20g of well-washed fresh parsley. In a large mixing bowl, combine these herbs with 100g of wholemeal breadcrumbs and the grated zest of one unwaxed lemon, ensuring you don't include the bitter white pith. Mix the dry ingredients with a fork to evenly distribute the essential oils from the lemon and parsley. You will obtain a highly fragrant mixture with vibrant flecks of green and yellow. Remember that lemon zest is the secret to balancing the natural saltiness of the anchovies.
  3. Transform the breadcrumbs into a 'wet sand' texture by adding 20ml of extra virgin olive oil, 2g of dried oregano, and a small portion of the 3g total salt to the bowl. Work the mixture with your fingertips, rubbing the breadcrumbs with the oil until they are uniformly moistened but still crumbly. This technique prevents the bread from burning prematurely in the oven and ensures a golden, crunchy crust. The correct consistency is reached when the breadcrumbs slightly clump together when pressed. Do not overdo the oil at this stage to keep the gratin light and airy.
  4. Wash 200g of cherry tomatoes and carefully cut them into quarters using a serrated knife for precision. Collect them in a small bowl and season with a drizzle of oil (about 5ml) and a pinch of salt, tossing them to encourage the release of their natural juices. Let them rest for a few minutes while you prepare the baking tray; this small step creates a moist and flavourful cooking base. The tomatoes should appear glossy and slightly watery. The released juice will act as a natural braising liquid for the base of the anchovies, preventing them from drying out.
  5. Preheat your oven to 200°C (fan-assisted 180°C) and prepare a large baking tray by lining it with parchment paper. Lightly grease the bottom of the paper with 5ml of extra virgin olive oil, spreading it evenly with a pastry brush or the back of a spoon. This creates a non-stick barrier and helps the bottom of the fish cook evenly without sticking to the paper. The tray must be large enough to hold all the anchovies without excessive overlapping. Proper tray preparation is the first essential step towards achieving an impeccable bake.
  6. Arrange the butterflied anchovies on the tray, skin-side down, positioning them side by side in an orderly fashion. Try not to leave too many empty gaps, but avoid creating multiple layers, as heat must circulate freely around each fillet. You will see the silvery reflections of the fish starting to cover almost the entire bottom of the tray. This mosaic-like arrangement ensures that every portion receives the same amount of heat and seasoning. The chef's tip is to point all the tails in the same direction for a professional aesthetic finish.
  7. Distribute the 200g of prepared cherry tomatoes over and in the spaces between the anchovies, aiming for an even spread. Ensure that every area of the tray also receives some of the juices released by the tomatoes to maintain hydration during the cooking process. The vibrant red of the tomatoes against the silver of the anchovies makes the dish visually appealing even before it enters the oven. This vegetable layer provides the necessary acidity to cut through the richness of the oily fish. Do not drain the tomatoes: their juice is precious for the final flavour.
  8. Cover the entire surface with the prepared aromatic breadcrumbs, using a spoon to sprinkle them like rain over each fish fillet. Once the breadcrumbs are used up, press very lightly with the palm of your hand to help the crumbs adhere to the moist flesh of the anchovies. Finish by drizzling the remaining 10ml of extra virgin olive oil over the top to promote browning. The fish should be almost completely hidden under this golden, fragrant mantle. Ensure the breading is spread evenly to avoid uncovered areas that might dry out too much.
  9. Place the tray on the middle rack of the preheated oven at 200°C and bake for approximately 10-12 minutes without opening the door. Monitor the progress through the glass: the anchovies are ready when the breading turns a beautiful golden blonde and the cherry tomatoes appear shrivelled and slightly caramelised. The scent of the sea combined with garlic and lemon should fill your kitchen. Do not overcook, as anchovies are very thin and tend to become tough and dry if left in the heat for too long. Speed is key to maintaining internal succulence.
  10. Remove the tray from the oven and let the anchovies rest for at least 2 minutes before plating. This short rest allows the juices to redistribute within the fillets and the crust to set, making it even crunchier to the bite. Serve the anchovies using a wide spatula to avoid breaking the gratin and accompany with a fresh lemon wedge if desired. The result will be a balanced dish with a sharp contrast between the tenderness of the fish and the crunch of the wholemeal bread. Avoid covering the freshly baked dish with foil, or the steam will soften the crust.

Tips

  • Lightly press the cherry tomatoes onto the anchovies so their juice flavors the breading while cooking.
  • Ensure you place the anchovies skin-side down to keep them moist and tender.
  • Serve the dish immediately out of the oven to enjoy the contrast between the crispy crust and juicy filling.

Variations

  • Omit the cherry tomatoes for a drier version focused on the crunchiness of the breadcrumbs.
  • Use only lemon zest and parsley without oregano for a fresher and more citrusy aromatic profile.
