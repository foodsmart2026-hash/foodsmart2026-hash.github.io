Pasta with sardines

Sicilia · 4 servings · Prep 25 min · Cooking 30 min · Medium

Palermitan-style bucatini with fresh sardines, wild fennel, raisins and pine nuts.

The quintessential Sicilian first course, it combines sweet and salty flavors in a surprising balance between sea and land. Wild fennel and saffron provide an unmistakable aromatic note to this history-rich recipe.

Ingredients

  • Bucatini360 g
  • Fresh sardines, cleaned300 g
  • Wild fennel200 g
  • Onion80 g
  • Raisins20 g
  • Pine nuts20 g
  • Anchovy fillets in oil15 g
  • Saffron powder0.2 g
  • Breadcrumbs40 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil60 ml
  • Fine salt8 g

Allergens

Tree nuts, Gluten, Fish, Sulphites
May contain traces of: Soy.

Method

  1. Start by carefully washing 200 g of wild fennel and boil it in plenty of water with some of the 8 g of salt for about 10 minutes. Once it becomes tender, drain it but make sure to save the cooking water, which you will use later to cook the pasta and infuse it with all the fennel aroma.
  2. Squeeze the boiled fennel well and chop it finely with a knife. Meanwhile, soak 20 g of raisins in a little warm water to rehydrate them and dissolve 0.2 g of saffron in a tablespoon of the hot fennel water; this step helps release color and fragrance evenly throughout the sauce.
  3. In a large skillet, heat 60 ml of extra virgin olive oil and add 80 g of finely chopped onion along with 15 g of anchovy fillets. Let them soften over low heat for about 5 minutes, stirring until the anchovies have completely dissolved, creating a creamy and savory base.
  4. Add the well-squeezed raisins, 20 g of pine nuts, and the previously chopped fennel to the onion. Also, add the dissolved saffron and let everything flavor over medium heat for a couple of minutes until you see the sauce turn a deep yellow and smell an intense herbaceous and sweet aroma.
  5. Place 300 g of cleaned fresh sardines into the skillet and cook them for about 5 minutes over moderate heat. Gently break them with a wooden spoon as they cook, but make sure to leave some larger chunks to provide texture and make the dish more rustic.
  6. While the sauce finishes simmering, toast 40 g of breadcrumbs in a small non-stick pan without adding any fats. Move the pan often for about 2-3 minutes until the bread turns a beautiful amber color and releases a toasted scent; this is the famous Palermo-style 'muddica atturrata'.
  7. Bring the fennel cooking water back to a boil and cook 360 g of bucatini for the time indicated on the package, keeping them very 'al dente'. The hollow shape of bucatini is ideal because it captures the sauce both inside and out, ensuring a burst of flavor in every bite.
  8. Drain the pasta and toss it vigorously in the pan with the sardine sauce for about a minute, adding a splash of cooking water if necessary to bind the ingredients well. Serve the bucatini immediately, finishing each plate with a generous handful of toasted breadcrumbs for the final touch of crunchiness.

Tips

  • Cook the pasta in the fennel cooking water to soak up all the natural aromas.
  • Toast the breadcrumbs until amber without burning them to ensure the right crunchiness.
  • Clean the sardines thoroughly, removing all bones for a more pleasant texture on the palate.

Variations

  • Add some chopped sun-dried tomatoes for an extra hint of acidity and color.
  • Use spaghetti instead of bucatini if you prefer a thinner pasta shape.
