Pan-Fried Potatoes with Garlic and Bay Leaves

4 servings · Prep 15 min · Cooking 25 min · Medium

A rustic and fragrant side dish, a budget-friendly alternative to classic roast potatoes. The combination of garlic and bay leaves provides an intense aroma without extra costs.

Sautéed potatoes are a crispy and flavorful alternative to traditional roasted potatoes. The combination of garlic and bay leaf provides an inviting aroma, resulting in a golden exterior and a tender center.

Ingredients

  • Yellow-fleshed potatoes800 g
  • Garlic2 cloves
  • Fresh or dried bay leaves3 leaves

Allergens

None of the 14 main allergens in the ingredients.

Method

  1. Start by carefully peeling 800g of yellow-fleshed potatoes and cutting them into regular cubes of about 2cm using a sharp knife. It is essential that the pieces are the same size to ensure they all cook at the same time without leaving some raw or others overcooked. Work calmly on the chopping board to achieve perfect symmetry. You will obtain uniform cubes ready for the next treatment. Chef's tip: cutting potatoes uniformly is not just an aesthetic choice, but the technical secret for a professional and even cook.
  2. Submerge the potato cubes in a large bowl filled with cold water and let them rest for about 10 minutes, moving them occasionally with your hands. This step serves to release surface starch, which is the main culprit for potatoes sticking to each other or to the pan during frying. Do not rush and watch the water turn milky. The result will be slightly opaque water and potatoes that will turn out much crispier when cooked. Chef's tip: use very cold water, perhaps adding an ice cube, to keep the potato's cellular structure firm.
  3. Drain the potatoes and arrange them on a clean cotton tea towel, patting them with extreme care until they are perfectly dry. Residual moisture is the number one enemy of the Maillard reaction: if the potatoes are wet, the heat will produce steam instead of creating a crust, making them soggy. Spend a few minutes on this task, ensuring no water droplets remain on the surface of the cubes. The potatoes should appear matte and feel completely dry to the touch. Chef's tip: you can also use kitchen paper to be absolutely certain you have removed every trace of water.
  4. Pour 3 tablespoons of extra virgin olive oil into a very large non-stick frying pan and add 2 crushed garlic cloves and 3 bay leaves. Heat over medium heat for about 2-3 minutes, allowing the essential oils from the aromatics to infuse the cooking fat without burning the garlic. The oil will start to sizzle gently and release an intense scent of Mediterranean herbs. The result is a technical aromatic base that will give depth of flavour to the potatoes. Chef's tip: crush the garlic 'in its skin' (unpeeled) to obtain a more elegant and less pungent aroma that doesn't risk becoming bitter.
  5. Distribute the potatoes in the pan in a single layer, strictly avoiding overlapping them, and turn the heat up to medium-high. Cook for the first 5 minutes without ever touching or turning them, allowing the lively heat to sear the surface in contact with the bottom of the pan. You will hear a steady, lively sizzle and start to see the bottom edges of the cubes turning slightly amber. This step is crucial for forming the first barrier of crispiness. Chef's tip: if the pan is not large enough to hold the potatoes in a single layer, use two separate pans to avoid a 'boiled' effect.
  6. Turn the potatoes with a firm toss of the pan or using a flat silicone spatula, then continue cooking for about 15 minutes over medium heat. Toss the potatoes only every 3-4 minutes to ensure every side of the cube comes into contact with the heat and the scented oil. The kitchen will fill with the classic scent of roast potatoes and the cubes will start to take on a uniform golden colour. The final result of this phase will be an inviting hazelnut colour over most of the surface. Chef's tip: avoid using a fork to turn them, as you would pierce the surface, letting out internal moisture and breaking the cubes.
  7. Lower the heat slightly and cover the pan with a lid for the final 5 minutes of cooking. This technical trick uses the potato's internal moisture to cook the core of the cube, making it melt-in-the-mouth, while the heat from the base continues to maintain the crust. Check the texture by piercing a cube with a skewer: it should go in without meeting any resistance. You will achieve the perfect contrast between a crispy exterior and an interior as soft as mash. Chef's tip: do not exceed the time under the lid, or the condensation will excessively soften the newly created outer crust.
  8. Remove the lid, take out the now-spent garlic and bay leaves, and turn the heat to maximum for one last minute to eliminate any surface moisture. Season with salt and freshly ground black pepper only at this moment, tossing the potatoes one last time to distribute the seasoning thoroughly. The potatoes will appear shiny, golden, and will make a 'dry' sound when tossed in the pan. Serve them immediately on a warm serving plate to maximise the tactile experience of the crunchiness. Chef's tip: adding salt only at the end is the fundamental secret to preventing the potatoes from releasing water and becoming soft.

Tips

  • Dry the potato cubes thoroughly after soaking to achieve a perfectly crispy and golden outer crust.
  • Do not overcrowd the pan to ensure every piece browns evenly in direct contact with the hot surface.
  • Avoid flipping the potatoes too frequently in the first few minutes to prevent them from breaking and to encourage browning.

Variations

  • Add a pinch of dried chili flakes at the end of cooking for a touch of color and a sharper aroma.
  • You can replace fresh garlic with black garlic for a sweeter, more balsamic-like undertone in the final dish.
