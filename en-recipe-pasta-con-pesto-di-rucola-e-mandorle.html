Pasta with Rocket and Almond Pesto

4 servings · Prep 10 min · Cooking 10 min · Easy

A very quick main course with a bold and slightly peppery flavour. Rocket is cheap and available all year round, a great alternative to basil.

💰 Save Euro

Ingredients

  • Mezze maniche (short, wide pasta tubes)320 g
  • Fresh rocket (a peppery salad leaf)100 g
  • Whole almonds (with or without skin)30 g
  • Grated Grana Padano (a savoury, crumbly hard cheese)40 g
  • Garlic1/2 spicchio
  • Extra virgin olive oil50 ml
  • Saltq.b.

Allergens

Gluten, Milk, Tree nuts

Method

  1. Start by bringing a large pot with approximately 3-4 litres of water to a boil, adding about 30g of rock salt only once it has reached a vigorous rolling boil. This stage is crucial to ensure the pasta cooks evenly and maintains a firm texture. Keep the heat high until the water boils, then adjust it to maintain a steady simmer. You will achieve clear, ready water that will properly season the pasta from within. Chef's tip: do not over-salt the water, as the pesto already contains savoury Grana Padano cheese.
  2. While waiting for the water to boil, thoroughly wash 100g of fresh rocket under cold running water and dry it perfectly using a salad spinner. Removing excess water is a critical step; any residual moisture will make the pesto watery rather than creamy. Spend about 2 minutes on this task, ensuring the leaves are dry to the touch. The rocket should appear turgid, bright green, and free of any grit. Chef's tip: pat the leaves with kitchen paper if you still see droplets, as perfect drying is the secret to a stable emulsion.
  3. Take a small non-stick pan and lightly toast 30g of almonds over medium heat for about 2-3 minutes, moving them constantly to prevent burning. Toasting serves to release the essential oils of the nuts, making the pesto much more fragrant and deep in flavour. Stop when the almonds emit a toasted aroma and take on a light golden hue. Let them cool for a moment before proceeding so they retain their crunchiness. Chef's tip: keep a constant eye on the almonds, as they can go from golden to burnt in just a few seconds.
  4. Place the toasted almonds and half a garlic clove (with the central germ removed for better digestibility) into a food processor or a marble mortar. Operate the appliance in short pulses for about 30 seconds, grinding everything until you reach a fine crumble but not yet a paste. Working in bursts prevents the blades from overheating, which preserves the delicate aromas of the garlic and almonds. The result should be a fragrant, coarse powder. Chef's tip: if you prefer a milder flavour, you can blanch the garlic for one minute before using it.
  5. Now add the dry rocket in handfuls to the processor along with 50ml of extra virgin olive oil poured in a thin stream. Pulse again very briefly, only for the time necessary to chop the leaves and incorporate the oil into a rough emulsion. This technique prevents the rocket from oxidising, which would otherwise make it dark and bitter due to the heat generated by the blades. You will obtain a vivid green mixture with a grainy texture. Chef's tip: place the processor blades in the freezer for 10 minutes before use to keep the pesto a brilliant emerald green.
  6. Add 40g of grated Grana Padano cheese and a pinch of salt to the mixture, running the processor one last time for only 5-10 seconds. The cheese must blend perfectly with the other ingredients, providing structure and a balanced umami note to the rocket's pungency. If the consistency seems too thick, add a tablespoon of fridge-cold water to thin the cream without heating it. The final result should be a thick, velvety, and homogeneous sauce. Chef's tip: do not over-salt at this stage; always taste first as the cheese is already quite salty.
  7. Add 320g of mezze maniche to the boiling water and cook following the package instructions, usually about 10 minutes for 'al dente'. Stir immediately with a wooden spoon to prevent the pasta from sticking to the bottom and maintain medium heat so the boil remains constant. Taste the pasta one minute before the end to check its texture: it should offer a slight resistance to the bite. Drain it, making sure to reserve a small cup of the starchy cooking water. Chef's tip: cook the pasta in plenty of water to allow for the optimal release of surface starches.
  8. In a large, pre-warmed bowl, pour the rocket pesto and dilute it with a couple of tablespoons of the reserved cooking water. Stir with a whisk or spoon until you have a fluid and glossy cream that can perfectly coat the pasta. This pre-emulsion step is a chef's secret to prevent the pesto from separating or clumping. The consistency should be similar to a light béchamel sauce. Chef's tip: never heat the pesto directly on the stove, as the residual heat from the pasta and water is sufficient.
  9. Toss the drained mezze maniche into the bowl with the pesto and mix vigorously for 30 seconds, adding more cooking water if necessary. The starch in the water will bind the fats from the oil and cheese, creating a delicious cream that clings perfectly to the pasta's ridges. Every piece of pasta should be uniformly coated and shiny. Serve immediately in shallow bowls, garnishing each portion with the coarsely chopped almonds set aside earlier. Chef's tip: serve in warmed plates to maintain the pesto's creaminess for longer during the meal.
